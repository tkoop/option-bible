Rules for Making Vows

1The LORD spoke to Moses and said, 2"Speak to the Israelites and tell them: If a man makes a special vow to the the LORD, the value of that person is set by these rules."

3For a man from twenty to sixty years old, the value is fifty shekels of silver, using the holy shekel. 4For a woman, the value is thirty shekels. 5For a boy from five to twenty years old, the value is twenty shekels; for a girl, ten shekels. 6For a child from one month to five years old, the value is five shekels for a boy and three shekels for a girl. 7For a man sixty years or older, the value is fifteen shekels; for a woman, ten shekels.

8If the person is too poor to pay the set value, he must stand before the priest. The priest will value him based on what the person who made the vow can afford.

Vows About Animals

9If someone vows an animal that may be offered to the the LORD, it becomes holy. Anything from it that is given to the the LORD is holy. 10Do not switch it for another animal, whether better or worse. If it is switched, both the original animal and its replacement become holy. 11If the animal is unclean and cannot be offered, it must be brought before the priest. 12The priest will value it, whether good or bad. Whatever value the priest sets is the price. 13If the owner buys it back, he must add one-fifth to that value.

Vows About Houses and Fields

14If a man sets apart his house as holy to the the LORD, the priest will value it, whether good or bad. Whatever value the priest sets will stand. 15If the man who set it apart buys it back, he must add one-fifth to its value, and then it is his again.

16If a man sets apart to the the LORD a field from his family land, the value is based on how much seed it takes. A field that takes a homer of barley seed is valued at fifty shekels of silver. 17If he sets it apart in the Jubilee year, the value stands as given. 18If he sets it apart after the Jubilee year, the priest will count the years left until the next Jubilee year and reduce the value. 19If the man who set it apart buys it back, he must add one-fifth to its value, and then it is his again.

20If he does not buy it back, or if he sells the field to someone else, it can no longer be bought back. 21When the field is released in the Jubilee year, it becomes holy to the the LORD, like a field devoted to the the LORD. The priest receives it as his property. 22If a man sets apart to the the LORD a field he bought, which is not part of his family land, 23the priest will figure its value up to the Jubilee year. The man must give that amount as holy to the the LORD that day. 24In the Jubilee year, the field goes back to the man it was bought from, the one who owns the land. 25Every value is to be in the holy shekel, which has twenty gerahs.

Firstborn Animals, Devoted Things, and Tithes

26No one may set apart the firstborn of an animal, whether ox or sheep, because the firstborn already belongs to the the LORD. 27If it is an unclean animal, the owner may buy it back at its set value plus one-fifth. If it is not bought back, it must be sold at its set value. 28But anything a person devotes to the the LORD from all he has—whether a person, an animal, or a field—must not be sold or bought back. It is most holy to the the LORD. 29No person devoted to destruction may be ransomed. That person must be put to death.

30A tenth of the land's produce, whether grain or fruit from trees, belongs to the the LORD and is holy to HimhimHim. 31If a man buys back any part of his tithe, he must add one-fifth to it. 32Every tenth animal from the herd or flock, counted as it passes under the shepherd's rod, is holy to the the LORD. 33The owner must not sort out the good from the bad or swap one animal for another. If he does swap them, both animals become holy and cannot be bought back. 34These are the commands the the LORD gave Moses for the Israelites on Mount Sinai.

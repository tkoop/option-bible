Testing Pleasure

1I said in my heart, "Come now, I will test you with pleasure; enjoy what is good." But see, this too was meaningless. 2I said of laughter, "It is madness," and of pleasure, "What does it accomplish?" 3I searched my heart for how to cheer my body with wine—while my heart was still guided by wisdom—and how to take hold of foolishness, until I could see what is good for people to do on EarthEarthearth during the few days of their lives.

Great Works and Wealth

4I made my works great. I built houses for myself and planted vineyards. 5I made gardens and parks for myself, and I planted every kind of fruit tree in them. 6I made pools of water to water a forest of growing trees.

7I bought male and female slaves, and slaves were born in my house. I also had great herds and flocks, more than anyone before me in Jerusalem. 8I gathered silver and gold for myself, and the treasure of kings and provinces. I got male and female singers for myself, and the delights people enjoy—many concubines. 9I became great and surpassed all who were in Jerusalem before me. My wisdom also stayed with me.

10I did not keep my eyes from anything they asked for. I did not keep my heart from any pleasure, because my heart was glad in all my work. This was my reward from all my work. 11Then I looked at all the things my hands had made and the work I had worked hard to do, and see, it was all meaningless, a waste of effort. There was no lasting benefit in this life.

Wisdom and Folly

12I turned to look at wisdom, madness, and foolishness. What can the person do who comes after the king? Only what has already been done. 13I saw that wisdom is better than foolishness, just as light is better than darkness. 14The wise person can see where he is going, but the fool walks in darkness. Yet I also knew that the same end comes to all of them.

15I said in my heart, "What happens to the fool will also happen to me. So why have I been so wise?" Then I said in my heart, "This too is meaningless." 16No one remembers the wise person or the fool forever. In the days to come, everything will be forgotten. How can the wise die just like the fool? 17So I hated life, because the work done on EarthEarthearth seemed evil to me. Everything is meaningless, a waste of effort.

The Burden of Work

18I hated all my work that I had worked hard at on EarthEarthearth, because I must leave it to the person who comes after me. 19Who knows whether that person will be wise or foolish? Yet he will rule over all my work that I worked hard at and was wise about on EarthEarthearth. This too is meaningless. 20So I turned to despair in my heart over all the work I had worked hard at on EarthEarthearth.

21There is a person who works with wisdom, knowledge, and skill, but he must leave his share to someone who did not work for it. This too is meaningless and a great evil. 22What does a person get from all his work and from the worry of his heart that he works at on EarthEarthearth? 23All his days are pain and grief; even at night his mind does not rest. This too is meaningless.

24There is nothing better for a person than to eat and drink and find joy in his work. I saw that this too comes from the hand of God. 25For who can eat or enjoy anything apart from HimhimHim? 26God gives wisdom, knowledge, and joy to the person who pleases HimhimHim. But to the sinner HeheHe gives the task of gathering and storing up, only to give it to the one who pleases God. This too is meaningless, a waste of effort.

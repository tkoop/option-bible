Solomon Builds His Palace

1It took Solomon thirteen years to finish building his palace.

2He built the House of the ForestforestForest of Lebanon. It was one hundred cubits long, fifty cubits wide, and thirty cubits high. It stood on four rows of cedar pillars, with cedar beams resting on the pillars. 3Cedar panels covered the beams above the pillars. There were forty-five beams, with fifteen in each row. 4There were three rows of windows, and each row faced the one across from it. 5All the doorways and posts were square, and the windows were set in three matching rows.

6He made the Hall of Pillars fifty cubits long and thirty cubits wide. It had a porch in front, with a roof supported by pillars. 7He also built the Hall of the Throne, where he judged cases. Cedar panels covered it from floor to ceiling.

8His own house, where he lived, was in another courtyard behind the hall. It was built in the same style. He also built a house for Pharaoh's daughter, whom he had married. It was like the hall.

9All these buildings were made with costly stones. The stones were cut to size and trimmed with saws, both inside and outside. This was done from the foundation to the roofline, and from the outside to the large courtyard. 10The foundation was made of large, costly stones. Some were ten cubits long, and some were eight cubits long. 11Above them were high-quality stones, cut to size, and cedar beams. 12The large courtyard was surrounded by three rows of cut stone and one row of cedar beams. The inner courtyard of the house of the the LORD and its porch had the same kind of wall.

Hiram Makes the Bronze Pillars

13King Solomon sent for Hiram and brought him from Tyre. 14Hiram was the son of a widow from the tribe of Naphtali. His father was from Tyre and worked with bronze. Hiram had great wisdom, understanding, and skill for all kinds of bronze work. He came to King Solomon and did all the work Solomon asked for.

15He cast two bronze pillars. Each one was eighteen cubits high and twelve cubits around. 16He made two bronze capitals to put on top of the pillars. Each capital was five cubits high. 17He decorated the capitals with a network of lattice work and chains. There were seven chains for each capital. 18He placed two rows of pomegranates around each network to cover the capitals on top. He did the same for the other capital. 19The capitals on top of the pillars in the porch were shaped like lilies. They were four cubits high. 20On both pillars, just above the rounded part near the network, there were two hundred pomegranates in rows around each capital.

21He set up the pillars at the porch of the temple. The pillar on the south he named Jachin, and the pillar on the north he named Boaz. 22The tops of the pillars were shaped like lilies. In this way the work on the pillars was finished.

The Bronze Sea and Stands

23He made the SeaseaSea of cast bronze. It was round, ten cubits from rim to rim, five cubits high, and thirty cubits around. 24Below the rim, gourds went all the way around it. There were ten gourds for every cubit. They were cast in two rows as part of the SeaseaSea. 25The SeaseaSea rested on twelve oxen. Three faced north, three faced west, three faced south, and three faced east. All their hind parts pointed toward the centrecentrecenter. 26The SeaseaSea was a handbreadth thick. Its rim was shaped like the rim of a cup, like a lily flower. It held two thousand baths.

27He made ten bronze stands. Each stand was four cubits long, four cubits wide, and three cubits high. 28This was how the stands were made. They had panels, and the panels were set between the frames. 29On the panels between the frames were lions, oxen, and cherubim. On the frames above and below the lions and oxen were wreaths of hammered work. 30Each stand had four bronze wheels with bronze axles. Four legs supported the basin from below. The supports were cast with wreaths on each side. 31The opening was inside the crown, which rose one cubit above it. The opening was round, like a bowl, one and a half cubits across. Its sides had engravings, and the panels were square, not round. 32The four wheels were under the panels. The axles were attached to the stand. Each wheel was one and a half cubits across.

33The wheels were made like chariot wheels. Their axles, rims, spokes, and hubs were all cast metal. 34There were four supports at the four corners of each stand. The supports were cast as part of the stand. 35On top of each stand was a round band half a cubit high. The supports and panels were cast as part of the stand. 36He engraved cherubim, lions, and palm trees on the supports and panels. Wreaths went all around. 37He made all ten stands the same size and shape. 38He made ten bronze basins. Each one held forty baths. Each basin was four cubits across. One basin sat on each stand. 39He put five stands on the south side of the house and five on the north side. He placed the SeaseaSea on the south side, at the southeast corner.

Finishing the Temple Furnishings

40Hiram also made the pots, shovels, and sprinkling bowls. So Hiram finished all the work he had promised to do for King Solomon in the house of the the LORD:

41the two pillars; the two bowl-shaped capitals on top of the pillars; the two sets of network that covered the bowl-shaped capitals; 42the four hundred pomegranates for the two sets of network, with two rows of pomegranates for each network that covered the two bowl-shaped capitals on the pillars; 43the ten stands with the ten basins on them; 44the SeaseaSea and the twelve oxen under it; 45the pots, shovels, and sprinkling bowls.

46All these things Hiram made for King Solomon for the house of the the LORD were made of polished bronze. 47Solomon did not weigh these objects, because there were so many. The weight of the bronze could not be measured.

48Solomon also made all the furnishings for the house of the the LORD: the gold altar; the gold table that held the BreadbreadBread of the PresencepresencePresence; 49the lampstands of pure gold, five on the right and five on the left in front of the inner sanctuary; the gold flowers, lamps, and tongs; 50the cups, snuffers, sprinkling bowls, dishes, and censers of pure gold; and the gold hinges for the doors of the inner sanctuary, which is the MostmostMost HolyholyHoly PlaceplacePlace, and for the doors of the main hall. 51So all the work King Solomon had done for the house of the the LORD was finished. Then Solomon brought in the things his father David had set apart as holy: the silver, the gold, and the furnishings. He placed them in the treasuries of the house of the the LORD.

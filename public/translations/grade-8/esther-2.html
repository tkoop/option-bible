1After these things, King Ahasuerus's anger cooled down. He remembered Vashti, what she had done, and what had been decided about her.

The King Looks for a New Queen

2Then the king's servants who served him said, "Let beautiful young women be found for the king. 3Let the king choose officials in every part of his kingdom. They can gather all the beautiful young women to the women's house in the fortress of Susa. Put them under Hegai, the king's eunuch who guards the women, and give them beauty treatments. 4The young woman who pleases the king will become queen instead of Vashti." This idea pleased the king, so he did it.

Mordecai and Esther

5In the fortress of Susa there was a Jewish man named Mordecai. He was the son of Jair, the son of Shimei, the son of Kish, from the tribe of Benjamin. 6He had been carried away from Jerusalem with the captives taken with Jeconiah, king of Judah, when Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, carried them away. 7Mordecai had raised Hadassah, also called Esther. She was his uncle's daughter, and she had no father or mother. She was beautiful in face and form. When her parents died, Mordecai took her in as his own daughter.

8When the king's order and law were heard, many young women were gathered to the fortress of Susa under Hegai. Esther was also taken to the king's house and placed under Hegai, who guarded the women. 9She pleased him and won his favourfavourfavor. He quickly gave her beauty treatments, special food, and seven chosen young women from the king's house. He also moved her and her young women to the best place in the women's house. 10Esther did not tell anyone her people or family, because Mordecai had told her not to. 11Every day Mordecai walked in front of the courtyard of the women's house to learn how Esther was doing and what would happen to her.

12Each young woman had twelve months of beauty treatments before she could go to King Ahasuerus. This followed the women's rules: six months with oil of myrrh and six months with perfumes and other beauty treatments. 13When a young woman went to the king, she could take from the women's house to the king anything she asked for. 14She would go in the evening and return in the morning to the second women's house, under Shaashgaz, the king's eunuch who guarded the concubines. She would not go to the king again unless he was pleased with her and called her by name.

Esther Becomes Queen

15When it was Esther's turn to go to the king, she asked for nothing except what Hegai, the king's eunuch in charge of the women, suggested. Esther was the daughter of Abihail, Mordecai's uncle, and Mordecai had adopted her. Esther won favourfavourfavor with everyone who saw her. 16Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus in the royal house in the tenth month, the month of Tebeth, in the seventh year of his reign. 17The king loved Esther more than all the other women. She won more favourfavourfavor and kindness from him than all the other young women. He placed the royal crown on her head and made her queen instead of Vashti. 18The king gave a great feast for all his officials and servants. It was Esther's feast. He also gave tax relief to the provinces and gave gifts with royal generosity.

19When the young women were gathered a second time, Mordecai was sitting at the king's gate. 20Esther still did not tell anyone her family or people, just as Mordecai had commanded her. She continued to follow Mordecai's instructions, as she had done when he raised her.

Mordecai Saves the King

21In those days, while Mordecai sat at the king's gate, two of the king's eunuchs, Bigthan and Teresh, who guarded the doorway, became angry. They planned to harm King Ahasuerus. 22Mordecai learned about it and told Queen Esther. Esther told the king, using Mordecai's name. 23After the matter was looked into and found true, the two men were hanged on a gallows. The event was written down in the book of the king's daily records.

The Queen of Sheba Visits Solomon

1The queen of Sheba heard about Solomon's fame and about the name of the LORD, so she came to test him with hard questions. 2She arrived in Jerusalem with a very large group, with camels carrying spices, a great deal of gold, and precious stones. She came to Solomon and talked with him about everything she had on her mind. 3Solomon answered all her questions. Nothing was hidden from the king that he did not explain to her. 4When the queen of Sheba saw all of Solomon's wisdom and the palace he had built, 5along with the food on his table, the seating of his servants, the duties of his attendants, their clothing, his cupbearers, and the burnt offering he made at the house of the LORD, she was completely overwhelmed.

6She said to the king, "ThetheThe report I heard in my own country about your words and your wisdom was true. 7I did not believe it until I came and saw it with my own eyes. In fact, I had not been told even half of it. Your wisdom and wealth are far greater than the report I heard. 8How happy your people must be! How happy your servants are who stand before you all the time and listen to your wisdom! 9Praise be to the LORD your God, who delighted in you and put you on the throne of Israel. Because the LORD loved Israel forever, HeheHe made you king so you could carry out justice and righteousness."

10She gave the king one hundred twenty talents of gold, a very large amount of spices, and precious stones. Never again was so much spice brought in as the queen of Sheba gave King Solomon. 11Hiram's ships also brought gold from Ophir, along with a very large amount of almug wood and precious stones. 12King Solomon used the almug wood to make supports for the house of the LORD and the royal palace, and to make lyres and harps for the singers. No almug wood like that has ever been brought in or seen to this day. 13King Solomon gave the queen everything she wanted and asked for, besides what he gave her from his royal generosity. Then she and her servants left and returned to her own country.

Solomon's Great Wealth

14The gold that came to Solomon in one year weighed six hundred sixty-six talents. 15This was besides what came from the traders, the merchants, and all the kings of Arabia and the governors of the land. 16King Solomon made two hundred large shields of hammered gold; six hundred shekels of gold went into each shield. 17He also made three hundred smaller shields of hammered gold; three minas of gold went into each one. The king put them in the House of the ForestforestForest of Lebanon. 18The king also made a great throne of ivory and overlaid it with pure gold. 19The throne had six steps, and its back was rounded at the top. There were armrests on both sides of the seat, and two lions stood beside the armrests. 20Twelve lions stood on the six steps, one at each end of every step. Nothing like this had ever been made for any kingdom. 21All of King Solomon's drinking cups were gold, and all the cups in the House of the ForestforestForest of Lebanon were pure gold. In Solomon's days, silver was considered worthless. 22The king had a fleet of trading ships at sea with Hiram's fleet. Once every three years the fleet came in bringing gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks. 23So King Solomon became greater than all the kings of the earth in wealth and wisdom. 24The whole world wanted to see Solomon and hear the wisdom God had put in his heart. 25Year after year, everyone brought gifts: articles of silver and gold, clothing, weapons, spices, horses, and mules.

Solomon's Horses and Chariots

26Solomon built up his chariots and horsemen. He had fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen. He stationed them in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem. 27The king made silver as common in Jerusalem as stones, and cedar as plentiful as sycamore trees in the foothills. 28Solomon's horses were brought from Egypt and from Kue. The king's merchants bought them from Kue at a set price. 29A chariot could be imported from Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver, and a horse for one hundred fifty. They also exported them to all the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Aram.

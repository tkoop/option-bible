The Jews Fight Back

1On the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month of Adar, the king's order was to be carried out. That was the day the enemies of the Jews had hoped to crush them, but instead the Jews gained power over those who hated them.

2The Jews gathered in their cities in every province of King Xerxes to attack anyone who wanted to hurt them. No one could stand against them, because all the peoples were afraid of them.

3All the officials of the provinces, the governors, and the king's administrators helped the Jews, because they were afraid of Mordecai. 4Mordecai was powerful in the king's palace, and his fame spread through all the provinces. He kept growing more and more powerful.

5The Jews struck down all their enemies with the sword, killing and destroying them. They did as they pleased to those who hated them. 6In the fortress of Susa the Jews killed and destroyed five hundred men.

7They also killed Parshandatha, Dalphon, and Aspatha, 8Poratha, Adalia, and Aridatha, 9Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai, and Vaizatha. 10These were the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews. But they did not take any plunder.

11On that day the number of those killed in the fortress of Susa was reported to the king.

12The king said to Queen Esther, "In the fortress of Susa the Jews have killed and destroyed five hundred men and the ten sons of Haman. What must they have done in the rest of the provinces! What is your request? It will be given to you. What else do you want? It will be done."

13Esther said, "If it pleases the king, let the Jews in Susa be allowed to do tomorrow what they did today, and let the ten sons of Haman be hanged on the gallows."

14So the king commanded that this be done. An order was issued in Susa, and they hanged the ten sons of Haman. 15On the fourteenth day of the month of Adar, the Jews in Susa gathered together and killed three hundred men in the city, but they did not take any plunder.

16The rest of the Jews in the king's provinces also gathered to defend themselves and gain relief from their enemies. They killed seventy-five thousand of those who hated them, but they did not take any plunder.

17This happened on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar. On the fourteenth day they rested and made it a day of feasting and joy. 18But the Jews in Susa had gathered on the thirteenth and fourteenth days, and they rested on the fifteenth day and made it a day of feasting and joy.

19That is why the Jews who live in country villages celebrate the fourteenth day of Adar as a day of joy and feasting, a holiday, and a day for sending food to one another.

Mordecai Writes to the Jews

20Mordecai recorded these events and sent letters to all the Jews in all the provinces of King Xerxes, both near and far. 21He told them to celebrate the fourteenth and fifteenth days of the month of Adar every year. 22He told them to celebrate these days as the time when the Jews got relief from their enemies, and as the month when their sorrow was turned into joy and their mourning into a holiday. They were to celebrate with feasting and joy, sending food to one another and gifts to the poor.

23So the Jews agreed to continue the celebration they had begun, doing what Mordecai had written to them.

24For Haman son of Hammedatha, the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had plotted against the Jews to destroy them and had cast the Pur, that is, the lot, to crush and destroy them. 25But when the plot came to the king's attention, he issued written orders that the evil scheme Haman had devised against the Jews should come back on his own head. He and his sons were to be hanged on the gallows. 26Therefore these days were called Purim, from the word Pur. Because of everything written in this letter and because of what they had seen and what had happened to them, 27the Jews established and accepted as a lasting rule for themselves, their descendants, and all who joined them, that they would not fail to celebrate these two days every year, as written and at the appointed time. 28These days were to be remembered and celebrated by every generation, every family, every province, and every city. These days of Purim were never to pass from among the Jews, and their memory was never to die out among their descendants.

Esther Confirms the Holiday

29So Queen Esther daughter of Abihail, along with Mordecai the Jew, wrote with full authority to confirm this second letter about Purim. 30Letters were sent to all the Jews in the one hundred twenty-seven provinces of the kingdom of Xerxes, with words of peace and truth. 31They confirmed these days of Purim at their appointed times, just as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had established them and just as the people had established them for themselves and their descendants, with instructions for their fasting and mourning. 32So Esther's command confirmed these matters of Purim, and it was written in the book.

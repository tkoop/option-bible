The Workers and the Offerings

1Bezalel, Oholiab, and every skilled worker whose heart the LORD had filled with wisdom and understanding to know how to do all the work for the holy place did everything just as the LORD had commanded. The LORD had given them that wisdom and understanding.

2Moses called Bezalel, Oholiab, and every skilled worker whose heart the LORD had filled with wisdom and whose heart moved him to come and do the work.

3They received from Moses all the offering the Israelites had brought for the work of serving in the holy place. The people kept bringing more freewill offerings every morning.

4All the skilled workers who were doing the work for the holy place came, each from his own task.

5They said to Moses, "ThetheThe people are bringing much more than enough for the work the LORD commanded us to do."

6Moses gave a command, and they sent word through the camp: "No man or woman should make anything more as an offering for the holy place." So the people stopped bringing gifts.

7The materials were enough for all the work, with some left over.

The Tabernacle Curtains

8All the skilled workers made the tabernacle with ten curtains of fine twisted linen, blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, with cherubim skillfully worked into them. 9Each curtain was 28 cubits long and 4 cubits wide. All the curtains were the same size.

10They joined five curtains together, and the other five curtains together. 11They made loops of blue yarn on the edge of the end curtain in the first set, and did the same on the edge of the end curtain in the second set. 12They made fifty loops on one curtain and fifty loops on the edge of the curtain in the second set, with the loops opposite each other. 13They made fifty gold clasps and joined the curtains to one another with the clasps, so the tabernacle became one piece.

The Tent Coverings and Frame

14They made curtains of goat hair for a tent over the tabernacle. They made eleven curtains. 15Each curtain was 30 cubits long and 4 cubits wide. All eleven curtains were the same size. 16They joined five curtains together and six curtains together. 17They made fifty loops on the edge of the end curtain in the first set, and fifty loops on the edge of the matching curtain in the second set. 18They made fifty bronze clasps to join the tent together into one piece. 19They made a covering for the tent of ram skins dyed red, and over that a covering of fine leather.

20They made upright frames of acacia wood for the tabernacle. 21Each frame was 10 cubits long and 1.5 cubits wide. 22Each frame had two tenons joined together. They made all the frames of the tabernacle this way. 23They made twenty frames for the south side of the tabernacle. 24They made forty silver bases to go under the twenty frames—two bases under each frame for its two tenons.

25For the second side of the tabernacle, the north side, they made twenty frames. 26Their forty silver bases were two bases under each frame. 27For the west side of the tabernacle, they made six frames. 28They made two frames for the corners of the tabernacle at the back. 29These corner frames were joined at the bottom and joined at the top to one ring. They made both corners this way. 30So there were eight frames with sixteen silver bases—two bases under each frame.

31They made five crossbars of acacia wood for the frames on one side of the tabernacle. 32They made five crossbars for the frames on the other side, and five crossbars for the frames at the back of the tabernacle, on the west. 33They made the middle crossbar to run from end to end through the middle of the frames. 34They overlaid the frames with gold and made gold rings to hold the crossbars. They also overlaid the crossbars with gold.

The Inner Curtain and Entrance Screen

35They made the inner curtain of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine twisted linen, with cherubim skillfully worked into it. 36They made four pillars of acacia wood for it and overlaid them with gold. They also made gold hooks for them and cast four silver bases for them. 37For the entrance to the tent, they made a screen of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine twisted linen, embroidered with needlework. 38They made five pillars with hooks for them. They overlaid the tops of the pillars and their bands with gold, and their five bases were bronze.

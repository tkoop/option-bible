Enemies Offer to Help Build

1When the enemies of Judah and Benjamin heard that the people who had been taken into exile were building a temple for the LORD, the God of Israel, 2they came to Zerubbabel and the family leaders and said, "Let us build with you, for we seek your God just as you do, and we have sacrificed to HimhimHim since the days of Esarhaddon, king of Assyria, who brought us here." 3But Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the other family leaders told them, "YouyouYou have no share with us in building a house for our God. We will build it ourselves for the LORD, the God of Israel, just as King Cyrus of Persia commanded us."

Opposition Slows the Work

4Then the people of the land discouraged the people of Judah and made them afraid to build. 5They also hired counselors to work against them and frustrate their plans all the days of King Cyrus of Persia, and until the reign of King Darius of Persia. 6At the beginning of the reign of King Ahasuerus, they wrote an accusation against the people living in Judah and Jerusalem.

A Letter to King Artaxerxes

7In the days of King Artaxerxes of Persia, Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel, and their associates wrote to King Artaxerxes. The letter was written in Aramaic and translated. 8Rehum the commander and Shimshai the scribe wrote a letter against Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes in this way: 9Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe, and their associates—the Dinaites, Apharsathchites, Tarpelites, Apharsites, Arkvites, Babylonians, Susanchites, Dehavites, Elamites, 10and the rest of the nations whom the great and noble Osnappar brought over and settled in the cities of Samaria and in the rest of the region beyond the River—wrote this letter. 11This is a copy of the letter they sent to him: 12"Let it be known to the king that the Jews who came up from you to us have gone to Jerusalem. They are rebuilding that rebellious and wicked city. They are finishing its walls and repairing its foundations. 13Let it now be known to the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are completed, they will not pay tribute, taxes, or tolls, and the royal revenue will suffer. 14Now, because we serve the king and do not want to see him dishonoureddishonoureddishonored, we are sending this to inform the king. 15Let a search be made in the records of your ancestors. In those records you will find that this city has long been rebellious and harmful to kings and provinces. Uprisings have been stirred up in it since ancient times. That is why this city was destroyed. 16We are informing the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are completed, you will have no share in the region beyond the River."

The King Orders Work to Stop

17King Artaxerxes sent this reply to Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe, and their associates in Samaria and the rest of the region beyond the River: 18"Greetings. Peace be with you. The letter you sent has been read clearly in my presence. 19I ordered a search, and it was found that this city has risen up against kings for a long time. Rebellion and revolt have been made in it. 20Powerful kings have ruled over Jerusalem and governed the whole region beyond the River, and tribute, taxes, and tolls were paid to them. 21Now issue an order to stop these men. This city must not be rebuilt until I give further orders. 22Be careful not to neglect this matter. Why should the damage increase and harm the kings?" 23When the copy of the letter was read before Rehum, Shimshai, and their associates, they hurried to Jerusalem and forced the Jews to stop by force. 24Then the work on the house of God in Jerusalem stopped until the second year of the reign of King Darius of Persia.

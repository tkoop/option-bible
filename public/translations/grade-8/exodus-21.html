1These are the rules you must set before them.

Rules for Servants

2If you buy a Hebrew servant, he must serve six years. In the seventh year, he goes free without paying anything. 3If he came alone, he leaves alone. If he has a wife, she leaves with him. 4If his master gives him a wife and she has sons or daughters for him, the wife and her children belong to the master. The man leaves alone.

5But if the servant says, “I love my master, my wife, and my children. I will not go free,” 6then his master must take him to God. He must take him to the door or the doorpost and pierce his ear with an awl. Then the servant will serve his master for life.

7If a man sells his daughter as a servant, she does not go free the way male servants do. 8If she does not please the master who chose her for himself, he must let her be bought back. He may not sell her to foreigners, because he broke his promise to her. 9If he chooses her for his son, he must treat her like a daughter. 10If he takes another wife, he must not reduce the first wife’s food, clothing, or rights as a wife. 11If he does not do these three things for her, she goes free without paying money.

Laws About Injury and Death

12Anyone who hits a person and kills him must be put to death. 13But if he did not plan it, and God let it happen, I will give you a place where he can run. 14But if someone plans to kill his neighbourneighbourneighbor on purpose, you must take him from MymyMy altar so he can be put to death.

15Anyone who hits his father or mother must be put to death. 16Anyone who kidnaps a person and sells him, or is caught with him, must be put to death. 17Anyone who curses his father or mother must be put to death.

18If men quarrel and one hits the other with a stone or his fist, and the man does not die but stays in bed, 19and later gets up and walks outside with a cane, the one who hit him is not guilty. He only has to pay for the time the man could not work and pay for his healing.

20If a man beats his male or female servant with a rod and the servant dies from it, the man must be punished. 21But if the servant lives for a day or two, the owner is not punished, because the servant is his property.

22If men fight and hit a pregnant woman, causing her baby to be born too early, but no serious harm comes to her or the baby, the man who hit her must pay a fine. The husband sets the amount, and he pays what the judges decide. 23But if there is serious harm, you must take a life for a life. 24Eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot. 25Burn for burn, wound for wound, and bruise for bruise.

26If a man hits his male or female servant and ruins the servant’s eye, he must set the servant free because of the eye. 27If he knocks out the servant’s tooth, he must set the servant free because of the tooth.

Laws About Animals and Property

28If an ox gores a man or woman to death, the ox must be stoned, and no one may eat its meat. The owner is not guilty. 29But if the ox has gored before and the owner was warned but did not restrain it, and it kills a man or woman, the ox must be stoned and the owner must also be put to death. 30If a payment is set to save his life, he must pay whatever amount is set. 31This same rule applies if the ox gores a son or a daughter.

32If the ox gores a male or female servant, the owner must pay the servant’s master thirty silver coins, and the ox must be stoned.

33If a man uncovers a pit or digs one and does not cover it, and an ox or donkey falls into it, 34the owner of the pit must pay. He must give money to the animal’s owner, and he keeps the dead animal.

35If one man’s ox kills another man’s ox, they must sell the living ox and divide the money. They must also divide the dead ox. 36But if it was known that the ox had gored before and the owner did not restrain it, the owner must pay one ox for the other, and he keeps the dead ox.

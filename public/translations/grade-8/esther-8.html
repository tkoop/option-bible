Haman's House Is Given to Esther

1On that day King Ahasuerus gave Queen Esther the house of Haman, the enemy of the Jews. Then Mordecai came before the king, because Esther had told the king how Mordecai was related to her. 2The king took off his signet ring, the one he had taken from Haman, and gave it to Mordecai. Esther put Mordecai in charge of Haman's house.

Esther Pleads for the Jews

3Esther spoke to the king again. She fell at his feet, cried, and begged him to stop the evil plan of Haman the Agagite and the plot he had made against the Jews. 4The king held out his golden sceptresceptrescepter to Esther. She got up and stood in front of the king.

5Then she said, "If it pleases the king, and if I have found favourfavourfavor with him, and if the king thinks this is right and I am pleasing to him, let a written order be sent to cancel the letters that Haman son of Hammedatha, the Agagite, wrote. He wrote them to destroy the Jews in all the king's provinces. 6How could I bear to see the terrible trouble coming to my people? How could I bear to see my own family destroyed?"

The King Issues a New Decree

7The king said to Queen Esther and Mordecai the Jew, "I have given Esther Haman's house, and Haman has been hanged on the gallows because he tried to harm the Jews. 8Now you write to the Jews in the king's name, whatever you think is best. Seal it with the king's signet ring. A law written in the king's name and sealed with his ring cannot be changed."

9In the third month, the month of Sivan, on the twenty-third day, the king's scribes were called. They wrote everything Mordecai commanded for the Jews and for the rulers of the provinces from India to Ethiopia—127 provinces in all. The message was written in each province's own writing and language, and also in the Jews' writing and language. 10The letters were written in King Ahasuerus's name and sealed with his signet ring. They were sent by couriers on fast horses from the royal stables.

11The letters gave the Jews in every city permission to gather together and defend their lives. They could destroy, kill, and wipe out any armed force from any people or province that attacked them, along with their women and children. They could also take their enemies' property. 12This was to happen on one day in all the king's provinces: the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month of Adar. 13A copy of the order was to be made into law in every province and shown to all the peoples. This way the Jews would be ready on that day to pay back their enemies. 14The couriers, riding the royal horses, left quickly because the king commanded it. The order was also announced in the fortress of Susa.

The Jews Rejoice

15Mordecai left the king's presence wearing royal clothes of blue and white, a large gold crown, and a robe of fine linen and purple. The city of Susa cheered and celebrated. 16For the Jews, it was a time of light, gladness, joy, and honourhonourhonor. 17In every province and city, wherever the king's order and law reached, the Jews had gladness and joy. They held feasts and holidays. Many people in the land became Jews, because they were afraid of the Jews.

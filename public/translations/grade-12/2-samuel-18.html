David Prepares for Battle

1David mustered the troops who were with him and appointed commanders over thousands and commanders over hundreds. 2He dispatched a third of the forces under Joab's command, a third under Abishai son of Zeruiah—Joab's brother—and a third under Ittai the Gittite. Then the king told the people, "I myself will certainly march out with you."

3But the people answered, "YouyouYou must not go out. If we flee, they will not care about us; even if half of us die, they will not care about us. But you are worth ten thousand of us, so it is better for you to support us from the city."

4The king replied, "I will do whatever seems best to you." Then the king stood beside the gate, and all the people marched out by hundreds and by thousands.

5The king ordered Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, "Deal gently with the young man Absalom, for my sake." All the troops heard the king give this order concerning Absalom.

The Battle in the Forest of Ephraim

6So the army marched into the field against Israel, and the battle took place in the forest of Ephraim. 7There the Israelites were defeated by David's servants, and the slaughter that day was staggering—twenty thousand men. 8The fighting spread across the whole countryside, and the forest claimed more lives that day than the sword did.

9Absalom happened to meet David's servants while riding his mule. The mule went under the thick branches of a great oak, and Absalom's head caught in the tree; he was left hanging between Heavenheavenheaven and EarthEarthearth while the mule kept going.

10When one of the men saw this, he reported to Joab, "I have just seen Absalom hanging from an oak."

11Joab said to the man who had reported it, "What! You saw him, and you did not strike him to the ground right there? I would have given you ten shekels of silver and a belt."

12The man replied, "Even if a thousand shekels of silver were weighed into my hand, I would not raise my hand against the king's son, because we all heard the king command you, Abishai, and Ittai, 'Protect the young man Absalom.'" 13"Otherwise I would have betrayed my own life, for nothing is hidden from the king, and you would have turned against me."

14Joab said, "I will not waste time arguing with you." He took three javelins in his hand and drove them into Absalom's heart while Absalom was still alive in the oak.

15Then ten of Joab's young armourarmourarmor-bearers surrounded Absalom, struck him, and killed him.

16Joab blew the trumpet, and the troops stopped pursuing Israel, because Joab restrained them.

17They took Absalom's body, threw it into a large pit in the forest, and piled a huge heap of stones over it. Meanwhile all Israel fled, each to his own home.

18During his lifetime Absalom had taken a pillar and erected it for himself in the King's ValleyvalleyValley, saying, "I have no son to preserve my name." He named the pillar after himself, and it is still called Absalom's MonumentmonumentMonument.

News Reaches David

19Then Ahimaaz son of Zadok said, "Let me run and bring the king the news that the LORD has vindicated him against his enemies."

20Joab replied, "YouyouYou are not the man to carry news today. You may do so another time, but not today, because the king's son is dead."

21Then Joab said to the Cushite, "Go and tell the king what you have seen." The Cushite bowed to Joab and ran off.

22Ahimaaz son of Zadok said again to Joab, "Whatever happens, please let me run after the Cushite."

Joab asked, "Why do you want to run, my son? You will gain no reward for this news."

"But I want to run," Ahimaaz said.

So Joab said, "Run." 23Then Ahimaaz ran by way of the plain and outran the Cushite.

24David was sitting between the inner and outer gates. The watchman went up to the roof of the gate by the wall, looked out, and saw a man running alone.

25The watchman called out and reported to the king. The king said, "If he is alone, he brings good news." The runner kept coming closer.

26The watchman saw another man running and called to the gatekeeper, "Look, another man running alone!"

The king said, "HeheHe too brings good news."

27The watchman said, "I recognizerecogniserecognize the first runner's stride; it is like Ahimaaz son of Zadok."

The king said, "HeheHe is a good man and comes with good news."

28Ahimaaz called out to the king, "All is well!" He bowed down before the king with his face to the ground and said, "BlessedblessedBlessed be the LORD your God, who has delivered up the men who raised their hands against my lord the king."

29The king asked, "IsisIs the young man Absalom safe?"

Ahimaaz answered, "When Joab sent your servant off, I saw a great commotion, but I did not know what it was."

30The king said, "Step aside and stand here." So Ahimaaz stepped aside and stood still.

31Then the Cushite arrived and said, "Good news for my lord the king! Today the LORD has vindicated you against all who rose up against you."

32The king asked, "IsisIs the young man Absalom safe?"

The Cushite replied, "May the enemies of my lord the king, and all who rise against you to harm you, become like that young man."

David Mourns Absalom

33The king was deeply shaken. He went up to the room above the gate and wept. As he walked, he cried out, "O my son Absalom—my son, my son Absalom! If only I had died instead of you, Absalom, my son, my son!"

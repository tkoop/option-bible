Paul’s Godly Jealousy and Concern for the Corinthians

1I wish you would bear with me in a little foolishness; but indeed bear with me. 2For I am jealous for you with a godly jealousy, for I betrothed you to one husband, to present you as a pure virgin to Christ. 3But I am afraid that, just as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, your thoughts may be led astray from your sincere and pure devotion to Christ. 4For if someone comes and proclaims another Jesus than the one we proclaimed, or if you receive a different spirit from the one you received, or a different gospel from the one you accepted, you readily put up with it.

Paul’s Apostolic Equality and Selfless Service

5For I consider that I am not at all inferior to these super-apostles. 6Even if I am unskilled in speech, I am not lacking in knowledge; indeed, in every way we have made this clear to you in all things. 7Did I commit a sin in humbling myself so that you might be exalted, because I preached the gospel of God to you free of charge? 8I robbed other churches by accepting support from them in order to serve you. 9And when I was with you and was in need, I did not burden anyone, for the brothers who came from Macedonia supplied my need. So I refrained and will refrain from burdening you in any way.

Paul’s Boasting and Warning Against Deceivers

10As the truth of Christ is in me, this boasting of mine will not be silenced in the regions of Achaia. 11Why? Because I do not love you? God knows. 12And what I am doing I will continue to do, in order to cut off the opportunity of those who want an opportunity, so that they may be found just as we are in the very matter of which they boast. 13For such men are false apostles, deceitful workmen, disguising themselves as apostles of Christ. 14And no wonder, for even Satan disguises himself as an angel of light. 15So it is no great thing if his servants also disguise themselves as servants of righteousness. Their end will correspond to their deeds.

Paul’s Foolish Boasting and Record of Suffering

16I repeat, let no one think me foolish. But even if you do, receive me as a fool so that I too may boast a little. 17What I am saying in this matter of boasting, I am not saying as the Lord would, but as a fool. 18Since many boast according to the flesh, I too will boast. 19For you gladly bear with fools, being wise yourselves. 20For you bear with it if anyone enslaves you, if anyone devours you, if anyone takes from you, if anyone exalts himself, if anyone strikes you on the face. 21I say this to my shame: we were too weak for that! Yet in whatever respect anyone else dares to boast—I am speaking foolishly—I too dare to boast.

22Are they Hebrews? So am I. Are they Israelites? So am I. Are they offspring of Abraham? So am I. 23Are they servants of Christ? I am speaking as a madman—I am more so: with far greater labourslabourslabors, with far more imprisonments, with beatings beyond measure, with frequent brushes with death.

24Five times I received from the Jews the forty lashes minus one. 25Three times I was beaten with rods, once I was stoned, three times I was shipwrecked; a night and a day I spent in the open sea. 26In frequent journeys I have faced dangers from rivers, from robbers, from my own countrymen, from Gentiles, in the city, in the wilderness, at sea, and among false brothers. 27I have endured toil and hardship, often without sleep, hunger and thirst, often without food, cold and exposure.

28And apart from other things, there is the daily pressure on me of my anxiety for all the churches. 29Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is made to stumble, and I do not burn with indignation? 30If I must boast, I will boast of the things that show my weakness. 31The God and FatherfatherFather of the Lord Jesus, HeheHe who is blessed forever, knows that I am not lying. 32In Damascus the governor under King Aretas was guarding the city of the Damascenes in order to seize me. 33But I was let down in a basket through a window in the wall, and escaped his hands.

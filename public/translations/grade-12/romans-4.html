Abraham and the Question of Justification

1What, then, shall we say that Abraham, our forefather according to the flesh, discovered? 2If Abraham was justified by works, he would have grounds for boasting—but not before God.

3For what does ScripturescriptureScripture say?

“Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness.”

4Now to the one who works, wages are not reckoned as grace but as debt. 5Yet to the one who does not work but believes in HimhimHim who justifies the ungodly, that person’s faith is reckoned as righteousness.

David's Witness to Grace

6David likewise speaks of the blessedness of the man to whom God credits righteousness apart from works:

7“Blessed are those whose lawless deeds are forgiven and whose sins are covered. 8Blessed is the man whose sin the Lord will never count against him.”

Circumcision, Promise, and Faith

9Is this blessedness confined to the circumcised, or does it extend to the uncircumcised as well? We affirm that faith was credited to Abraham as righteousness. 10How, then, was it credited—while he was circumcised or while he was uncircumcised? It was not after circumcision, but before it. 11He received circumcision as a sign, a seal of the righteousness he already possessed through faith while uncircumcised, so that he might become the father of all who believe though uncircumcised, with righteousness credited to them. 12And he is likewise the father of the circumcised who not merely bear the mark but follow in the footsteps of the faith our father Abraham exercised while uncircumcised.

13For the promise to Abraham and his offspring that he would be heir of the world was not through the law but through the righteousness of faith. 14For if those who adhere to the law are heirs, faith is emptied and the promise is nullified. 15For the law produces wrath, but where there is no law, there is no transgression.

16Therefore it is by faith, so that the promise may rest on grace and be guaranteed to all his offspring—not only to those of the law but also to those of the faith of Abraham, who is the father of us all.

17As it is written,

“I have made you a father of many nations.”

He is our father in the sight of God, in whom he believed—the God who gives life to the dead and calls into being things that do not exist.

Abraham's Unwavering Faith

18Against all hope, Abraham in hope believed and so became the father of many nations, just as it had been said to him,

“So shall your offspring be.”

19Without weakening in faith, he acknowledged the decrepitude of his own body—since he was about a hundred years old—and the deadness of Sarah’s womb. 20Yet he did not waver through unbelief regarding the promise of God, but was strengthened in faith and gave glory to God. 21He was fully persuaded that God was able to do what HeheHe had promised.

22That is why

“it was credited to him as righteousness.”

23The words “it was credited to him” were not written for him alone, 24but also for us, to whom it will be credited—those who believe in HimhimHim who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead. 25He was delivered over to death for our trespasses and was raised for our justification.

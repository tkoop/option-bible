Acceptable Sacrifices and the Rejection of Idolatry

1Do not sacrifice to the LORD your God an ox or a sheep that has any blemish or defect, for such an offering is detestable to HimhimHim.

2If a man or a woman among you in one of the towns that the LORD your God gives you is found doing evil in the sight of the LORD your God by breaking HishisHis covenant, 3and, contrary to MymyMy command, has worshiped other gods—bowing down to them, or to the sun, the moon, or any of the heavenly bodies— 4and this is reported to you, then you must investigate it meticulously. If the report proves true and the matter is established—that this detestable thing has been done in Israel— 5you must bring that man or woman who has committed this wickedness to the gates of your city and stone them to death.

6On the testimony of two or three witnesses the accused must be put to death, but no one may be executed on the testimony of a single witness. 7The witnesses must be the first to put the person to death, and then all the people must take part. You must remove the evil from among you.

Judicial Appeals to the Central Sanctuary

8If a case comes before your courts that is too difficult for you to judge—whether it concerns bloodshed, legal disputes, or bodily injury—then you must go up to the place that the LORD your God will choose. 9You must go to the Levitical priests and to the judge in office at that time, inquire of them, and they will declare to you the verdict in the case.

10You must act according to the decision they give you at the place the LORD will choose, and you must be careful to do everything they instruct you to do. 11You must act according to the law they teach you and according to the judgmentjudgementjudgment they pronounce; do not deviate from what they tell you in any way. 12The man who acts arrogantly, refusing to listen either to the priest who stands there to minister before the LORD your God or to the judge, must be put to death. You must purge the evil from Israel. 13Then all the people will hear and be afraid, and they will not act so arrogantly again.

The Appointment and Restraint of a King

14When you enter the land that the LORD your God is giving you, and you take possession of it, and you settle in it, and then you say, "Let us appoint a king over us, as all the nations around us have," 15be sure to set over you a king whom the LORD your God chooses. He must be one of your own people; do not set a foreigner over you, one who is not an Israelite.

16The king must not acquire many horses for himself, and he must not make the people return to Egypt to obtain more horses, for the LORD has said to you, "YouyouYou are not to go back that way again."

17He must not take many wives, lest he be drawn into moral compromise, nor may he accumulate excessive silver and gold.

18When he sits on the throne of his kingdom, he must write for himself a copy of this law on a scroll, taken from the one kept before the Levitical priests. 19It must remain with him, and he must read it all the days of his life, so that he may learn to revere the LORD his God and to observe faithfully all the words of this law and these statutes. 20Then he will not regard himself as superior to his fellow Israelites, and he will not deviate from the commandment in any respect, so that he and his sons may reign long over his kingdom in Israel.

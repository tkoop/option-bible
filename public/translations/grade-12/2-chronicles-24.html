Joash’s Reign and the Repair of the Temple

1Joash was seven years old when he became king, and he reigned forty years in Jerusalem. His mother was Zibiah, from Beersheba. 2Joash did what was right in the eyes of the LORD throughout the lifetime of Jehoiada the priest.

3Jehoiada chose two wives for him, and he fathered sons and daughters.

4Afterward Joash resolved in his heart to restore the house of the LORD. 5He gathered the priests and Levites and said to them, “Go into the cities of Judah and collect, year by year, the money due from all Israel for repairing the house of your God, and see that you carry out the matter without delay.” But the Levites did not act promptly.

6So the king summoned Jehoiada the chief priest and asked him, “Why have you not required the Levites to bring from Judah and Jerusalem the tax that Moses, the servant of the LORD, imposed on Israel for the tent of testimony?”

7For the sons of Athaliah, that wicked woman, had broken into the house of God and had even used all the dedicated things of the house of the LORD for the Baals.

8At the king’s command they made a chest and placed it outside, at the gate of the house of the LORD. 9Then they issued a proclamation throughout Judah and Jerusalem, calling the people to bring to the LORD the tax that Moses, the servant of God, had imposed on Israel in the wilderness. 10All the leaders and all the people rejoiced; they brought their contributions and cast them into the chest until it was full.

11Whenever the Levites brought the chest to the king’s officials, and when they saw that there was much money, the king’s secretary and the officer of the chief priest would come, empty the chest, and carry it back to its place. They did this day after day, and they gathered money in abundance.

12The king and Jehoiada gave it to those who were doing the work of the service of the house of the LORD, and they hired masons and carpenters to repair the house of the LORD, as well as ironworkers and bronzeworkers to strengthen it. 13The workmen labouredlabouredlabored, and the restoration progressed under their hands; they restored the house of God to its original design and reinforced it. 14When they had finished, they brought the rest of the money before the king and Jehoiada, and it was made into vessels for the house of the LORD—vessels for the service and for the burnt offerings, and dishes, and vessels of gold and silver. All the days of Jehoiada, burnt offerings were presented continually in the house of the LORD.

Jehoiada’s Death and Joash’s Apostasy

15Jehoiada grew old and full of days, and he died at the age of one hundred thirty years. 16They buried him with the kings in the CitycityCity of David, because he had done good in Israel, both toward God and toward HishisHis house.

17After Jehoiada’s death, the leaders of Judah came and paid homage to the king, and the king listened to them. 18They abandoned the house of the LORD, the God of their fathers, and served the Asherah poles and the idols; and wrath came upon Judah and Jerusalem because of this guilt of theirs. 19Yet HeheHe sent prophets among them to bring them back to the LORD, and they testified against them; but they would not listen.

20Then the SpiritspiritSpirit of God clothed Zechariah son of Jehoiada the priest. He stood above the people and said to them, “Thus says God: Why do you transgress the commandments of the LORD, so that you cannot prosper? Because you have forsaken the LORD, HeheHe has forsaken you.”

21But they conspired against him, and at the king’s command they stoned him in the court of the house of the LORD. 22King Joash did not remember the kindness that Jehoiada his father had shown him, but killed his son. As he died, he said, “May the LORD see and avenge.”

Divine JudgmentJudgementJudgment and the Death of Joash

23At the turn of the year, the army of Aram came up against him; they came to Judah and Jerusalem and destroyed all the leaders of the people, and sent all their plunder to the king of Damascus. 24Although the army of Aram had come with only a small force, the LORD delivered a very great army into their hand, because they had forsaken the LORD, the God of their fathers. So they executed judgmentjudgementjudgment on Joash.

25When they departed from him—for they left him gravely wounded—his servants conspired against him because of the blood of the sons of Jehoiada the priest, and killed him on his bed, and he died. They buried him in the CitycityCity of David, but they did not bury him in the tombs of the kings. 26These were the conspirators against him: Zabad son of Shimeath the Ammonitess, and Jehozabad son of Shimrith the Moabitess. 27As for his sons, the many prophecies concerning him, and the restoration of the house of God, behold, they are recorded in the commentary on the book of the kings. And Amaziah his son reigned in his place.

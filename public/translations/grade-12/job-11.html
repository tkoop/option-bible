Zophar Accuses Job of Empty Words

1Then Zophar the Naamathite answered and said:

2"Should a torrent of words go unanswered, and should a man of many speeches be vindicated? 3Should your idle talk silence men, and when you scoff, should no one put you to shame? 4For you say, 'My teaching is pure, and I am clean in YouryourYour eyes.'

God's Wisdom Beyond Human Reach

5"But oh, that God would speak and open HishisHis lips against you, 6and disclose to you the secrets of wisdom—for true wisdom has a double measure. Know, then, that God exacts from you less than your guilt deserves. 7Can you discover the deep things of God? Can you find the limit of the AlmightyalmightyAlmighty? 8It is higher than the heavens—what can you do? Deeper than Sheol—what can you know? 9Its measure is longer than the earth and broader than the sea. 10If HeheHe passes through, imprisons, and summons to judgmentjudgementjudgment, who can restrain HimhimHim? 11For HeheHe knows deceitful men; when HeheHe sees iniquity, does HeheHe not take note? 12An empty-headed man will never gain understanding, no more than a wild donkey can bear a human child.

A Call to Repentance and Restoration

13"If you prepare your heart and spread out your hands to HimhimHim, 14if you put away the iniquity in your hand and do not let wickedness dwell in your tents, 15then you will lift up your face without blemish; you will stand firm and not fear. 16You will forget your misery, remembering it only as water that has flowed past. 17your life will shine brighter than noonday; even its darkness will be like the morning. 18You will be secure, because there is hope; you will look about and lie down in safety. 19You will lie down, and no one will make you afraid; many will seek your favourfavourfavor.

The Fate of the Wicked

20"But the eyes of the wicked will fail, escape will elude them, and their hope will be a dying gasp."

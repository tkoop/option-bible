The Grandeur of Xerxes' Court

1In the days of Xerxes—that is, the Xerxes who reigned over one hundred twenty-seven provinces stretching from India to Cush— 2during those days King Xerxes sat upon his royal throne in the citadel of Susa. 3In the third year of his reign, he held a feast for all his officials and servants; the military leaders of Persia and Media were present, together with the nobles and princes of the provinces. 4For one hundred eighty days he displayed the glorious riches of his kingdom and the magnificent splendoursplendoursplendor of his greatness.

The Royal Banquets in Susa

5When those days had ended, the king held a banquet lasting seven days in the courtyard of the royal palace in Susa, for all the people from the least to the greatest who were present in the citadel. 6White and blue linen hangings were fastened with cords of fine linen and purple to silver rings on marble pillars; couches of gold and silver were arranged on a mosaic pavementroadwaypavement of porphyry, marble, mother-of-pearl, and other costly stones. 7Drinks were served in golden goblets, each one unlike the others, and royal wine flowed abundantly, according to the king's generosity. 8The drinking was conducted according to law; no one was compelled, for the king had instructed every official of his household to do as each person desired. 9Queen Vashti likewise gave a banquet for the women in the royal palace of King Xerxes.

Vashti's Refusal and the Royal Decree

10On the seventh day, when the king's heart was gladdened by wine, he commanded the seven eunuchs who served in his presence—Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha, Abagtha, Zethar, and Carkas— 11to bring Queen Vashti before him, wearing the royal crown, to display her beauty to the people and the officials, for she was remarkably beautiful. 12Queen Vashti, however, refused to come at the king's command delivered by the eunuchs. Then the king became furious, and his anger burned within him.

13Then the king consulted the wise men who knew the times, for it was customary for him to confer with all who knew law and justice. 14Those closest to him were Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena, and Memucan, the seven princes of Persia and Media who had privileged access to the king and ranked highest in the kingdom. 15"According to law," he asked, "what should be done to Queen Vashti, since she has failed to obey the command of King Xerxes delivered by the eunuchs?"

16Memucan replied in the presence of the king and his nobles, "Queen Vashti has wronged not only the king, but also all the officials and all the peoples in every province of King Xerxes. 17For the queen's conduct will become known to all women, causing them to despise their husbands and say, 'King Xerxes ordered Queen Vashti to be brought before him, but she refused to come.' 18This very day the noblewomen of Persia and Media who have heard of the queen's conduct will say the same to all the king's officials, and there will be no end of contempt and discord.

19"So if it pleases the king, let a royal decree be issued and written into the laws of Persia and Media, which cannot be repealed, that Vashti is never again to enter the presence of King Xerxes, and that her royal position be given to a woman more worthy than she. 20Then the king's decree will be published throughout his vast kingdom, and all women, from the least to the greatest, will honourhonourhonor their husbands."

21The king and his nobles were pleased with this advice, and the king did as Memucan had advised. 22He sent letters to all the king's provinces, to each province in its own script and to each people in their own language, proclaiming that every man should be master in his own household.

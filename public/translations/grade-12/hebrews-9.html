The Earthly Sanctuary and Its Ordinances

1Indeed, the first covenant itself possessed ordinances governing worship and an earthly sanctuary. 2For a tabernacle was constructed whose first chamber contained the lampstand, the table, and the bread of the PresencepresencePresence—a space called the HolyholyHoly PlaceplacePlace. 3Behind the second veil lay a chamber called the MostmostMost HolyholyHoly PlaceplacePlace, 4which contained a golden altar of incense and the ark of the covenant, overlaid on every side with gold, within which were a golden jar holding the manna, Aaron’s rod that budded, and the tablets of the covenant. 5Above it stood the cherubim of glory, overshadowing the mercy seat; yet we cannot now speak of these things in detail.

6With these preparations completed, the priests continually enter the first chamber, performing their liturgical ministrations; 7but into the second, the high priest alone enters, once a year, and never without blood, which he offers for himself and for the people’s inadvertent sins.

8By this arrangement, the HolyholyHoly SpiritspiritSpirit signifies that the way into the holy places has not yet been revealed while the first tabernacle still stands. 9This is a parable for the present time: according to this arrangement, gifts and sacrifices are offered that cannot render the worshiper’s conscience perfect. 10They consist only in food and drink and various ceremonial washings—regulations for the flesh imposed until the time of reformation.

Christ’s Superior Sacrifice and Heavenly Ministry

11But when Christ appeared as high priest of the good things that have come, HeheHe entered through the greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands—that is, not of this creation. 12He entered the holy places once for all, not by the blood of goats and calves, but by HishisHis own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption. 13For if the blood of goats and bulls, together with the ashes of a heifer sprinkled on the defiled, sanctifies them for the purification of the flesh, 14how much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal SpiritspiritSpirit offered HimselfhimselfHimself without blemish to God, cleanse our consciences from dead works to serve the living God.

The Mediator of a New Covenant and Final Redemption

15And for this reason HeheHe is the mediator of a new covenant, so that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance, now that a death has occurred for redemption from the transgressions committed under the first covenant.

16For where a will is involved, it is necessary to establish the death of the one who made it. 17For a will takes effect only at death, since it is not in force while the one who made it lives.

18Therefore even the first covenant was not inaugurated without blood. 19For when every commandment had been spoken by Moses to all the people according to the law, he took the blood of calves and goats, with water, scarlet wool, and hyssop, and sprinkled both the book itself and all the people, 20saying, “This is the blood of the covenant that God commanded for you.” 21And in the same way he sprinkled with the blood both the tabernacle and all the vessels of the ministry. 22Indeed, according to the law, almost everything is purified with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness.

23Thus it was necessary for the copies of the heavenly things to be purified with these rites, but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these. 24For Christ did not enter holy places made with hands, which are copies of the true things, but into Heavenheavenheaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf. 25Nor was it to offer HimselfhimselfHimself repeatedly, as the high priest enters the holy places every year with blood not his own; 26for then HeheHe would have had to suffer repeatedly since the foundation of the world. But now HeheHe has appeared once for all at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of HimselfhimselfHimself.

Ordinances Delivered to Israel

1These are the ordinances that you shall set before them. 2When you purchase a Hebrew servant, he shall serve six years, and in the seventh he shall go free without payment. 3If he enters alone, he shall leave alone; if he has a wife, his wife shall leave with him. 4If his master provides a wife for him and she bears sons or daughters, the wife and children shall belong to the master, and he shall leave alone. 5But if the servant says plainly, “I love my master, my wife, and my children; I do not wish to go free,” 6then his master shall bring him before God and to the door or doorpost, and shall pierce his ear with an awl; thereafter he shall serve him permanently.

Provisions for Female Servants

7If a man sells his daughter as a maidservant, she shall not be released under the same terms as male servants. 8If she fails to please the master who designated her, he must allow her redemption; he may not sell her to a foreign people, because he has broken faith with her. 9If he designates her for his son, he shall grant her the rights of a daughter. 10If he takes another wife, he must not reduce the first wife's food, clothing, or marital rights. 11If he fails to provide these three things, she shall go free without payment.

Laws of Homicide and Bodily Injury

12Whoever strikes a person so that he dies shall certainly be put to death. 13But if he did not lie in wait, and God delivered him into his power, I will designate a place for you to which he may flee. 14But if a person acts with premeditation against his neighbourneighbourneighbor in order to kill him treacherously, you shall take him even from MymyMy altar, so that he may die. 15Anyone who strikes his father or mother shall certainly be put to death. 16Whoever kidnaps a person and sells him, or is found holding him in possession, shall certainly be put to death. 17Anyone who curses his father or mother shall certainly be put to death.

18If men quarrel and one strikes the other with a stone or his fist, and the victim does not die but is confined to bed, 19and if the injured man later rises and walks about with a staff, the one who struck him shall be acquitted, provided he pays for the lost time and covers the cost of healing.

20If a man strikes his male or female servant with a rod so that the servant dies under his hand, he shall be punished. 21But if the servant survives a day or two, the master shall not be punished, because the servant is his property.

22If men are fighting and strike a pregnant woman so that she gives birth prematurely, but no further injury occurs, the offender shall be fined as the woman's husband imposes, subject to the judges' approval. 23But if further injury occurs, you shall give life for life. 24An eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth, a hand for a hand, a foot for a foot. 25A burn for a burn, a wound for a wound, a bruise for a bruise.

26If a man strikes the eye of his male or female servant and destroys it, he shall release the servant in compensation for the eye. 27If he knocks out a servant's tooth, he shall release that servant in compensation for the tooth.

Liability for Animals and Property

28If an ox gores a man or a woman to death, the ox shall be stoned and its flesh shall not be eaten, but the owner shall be acquitted. 29But if the ox has been in the habit of goring and its owner has been warned yet fails to confine it, and it kills a man or a woman, the ox shall be stoned and its owner shall also be put to death. 30If a ransom is imposed on him, he shall pay whatever is required to redeem his life. 31The same judgmentjudgementjudgment applies whether the ox gores a son or a daughter. 32If the ox gores a male or female servant, the owner shall pay the master thirty shekels of silver, and the ox shall be stoned.

33If a man opens or digs a pit and fails to cover it, and an ox or a donkey falls into it, 34the owner of the pit shall make restitution, paying money to the animal's owner, and the dead animal shall belong to him. 35If one man's ox injures another's so that it dies, they shall sell the living ox and divide the proceeds, and they shall also divide the carcass. 36But if it is known that the ox was previously in the habit of goring and its owner did not confine it, he shall pay ox for ox, and the dead animal shall be his.

The LORD’s Sovereignty over Human Affairs

1A king’s heart is like a watercourse in the LORD’s hand; HeheHe turns it wherever HeheHe wills. 2Every person’s way seems right in his own eyes, yet the LORD weighs the heart. 3Doing what is right and just is more acceptable to the LORD than offering sacrifices. 4Haughty eyes and a proud heart—the very lamp of the wicked—constitute sin.

Diligence, Wealth, and Speech

5The plans of the diligent surely bring abundance, whereas every hasty scheme surely leads to poverty. 6Wealth amassed by a deceitful tongue is a vanishing vapourvapourvapor, a pursuit that courts death. 7The violence of the wicked will drag them away, because they refuse to act justly. 8A guilty person’s path is devious and strange, but the pure person’s conduct is upright. 9It is better to live in a corner of the roof than to share a house with a quarrelsome wife.

Righteousness and the Fate of the Wicked

10The wicked person’s soul craves evil, and his neighbourneighbourneighbor finds no favourfavourfavor in his sight. 11When a scoffer is punished, the simple gain wisdom; when a wise person is instructed, he gains knowledge. 12The righteous person wisely considers the house of the wicked and brings them to ruin. 13Whoever closes his ears to the cry of the poor will himself cry out and receive no answer. 14A gift given secretly soothes anger, and a bribe concealed in the cloak pacifies great wrath. 15Carrying out justice brings joy to the righteous but terror to evildoers. 16A person who strays from the path of understanding will take his place among the dead.

17Whoever loves pleasure will become poor; whoever loves wine and oil will never grow rich. 18The wicked become a ransom for the righteous, and the treacherous for the upright. 19It is better to live in a desert than with a quarrelsome and hot-tempered wife. 20Precious treasure and oil are found in the wise person’s home, but a fool squanders them. 21Whoever pursues righteousness and faithful love finds life, righteousness, and honourhonourhonor. 22A wise person scales the city of the mighty and tears down the stronghold in which they trust. 23Whoever guards his mouth and tongue keeps himself from trouble. 24A proud, haughty scoffer is called “Mocker,” and he acts in arrogant fury.

Human Limits and Divine Victory

25The sluggard’s craving kills him, because his hands refuse to labourlabourlabor. 26All day long he covets, while the righteous gives without holding back. 27The wicked person’s sacrifice is detestable—especially when offered with evil intent. 28A false witness will perish, but the person who listens will be heard forever. 29A wicked person hardens his face, but the upright person makes his way secure. 30No wisdom, understanding, or counsel can stand against the LORD. 31The horse may be prepared for battle, but victory belongs to the LORD.

1King Solomon reigned over all Israel.

Solomon’s Officials and Administrators

2These were his officials: Azariah son of Zadok served as priest; Elihoreph and Ahijah, the sons of Shisha, were scribes; Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was the recorder; Benaiah son of Jehoiada commanded the army; Zadok and Abiathar served as priests; Azariah son of Nathan supervised the officers; Zabud son of Nathan, also a priest, was the king’s trusted friend; Ahishar directed the household; and Adoniram son of Abda oversaw the forced labourlabourlabor. 7Solomon appointed twelve officers over all Israel to supply food for the king and his household, each responsible for one month of the year. Their names and districts were as follows: 8Ben-hur in the hill country of Ephraim; 9Ben-deker in Makaz, Shaalbim, Beth-shemesh, and Elon Beth-hanan; 10Ben-hesed in Arubboth, with Socoh and all the land of Hepher under his jurisdiction; 11Ben-abinadab in all Naphath-dor, who had married Taphath, Solomon’s daughter; 12Baana son of Ahilud in Taanach, Megiddo, and all Beth-shean near Zaretan below Jezreel, extending from Beth-shean to Abel-meholah and beyond Jokneam; 13Ben-geber in Ramoth-gilead, including the towns of Jair son of Manasseh in Gilead and the region of Argob in Bashan—sixty great cities fortified with walls and bronze bars; 14Ahinadab son of Iddo in Mahanaim; 15Ahimaaz in Naphtali, who had married Basemath, another daughter of Solomon; 16Baana son of Hushai in Asher and Bealoth; 17Jehoshaphat son of Paruah in Issachar; 18Shimei son of Ela in Benjamin; and 19Geber son of Uri in the land of Gilead—the territory once ruled by Sihon king of the Amorites and Og king of Bashan—with one officer assigned to that land.

The Prosperity and Peace of Solomon’s Realm

20Judah and Israel were as numerous as the sand on the seashore; they ate, drank, and rejoiced. 21Solomon ruled over all the kingdoms from the Euphrates to the land of the Philistines and to the border of Egypt. Those nations brought tribute and served Solomon throughout his life. 22Solomon’s provision for a single day amounted to thirty cors of fine flour and sixty cors of meal, 23together with ten fattened oxen, twenty oxen from the pasture, one hundred sheep, and additional game—deer, gazelles, roebucks, and fattened fowl. 24He held dominion over all the territory beyond the Euphrates, from Tiphsah to Gaza, and over all its kings; and he enjoyed peace on every side. 25Thus Judah and Israel lived in security, each man enjoying the tranquil prosperity of his own inheritance, from Dan to Beersheba, throughout Solomon’s reign. 26Solomon maintained forty thousand stalls for the horses of his chariots and twelve thousand horsemen. 27The officers supplied food for King Solomon and for everyone who dined at his table, ensuring that nothing was lacking. 28They also brought barley and straw for the horses and swift steeds to the appropriate place, each according to his assignment.

Solomon’s Wisdom and International Renown

29God gave Solomon wisdom and exceedingly great understanding, with a mind as expansive as the sand on the seashore. 30Solomon’s wisdom surpassed that of all the sons of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt. 31He was wiser than any man—wiser than Ethan the Ezrahite, Heman, Calcol, and Darda, the sons of Mahol—and his renown spread among all the surrounding nations. 32He composed three thousand proverbs, and his songs numbered one thousand five. 33He discoursed on trees, from the cedar of Lebanon to the hyssop that springs from the wall; he also discoursed on beasts, birds, creeping things, and fish. 34People from every nation came to hear Solomon’s wisdom, including envoys from all the kings of the earth who had heard of his wisdom.

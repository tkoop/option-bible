The Oracle of King Lemuel

1The words of King Lemuel—an oracle that his mother imparted to him: 2“What is it, my son? What is it, son of my womb? What is it, son of my vows? 3Do not lavish your strength upon women, nor surrender your ways to those who destroy kings. 4It is unbecoming for kings, Lemuel—it is unbecoming for kings to drink wine, nor for rulers to crave strong drink, 5lest they drink and forget what has been decreed, thereby perverting justice for all the afflicted. 6Give strong drink to the perishing, and wine to those whose souls are bitter. 7Let them drink and forget their poverty, and remember their misery no more. 8Open your mouth for those who have no voice, and plead the cause of all the dispossessed. 9Open your mouth, judge with righteousness, and defend the poor and the needy.”

The Woman of Noble Character

10Who can discover a wife of noble character? She is far more precious than rubies. 11The heart of her husband safely trusts in her, and he will suffer no lack of gain. 12She brings him good, and not harm, throughout all the days of her life. 13She procures wool and flax, and labourslabourslabors with willing hands. 14She is comparable to the merchant ships, bringing her provision from afar. 15She rises while it is still night to furnish food for her household and portions for her maidservants. 16She contemplates a field and acquires it; from her earnings she plants a vineyard. 17She equips herself with strength and fortifies her arms. 18She perceives that her merchandise is good, and her lamp is not extinguished at night. 19She stretches out her hands to the distaff, and her fingers grasp the spindle. 20She extends her hands to the indigent and reaches out to the needy. 21She is not afraid of snow for her household, for all her household are clothed in scarlet.

Her Household and Public Renown

22She fashions coverings for her bed; her raiment is fine linen and purple. 23Her husband is renowned at the city gate, where he sits among the elders of the land. 24She fashions linen garments and markets them; she delivers sashes to the merchant. 25Strength and dignity constitute her attire, and she faces the days to come without dread. 26She opens her mouth with wisdom, and faithful instruction is on her tongue. 27She watches over the conduct of her household and does not live in idleness. 28Her children rise up and call her blessed; her husband also praises her: 29“Many daughters have done valiantly, but you surpass them all.” 30Charm is deceitful and beauty is fleeting, but a woman who fears the LORD is to be praised. 31Give her the fruit of her hands, and let her works praise her publicly.

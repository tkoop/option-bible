The Oppression of the Powerless

1I turned again and surveyed all the oppression inflicted beneath the sun. I saw the tears of the oppressed, with no one to comfort them; power belonged to their oppressors, and still no one comforted them.

2So I declared the dead, who have already died, more fortunate than the living, who are still alive. 3Yet better than both is the one who has never been born, who has not seen the evil deeds done beneath the sun.

4Then I observed all toil and every skilfulskilfulskillful work, and I recognizedrecogniserecognized that it arises from one person's envy of another. This too is vanity and a chasing after wind.

5The fool folds his hands and destroys himself. 6Better is a handful of quietness than two handfuls of toil and a chasing after wind.

The Emptiness of Isolated LabourLabourLabor

7I turned again and saw vanity beneath the sun. 8There is someone alone, without companion, child, or brother, yet no end to his toil; his eyes are never satisfied with wealth. He asks, "For whom am I toiling and depriving myself of good?" This too is vanity and a grievous task.

The Value of Companionship

9Two are better than one, because they receive a good reward for their labourlabourlabor. 10If one falls, the other can lift up his companion; but woe to the one who falls alone and has no second to raise him. 11If two lie together, they keep warm, but how can one keep warm alone? 12And if someone overpowers one, two can withstand him; a threefold cord is not quickly broken.

Wisdom, Poverty, and Succession

13Better is a poor but wise youth than an old and foolish king who no longer knows how to take warning. 14For he came out of prison to reign, though he was born poor in his kingdom. 15I saw all the living who walk beneath the sun, along with the successor, the child who will take his place. 16There is no end to all the people who came before them, yet those who come after will not rejoice in him. This too is vanity and a chasing after wind.

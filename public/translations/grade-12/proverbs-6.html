Warning Against Rash Surety and Indolence

1My son, if you have pledged surety for your neighbourneighbourneighbor, if you have sealed a pledge with a handshake for a stranger, 2you are snared by the words of your mouth; you are trapped by your own utterances. 3Then do this, my son, and deliver yourself, for you have come into your neighbourneighbourneighbor's power: go, humble yourself, and importune your neighbourneighbourneighbor. 4Give no sleep to your eyes, nor slumber to your eyelids. 5Deliver yourself like a gazelle from the hunter's hand, and like a bird from the hand of the fowler.

6Go to the ant, you sluggard; observe her ways and grow wise. 7Though she has no commander, overseer, or ruler, 8she prepares her bread in summer and gathers her provender in harvest. 9How long will you recline, O sluggard? When will you rise from your slumber? 10A little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to rest, 11and your poverty will come like a robber, and your want like an armed man.

The Abhorrence of Wickedness

12A worthless man, a scoundrel, conducts himself with perverse speech, 13winking with his eyes, signallingsignallingsignaling with his feet, gesturing with his fingers; 14deceit is in his heart, he plots evil continually, and he sows discord. 15Therefore his ruin will arrive suddenly; in an instant he will be shattered beyond remedy.

16There are six things that the the LORD hates, indeed seven that are detestable to HimhimHim: 17haughty eyes, a deceitful tongue, hands that shed innocent blood, 18a heart that contrives wicked schemes, feet that hasten to do evil, 19a false witness who utters lies, and one who sows discord among brothers.

The Guidance of Parental Teaching

20My son, keep your father's commandment, and do not abandon the instruction of your mother. 21Bind them upon your heart continually; fasten them around your neck. 22When you walk, they will guide you; when you sleep, they will guard you; and when you awake, they will converse with you. 23For the commandment is a lamp, the teaching is a light, and the reproofs of discipline are the way of life, 24to preserve you from the adulterous woman, from the smooth tongue of a stranger.

The Ruin of Adultery

25Do not covet her beauty in your heart, nor let her captivate you with her glances. 26For a harlot reduces a man to a crust of bread, but an adulteress hunts for the precious life. 27Can a man carry fire in his bosom without burning his garments? 28Or can one walk upon hot coals without scorching his feet? 29So is the man who goes in to his neighbourneighbourneighbor's wife; no one who touches her will go unpunished. 30People do not despise a thief if he steals to satisfy his hunger; 31yet if he is caught, he must repay sevenfold; he must surrender all the wealth of his house. 32He who commits adultery with a woman is devoid of understanding; he who does so destroys his own soul. 33He will incur wounds and disgrace, and his reproach will never be erased. 34For jealousy provokes a husband's fury, and he will show no mercy in the day of vengeance. 35He will accept no ransom, nor will he be appeased even if you heap up bribes.

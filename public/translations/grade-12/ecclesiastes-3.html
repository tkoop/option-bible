A Time for Every Purpose on Earth

1There is a season for everything, and a time for every purpose on EarthEarthearth: 2a time to be born and a time to die, a time to plant and a time to uproot what is planted; 3a time to kill and a time to heal, a time to tear down and a time to build; 4a time to weep and a time to laugh, a time to mourn and a time to dance; 5a time to scatter stones and a time to gather them, a time to embrace and a time to refrain from embracing; 6a time to seek and a time to relinquish, a time to keep and a time to cast away; 7a time to tear and a time to mend, a time to be silent and a time to speak; 8a time to love and a time to hate, a time for war and a time for peace.

The Burden and Beauty of God’s Design

9What gain does the worker derive from his toil? 10I have seen the burden God has laid upon the human race to keep them occupied. 11He has made everything beautiful in its time; HeheHe has also set eternity in the human heart, yet no one can fathom what God has done from beginning to end.

12I know that there is nothing better for them than to rejoice and to do good while they live, 13and that every person should eat and drink and find satisfaction in all his labourlabourlabor—this is the gift of God.

14I know that whatever God does endures forever; nothing can be added to it, and nothing can be taken from it. God has done this so that people will fear HimhimHim. 15Whatever is has already been, and what is to come has already occurred; and God calls to account what has been driven away.

Justice, Mortality, and Joy in One’s Work

16Moreover, I saw in this world that in the place of judgmentjudgementjudgment there is wickedness, and in the place of righteousness there is wickedness. 17I said in my heart that God will judge both the righteous and the wicked, for there is a time for every purpose and for every work there.

18I said in my heart concerning the sons of men that God tests them, so that they may see that they themselves are like beasts. 19For the fate of human beings and the fate of beasts is the same: as one dies, so dies the other. They all have the same breath, and man has no advantage over the beasts, for all is meaningless. 20All go to one place: all are from the dust, and all return to dust. 21Who knows whether the spirit of man ascends upward and the spirit of the beast descends into the earth?

22So I saw that there is nothing better than that a man should rejoice in his works, for that is his portion. For who can bring him to see what will be after him?

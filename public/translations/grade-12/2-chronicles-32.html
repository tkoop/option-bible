Hezekiah Prepares and Encourages Jerusalem

1After these faithful deeds, Sennacherib, king of Assyria, invaded Judah and besieged its fortified cities, intending to seize them for himself. 2When Hezekiah saw that Sennacherib had come to make war against Jerusalem, 3he consulted with his officials and warriors about stopping up the water from the springs outside the city, and they supported his plan. 4A large crowd assembled and stopped up every spring and the stream that flowed through the land, asking, "Why should the kings of Assyria come and find an abundance of water?" 5Hezekiah then worked resolutely to rebuild every broken section of the wall, raise towers upon it, construct an additional outer wall, strengthen the supporting terraces of the CitycityCity of David, and manufacture an abundance of weapons and shields.

6He appointed military commanders over the people and gathered them in the square at the city gate, where he encouraged them, saying, 7"Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid or dismayed before the king of Assyria or before the vast army with him, for there is a greater power with us than with him. 8With him is merely the arm of flesh, but with us is the LORD our God to help us and to fight our battles." The people gained confidence from the words of Hezekiah, king of Judah.

Sennacherib's Blasphemous Threats

9Later, while Sennacherib, king of Assyria, and all his forces besieged Lachish, he sent his servants to Jerusalem with this message for Hezekiah, king of Judah, and for all the people of Judah who were in Jerusalem: 10"This is what Sennacherib, king of Assyria, says: On what are you relying, that you remain under siege in Jerusalem? 11Is not Hezekiah misleading you into surrendering yourselves to die by famine and thirst when he says, 'ThetheThe LORD our God will deliver us from the hand of the king of Assyria'? 12Did not Hezekiah himself remove HishisHis high places and altars and say to Judah and Jerusalem, 'YouyouYou must worship before one altar, and on it you must burn sacrifices'? 13Do you not know what I and my fathers have done to all the peoples of the lands? Were the gods of those nations ever able to deliver their land from my hand? 14Who among all the gods of these nations that my fathers devoted to destruction was able to deliver his people from my hand? How then can your God deliver you from my hand? 15Now, therefore, do not let Hezekiah deceive you, and do not let him mislead you in this way. Do not believe him, for no god of any nation or kingdom has been able to deliver his people from my hand or from the hand of my fathers. How much less will your God deliver you from my hand!"

16His servants spoke further against the LORD God and against HishisHis servant Hezekiah. 17He also wrote letters mocking the LORD, the God of Israel, and speaking against HimhimHim, saying, "Just as the gods of the nations did not deliver their people from my hand, so the God of Hezekiah will not deliver HishisHis people from my hand." 18Then the Assyrians called out in Hebrew to the people of Jerusalem who were on the wall, to frighten and terrify them, in order that they might capture the city. 19They spoke about the God of Jerusalem as they had about the gods of the other peoples of the earth—the work of human hands.

The LORD Delivers Jerusalem

20In response, King Hezekiah and the prophet Isaiah, son of Amoz, cried out to Heavenheavenheaven in prayer. 21Then the LORD sent an angel who annihilated every mighty warrior, commander, and officer in the camp of the king of Assyria. So he withdrew to his own land in disgrace, and when he entered the temple of his god, some of his own sons struck him down with the sword. 22Thus the LORD saved Hezekiah and the people of Jerusalem from the hand of Sennacherib, king of Assyria, and from the hand of all others. He gave them rest on every side. 23Many brought offerings to Jerusalem for the LORD and valuable gifts for Hezekiah, king of Judah, and from then on he was exalted in the eyes of all nations.

Hezekiah's Later Years

24In those days Hezekiah became ill and was at the point of death. He prayed to the LORD, who answered him and gave him a sign. 25But Hezekiah's heart was proud, and he did not respond to the kindness shown him; therefore wrath came upon him and upon Judah and Jerusalem. 26Then Hezekiah repented of the pride of his heart—he and the people of Jerusalem—so the wrath of the LORD did not come upon them during the days of Hezekiah.

27Hezekiah had very great riches and honourhonourhonor, and he made treasuries for his silver, gold, precious stones, spices, shields, and all kinds of valuable articles. 28He also built storehouses for the harvest of grain, new wine, and oil, as well as stalls for all kinds of livestock and pens for the flocks. 29He made cities for himself and acquired flocks and herds in abundance, for God gave him very great wealth. 30It was Hezekiah who blocked the upper outlet of the Gihon SpringspringSpring and channeled the water down to the west side of the CitycityCity of David. And Hezekiah prospered in all that he did.

31When envoys were sent by the rulers of Babylon to inquire about the wonder that had happened in the land, God left Hezekiah to test him, that HeheHe might know all that was in his heart. 32As for the rest of the acts of Hezekiah and his deeds of loving devotion, they are recorded in the vision of the prophet Isaiah, son of Amoz, in the BookbookBook of the Kings of Judah and Israel. 33Hezekiah rested with his fathers and was buried on the upper slopes of the tombs of David. All Judah and the people of Jerusalem paid him honourhonourhonor at his death, and his son Manasseh reigned in his place.

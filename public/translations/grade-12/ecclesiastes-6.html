The Emptiness of Unsatisfied Wealth

1There is an evil I have seen in this world, and it weighs heavily upon humanity: 2God gives a man riches, wealth, and honourhonourhonor, so that he lacks nothing his heart desires, yet God does not grant him the power to enjoy it; instead a stranger consumes it. This is futility and a grievous affliction.

The Stillborn Better Than the Restless Rich

3If a man begets a hundred children and lives many years—yes, many are the days of his years—but his soul is never satisfied with good, and moreover he has no burial, I say that the stillborn is better than he. 4For it comes in futility and goes in darkness, and in darkness its name is covered. 5Moreover, it has never seen the sun nor known anything, yet it has more rest than he. 6Even if he lived two thousand years and never enjoyed what is good—do not all meet the same end?

The Limits of Wisdom and Foresight

7All human toil is for the mouth, yet the appetite is never satisfied. 8What advantage has the wise over the fool? What does the poor man gain by knowing how to conduct himself among the living? 9Better is what the eyes see than the wandering of desire. This too is futility and an empty pursuit.

10Whatever exists has already been named, and it is known what man is; he cannot contend with the OneoneOne stronger than he. 11Indeed, the more words, the more futility; and what advantage is there for man? 12For who knows what is good for a man in life, during the few days of his fleeting existence, which pass like a shadow? Who can tell a man what will come after him in this world?

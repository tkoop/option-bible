David Hears of Saul’s Death

1After Saul’s death, David returned from striking down the Amalekites and remained in Ziklag for two days. 2On the third day, a man arrived from Saul’s camp with his clothes torn and dust on his head; when he reached David, he fell facedown in homage.

3“Where have you come from?” David asked him.

“I have escaped from the Israelite camp,” he replied.

4“What was the outcome?” David asked. “Please tell me.”

“The troops fled from the battle,” he replied. “Many of the people fell and died. And Saul and his son Jonathan are dead.”

5Then David asked the young man who brought the report, “How do you know that Saul and his son Jonathan are dead?”

6“I happened to be on Mount Gilboa,” the young man answered, “and there was Saul, leaning on his spear, with the chariots and horsemen closing in on him. 7When he turned around and saw me, he called out to me, and I answered, ‘Here I am.’ 8‘Who are you?’ he asked. ‘An Amalekite,’ I answered. 9Then he said to me, ‘Stand over me and kill me, for anguish has seized me, but my life still lingers.’ 10So I stood over him and killed him, because I knew that after he had fallen he could not survive. And I took the crown that was on his head and the band that was on his arm, and I have brought them here to my lord.”

David Mourns Saul and Jonathan

11Then David took hold of his clothes and tore them, and all the men who were with him did the same. 12They mourned and wept and fasted until evening for Saul and his son Jonathan, and for the people of the LORD and the house of Israel, because they had fallen by the sword.

13Then David asked the young man who had brought him the report, “Where are you from?”

“I am the son of a foreigner,” he answered. “I am an Amalekite.”

14David asked him, “Why were you not afraid to lift your hand to destroy the the LORD’s anointed?”

15Then David called one of his young men and said, “Go, strike him down!” So he struck him down, and he died.

16For David had said to him, “Your blood be on your own head, because your mouth has testified against you, saying, ‘I killed the the LORD’s anointed.’”

David’s Lament for Saul and Jonathan

17Then David took up this lament for Saul and his son Jonathan, 18and he ordered that the sons of Judah be taught the SongsongSong of the Bow. It is written in the BookbookBook of Jashar:

19“Your glory, O Israel, lies slain on your heights. How the mighty have fallen! 20Tell it not in Gath; proclaim it not in the streets of Ashkelon, lest the daughters of the Philistines rejoice, lest the daughters of the uncircumcised exult. 21O mountains of Gilboa, may you have neither dew nor rain, nor fields that yield offerings. For there the shield of the mighty was defiled, the shield of Saul, no longer anointed with oil.

22“From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, the bow of Jonathan did not turn back, and the sword of Saul did not return empty. 23Saul and Jonathan, beloved and pleasant in life, were not parted in death. They were swifter than eagles; they were stronger than lions. 24O daughters of Israel, weep for Saul, who clothed you in scarlet and finery, who decked your necks with gold jewelleryjewelleryjewelry.

25“How the mighty have fallen in the midst of battle! Jonathan lies slain on your heights. 26I am distressed for you, Jonathan my brother. You were delightful to me; your love to me was wonderful, surpassing the love of women. 27How the mighty have fallen, and the weapons of war have perished!”

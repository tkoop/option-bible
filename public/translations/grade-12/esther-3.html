Haman's Promotion and Mordecai's Refusal

1After these events, King Ahasuerus elevated Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, promoting him and placing his seat above all the officials who served with him. 2All the king's servants who were at the gate bowed and prostrated themselves before Haman, for the king had commanded that they do so; yet Mordecai neither bowed nor prostrated himself. 3The king's servants at the gate asked Mordecai, "Why do you transgress the king's command?" 4Day after day they spoke to him, but he would not listen; so they reported the matter to Haman to see whether Mordecai's defiance would stand, for he had told them that he was a Jew.

Haman's Fury and the Casting of Lots

5When Haman saw that Mordecai would neither kneel nor bow before him, he was filled with rage. 6Because they had told him who Mordecai's people were, Haman regarded it as beneath him to lay hands on Mordecai alone; instead, he sought to annihilate all the Jews throughout the realm of Ahasuerus—the people of Mordecai. 7In the twelfth year of King Ahasuerus, in the first month, Nisan, the lot—Pur—was cast before Haman from day to day and from month to month, and it fell on the twelfth month, Adar.

The Decree Against the Jews

8Then Haman said to King Ahasuerus, "There is a certain people, scattered and dispersed among the peoples in all the provinces of your kingdom, whose laws differ from those of every other people and who do not observe the king's laws. It is not in the king's interest to tolerate them. 9If it pleases the king, let it be written that they be destroyed, and I will pay ten thousand talents of silver into the royal treasury for those who execute the task." 10The king removed his signet ring from his hand and gave it to Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of the Jews.

11The king said to Haman, "ThetheThe silver is given to you, along with the people, to do with them as you see fit." 12On the thirteenth day of the first month, the king's scribes were summoned, and in accordance with all that Haman commanded, they wrote to the king's satraps, to the governors over each province, and to the leaders of each people—each province in its own script and each people in its own language. The decree was written in the name of King Ahasuerus and sealed with the king's signet ring.

13Letters were sent by couriers to all the king's provinces with orders to destroy, kill, and annihilate all the Jews—young and old, women and children—on a single day, the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, Adar, and to plunder their goods. 14A copy of the text of the edict was to be issued as law in every province and made public to all peoples, so that they might be ready for that day. 15The couriers went out, pressed forward by the king's command, and the decree was proclaimed in the citadel of Susa. The king and Haman sat down to drink, while the city of Susa was thrown into confusion.

Ahab and Jehoshaphat Plan War at Ramoth-gilead

1For three years, Aram and Israel remained at peace. 2In the third year, Jehoshaphat king of Judah went down to the king of Israel. 3The king of Israel said to his servants, "Do you know that Ramoth-gilead is rightfully ours, yet we have done nothing to recover it from the king of Aram?" 4Then he said to Jehoshaphat, "WillwillWill you march with me against Ramoth-gilead?" Jehoshaphat replied to the king of Israel, "I am as you are; my people are as your people; my horses are as your horses."

5Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, "Please seek a word from the LORD today."

6So the king of Israel assembled about four hundred prophets and asked them, "Shall I go to war against Ramoth-gilead, or shall I hold back?"

They replied, "March up, and the Lord will deliver it into the king's hand."

Micaiah Prophesies Against Ahab

7Jehoshaphat asked, "IsisIs there no other prophet of the LORD here, that we may consult him?"

8The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "There is still one man through whom we may inquire of the LORD—Micaiah son of Imlah—but I despise him, because he never prophesies prosperity for me, only calamity."

Jehoshaphat said, "Let the king not speak so."

9Then the king of Israel summoned one of his court officials and said, "BringbringBring Micaiah son of Imlah immediately." 10Meanwhile, the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah were seated on their thrones, arrayed in their robes, at the threshing floor beside the gate of Samaria, while all the prophets prophesied before them.

11Zedekiah son of Chenaanah had fashioned horns of iron for himself and declared, "Thus says the LORD: 'With these you shall gore the Arameans until they are annihilated.'"

12All the prophets prophesied similarly, saying, "Advance against Ramoth-gilead and succeed, for the LORD will deliver it into the hand of the king."

13The messenger who had gone to summon Micaiah said to him, "See, the prophets are unanimous in promising success to the king. Please let your word accord with theirs, and speak favourablyfavourablyfavorably."

14Micaiah replied, "As the LORD lives, I will speak only what the LORD says to me."

15When he arrived before the king, the king asked him, "Micaiah, shall we go to war against Ramoth-gilead, or shall we desist?"

He answered, "Go up and triumph, for the LORD will deliver it into the hand of the king."

16The king said to him, "How many times must I adjure you to speak to me nothing but the truth in the name of the LORD?"

17Micaiah said, "I saw all Israel scattered upon the mountains, like sheep without a shepherd. And the LORD said, 'These have no master; let each return to his home in peace.'"

And one said this, and another said that. 21Then a spirit came forward and stood before the LORD and said, 'I will entice him.' And the LORD said to him, 'By what means?' 22And he said, 'I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.' 24Then Zedekiah son of Chenaanah came near and struck Micaiah on the cheek and said, "Which way did the SpiritspiritSpirit of the LORD go from me to speak to you?"

25Micaiah said, "Behold, you will see on that day when you go into an inner room to hide."

26The king of Israel said, "Take Micaiah and carry him back to Amon the governor of the city and to Joash the king's son, 27and say, 'Thus says the king, "Put this fellow in prison and feed him with bread and water of affliction until I come in peace."'"

28Micaiah said, "If you return in peace, the LORD has not spoken by me." Then he said, "Hear, all you peoples!"

Ahab Falls in Battle at Ramoth-gilead

29So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah advanced against Ramoth-gilead.

30The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "I will disguise myself and enter the battle, but you wear your royal robes." So the king of Israel disguised himself and entered the battle.

31Meanwhile, the king of Aram had ordered his thirty-two chariot commanders, "Do not engage either the small or the great, but single out the king of Israel."

32When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they concluded, "That must be the king of Israel." They turned to attack him, and Jehoshaphat cried out. 33But when the commanders realizedrealisedrealized he was not the king of Israel, they broke off their pursuit.

34Yet a random archer drew his bow and struck the king of Israel between the joints of his armourarmourarmor and his breastplate. The king told his charioteer, "Wheel about and take me out of the ranks, for I am wounded."

35The battle intensified that day, and the king remained propped up in his chariot facing the Arameans while the blood from his wound pooled in the bottom of the chariot; by evening he was dead.

36At sunset a cry swept through the army: "Every man to his own city, and every man to his own land!"

37The king died and was conveyed to Samaria, where they buried him. 38They washed the chariot at the pool of Samaria, and the dogs licked up his blood while the prostitutes bathed there, fulfilling the word of the LORD that HeheHe had spoken. 39The remaining acts of Ahab—everything he accomplished, including the ivory palace he constructed and all the cities he built—are they not recorded in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel? 40Ahab rested with his fathers, and his son Ahaziah succeeded him.

The Reigns of Jehoshaphat and Ahaziah

41Jehoshaphat son of Asa began his reign over Judah in the fourth year of Ahab king of Israel. 42He was thirty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned twenty-five years in Jerusalem. His mother was Azubah daughter of Shilhi. 43He followed the example of his father Asa in every respect, never deviating from it, and he did what was right in the eyes of the LORD. Even so, the high places were not abolished; the people continued to sacrifice and burn incense on them. 44Jehoshaphat also maintained peace with the king of Israel. 45As for the rest of Jehoshaphat's acts—his valourvalourvalor and the wars he waged—are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

46He expelled from the land the remaining male shrine prostitutes who had survived from the days of his father Asa. 47At that time there was no king in Edom; a deputy served as king.

48Jehoshaphat built a fleet of Tarshish ships to sail to Ophir for gold, but the voyage never took place because the ships were wrecked at Ezion-geber. 49Ahaziah son of Ahab then proposed to Jehoshaphat, "Let my servants sail with your servants," but Jehoshaphat refused. 50Jehoshaphat died and was buried with his fathers in the city of David his father, and his son Jehoram succeeded him.

51Ahaziah son of Ahab became king over Israel in Samaria in the seventeenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, and he reigned two years over Israel. 52He did what was evil in the sight of the LORD, walking in the ways of his father and mother and in the way of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who had induced Israel to sin. 53He served Baal and worshiped him, provoking the LORD, the God of Israel, to anger, just as his father had done.

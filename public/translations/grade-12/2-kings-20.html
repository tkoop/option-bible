Hezekiah's Mortal Illness and Intercession

1In those days Hezekiah became mortally ill. The prophet Isaiah son of Amoz came to him and said, “Thus says the LORD: ‘Set your affairs in order, for you are about to die; you will not recover.’”

2He turned his face toward the wall and interceded with the LORD, saying, 3“Please, O LORD, remember how I have lived before you in integrity and with an undivided heart, and how I have done what is good in your sight.” Then Hezekiah wept bitterly.

4Before Isaiah had departed the middle courtyard, the word of the LORD came to him: 5“Go back and say to Hezekiah, the leader of MymyMy people, ‘Thus says the LORD, the God of your father David: I have heard your prayer; I have seen your tears. Behold, I will heal you, and on the third day you shall ascend to the house of the LORD. 6I will extend your life by fifteen years, and I will rescue you and this city from the king of Assyria. I will defend this city for MymyMy own sake and for the sake of MymyMy servant David.’”

7Then Isaiah said, “Take a cake of figs.” So they took it and applied it to the boil, and he recovered.

8Hezekiah asked Isaiah, “What will be the sign that the LORD will heal me, and that I shall ascend to the house of the LORD on the third day?”

The Sign on Ahaz's Stairway

9Isaiah replied, “This is the sign to you from the LORD that the LORD will fulfillfulfilfulfill what HeheHe has promised: Shall the shadow advance ten steps, or retreat ten steps?”

10Hezekiah answered, “It is a trivial matter for the shadow to lengthen ten steps. No—let the shadow retreat ten steps.”

11So Isaiah the prophet cried out to the LORD, and HeheHe made the shadow retreat ten steps from the point to which it had descended on the stairway of Ahaz.

Babylonian Envoys and Isaiah's Prophecy

12At that time Merodach-baladan son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a gift to Hezekiah, because he had heard of Hezekiah's illness.

13Hezekiah received the messengers and showed them his entire treasury—silver, gold, spices, precious oil, his armouryarmouryarmory, and everything found in his storehouses. There was nothing in his palace or throughout his realm that Hezekiah did not display to them.

14Then the prophet Isaiah came to King Hezekiah and asked, “What did these men say, and from where have they come to you?”

Hezekiah replied, “They have come from a distant land, from Babylon.”

15Isaiah asked, “What have they seen in your house?”

Hezekiah answered, “They have seen everything in my house. There is nothing among my treasures that I did not show them.”

16Then Isaiah said to Hezekiah, “Hear the word of the LORD: 17Behold, the days are coming when everything in your house—and what your fathers have accumulated to this day—will be carried off to Babylon. Nothing will be left, says the LORD. 18And some of your sons, descendants you yourself will father, will be taken away and made eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.”

19Hezekiah said to Isaiah, “The word of the LORD that you have spoken is good.” For he thought, “Will there not be peace and truth in my days?”

Hezekiah's Final Acts and Death

20The remaining deeds of Hezekiah, all his might, and how he constructed the pool and the conduit and brought water into the city—are they not recorded in the BookbookBook of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah?

21Hezekiah died and was gathered to his ancestors, and his son Manasseh reigned in his place.

Warnings Against Presumption and Rash Anger

1Do not boast about tomorrow, for you do not know what a day may bring forth. 2Let another praise you, not your own mouth; a stranger, not your own lips. 3A stone is heavy, and sand is weighty, but a fool's provocation outweighs them both. 4Wrath is cruel, and anger is a torrent, but who can stand before jealousy?

The Value of Honest Friendship and Wise Counsel

5Open rebuke is better than love concealed. 6Faithful are the wounds of a friend, but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful. 7A satisfied appetite loathes honey, yet to a hungry soul every bitter thing tastes sweet. 8Like a bird straying from its nest, so is a man who strays from his home.

9Oil and incense gladden the heart, and the sweetness of a friend's counsel refreshes the soul. 10Do not abandon your friend or your father's friend, and do not enter your brother's house on the day of your disaster; a neighbourneighbourneighbor nearby is better than a brother far away.

11Be wise, my son, and bring joy to my heart, so that I may answer the one who taunts me.

12The prudent foresee danger and take cover, but the naive proceed and suffer the penalty. 13Take the garment of one who guarantees a stranger's debt, and hold it in pledge for a foreign woman. 14Whoever loudly blesses his neighbourneighbourneighbor at dawn will have it reckoned to him as a curse.

Domestic Strife, Mutual Sharpening, and Testing

15A continual dripping on a rainy day and a contentious wife are alike. 16To restrain her is like restraining the wind or grasping oil in one's right hand. 17As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another.

18He who tends a fig tree will eat its fruit, and he who watches over his master will be honouredhonouredhonored. 19As water reflects the face, so a man's heart reflects the man. 20Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied, nor are human eyes ever satisfied.

21The crucible is for silver and the furnace for gold, and a man is tested by the praise he receives. 22Though you grind a fool in a mortar amid grain with a pestle, his folly will not depart from him.

Stewardship of Flocks and Household Provision

23Be sure to know the condition of your flocks, and give careful attention to your herds, 24for wealth is not forever, nor does a crown endure to all generations. 25When the grass disappears and new growth appears and the herbs of the mountains are gathered, 26the lambs will provide your clothing, and the goats will be the price of a field. 27And there will be enough goats' milk for your food, for the food of your household, and for the sustenance of your maidservants.

Jephthah's Background and Rejection

1Jephthah the Gileadite was a formidable warrior, yet he was born to a prostitute, and Gilead was his father.

2Gilead's wife bore him sons, and when they grew up, they drove Jephthah away, saying, YouyouYou shall have no inheritance in our father's house, because you are the son of another woman.

3So Jephthah fled from his brothers and settled in the land of Tob, where worthless men gathered around him and went out with him.

The Ammonite Threat and Jephthah's Leadership

4After some time, the Ammonites waged war against Israel. 5When the Ammonites made war against Israel, the elders of Gilead went to bring Jephthah back from the land of Tob.

6"Come," they said to Jephthah, "be our commander, so that we may fight against the Ammonites."

7But Jephthah said to the elders of Gilead, "Did you not hate me and drive me from my father's house? Why do you come to me now, when you are in distress?"

8The elders of Gilead replied to Jephthah, "That is why we now turn to you, that you may go with us, fight against the Ammonites, and become head over all who live in Gilead."

9Jephthah answered them, "If you bring me back to fight against the Ammonites, and the the LORD gives them into my hand, will I be your head?"

10The elders of Gilead said to Jephthah, "ThetheThe the LORD is witness between us; we will certainly do as you say."

11So Jephthah went with the elders of Gilead, and the people made him their leader and commander. Then Jephthah repeated all his words before the the LORD at Mizpah.

Jephthah's Negotiation with Ammon

12Then Jephthah sent messengers to the king of the Ammonites, saying, "What do you have against me, that you have come to fight against my land?"

13The king of the Ammonites answered Jephthah's messengers, "Because Israel, when coming up from Egypt, took my land from the Arnon to the Jabbok and to the Jordan. Now, therefore, restore it in peace."

14Jephthah again sent messengers to the king of the Ammonites.

15Jephthah said to him, "This is what Jephthah declares: Israel did not take the land of Moab or the land of the Ammonites. 16For when Israel came up from Egypt, they travelledtravelledtraveled through the wilderness to the Red SeaseaSea and came to Kadesh. 17Then Israel sent messengers to the king of Edom, saying, 'Please let us pass through your land.' But the king of Edom would not listen. They also sent to the king of Moab, but he refused. So Israel remained at Kadesh. 18Then they journeyed through the wilderness, skirted the lands of Edom and Moab, came to the eastern side of Moab, and camped beyond the Arnon. They did not enter Moab's territory, for the Arnon was Moab's border. 19Then Israel sent messengers to Sihon, king of the Amorites, king of Heshbon, saying, 'Please let us pass through your land to our own territory.' 20But Sihon did not trust Israel to pass through his territory; instead, he gathered all his people, encamped at Jahaz, and fought against Israel. 21And the the LORD, the God of Israel, delivered Sihon and all his people into Israel's hand, and they defeated them. So Israel took possession of all the land of the Amorites who inhabited that region. 22They took possession of all the Amorite territory from the Arnon to the Jabbok and from the wilderness to the Jordan. 23So now the the LORD, the God of Israel, has driven out the Amorites before HishisHis people Israel; and should you possess their land? 24Do you not possess what Chemosh your god gives you to possess? So whatever the the LORD our God has driven out before us, we will possess. 25Are you now better than Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab? Did he ever contend with Israel, or did he ever go to war against them? 26For three hundred years Israel has lived in Heshbon and its villages, in Aroer and its villages, and in all the cities along the Arnon. Why did you not recover them during that time? 27Therefore I have not sinned against you, but you are doing me wrong by waging war against me. May the the LORD, the JudgejudgeJudge, decide today between the Israelites and the Ammonites."

28But the king of the Ammonites would not listen to the message Jephthah sent him.

The Vow and Its Tragic Consequence

29Then the SpiritspiritSpirit of the the LORD came upon Jephthah, and he passed through Gilead and Manasseh, and through Mizpah of Gilead; from there he advanced against the Ammonites.

30And Jephthah made a vow to the the LORD, saying, "If YouyouYou will indeed deliver the Ammonites into my hand, 31then whatever comes out of the door of my house to meet me when I return in peace from the Ammonites shall belong to the the LORD, and I will offer it as a burnt offering."

32So Jephthah crossed over to the Ammonites to fight against them, and the the LORD delivered them into his hand. 33He struck them down from Aroer to the entrance of Minnith—twenty cities—and as far as Abel-keramim. Thus the Ammonites were subdued before the Israelites.

34When Jephthah returned to his home in Mizpah, his daughter came out to meet him with tambourines and dancing. She was his only child; he had no son or daughter besides her.

35As soon as he saw her, he tore his clothes and said, "Alas, my daughter! You have brought me to ruin; you have brought disaster upon me. For I have opened my mouth to the the LORD and cannot retract it."

36"My father," she replied, "you have given your word to the the LORD. Do to me as you have said, since the the LORD has avenged you of your enemies, the Ammonites." 37She also said to her father, "Let this be done for me: allow me two months to wander in the mountains with my friends and mourn my virginity."

38"Go," he said, and he sent her away for two months. So she and her friends went into the mountains and wept over her virginity.

39At the end of two months she returned to her father, and he did to her as he had vowed; she had never known a man. And it became a custom in Israel 40that the daughters of Israel would go each year to commemorate the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite for four days.

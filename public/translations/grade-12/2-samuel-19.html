David's Grief and Joab's Rebuke

1Joab was informed that the king was weeping and mourning over Absalom. 2The triumph of that day was turned into mourning for the whole nation, because the troops heard that the king was grieving over his son. 3The soldiers slipped into the city that day like men ashamed, stealing away as though they had fled from battle. 4The king veiled his face and cried out, “My son Absalom! Absalom, my son, my son!”

5Joab entered the house and said to the king, “Today you have disgraced every servant who preserved your life today, along with the lives of your sons, daughters, wives, and concubines, because you have favouredfavouredfavored those who hate you and scorned those who love you. 6You love your enemies and hate your friends; for today you have declared that commanders and servants mean nothing to you, since today I perceive that if Absalom were alive and all of us dead, it would seem right in your eyes. 7Now arise, go out, and speak kindly to your servants; for I swear by the LORD that if you do not go out, not one man will remain with you tonight, and that would be worse for you than all the calamity that has befallen you from your youth until now.”

8Then the king arose and sat in the gate. When the people were told, “The king is sitting in the gate,” all the people came before the king, while Israel had fled, each man to his home.

Israel and Judah Call David Back

9All the people were arguing throughout the tribes of Israel, saying, “The king delivered us from our enemies and saved us from the Philistines, yet now he has fled the land because of Absalom. 10Absalom, whom we anointed over us, is dead in battle. Now why are you silent about bringing the king back?”

11King David sent word to Zadok and Abiathar the priests: “Speak to the elders of Judah and ask, ‘Why should you be the last to bring the king back to his house, when all Israel is already calling for his return? 12You are my brothers, my own flesh and blood. Why then should you be the last to bring back the king?’ 13And say to Amasa, ‘Are you not my own flesh and blood? May God punish me severely if I do not appoint you commander of the army in Joab’s place from this day forward.’”

14So David won over the hearts of all the men of Judah as though they were one man, and they sent this message to the king: “Return, you and all your servants.” 15The king returned and came as far as the Jordan, while Judah came to Gilgal to meet the king and escort him across the Jordan.

Mercy, Justice, and Farewells on the Road

16Shimei son of Gera, the Benjamite from Bahurim, hurried down with the men of Judah to meet King David. 17A thousand men from Benjamin were with him, along with Ziba, the servant of Saul’s house, and his fifteen sons and twenty servants; they rushed to the Jordan ahead of the king. 18They crossed the ford to bring the king’s household over and to do whatever seemed good to him, and Shimei son of Gera fell down before the king as he crossed the Jordan.

19He said to the king, “Let not my lord hold me guilty, and do not remember what your servant did wrong on the day my lord the king left Jerusalem; let the king not take it to heart. 20For your servant knows that I have sinned, and therefore I have come today, the first of all the house of Joseph, to go down and meet my lord the king.”

21Abishai son of Zeruiah answered, “Should not Shimei be put to death for this, because he cursed the the LORD’s anointed?” 22David replied, “What have I to do with you, sons of Zeruiah, that you should be adversaries to me today? Shall any man be put to death in Israel today? Do I not know that today I am king over Israel?” 23Then the king swore to him, “You shall not die.”

24Mephibosheth son of Saul came down to meet the king. He had not cared for his feet, nor trimmed his moustachemoustachemustache, nor washed his clothes from the day the king departed until the day he returned in peace.

25When he came to Jerusalem to meet the king, the king asked, “Why did you not go with me, Mephibosheth?” 26He answered, “My lord the king, my servant deceived me; for your servant said, ‘I will saddle a donkey for myself, ride on it, and go with the king,’ because your servant is lame. 27He has slandered your servant to my lord the king; but my lord the king is like the angel of God. Do therefore what is good in your eyes. 28For all my father’s house were but dead men before my lord the king, yet you set your servant among those who eat at your table. What right have I still to cry out to the king?” 29The king said, “Why do you speak any more of your matters? I have decided that you and Ziba shall divide the field.” 30Mephibosheth replied, “Let him take it all, since my lord the king has come safely home.”

31Barzillai the Gileadite came down from Rogelim and crossed the Jordan with the king to see him on his way. 32Barzillai was very old, eighty years of age, and he had provided for the king during his stay at Mahanaim; he was a man of great wealth.

33The king said to Barzillai, “Cross over with me, and I will provide for you in Jerusalem.” 34Barzillai answered, “How many more days have I to live, that I should go up with the king to Jerusalem? 35I am eighty years old today. Can I discern between good and evil? Can your servant taste what he eats or drinks? Can I still hear the voices of singing men and singing women? Why then should your servant be a burden to my lord the king? 36Your servant will go a little way over the Jordan with the king. Why should the king repay me with such a reward? 37Please let your servant return, that I may die in my own city near the grave of my father and mother. But here is your servant Chimham; let him cross over with my lord the king, and do for him whatever seems good to you.” 38The king said, “Chimham shall cross over with me, and I will do for him whatever seems good to you; indeed, I will do for you whatever you choose for me.” 39All the people crossed over the Jordan, and the king crossed over. The king kissed Barzillai and blessed him, and he returned to his place.

40The king crossed over to Gilgal, and Chimham crossed with him. All the people of Judah and half the people of Israel brought the king over.

Dispute Between Israel and Judah

41All the men of Israel came to the king and said, “Why have our brothers, the men of Judah, stolen you away and brought the king and his household over the Jordan, along with all David’s men?” 42All the men of Judah answered the men of Israel, “The king is our close relative. Why are you angry over this? Have we eaten at the king’s expense, or has any gift been given to us?” 43The men of Israel answered the men of Judah, “We have ten shares in the king, and we have more right to David than you do. Why then did you despise us? Were we not the first to speak of bringing back our king?” Then the words of the men of Judah were fiercer than the words of the men of Israel.

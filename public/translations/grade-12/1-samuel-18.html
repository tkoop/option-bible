Jonathan's Covenant with David

1After David had finished speaking to Saul, the souls of Jonathan and David were bound together, and Jonathan loved him as he loved his own life. 2Saul took David into his service that day and would not let him return to his father's house. 3Jonathan made a covenant with David because he loved him as himself. 4Jonathan stripped off the robe he was wearing and gave it to David, along with his tunic, sword, bow, and belt.

5So David went out wherever Saul sent him and succeeded in all he did, and Saul appointed him over the men of war. The appointment pleased all the people and Saul's servants as well.

Saul's Jealousy and Fear

6When the troops returned home after David had killed the Philistine, women came out of all the cities of Israel to meet King Saul, singing and dancing with tambourines, joyful songs, and stringed instruments.

7As the women danced, they sang, "Saul has slain his thousands, and David his tens of thousands."

8Saul was very angry, and the saying displeased him. He said, "They have credited David with tens of thousands, but me with only thousands. What more can he have but the kingdom?" 9From that day on, Saul kept a jealous eye on David.

10The next day an evil spirit from God came forcefully upon Saul. He prophesied inside the house while David played the harp, as he did each day. Now there was a spear in Saul's hand, 11and Saul hurled it, thinking, "I will pin David to the wall." But David eluded him twice.

12Saul was afraid of David, because the LORD was with him but had departed from Saul.

13So Saul removed David from his presence and made him commander of a thousand. David led the troops on their military campaigns.

14David was successful in all his ways, for the LORD was with him. 15When Saul saw that David was highly successful, he was afraid of him. 16But all Israel and Judah loved David, because he led them on their military campaigns.

Saul's Scheme to Entrap David

17Then Saul said to David, "Here is my older daughter Merab. I will give her to you in marriage. Only be valiant for me and fight the LORD's battles." For Saul thought, "MymyMy hand need not be against him; let the hand of the Philistines be against him."

18David said to Saul, "Who am I, and what is my family or my father's clan in Israel, that I should be the king's son-in-law?"

19But when the time came for Merab, Saul's daughter, to be given to David, she was given to Adriel the Meholathite as wife. 20Now Saul's daughter Michal loved David, and when they told Saul, the matter pleased him.

21Saul thought, "I will give her to him, that she may be a snare to him, and that the hand of the Philistines may be against him." So Saul said to David, "For a second time you may become my son-in-law today."

22Then Saul ordered his servants, "Speak to David privately and tell him, 'Look, the king is pleased with you, and all his servants love you. Now then, become the king's son-in-law.'"

23Saul's servants spoke these words to David.

David replied, "Does it seem to you a small thing to become the son-in-law of the king? I am a poor man and lightly esteemed."

24The servants reported these words to Saul.

25Then Saul replied, "Tell David, 'The king desires no dowry except a hundred Philistine foreskins, to take vengeance on the king's enemies.'" Now Saul intended to cause David's death at the hands of the Philistines.

26When the servants reported this to David, he was pleased to become the king's son-in-law. The appointed time had not yet passed. 27David and his men went out and killed two hundred Philistines. He brought their foreskins and presented them as the full number to the king, so that he might become the king's son-in-law. Then Saul gave his daughter Michal to David in marriage.

David's Triumph and Saul's Enduring Fear

28When Saul realizedrealisedrealized that the LORD was with David and that his daughter Michal loved him, 29he became even more afraid of David. And Saul remained David's enemy for the rest of his days.

30Then whenever the Philistine commanders came out for battle, David was more successful than all of Saul's servants, so that his name was highly esteemed.

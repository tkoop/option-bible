The Emptiness of Pleasure and Great Works

1I said in my heart, “Come now, I will test you with pleasure; enjoy what is good.” Yet, as I discovered, this too was futility. 2I said of laughter, “It is madness,” and of pleasure, “What does it accomplish?”

3I searched my heart to cheer my flesh with wine—my heart still guiding me with wisdom—and to embrace folly, until I could see what is good for people to do under Heavenheavenheaven during the few days of their lives.

4I undertook great projects. I built houses for myself and planted vineyards for myself. 5I made gardens and parks for myself, and I planted in them every kind of fruit tree. 6I made reservoirs of water to irrigate a forest of growing trees.

7I acquired male and female servants, and slaves were born in my household. I also owned more herds and flocks than anyone who had lived before me in Jerusalem. 8I amassed silver and gold for myself, along with the treasure of kings and provinces. I acquired male and female singers, and a harem—the delights of mankind.

9So I became greater and surpassed all who had been before me in Jerusalem, and my wisdom remained with me. 10I did not deny my eyes anything they desired; I withheld no pleasure from my heart, for my heart took delight in all my labourlabourlabor, and this was my portion from all my labourlabourlabor. 11Then I considered all that my hands had accomplished and all the toil I had expended, and I saw that all was futility—a fruitless pursuit; there was no profit under the sun.

Wisdom, Madness, and the Shared Fate of All

12Then I turned to consider wisdom, madness, and folly, for what can the person who comes after the king do except what has already been done? 13I saw that wisdom has an advantage over folly, just as light has an advantage over darkness. 14The wise person sees ahead, while the fool gropes in darkness; yet I perceived that the same fate overtakes them all.

15Then I said to myself, “The fate of the fool will befall me as well. Why, then, have I been so exceedingly wise?” And I said to myself that this too is futility. 16For there is no lasting memory of the wise, just as there is for the fool, since in the days to come all will have been forgotten. How tragic that the wise die just like the fool!

17So I hated life, because the work done under the sun was grievous to me; for all is futility and a waste of effort. 18I hated all my toil in which I had toiled under the sun, because I must leave it to the person who comes after me. 19And who knows whether he will be wise or a fool? Yet he will take possession of all my labourlabourlabor and skill with which I toiled under the sun. This too is futility. 20So I turned and gave my heart over to despair concerning all the toil of my labourlabourlabor under the sun.

21For there is one whose labourlabourlabor is with wisdom, knowledge, and skill, yet he must leave his estate to one who has not worked for it. This too is futility.

24There is nothing better for a person than to eat and drink and find enjoyment in his toil.

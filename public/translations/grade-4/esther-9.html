The Jews Win Over Their Enemies

1On the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, called Adar, the king's order was to happen. On that day, the enemies of the Jews wanted to rule them. But it turned around. The Jews ruled those who hated them. 2In every part of King Ahasuerus's land, the Jews came together in their towns. They stood against people who wanted to hurt them. No one could stand against them, because all the people were afraid of them. 3All the leaders, helpers, and workers for the king helped the Jews. They did this because they were afraid of Mordecai. 4Mordecai was great in the king's house. People in every land heard about him. Mordecai kept getting greater. 5The Jews hit all their enemies with swords. They killed and destroyed them. They did what they wanted to their enemies.

6In the strong city of Susa, the Jews killed and destroyed 500 men. 7They killed these sons of Haman: Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha, 8Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha, 9Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai, and Vajezatha. 10These ten were the sons of Haman, son of Hammedatha. He had hated the Jews. The Jews did not take their things. 11That day, the number of dead in Susa was told to the king.

Esther Talks to the King

12The king said to Queen Esther, "In Susa, the Jews killed 500 men. They also killed Haman's ten sons. What have they done in the rest of the land? What do you want? It will be given. What else do you ask? It will be done."

13Esther said, "If it pleases the king, let the Jews in Susa do tomorrow what they did today. Also, hang Haman's ten sons on poles."

14The king said, "Yes. Do it." The order was given in Susa. They hanged Haman's ten sons.

The Jews Rest and Celebrate

15On the fourteenth day of Adar, the Jews in Susa came together again. They killed 300 men in Susa. They did not take their things. 16The other Jews in the king's lands came together. They stood up for their lives. They rested from their enemies. They killed 75,000 people who hated them. They did not take their things.

17This was on the thirteenth day of Adar. They rested on the fourteenth day. They made it a day of feasts and joy. 18The Jews in Susa came together on the thirteenth and fourteenth days. They rested on the fifteenth day. They made it a day of feasts and joy. 19So the Jews who lived in small towns make the fourteenth day of Adar a day of joy, feasts, and good times. They send food to each other.

Mordecai Writes About Purim

20Mordecai wrote these things down. He sent letters to all the Jews in King Ahasuerus's lands, near and far. 21He told them to keep the fourteenth and fifteenth days of Adar every year. 22These were the days the Jews rested from their enemies. Their sad month turned into a happy month. Their crying turned into a good day. They were to make these days into feasts and joy. They were to send food to each other and gifts to poor people. 23The Jews agreed to do what they had started. They also agreed to do what Mordecai wrote to them. 24Haman, son of Hammedatha, was from Agag. He hated all the Jews. He planned to destroy them. He cast lots to pick a day to crush and destroy them. 25When Esther went to the king, he wrote that Haman's bad plan should come back on Haman. Haman and his sons were hanged on poles. 26So these days are called Purim, after the word Pur. This is because of all the words in this letter, and because of what the Jews saw and what happened to them. 27The Jews set these days for themselves, their children, and all who joined them. They would not skip them. They would do these two days every year, at the right time, the way it was written. 28These days are remembered and done in every family and every town, in every age. The days of Purim will not pass from the Jews. Their memory will not end for their children.

Esther's Letter Makes Purim Strong

29Queen Esther, daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew wrote with full power to make the second letter about Purim strong. 30He sent letters to all the Jews in the 127 parts of Ahasuerus's land. The letters had words of peace and truth. 31They set up these days of Purim at their times. Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther set them up. They set them up for themselves and their children. They also set up the times of fasting and crying out. 32Esther's command made these words about Purim strong. They were written in a book.

Paul Sails to Italy

1It was decided that we would sail to Italy. They handed Paul and some other prisoners to a Roman officer named Julius. He led soldiers from the Augustan group. 2We got on a ship from Adramyttium. It was going to ports in Asia. Aristarchus from Thessalonica in Macedonia came with us. 3The next day we landed at Sidon. Julius was kind to Paul. He let Paul go to his friends to get care.

4We left there and sailed under Cyprus. The wind was against us. 5We sailed across the sea near Cilicia and Pamphylia. Then we came to Myra in Lycia. 6There the officer found a ship from Alexandria that was going to Italy. He put us on it. 7We sailed slowly for many days. It was hard to get near Cnidus because the wind would not let us. So we sailed under Crete near Salmone. 8We moved along the coast and barely reached a place called FairfairFair Havens. It was near the city of Lasea.

Paul Warns About the Trip

9A long time passed. Sailing was now dangerous because the special fast day had already gone by. Paul warned them. 10He said, "Men, I see that this trip will bring trouble and great loss. We could lose the ship and the cargo, and even our lives." 11But the officer listened to the pilot and the ship's owner more than to Paul.

12The harbourharbourharbor was not a good place to stay for winter. Most of the men wanted to sail from there. They hoped to reach Phoenix, a harbourharbourharbor in Crete that faced southwest and northwest, and stay there for winter. 13A south wind began to blow. They thought their plan had worked. They raised the anchor and sailed close along Crete.

The Storm Hits

14But soon a strong wind called Euraquilo hit us. 15The ship could not face the wind. It was caught and driven. 16We sailed under a small island called Cauda. We barely got the lifeboat under control. 17They pulled it up. They used ropes to hold the ship together. They were afraid the ship would run aground on the sandbars called Syrtis. So they lowered the sail gear and let the wind push us. 18The storm hit us hard. The next day they threw cargo overboard. 19On the third day they threw the ship's gear overboard with their own hands. 20The sun and stars did not show for many days. The storm kept hitting us. At last we gave up all hope of being saved.

Paul Gives Hope

21After they had gone a long time without food, Paul stood among them and said, "Men, you should have listened to me. You should not have sailed from Crete. Then you would not have had this trouble and loss. 22But now I tell you to be brave. No one will die. Only the ship will be lost. 23Last night an angel of God stood by me. I belong to God, and I serve HimhimHim. 24The angel said, 'Do not be afraid, Paul. You must stand before Caesar. God has given you everyone sailing with you.' 25So be brave, men. I trust God. It will happen just as HeheHe told me. 26But we must run aground on an island."

The Ship Runs Aground

27On the fourteenth night, we were driven in the Adriatic SeaseaSea. About midnight the sailors thought land was near. 28They measured the water and found it was about 120 feet deep. A little later they measured again and found it was about 90 feet deep. 29They were afraid we might hit rocks. So they dropped four anchors from the back of the ship. They prayed for day to come. 30Some sailors tried to escape from the ship. They pretended they were going to drop anchors from the front. They lowered the lifeboat into the sea. 31Paul said to the officer and the soldiers, "If these men do not stay in the ship, you cannot be saved." 32Then the soldiers cut the ropes of the lifeboat and let it fall.

Paul Urges Them to Eat

33Until day was about to come, Paul urged everyone to eat. He said, "Today is the fourteenth day you have waited and gone without food. You have eaten nothing. 34So I urge you to eat. This will help save you. Not one hair from your head will be lost." 35After he said this, he took bread. He gave thanks to God in front of everyone. He broke it and began to eat. 36Then everyone felt better. They ate too. 37There were 276 of us on the ship. 38When they had eaten enough, they made the ship lighter. They threw the wheat into the sea.

They Reach Land

39When day came, they did not know the land. But they saw a bay with a beach. They planned to drive the ship onto it if they could. 40They cut the anchors and left them in the sea. They untied the ropes of the rudders. They raised the front sail to the wind. They headed for the beach. 41The ship hit a place where two seas meet. It ran aground. The front stuck fast and did not move. The back broke apart from the force of the waves. 42The soldiers planned to kill the prisoners. They did not want anyone to swim away and escape. 43But the officer wanted to save Paul. He stopped them. He told those who could swim to jump first and get to land. 44The rest came on boards and pieces of the ship. In this way, everyone was saved and got to land.

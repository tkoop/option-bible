David and Jonathan Become Friends

1After David stopped talking with Saul, Jonathan loved David like he loved himself. Their hearts were joined together. 2From that day, Saul kept David with him. He did not let David go back to his father’s house. 3Jonathan and David made a promise to be friends. Jonathan loved David like he loved himself. 4Jonathan took off the coat he was wearing and gave it to David. He also gave David his clothes, his sword, his bow, and his belt.

David Wins Battles for Saul

5David went out and did well in everything Saul sent him to do. Saul put him over the soldiers. The people and Saul’s servants were happy about this.

The Women Praise David

6When the soldiers came back, David had beaten the Philistines. Women came out of all the cities of Israel. They met King Saul with happy songs, drums, and small harps. 7The women sang and danced. They sang, “Saul has killed thousands, but David has killed tens of thousands.” 8Saul was very angry. He did not like this song. He said, “They give David tens of thousands, but they give me only thousands. What else can he get but the kingdom?” 9From that day on, Saul watched David with jealousy.

Saul Tries to Kill David

10The next day, a bad spirit from God came on Saul. He acted like a prophet in the house. David played the harp for him, as he did each day. Saul had a spear in his hand. 11Saul threw the spear and said, “I will pin David to the wall!” But David moved away from him two times. 12Saul was afraid of David, because the LORD was with David but had left Saul. 13So Saul sent David away from him. He made David a leader of a thousand men. David led the soldiers out to fight and back home. 14David did well in everything he did, because the LORD was with him. 15Saul saw that David did very well. Saul was afraid of him. 16But all Israel and Judah loved David, because he led them when they went out and came back.

Saul Offers His Daughter to David

17Saul said to David, “Here is my older daughter Merab. I will give her to you to be your wife. But you must be brave for me and fight the the LORD’s battles.” Saul thought, “I will not hurt him with my own hand. Let the Philistines hurt him.” 18David said to Saul, “Who am I? What is my family? My father’s family is not important in Israel. How can I be the king’s son-in-law?” 19When it was time to give Merab to David, she was given to Adriel the Meholathite to be his wife. 20Saul’s daughter Michal loved David. When someone told Saul, he was pleased. 21Saul thought, “I will give her to him. Then she can be a trap for him, and the Philistines can hurt him.” So Saul said to David, “Today you will be my son-in-law for the second time.” 22Saul told his servants, “Talk to David in private. Tell him, ‘Look, the king likes you. All his servants like you. Now become the king’s son-in-law.’” 23David said to Saul’s servants, “Does it seem like a small thing to you to become the king’s son-in-law? I am a poor man and not important.” 24The servants told Saul what David had said. 25Saul said, “Tell David this: The king does not want money. He wants one hundred Philistine foreskins. This will pay back the king’s enemies.” But Saul hoped David would die by the Philistines’ hands. 26When the servants told David this, he was happy to become the king’s son-in-law. Before the set time, 27David and his men went out and killed two hundred Philistines. David brought their foreskins and gave them to the king. He did this to become the king’s son-in-law. Then Saul gave his daughter Michal to David to be his wife. 28Saul saw that the LORD was with David and that his daughter Michal loved David. 29He became even more afraid of David. From that day on, Saul was David’s enemy. 30Every time the Philistine leaders came out to fight, David did better than all of Saul’s servants. His name became very respected.

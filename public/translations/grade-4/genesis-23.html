Sarah Dies

1Sarah lived to be 127 years old. 2She died in Hebron, in the land of Canaan. Abraham came to cry and mourn for her.

Abraham Asks for a Grave

3Then Abraham got up from beside his dead wife. He spoke to the Hittites.

4"I am a stranger here with you. Please sell me a place to bury my dead."

5The Hittites answered Abraham. 6"Listen to us, sir. You are a great leader among us. Bury your dead in the best grave we have. None of us will keep our grave from you."

7Abraham stood up. He bowed down to the people of that land, the Hittites.

8He said, "If you are willing to let me bury my dead, listen to me. Ask Ephron son of Zohar for me. 9Ask him to sell me the cave of Machpelah. It is at the end of his field. Let him sell it to me here with you for the full price. Then I will have a place to bury my dead."

Ephron Offers the Field

10Ephron was sitting with the Hittites. In front of all the Hittites who came to the city gate, Ephron the Hittite answered Abraham.

11"No, sir. Listen to me. I give you the field and the cave in it. I give it to you in front of my people. Bury your dead."

12Again Abraham bowed down to the people of the land.

13He said to Ephron so everyone could hear, "Please listen to me. I will pay for the field. Take the money from me. Then I can bury my dead there."

14Ephron answered Abraham.

15"Sir, listen to me. The land is worth four hundred pieces of silver. What is that between us? Bury your dead."

16Abraham agreed. He weighed out the silver Ephron had named. He did this in front of the Hittites. It was four hundred pieces of silver, the kind merchants use.

17So Ephron's field in Machpelah near Mamre was Abraham's. The field, the cave in it, and all the trees around it became his. 18This happened in front of all the Hittites at the city gate.

Sarah Is Buried

19After that, Abraham buried his wife Sarah in the cave in the field of Machpelah near Mamre. This is Hebron, in the land of Canaan. 20The field and the cave in it became Abraham's burial place. The Hittites gave it to him.

The Preacher's Experiment with Pleasure and Grand Achievement

1I said in my heart, “Come now, I will test you with mirth, and see what constitutes the good.” 2I said of laughter, “It is madness!” and of mirth, “What does it accomplish?” 3I searched my heart, purposing to draw my flesh with wine while my heart still guided me with wisdom, and to lay hold on folly, until I might discern what is good for the children of humanity to do in this world during the count of the days of their lives.

4I enlarged my works: I built houses for myself, and I planted vineyards for myself. 5I made gardens and orchards for myself, and in them I planted every species of fruit tree. 6I made pools of water for myself, to irrigate from them a forest of growing trees. 7I acquired male and female servants, and I had servants born in my house; I also possessed great herds of cattle and flocks, more than all who had been before me in Jerusalem. 8I also amassed silver and gold, together with the treasure of kings and provinces; I procured male and female singers, and the delights of the sons of men—concubines in abundance. 9Thus I became greater and increased beyond all who were before me in Jerusalem, and my wisdom also remained with me. 10Whatever my eyes desired I did not deny them; I did not withhold my heart from any joy, for my heart rejoiced in all my labourlabourlabor, and this was my portion from all my labourlabourlabor.

11Then I turned to consider all the works my hands had accomplished and the toil I had expended to accomplish them, and behold, all was vanity and an exercise in futility; there was no profit on EarthEarthearth.

The Advantage of Wisdom and the Commonality of Fate

12Then I turned to examine wisdom, madness, and folly; for what can the man who succeeds the king accomplish, save what has already been accomplished? 13I perceived that wisdom has an advantage over folly, as light has an advantage over darkness. 14The wise man has his eyes in his head, but the fool walks in darkness; yet I also knew that one fate befalls them all. 15Then I said in my heart, “As the fate of the fool befalls me also, why then have I been so surpassingly wise?” And I spoke in my heart that this too is vanity. 16For there is no enduring remembrance of the wise any more than of the fool, since in the days to come all will have been forgotten. How then does the wise man die?—along with the fool.

The Burden of Toil and Its Futility

17So I hated life, because the work done on EarthEarthearth was grievous to me; for all is vanity and an exercise in futility. 18I hated all my toil in which I had toiled on EarthEarthearth, because I must leave it to the man who comes after me. 19And who knows whether he will be wise or a fool? Yet he will have dominion over all my toil in which I toiled and in which I was wise on EarthEarthearth. This too is vanity. 20Therefore I turned to cause my heart to despair of all the toil in which I had toiled on EarthEarthearth.

21For there is a man whose toil is in wisdom, knowledge, and skill, yet he must give his portion to a man who has not toiled in it. This too is vanity and a great evil. 22For what has a man from all his toil and from the striving of his heart with which he toils on EarthEarthearth? 23For all his days are pains, and his occupation is vexation; even at night his heart does not rest. This too is vanity.

Enjoyment as the Gift of God

24There is nothing better for a man than to eat and drink and to let his soul take pleasure in his toil. I also perceived that this is from the hand of God. 25For who can eat and who can enjoy apart from HimhimHim? 26For to the man who is good in HishisHis sight HeheHe gives wisdom, knowledge, and joy; but to the sinner HeheHe gives the task of gathering and collecting, to give to the one who is good in the sight of God. This too is vanity and an exercise in futility.

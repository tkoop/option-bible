Hezekiah’s Righteous Reign and Reforms

1In the third regnal year of Hoshea son of Elah, king of Israel, Hezekiah son of Ahaz ascended the throne of Judah. 2He was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem; his mother’s name was Abi, daughter of Zechariah. 3He executed what was upright in the sight of the LORD, precisely as David, his father, had done. 4He extirpated the high places, shattered the sacred pillars, hewed down the Asherah, and pulverized the bronze serpent that Moses had fashioned, for up to that time the sons of Israel had been offering incense to it; and it was called Nehushtan.

5He reposed his trust in the LORD, the God of Israel; among all the kings of Judah who preceded or succeeded him, none was comparable to him. 6He adhered steadfastly to the LORD, never deviating from HishisHis pursuit, and observed the commandments that the LORD had enjoined upon Moses. 7The LORD was with him; wherever he marched, he prospered. He defied the king of Assyria and refused to render him homage. 8He smote the Philistines even unto Gaza and its borders, from watchtower to fortified city.

The Assyrian Conquest of Samaria

9In the fourth year of King Hezekiah—which was the seventh year of Hoshea son of Elah, king of Israel—Shalmaneser, king of Assyria, ascended against Samaria and invested it. 10At the close of three years they seized it; in the sixth year of Hezekiah—that is, the ninth year of Hoshea, king of Israel—Samaria fell. 11The king of Assyria deported Israel to Assyria and resettled them in Halah, along the Habor, the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes. 12Because they had not hearkened to the voice of the LORD their God, but had transgressed HishisHis covenant and all that Moses, the servant of the LORD, had commanded; they neither listened nor executed.

Sennacherib’s Invasion and Hezekiah’s Tribute

13In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah, Sennacherib, king of Assyria, advanced against all the fortified cities of Judah and subdued them. 14Hezekiah, king of Judah, dispatched envoys to the king of Assyria at Lachish, saying, “I have transgressed; withdraw from me; whatever tribute you impose upon me, I shall discharge.” And the king of Assyria exacted from Hezekiah, king of Judah, three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold. 15Hezekiah surrendered all the silver found in the house of the LORD and in the royal treasuries. 16At that juncture Hezekiah stripped the gold from the doors of the temple of the LORD and from the doorposts that Hezekiah, king of Judah, had overlaid, and delivered it to the king of Assyria.

The Assyrian Envoys and Rabshakeh’s Challenge

17The king of Assyria dispatched Tartan, Rab-saris, and Rabshakeh from Lachish to King Hezekiah with a formidable host against Jerusalem. They ascended and arrived at Jerusalem, and took position by the conduit of the upper pool, which is on the highway to the Fuller’s FieldfieldField. 18They summoned the king; and Eliakim son of Hilkiah, who was superintendent of the household, together with Shebna the scribe and Joah son of Asaph the recorder, went out to meet them.

19Rabshakeh said to them, “Pray, convey to Hezekiah: Thus speaks the great king, the king of Assyria: ‘What is this confidence upon which you rely?’ 20‘You assert—though it is but empty rhetoric—counsel and martial prowess. Now upon whom do you rely, that you have rebelled against me?’ 21‘Now observe: you have placed your confidence in Egypt, that splintered reed, this staff of cane; should a man lean upon it, it will penetrate his palm and lacerate it. Thus Pharaoh, king of Egypt, is to all who trust in him.’ 22‘Yet if you say to me, “We trust in the LORD our God,” is it not HeheHe whose high places and altars Hezekiah has removed, while he enjoined Judah and Jerusalem, “You shall prostrate yourselves before this altar in Jerusalem”?’ 23‘Now, then, strike a bargain with my lord the king of Assyria: I shall furnish you two thousand horses, if you can, on your side, mount riders upon them.’ 24‘How then could you repel even a single captain among the least of my master’s servants? Yet you have trusted in Egypt for chariots and cavalry.’ 25‘Have I, perchance, ascended against this place to devastate it apart from the LORD? The LORD HimselfhimselfHimself said to me, “Ascend against this land and ravage it.”’”

26Eliakim son of Hilkiah, Shebna, and Joah said to Rabshakeh, “Pray, address your servants in Aramaic, for we comprehend it; do not speak with us in the Judean tongue within earshot of the people upon the wall.”

27Rabshakeh replied, “Has my master dispatched me to utter these words to your master and to you alone, and not to the men seated upon the wall, who with you shall eat their own dung and drink their own urine?”

28Rabshakeh then stood and cried with a loud voice in the Judean language, saying, “Hear the word of the great king, the king of Assyria. 29Thus says the king: Let not Hezekiah beguile you, for he is powerless to deliver you from his hand. 30Nor let Hezekiah induce you to trust in the LORD by saying, ‘The LORD will certainly rescue us; this city shall not be surrendered into the hand of the king of Assyria.’ 31Do not heed Hezekiah; for thus says the king of Assyria: Conclude peace with me and surrender to me, and each of you shall eat of his own vine and fig tree and drink from his own cistern. 32Until I arrive and remove you to a land resembling your own—a land of grain and new wine, of bread and vineyards, of olive oil and honey—so that you may live and not perish. Do not heed Hezekiah when he deludes you, saying, ‘The LORD will deliver us.’ 33Has any god of the nations ever delivered his land from the hand of the king of Assyria? 34Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad? Where are the gods of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivvah? Did they deliver Samaria from my hand? 35Who among all the gods of the lands has delivered his land from my hand, that the LORD should deliver Jerusalem from my hand?”

36But the people held their peace and did not reply to him a single word, for the king’s injunction was, “Do not answer him.” 37Eliakim son of Hilkiah, who was over the household, Shebna the scribe, and Joah son of Asaph the recorder came to Hezekiah with their garments rentletrent, and reported to him the words of Rabshakeh.

Nathan’s Parable and David’s Condemnation

1The LORD sent Nathan to David. When he came to him, he said, “There were two men in a certain city—one affluent and the other impoverished. 2The affluent man possessed exceedingly many sheep and cattle, 3but the impoverished man had nothing at all save one diminutive ewe lamb, which he had acquired and nurtured; it grew up alongside him and his children, partaking of his bread, drinking from his cup, and resting in his bosom, and it became to him as a daughter. 4Now a wayfarer came to the affluent man, yet he spared his own flock and herd, unwilling to slaughter from them for the guest who had arrived; instead, he seized the impoverished man’s ewe lamb and prepared it for the man who had come to him.”

5David’s anger blazed fiercely against that man, and he said to Nathan, “As the the LORD lives, the man who has done this deserves death! 6Moreover, because he perpetrated this deed and showed no compassion, he shall repay the lamb fourfold.”

Divine JudgmentJudgementJudgment upon David’s House

7Nathan said to David, “You are the man! Thus says the the LORD, the God of Israel: ‘I anointed you king over Israel, and I delivered you from the hand of Saul. 8I gave you your master’s house and your master’s wives into your embrace; I gave you the house of Israel and Judah, and had that been too little, I would have added to you far more. 9Why, then, have you despised the word of the the LORD by doing what is evil in HishisHis sight? You have struck down Uriah the Hittite with the sword, taken his wife as your own, and slain him by the sword of the Ammonites. 10Now, therefore, the sword shall never depart from your house, because you despised MemeMe and took Uriah’s wife as your own.’ 11Thus says the the LORD: ‘Behold, I will raise up calamity against you from within your own house; before your very eyes I will take your wives and give them to one near you, and he shall lie with your wives in the sight of this sun. 12For you acted in secret, but I will do this thing before all Israel and before this sun.’”

13David said to Nathan, “I have sinned against the the LORD.”

Nathan replied, “The the LORD has taken away your sin; YouyouYou shall not die. 14Nevertheless, because by this deed you have utterly scorned the the LORD, the son born to you shall surely die.”

The Death of the Child and David’s Restoration

15After Nathan had departed to his house, the the LORD struck the child whom Uriah’s wife had borne to David, and he became gravely ill. 16David therefore sought God on behalf of the child; he fasted, went in, and lay all night upon the ground. 17The elders of his household arose beside him to raise him from the ground, but he refused, and he would not eat bread with them.

18On the seventh day the child died. David’s servants were afraid to inform him that the child was dead, for they said, “While the child was still alive, we spoke to him and he would not heed our voice; how then can we tell him that the child is dead? He might do himself harm.”

19When David saw that his servants were whispering among themselves, he perceived that the child was dead, and he asked them, “Is the child dead?”

They replied, “He is dead.”

20Then David arose from the ground, washed, anointed himself, changed his garments, and entered the house of the the LORD and worshiped. He then came to his own house and, at his request, they set food before him, and he ate.

21His servants said to him, “What is this thing that you have done? While the child was alive, you fasted and wept; but now that the child is dead, you arise and eat!”

22He answered, “While the child was still alive, I fasted and wept, for I said, ‘Who knows? Perhaps the the LORD will be gracious to me, and the child will live.’ 23But now that he is dead, why should I fast? Can I bring him back again? I shall go to him, but he will not return to me.”

24Then David comforted his wife Bathsheba; he went in to her and lay with her, and she bore a son, and he called his name Solomon. The the LORD loved him, 25and HeheHe sent word through Nathan the prophet, and he named him Jedidiah, because of the the LORD.

The Capture of Rabbah

26Meanwhile Joab fought against Rabbah of the Ammonites and captured the royal city.

27Joab sent messengers to David and said, “I have fought against Rabbah and have also captured the city’s water supply. 28Now therefore gather the rest of the people, encamp against the city, and take it, lest I take the city and it be named after me.”

29So David gathered all the people and went to Rabbah, fought against it, and captured it.

30He took the crown from the head of their king; its weight was a talent of gold, and it was set with precious stones, and it was placed upon David’s head. He also carried off a great amount of plunder from the city. 31He brought out the people who were in it and consigned them to labourlabourlabor with saws, iron picks, and iron axes, and made them pass through the brick kilns; and thus he did to all the cities of the Ammonites. Then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

The Conflict over Ancestral Traditions and Ritual Purity

1The Pharisees, together with certain scribes who had come from Jerusalem, assembled around HimhimHim. 2Observing that some of HishisHis disciples were eating bread with common—that is, unwashed—hands, 3they took offenceoffenceoffense, for the Pharisees and all the Jews, maintaining the tradition of the elders, do not eat unless they have washed their hands in the prescribed manner; 4and when they return from the marketplace they do not eat without first undergoing ritual ablution, while they have received and hold many other traditions, such as the washing of cups, pitchers, bronze vessels, and couches.

5Accordingly, the Pharisees and the scribes asked HimhimHim, “Why do your disciples not conduct themselves according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with common hands?”

6He replied, “Well did Isaiah prophesy concerning you hypocrites, as it is written: ‘This people honourshonourshonors MemeMe with their lips, but their heart is far from MemeMe. 7In vain do they worship MemeMe, teaching as doctrines the commandments of men.’ 8You have abandoned the commandment of God and hold fast to the tradition of men.”

9He continued, “You do well to set aside the commandment of God so that you may keep your tradition. 10For Moses said, ‘HonourHonourHonor your father and your mother,’ and, ‘Whoever reviles father or mother must surely be put to death.’ 11But you say that if a man declares to his father or mother, ‘Whatever assistance you might have received from me is Corban’—that is, a dedicated gift— 12you no longer permit him to do anything for his father or mother, 13thus nullifying the word of God by your tradition, which you have handed down; and you do many things like this.”

True Defilement and the Condition of the Heart

14Calling the crowd to HimhimHim again, HeheHe said to them, “Listen to MemeMe, all of you, and understand: 15nothing that enters a person from outside can defile him; rather, it is what proceeds from within a person that defiles him.”

17When HeheHe had left the crowd and entered the house, HishisHis disciples asked HimhimHim about the parable. 18He said to them, “Are you also so devoid of understanding? Do you not perceive that whatever enters a person from outside cannot defile him, 19because it does not enter his heart but his stomach, and is expelled into the latrine, thereby purging all foods?” 20He went on, “What comes out of a person—that is what defiles him. 21For from within, out of the human heart, proceed evil thoughts, sexual immorality, theft, murder, adultery, 22greed, malice, deceit, sensuality, envy, slander, pride, and folly. 23All these evils come from within and defile a person.”

Faith of the Syrophoenician Woman

24Rising from there, HeheHe departed into the region of Tyre. He wished no one to know, yet, having entered a house, HeheHe could not remain concealed. 25Immediately a woman whose little daughter was possessed by an unclean spirit heard of HimhimHim, came, and fell at HishisHis feet. 26The woman was a Gentile, a Syrophoenician by birth, and she begged HimhimHim to cast the demon out of her daughter.

27He said to her, “Let the children be satisfied first, for it is not right to take the children’s bread and throw it to the dogs.”

28She answered, “Yes, Lord; yet even the dogs beneath the table eat the children’s crumbs.”

29He said to her, “Because of this answer, go; the demon has left your daughter.” 30Returning to her house, she found the child lying on the bed and the demon gone.

Healing of a Deaf and Mute Man in the Decapolis

31Again leaving the region of Tyre, HeheHe passed through Sidon to the SeaseaSea of Galilee, through the midst of the region of the Decapolis. 32They brought to HimhimHim a man who was deaf and had an impediment in his speech, and they implored HimhimHim to lay HishisHis hand on him. 33Taking him aside from the crowd privately, HeheHe put HishisHis fingers into his ears, and after spitting, touched his tongue. 34Looking up to Heavenheavenheaven, HeheHe sighed and said to him, “Ephphatha,” that is, “Be opened.”

35Immediately his ears were opened, his tongue’s bond was released, and he spoke plainly. 36He charged them to tell no one; but the more HeheHe charged them, the more abundantly they proclaimed it. 37They were exceedingly astonished and said, “He has done all things well; HeheHe makes the deaf hear and the mute speak.”

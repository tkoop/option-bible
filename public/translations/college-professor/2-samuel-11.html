David's Dereliction of Duty and the Seduction of Bathsheba

1In the spring, at the season when kings customarily took the field, David dispatched Joab with his officers and all Israel; they devastated the Ammonites and besieged Rabbah, while David himself remained in Jerusalem. 2One evening David arose from his bed and walked upon the roof of the king's house, and from that vantage he beheld a woman bathing; the woman was exceedingly comely. 3David sent to inquire concerning the woman, and it was reported, "She is Bathsheba, daughter of Eliam and wife of Uriah the Hittite." 4David sent messengers and took her; she came to him, and he lay with her. She had been purifying herself from her uncleanness, and afterward she returned to her house. 5The woman conceived, and she sent word to David, saying, "I am pregnant."

Uriah's Integrity and David's Deceit

6David sent word to Joab, "Send me Uriah the Hittite." Joab sent Uriah to David. 7When Uriah came, David asked how Joab fared, how the people fared, and how the war progressed. 8David said to Uriah, "Go down to your house and wash your feet." Uriah departed from the king's house, and a present from the king followed him. 9Yet Uriah slept at the door of the king's house with all his lord's servants and did not go down to his house. 10When David was told that Uriah had not gone down to his house, he said to Uriah, "Did you not come from a journey? Why did you not go down to your house?" 11Uriah answered David, "ThetheThe ark and Israel and Judah dwell in booths, and my lord Joab and the servants of my lord are encamped in the open field; shall I then go to my house to eat and drink and lie with my wife? As surely as you live, I will not do this thing." 12David said to him, "Remain here today also, and tomorrow I will send you away." So Uriah remained in Jerusalem that day and the next. 13David invited him, and he ate and drank before him, and David made him drunk; in the evening Uriah went out to lie on his bed with his lord's servants, but he did not go down to his house.

The Conspiracy Against Uriah

14In the morning David wrote a letter to Joab and sent it by the hand of Uriah. 15In the letter he wrote, "Set Uriah in the forefront of the hardest fighting, and withdraw from him, that he may be struck down and die." 16As Joab besieged the city, he assigned Uriah to the place where he knew valiant men were. 17The men of the city came out and fought against Joab, and some of David's servants fell; Uriah the Hittite also died.

The Report of Uriah's Death and the LORD's Displeasure

18Joab sent to tell David all the news of the war. 19He instructed the messenger, "When you have finished telling the king all the news of the battle, if the king's anger rises and he asks you, 'Why did you approach so near to the city to fight? Did you not know that they shoot from the wall? Who struck down Abimelech son of Jerub-Besheth? Did not a woman cast an upper millstone upon him from the wall, so that he died at Thebez? Why did you approach so near to the wall?' then you shall say, 'Moreover, your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead.'" 202122ThetheThe messenger went and came and told David all that Joab had sent him to say. 23The messenger said to David, "ThetheThe men prevailed against us and came out to us in the field, but we drove them back to the gate. 24The archers shot at your servants from the wall, and some of the king's servants died; moreover, your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead." 25David said to the messenger, "Say thus to Joab: 'Do not let this matter distress you, for the sword devours now one and now another; strengthen your assault against the city and overthrow it,' and encourage him." 26When Uriah's wife heard that her husband was dead, she lamented for him. 27When her mourning was over, David sent and brought her to his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son. But the thing David had done displeased the LORD.

Guilt Offerings for Concealed Transgressions and Ritual Impurity

1Should an individual sin by withholding testimony after having heard a formal adjuration to bear witness, notwithstanding that he has either seen or known the matter, he shall bear his iniquity. 2Or if a person touches anything unclean—whether the carcass of an unclean wild animal, or the carcass of unclean livestock, or the carcass of an unclean creeping thing—and the fact is concealed from him, he is nonetheless unclean and incurs guilt. 3Or if he touches the uncleanness of a human being, whatever species of impurity renders a person unclean, and it escapes his notice, though he subsequently becomes aware of it, he is guilty. 4Or if a person rashly utters an oath with his lips, whether to do good or to do harm, in any matter concerning which one may swear thoughtlessly, and it escapes his notice, then upon discovering it he shall be guilty in one of these respects. 5When anyone becomes guilty in any of these ways, he shall confess the sin he has committed.

Graduated Sacrifices According to the Offender’s Means

6He shall bring to the LORD his guilt offering for the sin he has committed: a female from the flock, a lamb or a goat, as a sin offering; and the priest shall make atonement for him for his sin. 7If he cannot afford a lamb, he shall bring to the LORD, as his guilt offering for the sin he has committed, two turtledoves or two young pigeons—one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering. 8He shall bring them to the priest, who shall first present the one for the sin offering; he shall wring its head from its neck without severing it. 9He shall sprinkle some of the blood of the sin offering on the side of the altar, and the rest of the blood shall be drained out at the base of the altar; it is a sin offering. 10He shall then prepare the second bird as a burnt offering according to the prescribed ordinance; thus the priest shall make atonement for him for the sin he has committed, and he shall be forgiven. 11But if he cannot afford two turtledoves or two young pigeons, he shall bring, as his offering for the sin he has committed, a tenth of an ephah of fine flour for a sin offering; he shall put no oil on it and shall add no frankincense to it, because it is a sin offering. 12He shall bring it to the priest, who shall take a handful of it as its memorial portion and burn it on the altar, on top of the offerings made by fire to the LORD; it is a sin offering. 13In this way the priest shall make atonement for him for any of these sins he has committed, and he shall be forgiven; the remainder of the offering shall belong to the priest, as in the case of the grain offering.

Guilt Offerings for Trespass Against the Sanctuary

14The LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 15“If a person acts unfaithfully and sins unintentionally against the LORD’s holy things, he must bring to the LORD as his guilt offering a ram without blemish from the flock, valued according to your valuation in silver shekels, according to the sanctuary shekel; it is a guilt offering. 16He must make restitution for the holy thing in which he sinned, add a fifth of its value, and give it to the priest, who shall make atonement for him with the ram of the guilt offering, and he will be forgiven.

17“If someone sins and violates any of the LORD’s commandments concerning things not to be done, even though he is unaware of it, he is still guilty and shall bear his iniquity. 18He must bring to the priest a ram without blemish from the flock, at the proper valuation, as a guilt offering; the priest shall make atonement for him for the wrong he committed in ignorance, and he will be forgiven. 19It is a guilt offering; he is indeed guilty before the LORD.”

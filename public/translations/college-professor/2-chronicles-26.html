The Accession and Early Piety of Uzziah

1All the people of Judah took Uzziah, though he was only sixteen years of age, and enthroned him in place of his father Amaziah. 2He rebuilt Elath and restored it to Judah after the king had gone to rest with his fathers. 3Uzziah was sixteen years old when he began to reign, and he reigned fifty-two years in Jerusalem; his mother was Jecoliah, from Jerusalem.

4He did what was right in the sight of the LORD, following in every respect the example of his father Amaziah. 5In the days of Zechariah, a man conversant with divine visions, he devoted himself to seeking God; and throughout the entire period in which he sought the LORD, God caused him to prosper.

Conquests, Fortifications, and Prosperity

6He marched out against the Philistines, dismantled the walls of Gath, Jabneh, and Ashdod, and founded settlements in Ashdod and throughout Philistia. 7God sustained him against the Philistines, against the Arabs who dwelt in Gur-baal, and against the Meunites. 8The Ammonites paid tribute to Uzziah, whose renown extended to the frontier of Egypt, so greatly had his power increased.

9Uzziah erected towers in Jerusalem at the CornercornerCorner GategateGate, the ValleyvalleyValley GategateGate, and the Angle, and he strengthened them. 10He also built towers in the wilderness and hewed many cisterns, for he possessed abundant livestock in the Shephelah and on the plain; he maintained farmers and vinedressers in the hill country and in the fertile tracts, for he cherished the soil.

11Furthermore, Uzziah kept a standing force prepared for battle, organized into divisions according to the muster rolls compiled by Jeiel the scribe and Maaseiah the officer, under the command of Hananiah, one of the king's principal officers. 12The total number of patriarchal chiefs among the mighty warriors amounted to two thousand six hundred. 13Under their command stood an army of three hundred seven thousand five hundred, capable of waging war with formidable strength, to assist the king against his adversaries.

14Uzziah furnished the entire host with shields, spears, helmets, coats of mailpostmail, bows, and slingstones. 15In Jerusalem he devised ingenious machines, the handiwork of skilled artisans, to be mounted upon the towers and battlements for shooting arrows and hurling massive stones; his fame spread far and wide, for he received extraordinary divine assistance until he had grown mighty.

Pride, Sacrilege, and Leprosy

16Yet when he had become mighty, his heart was lifted up to his ruin; he dealt treacherously with the LORD his God by entering the temple of the LORD to burn incense upon the altar of incense. 17Azariah the priest followed him in, together with eighty priests of the LORD, valiant men. 18They confronted King Uzziah and said, "It is not your prerogative, Uzziah, to burn incense to the LORD, but the office of the priests, the sons of Aaron, who have been consecrated to offer it. Leave the sanctuary, for you have trespassed; nor will this bring you honourhonourhonor from the LORD God."

19Uzziah, who held a censer in his hand to burn incense, became incensed; and while his wrath raged against the priests in the house of the LORD beside the altar of incense, leprosy broke out upon his forehead. 20Azariah the chief priest and all the priests turned toward him, and behold, he was leprous on his forehead; they hurried him out, and he himself hastened to depart, because the LORD had afflicted him.

21King Uzziah remained a leper until the day of his death; he dwelt in a secluded residence, being leprous, for he had been cut off from the house of the LORD. Jotham his son oversaw the royal household and judged the people of the land.

Death and Succession

22The rest of Uzziah's acts, from first to last, were written by Isaiah the son of Amoz, the prophet. 23Uzziah slept with his fathers and was buried with them in the burial field belonging to the kings, because they said, "HeheHe is a leper." Jotham his son reigned in his stead.

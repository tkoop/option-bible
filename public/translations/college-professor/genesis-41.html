Pharaoh’s Ominous Dreams

1After two full years had elapsed, Pharaoh dreamed that he was standing beside the Nile. 2And behold, seven cows, comely in form and corpulent in flesh, ascended from the river and grazed in the marshland. 3After them, seven other cows—repulsive in appearance and emaciated in flesh—came up from the Nile and stood beside the first cows on the riverbank. 4The cows that were repulsive and lean devoured the seven comely, well-fattened cows; then Pharaoh awoke. 5He fell asleep again and dreamed a second time: behold, seven ears of grain, plump and excellent, sprouted on a single stalk. 6After them, seven ears, thin and scorched by the east wind, sprang up. 7The thin ears swallowed the seven plump, full ears; then Pharaoh awoke and realizedrealisedrealized it had been a dream. 8In the morning his spirit was agitated, so he summoned all the magicians of Egypt and all its sages; Pharaoh recounted his dreams to them, yet no one could interpret them for Pharaoh.

The Cupbearer Remembers Joseph

9Then the chief cupbearer addressed Pharaoh: “I am reminded today of my offencesoffencesoffenses. 10When Pharaoh was incensed against his servants and consigned me and the chief baker to the custody of the captain of the guard, 11we both dreamed in the same night, each of us a dream with its own interpretation. 12A Hebrew youth, a servant of the captain of the guard, was with us there; we related our dreams to him, and he interpreted them for us, assigning to each a meaning according to his dream. 13And it transpired exactly as he had interpreted: I was restored to my post, and the other was hanged.”

Joseph Interprets Pharaoh’s Dreams

14Pharaoh then sent for Joseph, and they brought him swiftly from the dungeon; he shaved, changed his garments, and entered Pharaoh’s presence.

15Pharaoh said to Joseph, “I have dreamed a dream, and no one can interpret it; but I have heard of you that, upon hearing a dream, you can interpret it.”

16Joseph answered Pharaoh, “It is not in me; God will give Pharaoh a favourablefavourablefavorable answer.”

17Pharaoh said to Joseph, “In my dream I was standing on the bank of the Nile. 18And behold, seven cows, fat in flesh and beautiful in form, came up from the Nile and grazed in the marshland. 19After them came seven other cows—scrawny, exceedingly ugly, and lean in flesh; I have never seen such hideous cattle anywhere in Egypt. 20The lean and ugly cows devoured the first seven fat cows. 21Yet when they had consumed them, one could not tell that they had done so, for their appearance remained as wretched as before. Then I awoke. 22I also saw in my dream seven ears, full and good, growing on one stalk. 23After them sprang up seven ears, withered, thin, and scorched by the east wind. 24The thin ears swallowed the seven good ears. I related this to the magicians, but no one could explain it to me.”

25Joseph said to Pharaoh, “The dreams of Pharaoh are one: God has revealed to Pharaoh what HeheHe is about to do. 26The seven good cows are seven years, and the seven good ears are seven years; the dreams are one. 27The seven lean and ugly cows that came up after them are seven years of famine, as are the seven empty ears scorched by the east wind; they shall be seven years of famine. 28It is just as I told Pharaoh: God has shown Pharaoh what HeheHe is about to do. 29Behold, seven years of great abundance are coming throughout the whole land of Egypt. 30After them seven years of famine will arise, and all the abundance will be forgotten in Egypt, and famine will consume the land. 31The abundance will not be remembered in the land because of the famine that follows, for it will be exceedingly severe. 32And because the dream was given to Pharaoh twice, the matter is firmly established by God, and God will bring it to pass soon.”

33“Now therefore, let Pharaoh seek out a discerning and wise man and set him over the land of Egypt. 34Let Pharaoh act thus: appoint overseers over the land and take a fifth of the produce of Egypt during the seven years of plenty. 35Let them gather all the food of the good years that are coming and store up grain under Pharaoh’s authority, keeping it in the cities for food. 36That food shall be a reserve for the land against the seven years of famine that will befall Egypt, so that the land may not be cut off by famine.”

Joseph’s Elevation and the Famine

37The proposal pleased Pharaoh and all his servants.

38Pharaoh said to his servants, “Can we find anyone like this, a man in whom is the SpiritspiritSpirit of God?”

39Pharaoh said to Joseph, “Since God has shown you all this, there is no one so discerning and wise as you. 40You shall be over my house, and all my people shall obey your orders; only in regard to the throne will I be greater than you.”

41Pharaoh said to Joseph, “See, I have set you over all the land of Egypt.”

42Pharaoh removed his signet ring from his hand and put it on Joseph’s hand; he clothed him in garments of fine linen and placed a gold chain around his neck. 43He made him ride in the second chariot, and they cried before him, “Bow the knee!” Thus he set him over all the land of Egypt.

44Pharaoh said to Joseph, “I am Pharaoh, and without your consent no one shall lift hand or foot in all the land of Egypt.”

45Pharaoh called Joseph’s name Zaphenath-paneah and gave him Asenath, the daughter of Potiphera, priest of On, as his wife. Joseph went out over the land of Egypt. 46Joseph was thirty years old when he stood before Pharaoh, king of Egypt. Joseph went out from Pharaoh’s presence and passed through all the land of Egypt.

47During the seven years of plenty the land produced abundantly. 48Joseph gathered all the food of those seven years in Egypt and stored the food in the cities; the food from the fields surrounding each city he stored within it. 49Joseph amassed grain in great abundance, like the sand of the sea, until he ceased to measure it, for it was beyond numbering.

50Before the year of famine came, two sons were born to Joseph by Asenath, daughter of Potiphera, priest of On. 51Joseph named his firstborn Manasseh, saying, “God has made me forget all my hardship and all my father’s house.” 52He named the second Ephraim, saying, “God has made me fruitful in the land of my affliction.”

53The seven years of plenty in Egypt came to an end, 54and the seven years of famine began to come, just as Joseph had said. There was famine in all lands, but throughout Egypt there was bread. 55When all Egypt was famished, the people cried out to Pharaoh for bread; Pharaoh said to all Egypt, “Go to Joseph; do whatever he tells you.” 56The famine was over all the face of the earth. Joseph opened all the storehouses and sold grain to the Egyptians, and the famine grew severe in Egypt. 57All the earth came to Egypt, to Joseph, to buy grain, because the famine was severe over all the earth.

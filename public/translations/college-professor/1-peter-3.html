Exhortations to Wives and Husbands

1Likewise, wives, submit yourselves to your own husbands, so that even if some are disobedient to the word, they may be won without a word by the conduct of their wives, 2when they behold your pure and reverent comportment.

3Do not let your adornment be merely external—elaborate braiding of the hair, the wearing of gold, or the putting on of fine garments— 4but let it be the hidden person of the heart, with the imperishable beauty of a gentle and tranquil spirit, which is of great worth in the sight of God.

5For in this manner the holy women of former times, who hoped in God, adorned themselves, submitting to their own husbands, 6as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord; and you have become her children if you do good and are not intimidated by any terror.

7Husbands, likewise, dwell with them in an understanding way, bestowing honourhonourhonor upon the woman as the weaker vessel, and as co-heirs of the grace of life, so that your prayers may not be hindered.

Unity, Compassion, and the Pursuit of Peace

8Finally, all of you, be harmonious, sympathetic, loving as brothers, tenderhearted, and humble-minded; 9do not render evil for evil or insult for insult, but, on the contrary, bless, for to this you were called, that you may inherit a blessing.

10For “Whoever desires to love life and to see good days, let him restrain his tongue from evil and his lips from speaking deceit; 11let him turn away from evil and do good; let him seek peace and pursue it. 12For the eyes of the Lord are upon the righteous, and HishisHis ears are open to their supplication, but the face of the Lord is against those who do evil.”

Suffering for Righteousness and Sanctifying Christ as Lord

13And who is there to harm you if you are zealous for what is good? 14But even if you should suffer for righteousness' sake, you are blessed; do not fear their threats, nor be distressed.

15But in your hearts sanctify Christ as Lord, always being prepared to make a defencedefencedefense to everyone who asks you for a reason concerning the hope that is in you, yet with gentleness and reverence; 16keep a good conscience, so that, when you are slandered, those who revile your good conduct in Christ may be put to shame.

17For it is better to suffer for doing good, if such be God's will, than for doing evil.

Christ's Suffering, Proclamation, and the Salvation of Baptism

18For Christ also suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, that HeheHe might bring you to God, having been put to death in the flesh but made alive in the SpiritspiritSpirit, 19in which HeheHe also went and proclaimed to the spirits in prison, 20who formerly were disobedient when God's patience waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was being prepared, in which a few—that is, eight souls—were brought safely through water.

21And baptism, which corresponds to this, now saves you—not as a removal of filth from the flesh, but as an appeal to God for a good conscience—through the resurrection of Jesus Christ, 22who has gone into Heavenheavenheaven and is at the right hand of God, with angels, authorities, and powers having been subjected to HimhimHim.

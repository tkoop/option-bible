The Solitude of Self-Interest and the Folly of Unguarded Speech

1A man who sequesters himself in isolation pursues nothing but his own craving, and in so doing sets himself in defiance of every manifestation of sound wisdom. 2The fool evinces no delight in understanding; rather, his sole ambition is to parade the unexamined contents of his own heart. 3When wickedness makes its appearance, contempt follows in its train, and disgrace arrives attended by reproach. 4The utterances that issue from a man's mouth are like deep waters, whereas the wellspring of wisdom resembles a gushing torrent. 5It is scarcely salutary to show partiality toward the wicked, or to wrest justice from the righteous in the adjudication of causes.

The Ruin of the Lips and the Security of the Divine Name

6The lips of a fool precipitate contention, and his mouth all but cries out for blows. 7A fool's mouth proves his own undoing, while his lips become a snare for his very life. 8The whisperings of a slanderer are like delectable morsels, and they descend into the innermost recesses of a man's being. 9He who slackens in his labourlabourlabor is brother to the one who perpetrates destruction. 10The name of the the LORD constitutes a fortified tower; the righteous hasten into it and are rendered secure. 11The wealthy man's riches serve as his fortified city, and in his own conceit they stand as a lofty rampart.

Humility, Discernment, and the Adjudication of Disputes

12Before ruin, a man's heart exalts itself; yet humility precedes honourhonourhonor. 13To render a reply before one has heard is folly and disgrace to the speaker. 14A man's spirit can sustain him through infirmity, but a crushed spirit—who could possibly endure it? 15The discerning heart acquires knowledge, and the ear of the wise actively pursues it. 16A gift clears a path for a man and ushers him into the presence of the great. 17The one who first presents his case appears just, until his neighbourneighbourneighbor arrives and subjects it to rigorous scrutiny. 18The casting of lots puts an end to contention and adjudicates between the mighty.

The Power of the Tongue and the Bonds of Friendship

19A brother who has been offended is more formidable than a fortified city, and quarrels are like the bars of a citadel. 20From the fruit of a man's mouth his belly is filled, and by the yield of his lips he is satiated. 21Death and life lie within the power of the tongue, and those who cherish it shall eat of its fruit. 22He who discovers a wife discovers a good thing and secures favourfavourfavor from the the LORD. 23The impoverished man speaks in entreaties, whereas the rich man responds with harshness. 24A man of numerous companions may be brought to ruin, yet there exists a friend who cleaves more closely than a brother.

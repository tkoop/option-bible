Elisha’s Prophecy of Deliverance in Samaria

1Elisha said, “Hear the word of the LORD. This is what the LORD says: ‘At this time tomorrow, a seah of fine flour will be sold for a shekel, and two seahs of barley for a shekel, at the gate of Samaria.’”

2But the officer upon whose arm the king leaned answered the man of God, “Look—even if the LORD were to open the floodgates of Heavenheavenheaven, could such a thing come to pass?”

“You shall see it with your own eyes,” Elisha replied, “but you will not eat of it.”

The Lepers’ Desperate Gamble and Discovery

3Now four men afflicted with leprosy were stationed at the entrance to the city gate, and they said to one another, “Why do we linger here until we die? 4If we propose to enter the city, the famine is there, and we shall perish; if we remain here, we shall perish all the same. Come, then—let us defect to the Aramean camp. If they spare us, we shall live; if they put us to death, we shall die.”

5So they rose at twilight and made their way toward the Aramean camp, and when they reached its outskirts, they discovered that no one was there.

6For the Lord had caused the Aramean camp to hear the sound of chariots, horses, and a mighty host, so they said to one another, “Behold, the king of Israel has hired against us the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Egypt to come upon us.”

7Thus they rose and fled in the twilight, abandoning their tents, their horses, and their donkeys, leaving the camp exactly as it stood, and fled for their lives.

8When these lepers reached the outskirts of the camp, they entered a tent, ate and drank, and carried away silver, gold, and garments, which they hid. Then they returned, entered another tent, took what was in it, and hid that as well.

9Then they said to one another, “We are not acting rightly. This day is a day of good tidings, and we are holding our peace. If we wait until morning light, retribution will overtake us. Come, then—let us go and report this to the king’s household.”

10So they went and called to the gatekeepers of the city, saying, “We went to the Aramean camp, and behold, no one is there—not even the voice of a man—only horses and donkeys tethered, and the tents just as they were.”

11The gatekeepers proclaimed the news, and it was reported to the king’s household.

The King’s Midnight Counsel and the Scouting Expedition

12Then the king rose in the night and said to his servants, “Let me tell you what the Arameans have done to us. They know that we are famished, so they have left the camp to conceal themselves in the fields, thinking, ‘When they come out of the city, we will capture them alive and enter the city.’”

13One of his servants answered, “Please, let some men take five of the horses that remain in the city. Their plight is the same as that of all the Israelites who remain; their plight is the same as that of all the Israelites who have perished. Let us send them and see.”

14So they took two chariots with horses, and the king sent them after the Aramean army, saying, “Go and see.”

15They pursued them as far as the Jordan, and indeed the whole way was littered with garments and equipment that the Arameans had cast away in their haste. So the messengers returned and reported to the king.

16Then the people went out and plundered the Aramean camp, and a seah of fine flour sold for a shekel, and two seahs of barley for a shekel, according to the word of the LORD.

17Now the king had put the officer upon whose arm he leaned in charge of the gate, and the people trampled him in the gateway, and he died, just as the man of God had said when the king came down to him.

18It happened just as the man of God had told the king: “At this time tomorrow, two seahs of barley will sell for a shekel, and a seah of fine flour for a shekel, at the gate of Samaria.”

19And the officer had answered the man of God, “Look—even if the LORD were to open the floodgates of Heavenheavenheaven, could such a thing come to pass?”

“You shall see it with your own eyes,” Elisha had replied, “but you will not eat of it.”

20And so it befell him: the people trampled him in the gate, and he died.

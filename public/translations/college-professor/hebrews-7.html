The Enigmatic Priesthood of Melchizedek

1For this Melchizedek—king of Salem, priest of the MostmostMost HighhighHigh God—who encountered Abraham as he returned from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him, 2and to whom Abraham apportioned a tenth of all things, is first, by interpretation, “King of RighteousnessrighteousnessRighteousness,” and thereafter also “King of Salem,” that is, “King of Peace”; 3bereft of father, mother, and genealogy, having neither commencement of days nor consummation of life, but rendered like unto the SonsonSon of God, he abides a priest in perpetuity.

Melchizedek’s Preeminence over Abraham and Levi

4Now contemplate the magnitude of this figure, to whom even the patriarch Abraham tendered a tenth of the spoils. 5For those among the sons of Levi who receive the priesthood possess, according to the law, a mandate to tithe the people—that is, their brethren—although they too have issued from Abraham’s loins; 6yet this OneoneOne, whose lineage is not reckoned from among them, tithed Abraham and blessed him who possessed the promises. 7And it is without any contradiction that the inferior is blessed by the superior. 8Moreover, whereas here mortal men receive tithes, there it is attested that he lives; 9and, to speak approximately, Levi himself, who receives tithes, was tithed through Abraham, 10for he was still in the loins of his father when Melchizedek met him.

The Insufficiency of the Levitical Order and the Rise of a New Priesthood

11Hence, if perfection had been attainable through the Levitical priesthood—for upon it the people were given the law—what further necessity would there be for another priest to arise according to the order of Melchizedek, rather than according to the order of Aaron? 12For when the priesthood is transposed, a transposition of the law necessarily follows. 13For the OneoneOne concerning whom these things are spoken partakes of another tribe, from which no one has ever attended the altar; 14for it is manifest that our Lord sprang from Judah, a tribe concerning which Moses spoke nothing regarding priests. 15And this becomes still more abundantly manifest if another priest arises according to the likeness of Melchizedek, 16who became such not according to a law of carnal commandment but according to the power of an indissoluble life. 17For it is testified, “You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.” 18For, on the one hand, there occurs an annulment of the preceding commandment by reason of its infirmity and uselessness—for the law perfected nothing—and, 19on the other hand, an introduction of a better hope, through which we draw near to God.

Christ’s Eternal Priesthood and Perfect Sacrifice

20And inasmuch as this was not without an oath—for those who formerly became priests were made so without an oath— 21but HeheHe with an oath through the OneoneOne saying to HimhimHim, “The Lord has sworn and will not change HishisHis mind: You are a priest forever”— 22by so much also Jesus has become the surety of a better covenant. 23Furthermore, there have been many priests, because death hindered them from remaining; 24but HeheHe, because HeheHe abides forever, possesses an untransferable priesthood, 25whence HeheHe is able to save to the uttermost those who approach God through HimhimHim, since HeheHe ever lives to make intercession for them. 26For such a HighhighHigh Priest was befitting for us: holy, guileless, undefiled, separated from sinners, and exalted above the heavens, 27who has no need, like those high priests, to offer sacrifices daily—first for HishisHis own sins and then for those of the people—for HeheHe accomplished this once for all when HeheHe offered up HimselfhimselfHimself. 28For the law appoints men as high priests who possess infirmity, but the word of the oath, which came after the law, appoints a SonsonSon who has been perfected forever.

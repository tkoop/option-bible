Joseph's Stratagem and the Departure of His Brothers

1Then he commanded the steward of his house, saying, “Fill the men's sacks with food, as much as they can bear, and put each man's silver in the mouth of his sack. 2Put my cup, the silver one, in the mouth of the youngest's sack, along with the silver for his grain.” And he did according to Joseph's word. 3At first light the men were dismissed, they and their donkeys.

4They had not gone far from the city when Joseph said to his steward, “Rise, pursue the men; overtake them and say to them, ‘Why have you repaid good with evil? 5Is this not the cup from which my lord drinks, and by which he divines? You have committed a grave wrong in doing so.’”

6So he overtook them and repeated these words to them.

7They replied, “Why does my lord speak in such terms? Such a deed is unthinkable for your servants. 8Behold, the silver we found in the mouths of our sacks we brought back to you from Canaan. How then could we steal silver or gold from your house? 9Whichever of your servants is found with it shall die, and we too shall be slaves to my lord.”

10He said, “Very well; let it be as you have said: the one with whom it is found shall be my slave, and the rest of you shall be innocent.”

The Cup Found and the Return to the City

11Then each man hastily lowered his sack to the ground and opened it. 12He searched, beginning with the eldest and ending with the youngest, and the cup was found in Benjamin's sack. 13At this they tore their garments; each man loaded his donkey, and they returned to the city.

Judah and His Brothers Before Joseph

14When Judah and his brothers entered Joseph's house—Joseph was still there—they fell to the ground before him. 15Joseph said to them, “What is this deed you have done? Did you not know that a man like me practices divination?”

16Judah replied, “What can we say to my lord? What can we speak? How can we justify ourselves? God has discovered the guilt of your servants. Here we are, slaves to my lord—both we and the one in whose hand the cup was found.”

17He said, “Far be it from me to do so. Only the man in whose hand the cup was found shall be my slave; but you may go up in peace to your father.”

Judah's Supplication and Offer of Substitution

18Then Judah approached him and said, “O my lord, pray let your servant speak a word in my lord's hearing, and do not let your anger burn against your servant; for you are like Pharaoh. 19My lord asked his servants, ‘Do you have a father or a brother?’ 20We answered my lord, ‘We have an aged father and a young child of his old age; his brother is dead, so he alone remains of his mother, and his father loves him.’ 21Then you said to your servants, ‘Bring him down to me, that I may look upon him.’ 22We said to my lord, ‘The boy cannot leave his father; for if he leaves his father, his father will die.’ 23But you said to your servants, ‘Unless your youngest brother comes down with you, you shall not see my face again.’ 24When we went up to your servant my father, we told him my lord's words. 25Our father said, ‘Go back and buy us a little food.’ 26We replied, ‘We cannot go down unless our youngest brother is with us; then we will go down, for we cannot see the man's face unless our youngest brother is with us.’ 27Then your servant my father said to us, ‘You know that my wife bore me two sons.’ 28‘The one left me, and I thought, “Surely he has been torn to pieces,” and I have not seen him since.’ 29‘If you take this one from me too, and disaster befalls him, you will bring my greygreygray head down to Sheol in sorrow.’ 30Now, then, when I return to your servant my father without the boy, his life being bound up with the boy's life, 31it will happen that, when he sees the boy is not with us, he will die; and your servants will bring the greygreygray hairs of your servant our father down to Sheol in sorrow. 32For your servant pledged himself for the boy to my father, saying, ‘If I do not bring him back to you, I will bear the blame before my father forever.’ 33Now therefore, please let your servant remain in the boy's place as a slave to my lord, and let the boy go up with his brothers.’ 34For how can I go up to my father if the boy is not with me? I could not endure seeing the calamity that would befall my father.”

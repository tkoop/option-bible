The Sovereign Governance of Human Affairs

1The heart of a sovereign is like a watercourse in the hand of the LORD, and HeheHe inclines it toward whatever HeheHe delights in. 2Every man's course appears upright in his own eyes, yet the LORD examines the motives of hearts. 3To execute righteousness and justice is more acceptable to the LORD than sacrifice.

The Moral Pathology of Arrogance and Avarice

4Arrogant eyes and an expansive heart—the very lamp of the wicked—are sin. 5The deliberations of the industrious redound to surplus, whereas every precipitate impulse issues in penury. 6Treasures amassed by a mendacious tongue are a dissipating vapourvapourvapor and a deadly snare; 7the violence of the wicked sweeps them away, because they refuse to execute justice.

Domestic Concord, Social Compassion, and the Discipline of Fools

8The path of the perverse is devious, but the conduct of the pure is upright. 9It is preferable to inhabit a corner of the rooftop than to share a dwelling with a contentious wife. 10The wicked soul covets evil, and his neighbourneighbourneighbor finds no favourfavourfavor in his eyes. 11When a scoffer is chastised, the naive acquires prudence; and when a wise man is admonished, he apprehends knowledge. 12The RighteousrighteousRighteous OneoneOne considers the household of the wicked and subverts the wicked unto calamity. 13Whoever seals his ears against the outcry of the indigent shall himself cry out and receive no answer.

Secret Generosity, Judicial Joy, and the Perils of Dissipation

14A clandestine gift appeases wrath, and a bribe concealed in the bosom pacifies fierce indignation. 15To administer justice is joy to the righteous and dismay to workers of iniquity. 16A man who strays from the path of discernment shall abide among the congregation of the shades. 17He who delights in merriment shall become impoverished; whoever loves wine and oil shall not grow wealthy. 18The wicked serves as a ransom for the righteous, and the faithless for the upright. 19It is better to dwell in a wilderness than with a contentious and vexatious wife.

Wisdom, Speech, and the Supremacy of Divine Counsel

20Desirable treasure and oil reside in the abode of the wise, but a foolish man squanders them. 21Whoever pursues righteousness and steadfast love discovers life, righteousness, and honourhonourhonor. 22A wise man ascends the city of the mighty and overthrows the stronghold in which they confide. 23Whoever guards his mouth and his tongue preserves his soul from calamities. 24Proud, haughty, mocker—such is the designation of him who acts in arrogant wrath. 25The desire of the sluggard slays him, for his hands decline to labourlabourlabor. 26All the day he covets greedily, yet the righteous gives and does not withhold. 27The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination; how much more when he offers it with wicked design! 28A mendacious witness shall perish, but the attentive man speaks in perpetuity. 29A wicked man hardens his countenance, but the upright considers his conduct. 30There is no wisdom, nor understanding, nor counsel that can avail against the LORD. 31The horse is made ready for the day of battle, but deliverance belongs to the LORD.

The Reign of Abijam and the Davidic Promise

1In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam son of Nebat, Abijam ascended the throne over Judah. 2He reigned in Jerusalem for three years; his mother was Maacah, the daughter of Abishalom. 3Abijam walked in all the sins of his father that had preceded him, and his heart was not wholly devoted to the LORD his God, as the heart of David his father had been. 4Yet for David’s sake the LORD his God granted him a continuing dynasty in Jerusalem, raising up his son after him and establishing Jerusalem. 5For David had done what was right in the eyes of the LORD and had not turned aside from anything the LORD commanded him throughout the days of his life, except in the matter of Uriah the Hittite.

6Throughout Abijam’s life, hostilities persisted between Rehoboam and Jeroboam. 7The remaining acts of Abijam, and all that he did, are they not recorded in the BookbookBook of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah? Moreover, war continued between Abijam and Jeroboam. 8Abijam rested with his fathers and was buried in the CitycityCity of David; his son Asa succeeded him as king.

Asa’s Reforms, Religious Purge, and Aramean Alliance

9In the twentieth year of Jeroboam, king of Israel, Asa became king over Judah. 10He reigned in Jerusalem forty-one years, and his mother was Maacah, the daughter of Abishalom. 11Asa did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, as David his father had done. 12He expelled the cultic male prostitutes from the land and eradicated all the idols his ancestors had fashioned. 13He even removed his grandmother Maacah from her office as queen mother, because she had fashioned a detestable idol for Asherah; Asa cut down her image and burned it in the Kidron ValleyvalleyValley. 14Yet the high places were not removed; nevertheless, Asa’s heart was wholly devoted to the LORD throughout all his days. 15He brought into the house of the LORD the silver, gold, and vessels that he and his father had consecrated.

16War persisted between Asa and Baasha, king of Israel, throughout their days. 17Baasha, king of Israel, advanced against Judah and fortified Ramah, so that no one could leave or enter the domain of Asa, king of Judah. 18Asa then took all the remaining silver and gold from the treasuries of the house of the LORD and the royal palace, entrusted it to his servants, and sent them to Ben-Hadad son of Tabrimmon, son of Hezion, king of Aram, who resided in Damascus, with this message: 19“Let there be a covenant between me and you, as there was between my father and your father. Behold, I have sent you a gift of silver and gold; go and break your covenant with Baasha, king of Israel, so that he may withdraw from me.” 20Ben-Hadad heeded King Asa and dispatched the commanders of his forces against the cities of Israel; he conquered Ijon, Dan, Abel-beth-maacah, and all the region of Chinneroth, together with the entire land of Naphtali. 21When Baasha heard of this, he ceased fortifying Ramah and withdrew to Tirzah. 22Then King Asa summoned all Judah—none were exempted—and they carried away the stones and timber of Ramah that Baasha had been building; with these materials King Asa fortified Geba of Benjamin and Mizpah. 23The rest of Asa’s acts, all his might, everything he accomplished, and the cities he built—are they not written in the BookbookBook of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah? In his old age, however, he became afflicted in his feet. 24Asa rested with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the CitycityCity of David his father; his son Jehoshaphat reigned in his place.

The Northern Kingdom: Nadab’s Fall and Baasha’s Usurpation

25In the second year of Asa, king of Judah, Nadab son of Jeroboam became king over Israel and reigned over Israel for two years. 26He did evil in the sight of the LORD and walked in the way of his father and in the sin by which he had caused Israel to sin. 27Then Baasha son of Ahijah, of the house of Issachar, conspired against him; Baasha struck him down at Gibbethon, which belonged to the Philistines, while Nadab and all Israel were besieging Gibbethon. 28In the third year of Asa, king of Judah, Baasha killed Nadab and succeeded him as king. 29As soon as he assumed the throne, he struck down the entire house of Jeroboam; he left Jeroboam not a single survivor, but destroyed them all, in accordance with the word of the LORD spoken through HishisHis servant Ahijah the Shilonite. 30This occurred because of the sins Jeroboam had committed and had caused Israel to commit, and because he had provoked the LORD, the God of Israel, to anger. 31The rest of Nadab’s acts, and all that he did, are they not written in the BookbookBook of the Chronicles of the Kings of Israel? 32War persisted between Asa and Baasha, king of Israel, throughout their days.

33In the third year of Asa, king of Judah, Baasha son of Ahijah became king over all Israel in Tirzah, and he reigned twenty-four years. 34He did evil in the sight of the LORD and walked in the way of Jeroboam and in the sin by which he had caused Israel to sin.

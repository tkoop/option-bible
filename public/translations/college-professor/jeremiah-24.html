The Vision of Two Baskets of Figs

1After Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, had exiled Jeconiah son of Jehoiakim, king of Judah, together with the officials of Judah, the artisans, and the metalworkers from Jerusalem, and had transported them to Babylon, the LORD showed me two baskets of figs placed before the temple of the LORD. 2One basket held figs of exceptional quality, comparable to the first ripe figs; the other held figs so thoroughly spoiled that they were inedible.

3Then the LORD said to me, “What do you see, Jeremiah?”

I answered, “Figs: the very good ones are exceptionally fine, and the very bad ones are so thoroughly spoiled that they cannot be eaten.”

The Good Figs and the Promise of Restoration

4And the word of the LORD came to me:

5“Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: Like these good figs, so I will regard with favourfavourfavor the exiles of Judah whom I have sent away from this place to the land of the Chaldeans. 6I will set MymyMy eyes upon them for good, and I will restore them to this land; I will build them up and not tear them down, and I will plant them and not uproot them. 7I will give them a heart to know MemeMe, that I am the LORD. They shall be MymyMy people, and I will be their God, because they will return to MemeMe with all their heart.”

The Bad Figs and JudgmentJudgementJudgment on the Remnant

8“But like the bad figs, which are so rotten that they cannot be eaten—thus says the LORD—so I will deal with Zedekiah king of Judah, his officials, and the remnant of Jerusalem who remain in this land, together with those who dwell in the land of Egypt. 9I will make them an object of horror and calamity to all the kingdoms of the earth, a reproach, a proverb, a taunt, and a curse in all the places to which I banish them. 10I will send upon them sword, famine, and pestilence, until they are consumed from the land that I gave to them and to their fathers.”

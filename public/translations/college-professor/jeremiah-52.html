Zedekiah’s Reign and the Siege of Jerusalem

1Zedekiah was twenty-one years of age when he assumed the throne, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem; his mother was Hamutal, daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah. 2He did what was evil in the sight of the LORD, reproducing in every respect the conduct of Jehoiakim. 3For it was on account of the LORD’s anger against Jerusalem and Judah—anger so inexorable that HeheHe ultimately cast them from HishisHis presence—that Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.

4In the ninth year of his reign, on the tenth day of the tenth month, Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, advanced against Jerusalem with his entire host; they encamped around it and constructed siege works against it. 5The city remained under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah. 6By the ninth day of the fourth month, famine had become so severe in the city that the populace had no bread. 7Then the city wall was breached; the Chaldeans, who encircled the city, prompted the warriors to flee by night through the gate between the two walls beside the king’s garden, making for the Arabah.

The Capture of Zedekiah and the Fall of the City

8The Chaldean forces pursued the king and overtook Zedekiah on the plains of Jericho, for his entire army had been scattered from him. 9They seized him and conducted him to Riblah in the land of Hamath, before the king of Babylon, who pronounced judgmentjudgementjudgment upon him. 10At Riblah, the king of Babylon slaughtered Zedekiah’s sons before his eyes; he also slaughtered all the princes of Judah. 11He then blinded Zedekiah, bound him in bronze fetters, and brought him to Babylon, where he confined him in a prison house until the day of his death.

12On the tenth day of the fifth month in the nineteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan, commander of the guard, who stood in attendance before the king of Babylon, entered Jerusalem. 13He burned the house of the LORD, the king’s palace, and all the great houses of Jerusalem; every significant dwelling he consigned to the flames. 14The entire Chaldean army under Nebuzaradan demolished the walls surrounding Jerusalem. 15Nebuzaradan, commander of the guard, deported the rest of the people who remained in the city, the deserters who had gone over to the king of Babylon, and the remainder of the populace. 16Only the poorest of the land did he leave behind, to serve as vinedressers and plowmen.

The Plunder of the Temple and the Deportation of Judah

17The Chaldeans shattered the bronze pillars, the stands, and the bronze sea that were in the house of the LORD, and they carried all the bronze to Babylon. 18They also took the pots, shovels, snuffers, basins, and all the bronze vessels employed in the temple service. 19The commander of the guard took the basins, firepans, bowls, pots, lampstands, pans, and libation bowls—whatever was of gold or silver. 20As for the two pillars, the single sea, and the twelve bronze bulls beneath the stands that King Solomon had fashioned for the house of the LORD, their bronze was too great to be weighed. 21Each pillar stood eighteen cubits high, with a circumference of twelve cubits and a thickness of four fingers, being hollow. 22The bronze capital atop one pillar was five cubits high, and it was crowned with a network and pomegranates of bronze all around; the second pillar was furnished identically. 23There were ninety-six pomegranates on the outer face, while the network bore one hundred pomegranates in total.

24The commander of the guard seized Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the second priest, and the three doorkeepers. 25From the city he also seized the eunuch who had been in charge of the men of war, seven of the king’s close counselors who were found in the city, the secretary of the army commander who mustered the people of the land, and sixty men of the land who were found within the city. 26Nebuzaradan, commander of the guard, took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah. 27The king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death in the land of Hamath. Thus Judah was carried away captive from its own soil.

28These are the people whom Nebuchadnezzar carried into exile: in the seventh year, three thousand twenty-three Judeans; 29in the eighteenth year, eight hundred thirty-two persons from Jerusalem; 30and in the twenty-third year, Nebuzaradan, commander of the guard, deported seven hundred forty-five Judeans. The total number of exiles was four thousand six hundred.

The Elevation of Jehoiachin

31In the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, on the twenty-fifth day of the twelfth month, Evil-merodach king of Babylon, in the first year of his reign, restored Jehoiachin king of Judah to favourfavourfavor and released him from prison. 32He spoke graciously to him and assigned him a seat above the seats of the kings who were with him in Babylon. 33Jehoiachin put off his prison garments and ate at the king’s table for the remainder of his days. 34A regular allowance was provided for him by the king of Babylon, a daily portion, every day of his life, until the day of his death.

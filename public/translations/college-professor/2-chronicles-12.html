Rehoboam’s Apostasy and Shishak’s Invasion

1When Rehoboam had established his reign and grown strong, with all Israel standing beside him, he abandoned the law of the LORD. 2In the fifth year of King Rehoboam—because they had acted treacherously toward the LORD—Shishak, king of Egypt, advanced against Jerusalem. 3He came with twelve hundred chariots and sixty thousand horsemen, and the people who accompanied him from Egypt—Libyans, Sukkites, and Cushites—were beyond numbering. 4He seized the fortified cities of Judah and pushed on to Jerusalem.

Prophetic Warning and Humiliation

5Then Shemaiah the prophet came to Rehoboam and to the leaders of Judah who had assembled in Jerusalem because of Shishak, and he said to them, “Thus says the LORD: ‘You have forsaken MemeMe, so I have forsaken you into Shishak’s hand.’”

6The leaders of Israel and the king humbled themselves and said, “The LORD is righteous.”

7When the LORD saw that they had humbled themselves, the word of the LORD came to Shemaiah: “Because they have humbled themselves, I will not destroy them; I will grant them some deliverance, and MymyMy wrath will not be poured out on Jerusalem by the hand of Shishak. 8Yet they shall become his servants, so that they may learn to distinguish service to MemeMe from service to the kingdoms of the lands.”

The Plunder of Jerusalem and the Bronze Shields

9Then Shishak, king of Egypt, advanced against Jerusalem and carried off the treasures of the LORD’s house and the treasures of the royal palace; he took everything, including the gold shields that Solomon had fashioned. 10King Rehoboam fashioned bronze shields to replace them and entrusted them to the commanders of the guard who watched the entrance to the royal palace. 11Whenever the king entered the LORD’s house, the guards would bring them out and then return them to the guardroom. 12When he humbled himself, the LORD’s anger turned away from him, so that HeheHe would not destroy him utterly; and, indeed, good things could still be found in Judah.

Rehoboam’s Reign and Death

13King Rehoboam consolidated his position in Jerusalem and continued to reign; he was forty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city the LORD had chosen from all the tribes of Israel to set HishisHis name there. His mother was Naamah the Ammonite. 14He did what was evil, because he had not fixed his heart on seeking the LORD. 15The earlier and later deeds of Rehoboam—are they not recorded in the annals of Shemaiah the prophet and of Iddo the seer, together with the genealogical records? Rehoboam and Jeroboam were at war throughout their days. 16Rehoboam rested with his fathers and was buried in the CitycityCity of David; his son Abijah succeeded him as king.

The King's Anger Subsides and a Search for a New Queen Commences

1After these events, once King Ahasuerus's fury had subsided, he recollected Vashti—both her conduct and the decree that had been imposed upon her. 2Then the king's attendants who ministered to him said, "Let them seek out for the king young women—virgins of exceptional beauty. 3And let the king appoint commissioners throughout every province of his realm, that they may gather all the beautiful young virgins into the women's quarters at the citadel of Susa, placing them under the custody of Hegai, the king's eunuch and guardian of the women, and let their cosmetics be provided for them. 4Then let the young woman who finds favourfavourfavor in the king's eyes reign in Vashti's stead." This pleased the king, and he acted accordingly.

Mordecai and Esther Enter the Narrative

5Now there was a Jew in the citadel of Susa named Mordecai son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjaminite, 6who had been exiled from Jerusalem with the captives deported along with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had carried into exile. 7He had reared Hadassah—that is, Esther—his uncle's daughter, for she had lost both father and mother. The young woman was of comely form and fair to behold; and upon the death of her parents, Mordecai had taken her as his own daughter.

8So, when the king's command and decree became known, and when many young women were assembled at the citadel of Susa under Hegai's custody, Esther too was taken into the royal palace and entrusted to Hegai, keeper of the women. 9The young woman pleased him and obtained his favourfavourfavor; he hastened to furnish her with cosmetics, her allotted provisions, and seven select maidens from the royal household, and he transferred her and her attendants to the most desirable quarters of the women's house. 10Esther did not disclose her people or her lineage, for Mordecai had enjoined her not to disclose it. 11And day by day Mordecai would walk before the court of the women's house, to ascertain Esther's welfare and what was being done concerning her.

The Royal Selection and Esther's Coronation

12Now when each young woman's turn arrived to go in to King Ahasuerus—after twelve months had been completed for the women according to their ordinance, for such was the full period of their beautification: six months with oil of myrrh, and six months with spices and women's cosmetics— 13when the young woman went in to the king, whatever she requested was given her to take with her from the women's house to the king's palace. 14In the evening she would enter, and in the morning she would return to the second women's house, under the custody of Shaashgaz, the king's eunuch, guardian of the concubines. She would not return to the king unless the king delighted in her and she was summoned by name.

15When Esther's turn came—she being the daughter of Abihail, Mordecai's uncle, whom he had adopted as his daughter—she requested nothing beyond what Hegai, the king's eunuch and guardian of the women, advised; and Esther found favourfavourfavor in the eyes of all who beheld her. 16So Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus, into his royal residence, in the tenth month—that is, the month of Tebeth—in the seventh year of his reign. 17The king loved Esther above all the women, and she obtained grace and favourfavourfavor before him beyond all the virgins, so that he placed the royal diadem upon her head and made her queen in Vashti's place. 18Then the king held a great banquet—Esther's banquet—for all his officials and servants; he granted a tax remission throughout the provinces and dispensed gifts with royal liberality.

A Conspiracy Uncovered at the King's Gate

19When the virgins were gathered a second time, Mordecai was sitting at the king's gate. 20Esther had not yet disclosed her kindred or her people, just as Mordecai had commanded her; for Esther continued to obey Mordecai's instruction as she had during her upbringing.

21In those days, while Mordecai sat at the king's gate, Bigthan and Teresh—two of the king's eunuchs, doorkeepers of the threshold—grew wroth and conspired to assassinate King Ahasuerus. 22The matter became known to Mordecai, who reported it to Queen Esther; Esther then informed the king in Mordecai's name. 23When the affair was investigated and confirmed, the two men were hanged on a gallows, and the incident was recorded in the book of the chronicles before the king.

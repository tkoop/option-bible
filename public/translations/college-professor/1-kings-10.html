The Queen of Sheba’s Royal Visit

1When the Queen of Sheba heard of Solomon’s renown, which was inextricably bound up with the name of the LORD, she journeyed to Jerusalem to interrogate him with recondite riddles. 2She arrived accompanied by a prodigiously large retinue, with camels laden with spices, an extraordinary abundance of gold, and precious stones; and upon her admission to Solomon’s presence, she laid before him every matter that occupied her heart. 3Solomon resolved each of her inquiries, and no question remained so abstruse that the king could not elucidate it for her.

4Having surveyed the entirety of Solomon’s wisdom, together with the palace he had erected, 5the viands of his table, the disposition of his courtiers, the attendance and attire of his servants, his cupbearers, and the burnt offerings he presented in the house of the LORD, she was left utterly breathless—there was no spirit left within her.

The Queen’s Confession and Blessing

6She said to the king, “The report that I heard in my own land concerning your words and your wisdom has proved veracious. 7I could not credit those tidings until I came and beheld them with my own eyes; indeed, not even half of your wisdom and prosperity had been reported to me, for your attainments transcend the rumourrumourrumor I had heard. 8How blessed are your people, and how blessed are those servants of yours who stand perpetually before you and hearken to your wisdom! 9Blessed be the LORD your God, who has delighted in you and enthroned you over Israel. Because the LORD has loved Israel forever, HeheHe has constituted you king, that you might administer justice and righteousness.”

10Then she presented the king with one hundred and twenty talents of gold, together with an exceedingly great quantity of spices and precious stones; never again did spices arrive in such profusion as those the Queen of Sheba bestowed upon King Solomon. 11Moreover, Hiram’s fleet, which had conveyed gold from Ophir, imported from Ophir an exceedingly great quantity of almug wood and precious stones. 12From this almug wood the king fashioned supports for the house of the LORD and for the royal residence, together with lyres and harps for the singers; such almug wood had never before been seen, nor has it appeared to this day. 13King Solomon granted the Queen of Sheba every desire she articulated, in addition to what he conferred upon her out of his own royal munificence. Thereupon she departed with her servants and returned to her native land.

Solomon’s Golden SplendourSplendourSplendor and Royal Magnificence

14The weight of gold that accrued to Solomon in a single year amounted to six hundred and sixty-six talents, 15apart from the revenue derived from the explorers and from the merchants, together with all the kings of Arabia and the governors of the land. 16King Solomon fashioned two hundred great shields of beaten gold, each consuming six hundred shekels of gold, 17and three hundred smaller shields of beaten gold, three minas of gold apiece; the king deposited them in the House of the ForestforestForest of Lebanon.

18The king likewise fashioned a great throne of ivory and overlaid it with the finest gold. 19The throne possessed six steps; its back was rounded at the top, and armrests projected from either side of the seat, with two lions standing beside the armrests. 20Twelve lions stood upon the six steps, one at each end of a step. Nothing comparable had ever been fashioned for any kingdom.

21All of King Solomon’s drinking vessels were of gold, and every vessel in the House of the ForestforestForest of Lebanon was of pure gold; silver was reckoned as nothing in the days of Solomon. 22For the king possessed a fleet of Tarshish ships at sea, in company with Hiram’s fleet; once every three years the Tarshish fleet arrived, bringing gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks.

International Tribute, Commerce, and Military Might

23Thus King Solomon surpassed all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom. 24The whole EarthEarthearth sought an audience with Solomon to hear the wisdom that God had implanted in his heart, 25and each visitor brought his tribute—articles of silver and gold, garments, weapons, spices, horses, and mules—year after year.

26Solomon amassed chariots and horsemen: he had fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen, whom he stationed in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem. 27The king made silver as abundant in Jerusalem as stones, and cedar as plentiful as the sycamores of the Shephelah. 28Solomon’s horses were imported from Egypt and from Kue; the king’s merchants procured them from Kue at a fixed price. 29A chariot could be imported from Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver, and a horse for one hundred and fifty; and through the same channels they supplied all the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Aram.

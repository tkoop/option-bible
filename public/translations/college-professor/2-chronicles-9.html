The Queen of Sheba’s Visit and Her Recognition of Solomon’s Wisdom

1And the queen of Sheba, having heard the report of Solomon, came to Jerusalem to test him with enigmas, accompanied by a retinue of surpassing magnitude, with camels laden with spices, gold in prodigious abundance, and precious stones; and when she came to Solomon, she communed with him concerning every matter that occupied her heart. 2Solomon expounded to her every one of her inquiries, and there was no matter so recondite that he did not elucidate it for her. 3When the queen of Sheba beheld Solomon’s wisdom and the palace he had erected, 4together with the fare of his table, the seating of his servants, the attendance and apparel of his ministers, his cupbearers and their raiment, and the ascent by which he went up to the house of the LORD, she was left utterly breathless.

5“The report that I heard in my own land concerning your words and your wisdom was true. 6Yet I gave no credence to those reports until I came and my own eyes had seen; and behold, not even half of the greatness of your wisdom had been recounted to me. You have surpassed the report that I heard.”

7“Blessed are your men, and blessed are these your servants, who stand continually before you and hear your wisdom. 8Blessed be the LORD your God, who has delighted in you and set you on HishisHis throne as king for the LORD your God, because your God, in HishisHis love for Israel, has established them forever and has made you king over them, to execute judgmentjudgementjudgment and righteousness.”

9And she presented to the king 120 talents of gold, an exceedingly great quantity, together with precious spices and stones; nor had there ever been spice like that which the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon. 10The servants of Huram and the servants of Solomon, who had brought gold from Ophir, likewise brought algum wood of great value. 11From this algum wood the king fashioned steps for the house of the LORD and for the royal palace, as well as lyres and harps for the singers; and nothing of the kind had before been seen in the land of Judah. 12King Solomon granted the queen of Sheba every desire she expressed, beyond what she had brought to the king; whereupon she turned and departed to her own land, she and her servants.

Solomon’s Annual Tribute and the SplendourSplendourSplendor of His Court

13The weight of gold that came to Solomon in a single year was 666 talents, 14besides that brought by the traders and merchants and by all the kings of Arabia and the governors of the land, who brought gold and silver to Solomon. 15King Solomon made 200 large shields of beaten gold, each overlaid with 600 shekels of gold, 16and 300 smaller shields of beaten gold, each overlaid with 300 shekels of gold; and the king placed them in the House of the ForestforestForest of Lebanon.

17The king also made a great throne of ivory and overlaid it with pure gold. 18The throne had six steps, and a footstool of gold was attached to it; armrests stood on either side of the seat, and two lions stood beside the armrests. 19Twelve lions stood there upon the six steps, one on either side of each step; and no kingdom had ever fashioned the like.

20All of King Solomon’s drinking vessels were of gold, and all the vessels of the House of the ForestforestForest of Lebanon were of pure gold; in the days of Solomon, silver was accounted as nothing. 21For the king had ships of Tarshish sailing with the servants of Huram; once every three years the ships of Tarshish arrived, bearing gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks.

Solomon’s Supremacy Among the Kings of the Earth

22Thus King Solomon surpassed all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom. 23And all the kings of the earth sought the presence of Solomon, to hear the wisdom that God had put in his heart. 24Year by year each brought his tribute: vessels of silver and gold, garments, weapons, spices, horses, and mules.

25Solomon had 4,000 stalls for horses and chariots, and 12,000 horsemen; he stationed them in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem. 26He ruled over all the kings from the River to the land of the Philistines and to the border of Egypt. 27The king made silver as common in Jerusalem as stones, and cedar as abundant as the sycamores of the Shephelah. 28Horses were brought for Solomon from Egypt and from all lands.

The Death of Solomon and the Succession of Rehoboam

29As for the rest of the acts of Solomon, from first to last, are they not written in the records of Nathan the prophet, in the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite, and in the visions of Iddo the seer concerning Jeroboam son of Nebat? 30Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel forty years. 31Then Solomon slept with his fathers and was buried in the city of David his father; and Rehoboam his son reigned in his stead.

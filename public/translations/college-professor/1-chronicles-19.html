The Death of Nahash and David’s Embassy of Consolation

1After these things, Nahash, king of the Ammonites, died, and his son succeeded him. 2David resolved, “I will show steadfast love to Hanun son of Nahash, because his father showed steadfast love to me.” Therefore David dispatched messengers to console him concerning his father; and David’s servants came into the land of the Ammonites, to Hanun, to comfort him.

The Humiliation of the Envoys and the Aramean Alliance

3But the princes of the Ammonites said to Hanun, “Do you suppose that David is honouringhonouringhonoring your father because he has sent comforters to you? Have not his servants come to you in order to explore the land, to spy it out, and to overthrow it?” 4So Hanun seized David’s servants, shaved them, cut off their garments at the middle, even to their posteriors, and sent them away. 5When some went and reported to David concerning the men, he sent to meet them, for the men were deeply humiliated. The king said, “Remain at Jericho until your beards have grown, and then return.”

6When the Ammonites perceived that they had made themselves abhorrent to David, Hanun and the Ammonites sent a thousand talents of silver to hire chariots and horsemen from Aram-naharaim, from Aram-maacah, and from Zobah. 7They hired thirty-two thousand chariots, together with the king of Maacah and his people, who came and encamped before Medeba; and the Ammonites gathered from their cities and came to the battle.

Joab’s Divided Deployment and the Defeat of the Arameans

8When David heard of it, he sent Joab and the whole host of the mighty men. 9The Ammonites came out and drew up in battle array at the entrance of the city, while the kings who had come were by themselves in the open country. 10When Joab saw that the battle was set against him both in front and in the rear, he chose some of the choice men of Israel and arrayed them against the Arameans; 11the rest of the people he placed under the command of Abishai his brother, and they arrayed themselves against the Ammonites.

12He said, “If the Arameans prove too strong for me, then you shall be my deliverance; but if the Ammonites prove too strong for you, then I will deliver you. 13Be strong, and let us be courageous for our people and for the cities of our God; and may the LORD do what is good in HishisHis eyes.”

14So Joab and the people with him drew near to battle against the Arameans, and they fled before him. 15When the Ammonites saw that the Arameans had fled, they too fled before Abishai and entered the city. Then Joab returned to Jerusalem.

The Final Campaign Beyond the Jordan

16When the Arameans saw that they had been defeated before Israel, they sent messengers and brought out the Arameans who were beyond the River, with Shophach, commander of Hadadezer’s army, at their head. 17When this was reported to David, he gathered all Israel, crossed the Jordan, came to them, and drew up in battle array against them; David drew up for battle and fought against them.

18The Arameans fled before Israel, and David killed seven thousand charioteers and forty thousand foot soldiers of the Arameans; he also put to death Shophach, the commander of the army. 19When the servants of Hadadezer saw that they had been defeated before Israel, they made peace with David and served him; nor would Aram any longer help the Ammonites.

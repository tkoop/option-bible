David's Mourning and Joab's Rebuke

1It was reported to Joab that the king was weeping and mourning over Absalom. 2Moreover, the triumph of that day had been transmuted into universal lamentation, for the people had heard that the king was grieving for his son. 3Thus the troops entered the city stealthily, as soldiers who have been routed slink back in shame. 4The king, meanwhile, veiled his face and wailed with a great cry, "O my son Absalom! O Absalom, my son, my son!"

5Joab then entered the king's residence and said, "Today you have humiliated all your servants who preserved your life this day, together with the lives of your sons and your daughters, your wives and your concubines. 6You cherish those who despise you and despise those who cherish you! Indeed, you have made unmistakably clear today that commanders and troops are of no account to you. I now perceive that if Absalom were alive and all of us dead today, you would be gratified. 7Now, therefore, arise, go forth, and speak to the heart of your servants; for I swear by the LORD that if you do not go out, not one man will remain with you tonight, and that calamity will be worse for you than every adversity that has befallen you from your youth until now."

Israel's Deliberation and David's Restoration

8So the king arose and took his seat at the gate. When all the people were informed that the king was sitting at the gate, they came before the king. Meanwhile, the Israelites had fled, each man to his own home. 9Throughout the tribes of Israel, the people were contending, saying, "ThetheThe king rescued us from the hand of our enemies and delivered us from the Philistines; yet now he has fled the land because of Absalom. 10But Absalom, whom we anointed over us, has perished in battle. Why, then, are you silent about restoring the king?"

11King David sent word to Zadok and Abiathar, the priests: "Say to the elders of Judah, 'Why should you be the last to restore the king to his palace, when word of all Israel has reached the king at his house? 12You are my brothers, my own flesh and blood. Why, then, should you be the last to restore the king?' And say to Amasa, 13'Are you not my own flesh and blood? May God deal with me, and ever so severely, if from now on you are not the commander of my army in place of Joab.'"

14Thus Amasa won the hearts of all the men of Judah as though they were one man, and they sent word to the king: "Return, you and all your servants." 15So the king returned and came to the Jordan. The men of Judah, having gone to Gilgal to meet the king, were prepared to escort him across the Jordan.

Encounters at the Jordan: Shimei, Mephibosheth, and Barzillai

16Shimei son of Gera, the Benjaminite from Bahurim, hurried down with the men of Judah to meet King David. 17A thousand men of Benjamin were with him, along with Ziba, the steward of Saul's house, with his fifteen sons and twenty servants; they pressed forward to the Jordan ahead of the king 18and crossed the ford to bring over the king's household and to do whatever seemed good to him. As Shimei son of Gera crossed the Jordan, he fell prostrate before the king 19and said, "MymyMy lord, do not impute guilt to me, and do not remember the wrong your servant committed on the day my lord the king departed from Jerusalem; let not the king take it to heart. 20For your servant knows that he has sinned, and behold, I have come today, first among all the house of Joseph, to go down and meet my lord the king."

21Abishai son of Zeruiah, however, said, "Should not Shimei be put to death for this, since he cursed the LORD's anointed?" 22David replied, "What have I to do with you, sons of Zeruiah, that you should be adversaries to me today? Is any man to be put to death in Israel today? Do I not know that today I am king over Israel?" 23So the king said to Shimei, "YouyouYou shall not die." And the king swore an oath to him.

24Mephibosheth, Saul's grandson, also went down to meet the king. He had neither dressed his feet nor trimmed his moustachemoustachemustache nor washed his clothes from the day the king departed until the day he returned in peace. 25When he came to Jerusalem to meet the king, the king said to him, "Why did you not go with me, Mephibosheth?" 26"My lord the king," he replied, "because your servant is lame, I said, 'I will saddle a donkey for myself, mount it, and accompany the king.' But my servant Ziba deceived me and slandered your servant to my lord the king. 27Yet my lord the king is like the angel of God; therefore do what is good in your eyes. 28For all my father's house were as good as dead before my lord the king, yet you placed your servant among those who eat at your table. What further claim, then, do I have to cry out to the king?" 29The king replied, "Why speak further of your affairs? I have decided that you and Ziba shall divide the field." 30Mephibosheth said to the king, "Let him take it all, since my lord the king has returned to his house in peace."

31Barzillai the Gileadite came down from Rogelim to escort the king across the Jordan. 32Barzillai was very old, eighty years of age, and he had sustained the king during his stay at Mahanaim, for he was a man of great wealth. 33The king said to Barzillai, "Cross over with me, and I will provide for you in Jerusalem." 34Barzillai replied to the king, "How many years of life remain to me, that I should go up to Jerusalem with the king?" 35"I am eighty years old today. Can I still distinguish good from evil? Can your servant taste what he eats or drinks? Can he hear the voices of singing men and women? Why should your servant be a burden to my lord the king?" 36"Your servant will go only a little way across the Jordan with the king. Why should the king repay me with such a reward?" 37"Please let your servant return, that I may die in my own city, near the tomb of my father and mother. But here is your servant Chimham; let him cross over with my lord the king, and do for him what seems good to you." 38The king replied, "Chimham shall cross over with me, and I will do for him what seems good to you; whatever you desire of me, I will do for you." 39So all the people crossed the Jordan, and then the king crossed. The king kissed Barzillai, blessed him, and he returned to his home.

Tribal Rivalry Resurfaces

40The king then went on to Gilgal, and Chimham accompanied him. All the people of Judah, and half the people of Israel, escorted the king across. 41Soon all the men of Israel came to the king and said, "Why have our brothers, the men of Judah, stolen you away and brought the king and his household across the Jordan, together with all David's men?" 42The men of Judah answered the men of Israel, "Because the king is our close kinsman. Why are you angry over this? Have we eaten at the king's expense, or has he given us any gift?" 43The men of Israel answered the men of Judah, "We have ten shares in the king, and we have even more claim on David than you. Why then did you despise us? Were we not the first to speak of restoring our king?" But the words of the men of Judah were harsher than those of the men of Israel.

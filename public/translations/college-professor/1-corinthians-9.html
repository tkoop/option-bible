Apostolic Credentials and Rights Affirmed

1Am I not at liberty? Am I not an apostle? Have I not beheld Jesus our Lord? Are you not my work in the Lord? 2If to others I am no apostle, yet to you I assuredly am; for you constitute the seal of my apostolic commission in the Lord. 3This is my defencedefencedefense to those who scrutinizescrutinisescrutinize me.

The Right to Material Support from the Gospel

4Do we not possess the prerogative to eat and drink? 5Do we not possess the prerogative to be accompanied by a believing wife, as the remaining apostles do, and the brothers of the Lord, and Cephas? 6Or is it solely Barnabas and I who lack the authority to refrain from labourlabourlabor? 7Who ever serves as a soldier at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard and does not eat of its fruit? Or who tends a flock and does not drink of the flock's milk?

8Do I articulate these matters merely from a human standpoint? Does not the LawlawLaw itself declare the same? 9For it is written in the LawlawLaw of Moses, “You shall not muzzle an ox while it is threshing.” Is it for oxen that God is concerned? 10Or does HeheHe not speak altogether for our sake? Assuredly, for our sake it was written, that the plowman should plow in hope and the thresher should thresh in hope of sharing in the harvest.

11If we have sown spiritual things among you, is it excessive that we should reap your material things? 12If others share in this right with respect to you, should not we do so all the more? Yet we have not made use of this right; instead, we endure everything, so that we may not hinder the gospel of Christ.

Voluntary Renunciation and the Compulsion to Preach

13Do you not know that those who labourlabourlabor in the temple eat of the temple's provisions, and those who attend the altar share in the altar's offerings? 14In like manner, the Lord has ordained that those who proclaim the gospel should derive their living from the gospel.

15But I have availed myself of none of these things, nor do I write these things so that they should be done for me; for it would be better for me to die than for anyone to deprive me of my boast. 16For if I preach the gospel, I have no ground for boasting, since necessity is laid upon me; woe to me, indeed, if I do not preach the gospel! 17For if I do this of my own accord, I have a reward; but if unwillingly, I have been entrusted with a stewardship. 18What, then, is my reward? That, in proclaiming the gospel, I may present it without charge, so as not to make full use of my prerogative in the gospel.

19For though I am free from all, I have enslaved myself to all, that I might gain the greater number. 20To the Jews I became as a Jew, that I might win the Jews; to those under the law I became as one under the law—though I myself am not under the law—that I might win those under the law. 21To those without the law I became as one without the law—not being without the law of God but under the law of Christ—that I might win those without the law. 22To the weak I became weak, that I might win the weak. I have become all things to all people, that by all means I might save some. 23And I do all things for the sake of the gospel, that I may become a co-partaker of it.

Athletic Discipline in Pursuit of an Imperishable Crown

24Do you not know that in a race all the runners run, but only one receives the prize? Run in such a way that you may obtain it. 25Every competitor who enters the contest exercises self-control in all things; they do it to receive a perishable crown, but we an imperishable one. 26Therefore I do not run as one running aimlessly; I do not box as one beating the air. 27Rather, I discipline my body and bring it into subjection, lest, after having preached to others, I myself should be disqualified.

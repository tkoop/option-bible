The Sabbatical Remission and the Promise of Prosperity

1At the expiration of every seventh year, you shall institute a remission. 2And this is the statute governing the release: every holder of a debt shall remit the obligation his hand has extended to his neighbourneighbourneighbor; he shall not press his neighbourneighbourneighbor, even his brother, for repayment, because the LORD’s release has been proclaimed. 3You may exact a debt from an alien, yet whatever claim you hold against your brother, your hand shall relinquish. 4Nevertheless, there shall be no indigent among you, for the LORD will surely bless you in the land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance to possess. 5If only you diligently heed the voice of the LORD your God, observing carefully to perform all this commandment that I am enjoining upon you this day. 6For the LORD your God will bless you, as HeheHe promised you; and you shall lend to many nations, but you shall not borrow; and you shall rule over many nations, but they shall not rule over you.

Open-Handed Generosity Toward the Indigent

7If there is among you an indigent person, one of your brothers, in any of your towns within the land that the LORD your God is giving you, you shall not harden your heart nor close your hand against your indigent brother. 8Rather, you shall open your hand liberally to him and lend him whatever suffices for his need, namely, whatever he lacks.

9Beware lest a base thought arise in your heart, and you say, “The seventh year, the year of release, is at hand,” and your eye look upon your indigent brother with grudging ill will, so that you give him nothing; then he will cry out to the LORD against you, and guilt shall be upon you.

10You shall give to him ungrudgingly, and your heart shall not be grieved when you give to him, because on account of this very matter the LORD your God will bless you in all your work and in every undertaking of your hand. 11For the indigent will never cease from the midst of the land; therefore I command you, saying, “You shall open your hand wide to your brother, to your afflicted, and to your indigent in your land.”

The Manumission of Hebrew Servants

12If your brother, a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman, is sold to you, he shall serve you six years, and in the seventh year you shall send him forth free from you. 13And when you send him forth free from you, you shall not send him away empty-handed. 14You shall furnish him liberally from your flock, from your threshing floor, and from your winepress; as the LORD your God has blessed you, you shall give to him. 15And you shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and the LORD your God redeemed you; therefore I command you this matter today.

16But if he says to you, “I will not go out from you,” because he loves you and your household, and because it is well with him with you, 17then you shall take an awl and thrust it through his ear into the door, and he shall be your servant forever; and to your maidservant you shall do likewise. 18It shall not be grievous in your eyes when you send him away free from you, for he has served you six years at half the cost of a hired worker; and the LORD your God will bless you in all that you do.

The Consecration of Firstborn Livestock

19Every firstborn male that is born among your herd and among your flock you shall consecrate to the LORD your God; you shall do no work with the firstborn of your herd, nor shear the firstborn of your flock. 20You shall eat it, you and your household, before the LORD your God year by year at the place that the LORD will choose. 21But if there is in it a blemish—if it is lame or blind or has any serious defect—you shall not sacrifice it to the LORD your God. 22You shall eat it within your towns; the unclean and the clean alike may eat it, as the gazelle and as the deer. 23Only you shall not eat its blood; you shall pour it out on the earth like water.

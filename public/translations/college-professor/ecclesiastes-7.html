A Good Name and the Wisdom of Mourning

1A good name is better than precious ointment, and the day of death better than the day of birth. 2It is better to go to the house of mourning than to go to the house of feasting, for that is the end of every human being, and the living should take it to heart. 3Sorrow is better than laughter, for by the sadness of countenance the heart is made good. 4The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning, but the heart of fools is in the house of mirth. 5It is better to hear the rebuke of the wise than for a man to hear the song of fools. 6For as the crackling of thorns beneath a pot, so is the laughter of fools; this too is vanity.

Patience, Providence, and the Limits of Human Foresight

7For oppression drives the wise to folly, and a bribe corrupts the heart. 8Better is the end of a matter than its beginning; patience is better than pride. 9Do not be quick to anger in your spirit, for anger lodges in the bosom of fools. 10Do not say, "Why were the former days better than these?" for it is not from wisdom that you ask this. 11Wisdom, alongside an inheritance, is good, and it is an even greater benefit to those who see the sun. 12For wisdom is a shelter, as money is a shelter, but the advantage of knowledge is that wisdom preserves the life of its possessors. 13Consider the work of God: who can straighten what HeheHe has made crooked? 14In the day of prosperity be joyful, and in the day of adversity consider that God has made the one as well as the other, so that no one may know what will come after him.

Moderation, Universal Fallibility, and the Quest for Wisdom

15In my days of vanity I have seen everything: there is a righteous man who perishes in his righteousness, and a wicked man who prolongs his life in his wickedness. 16Do not be excessively righteous, nor be overly wise; why should you destroy yourself? 17Do not be excessively wicked, nor be a fool; why should you die before your time? 18It is good that you grasp this, and that you do not withdraw your hand from that; for the one who fears God will avoid every extreme. 19Wisdom strengthens the wise more than ten rulers who are in a city. 20Surely there is no righteous man on EarthEarthearth who does good and never sins. 21Moreover, do not take to heart every word that is spoken, lest you hear your servant cursing you. 22For your own heart knows that many times you have cursed others.

23All this I have tested by wisdom; I said, "I will be wise," but it was far from me. 24That which is far off and exceedingly deep—who can find it out? 25I turned my heart to know, to search out, and to seek wisdom and the sum of things, and to know the wickedness of folly and the madness of foolishness. 26And I find more bitter than death the woman whose heart is snares and nets, whose hands are chains. The one who pleases God escapes from her, but the sinner is ensnared by her. 27"See, this is what I found," says the TeacherteacherTeacher, "adding one thing to another in order to discover the sum." 28My soul is still seeking, and I have not found it: one man among a thousand I have found, but a woman among all these I have not found. 29See, this alone I found: God made man upright, but they have sought out many schemes.

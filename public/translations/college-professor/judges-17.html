Micah’s Idolatry and His Mother’s Consecration

1In those days there was a man in the hill country of Ephraim whose name was Micah.

2He said to his mother, “The eleven hundred shekels of silver that were stolen from you, and over which you pronounced a curse in my hearing—look, the silver is with me; I took it.”

His mother answered, “Blessed be my son by the LORD.”

3He restored the eleven hundred shekels of silver to his mother, and his mother declared, “I have fully consecrated the silver to the LORD from my hand for my son, to fashion a carved image and a molded image; and now I give it back to you.”

4So he returned the silver to his mother. His mother took two hundred shekels of silver and gave them to a silversmith, who fashioned them into a carved image and a molded image, and these were placed in the house of Micah.

5The man Micah had a shrine, and he made an ephod and household idols, and he consecrated one of his sons to serve as his priest.

6In those days there was no king in Israel; every man did what was right in his own eyes.

A Wandering Levite Hired as Priest

7Now there was a young man from Bethlehem in Judah, of the clan of Judah, who was a Levite residing there as an alien.

8This man left the city, Bethlehem in Judah, to sojourn wherever he might find a place, and on his journey he came to the hill country of Ephraim, to the house of Micah.

9Micah asked him, “Where do you come from?” He replied, “I am a Levite from Bethlehem in Judah, and I am travellingtravellingtraveling to sojourn wherever I may find a place.”

10Micah said to him, “Stay with me and be to me a father and a priest, and I will give you ten shekels of silver per year, a set of garments, and your sustenance.” So the Levite went in.

11The Levite consented to dwell with the man, and the young man became to him like one of his sons.

12Micah consecrated the Levite, and the young man became his priest and remained in the house of Micah.

13Then Micah said, “Now I know that the LORD will prosper me, because I have a Levite as my priest.”

The Siege and Fall of Jerusalem

1In the ninth year of his reign, on the tenth day of the tenth month, Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, advanced against Jerusalem with his entire host; he encamped against the city and constructed siege works encircling it. 2The city remained under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah. 3By the ninth day of the fourth month, famine had become severe in the city, and the people had no bread.

4Then the city wall was breached, and all the men of war fled by night through the gate between the two walls near the king’s garden, though the Chaldeans surrounded the city; they took the road toward the Arabah. 5The Chaldean army pursued the king and overtook him in the plains of Jericho, and all his troops deserted him. 6They seized the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where judgmentjudgementjudgment was pronounced upon him. 7They slaughtered Zedekiah’s sons before his eyes, then blinded Zedekiah, bound him in bronze shackles, and carried him off to Babylon.

The Destruction of Jerusalem and the Temple

8In the fifth month, on the seventh day of the month—it was the nineteenth year of King Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon—Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard and a servant of the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem. 9He burned the house of the LORD, the king’s house, and all the houses of Jerusalem; every great house he consumed with fire. 10The whole Chaldean army under the captain of the guard tore down the walls surrounding Jerusalem. 11Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, carried into exile the rest of the people who remained in the city, the deserters who had gone over to the king of Babylon, and the rest of the multitude. 12But the captain of the guard left some of the poorest of the land to be vinedressers and plowmen.

13The Chaldeans broke in pieces the bronze pillars, the stands, and the bronze sea that were in the house of the LORD, and carried their bronze to Babylon. 14They also took the pots, the shovels, the snuffers, the spoons, and all the bronze vessels used in the temple service. 15The captain of the guard took the firepans and the bowls, together with whatever vessels were of gold or of silver. 16As for the two pillars, the one sea, and the stands that Solomon had made for the house of the LORD, the bronze of all these vessels was beyond weighing. 17The height of one pillar was eighteen cubits, its bronze capital was three cubits high, and a network with pomegranates of bronze encircled the capital all around; the second pillar was the same, with its network.

18The captain of the guard took Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the second priest, and the three keepers of the threshold. 19From the city he took one officer who had been in command of the men of war, five men of the king’s council who were found in the city, the scribe of the commander of the army who mustered the people of the land, and sixty men of the people of the land who were found in the city. 20Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah. 21The king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death at Riblah in the land of Hamath. Thus Judah was carried away from its land into exile.

Gedaliah’s Governorship and Its Violent End

22Over the people who remained in the land of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had left, he appointed Gedaliah son of Ahikam son of Shaphan. 23When all the commanders of the armies and their men heard that the king of Babylon had appointed Gedaliah, they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah: Ishmael son of Nethaniah, Johanan son of Kareah, Seraiah son of Tanhumeth the Netophathite, Jaazaniah son of the Maacathite, and their men. 24Gedaliah swore to them and their men and said, “Do not fear the servants of the Chaldeans. Dwell in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it will be well with you.”

25But in the seventh month, Ishmael son of Nethaniah son of Elishama, of the royal family, came with ten men and struck down Gedaliah, so that he died, together with the Jews and the Chaldeans who were with him at Mizpah. 26Then all the people, from the least to the greatest, and the commanders of the armies, arose and went to Egypt, for they were afraid of the Chaldeans.

Jehoiachin’s Release from Imprisonment

27In the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Judah, on the twenty-seventh day of the twelfth month, Evil-merodach king of Babylon, in the first year of his reign, lifted up the head of Jehoiachin king of Judah and brought him out of prison. 28He spoke kindly to him and set his throne above the thrones of the kings who were with him in Babylon. 29He changed Jehoiachin’s prison garments, and he ate bread continually in the king’s presence all the days of his life. 30And his allowance, a regular allowance from the king, was given to him daily, for all the days of his life.

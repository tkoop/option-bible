Absalom’s Subtle Usurpation of Israel’s Affections

1After these events, Absalom procured for himself a chariot and horses, together with fifty men to run before him. 2He would rise early and take his stand beside the road leading to the city gate; and whenever a man with a grievance came to the king for judgmentjudgementjudgment, Absalom would call out to him, “What town are you from?” and the man would reply, “Your servant is from one of the tribes of Israel.”

3Then Absalom would say to him, “See, your claims are good and right, but there is no one deputed by the king to hear you.” 4He would add, “If only I were appointed judge in the land! Then everyone who has any suit or case could come to me, and I would see that he received justice.”

5Moreover, whenever anyone approached him to bow down before him, Absalom would extend his hand, seize him, and kiss him. 6In this manner Absalom treated all the Israelites who came to the king for judgmentjudgementjudgment, and so he purloined the hearts of the people of Israel.

The Hebron Conspiracy and the Gathering Storm

7At the end of four years, Absalom said to the king, “Let me go to Hebron and fulfillfulfilfulfill the vow that I made to the LORD. 8For while your servant was living at Geshur in Aram, I made this vow: ‘If the LORD brings me back to Jerusalem, I will worship the LORD in Hebron.’” 9The king said to him, “Go in peace.” So he arose and went to Hebron.

10Then Absalom sent secret messengers throughout all the tribes of Israel, saying, “As soon as you hear the sound of the trumpet, you shall say, ‘Absalom is king in Hebron.’” 11Two hundred men from Jerusalem had accompanied Absalom; they had been invited as guests and went in all innocence, knowing nothing of the matter. 12While Absalom was offering sacrifices, he also sent for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David’s counsellorcounsellorcounselor, from his city Giloh; and the conspiracy became formidable, while Absalom’s following continued to increase.

David’s Flight from Jerusalem and the Ark’s Return

13A messenger came to David and said, “The hearts of the men of Israel have gone after Absalom.” 14David then said to all his servants who were with him in Jerusalem, “Arise, and let us flee, for otherwise we shall have no escape from Absalom. Let us depart in haste, lest he overtake us suddenly, bring disaster upon us, and strike the city with the edge of the sword.”

15The king’s servants answered him, “Behold, YouryourYour servants are ready to do whatever our lord the king chooses.” 16The king went out, and all his household followed him on foot; but the king left ten concubines to keep the house. 17The king went out, and all the people followed him on foot, and they halted at the last house.

18All his servants passed by him, and all the Cherethites and Pelethites, and all the six hundred Gittites who had come with him from Gath, passed before the king. 19The king said to Ittai the Gittite, “Why should you go along with us? Return and remain with the king, for you are a foreigner and an exile from your own country. 20You came only yesterday; should I make you wander with us when I do not know where I am going? Return and take your people with you. May the LORD show you kindness and faithfulness.”

21But Ittai answered the king, “As the LORD lives, and as my lord the king lives, wherever my lord the king shall be, whether for life or for death, there shall your servant be.” 22David said to Ittai, “Go then, and pass over.” So Ittai the Gittite passed over, with all his men and all the little ones who were with him. 23All the land wept with a loud voice as all the people passed by; the king also crossed the brook Kidron, and all the people passed on toward the wilderness.

24Zadok was there also, and all the Levites with him, bearing the ark of the covenant of God; they set down the ark of God, and Abiathar offered sacrifices until all the people had finished passing out of the city. 25The king said to Zadok, “Take the ark of God back into the city. If I find favourfavourfavor in the eyes of the LORD, HeheHe will bring me back and let me see it and HishisHis dwelling place again. 26But if HeheHe says, ‘I have no pleasure in you,’ then here I am; let HimhimHim do to me what seems good in HishisHis eyes.”

27The king also said to Zadok the priest, “Are you not a seer? Return to the city in peace, taking your son Ahimaaz and Jonathan son of Abiathar. You and Abiathar shall take your two sons with you. 28See, I will wait at the fords of the wilderness until word comes from you to inform me.” 29So Zadok and Abiathar carried the ark of God back to Jerusalem and remained there.

The Ascent of the Mount of Olives and Hushai’s Commission

30But David went up the ascent of the Mount of Olives, weeping as he went, with his head covered and walking barefoot; and all the people who were with him covered their heads and went up weeping. 31David had been told, “Ahithophel is among the conspirators with Absalom,” and David prayed, “O LORD, turn the counsel of Ahithophel into foolishness.”

32When David came to the summit, where God was worshipped, Hushai the Archite came to meet him with his coat torn and dust upon his head. 33David said to him, “If you pass on with me, you will be a burden to me. 34But if you return to the city, you can say to Absalom, ‘I will be your servant, O king; I was your father’s servant from the beginning, but now I am your servant.’ Then you can help me by frustrating Ahithophel’s advice.”

35“Are not Zadok and Abiathar the priests there with you? Tell them everything you hear in the king’s house. 36Behold, their two sons are there with them, Ahimaaz son of Zadok and Jonathan son of Abiathar; by them you shall send me word of everything you hear.” 37So Hushai, David’s friend, came into Jerusalem just as Absalom was entering the city.

The People's Outcry Against Economic Oppression

1A vociferous outcry arose among the people and their wives against their Jewish kinsmen.

2Some declared, “We, together with our sons and daughters, are numerous; therefore we must procure grain in order to eat and survive.”

3Others declared, “We are mortgaging our fields, vineyards, and houses merely to obtain grain amid the famine.”

4Still others declared, “We have borrowed silver to satisfy the king’s levy upon our fields and vineyards.”

5“And now,” they said, “our very flesh is as the flesh of our brothers, and our children are as their children; yet we are compelling our sons and daughters into servitude, and some of our daughters have already been enslaved. We are powerless, while our fields and vineyards have passed into the hands of others.”

Nehemiah's Rebuke of the Nobles and Officials

6When I heard their outcry and these particulars, I was exceedingly incensed. 7After deliberating within myself, I contended with the nobles and officials, saying, “Each of you is exacting usury from his brother.” Then I convened a great assembly against them. 8I said to them, “We, insofar as we were able, have redeemed our Jewish brothers who had been sold to the nations; but you are selling your brothers, and they are being sold to us!” They were silenced and could offer no reply.

9Then I said, “What you are doing is not good. Should you not walk in the fear of our God, so as not to furnish the nations—our enemies—with cause for reproach? 10I and my brothers and my servants are likewise lending them silver and grain. Let us, I pray, desist from this usury. 11Restore to them, this very day, their fields, vineyards, olive orchards, and houses, together with the hundredth of the silver, grain, new wine, and oil that you are exacting from them.”

12They replied, “We will restore these things and demand nothing from them; we will do exactly as you say.” Then I summoned the priests and bound them by oath to fulfillfulfilfulfill this promise.

13I likewise shook out the fold of my robe and declared, “So may God shake out from his house and his labourlabourlabor every man who fails to fulfillfulfilfulfill this promise; so may he be shaken out and left empty.”

And the entire assembly said, “Amen,” and praised the LORD. And the people acted in accordance with this word.

Nehemiah's Personal Example and Prayer

14Moreover, from the day King Artaxerxes appointed me governor over Judah—from the twentieth year to the thirty-second year of Artaxerxes, that is, twelve years—neither I nor my brothers consumed the governor’s provisions. 15The governors who preceded me had burdened the people heavily, taking from them bread and wine in addition to forty shekels of silver; their servants, moreover, domineered over the people. But I did not act thus, out of reverence for God.

16I also devoted myself steadfastly to the work on this wall, and all my servants were assembled there for the labourlabourlabor; we purchased no land. 17At my table sat one hundred fifty Jews and officials, in addition to those who came to us from the surrounding nations. 18Each day one ox, six choice sheep, and fowl were prepared for me, and every ten days an abundant supply of wine of every kind. Nevertheless, in view of all this, I did not claim the governor’s food allowance, for the burden of labourlabourlabor lay heavily upon this people.

19Remember me, my God, for good, for all that I have done on behalf of this people.

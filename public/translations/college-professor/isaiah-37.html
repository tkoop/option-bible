Hezekiah’s Distress and Appeal to Isaiah

1When King Hezekiah heard this, he tore his garments, clothed himself in sackcloth, and entered the house of the LORD. 2He sent Eliakim, who was over the household, together with Shebna the scribe and the senior priests, all clothed in sackcloth, to Isaiah son of Amoz, the prophet.

3They said to him, “Thus says Hezekiah: ‘This day is a day of distress, rebuke, and disgrace; for the people have reached the throes of travail, yet possess no strength to bring forth deliverance. 4Perhaps the LORD your God will hear the words of Rabshakeh, whom his master, the king of Assyria, sent to defy the living God, and will rebuke the words that the LORD your God has heard. Therefore lift up a prayer for the remnant that remains.’”

5So the servants of King Hezekiah came to Isaiah.

6Isaiah said to them, “Thus you shall say to your master:

“‘Thus says the LORD: Do not be afraid of the words that you have heard, with which the servants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed MemeMe. 7Behold, I will put a spirit in him, so that he will hear a report and return to his own land; and I will cause him to fall by the sword in his own land.’”

Sennacherib’s Renewed Threat

8Now when Rabshakeh heard that the king of Assyria had left Lachish, he withdrew and found him waging war against Libnah. 9Then the king heard concerning Tirhakah king of Cush, “He has come out to fight against you,” and, upon hearing it, he sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying:

10“Thus says the king of Assyria: Do not let your God in whom you trust deceive you by promising that Jerusalem will not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria. 11Behold, you have heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all the lands, devoting them to destruction. And shall you be delivered? 12Did the gods of the nations that my fathers destroyed deliver them—Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and the people of Eden who were in Telassar? 13Where is the king of Hamath, the king of Arpad, the king of the city of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivvah?”

Hezekiah’s Prayer before the LORD

14Hezekiah received the letter from the hand of the messengers and read it; then he went up to the house of the LORD and spread it before the LORD. 15And Hezekiah prayed to the LORD, saying:

“16O the LORD of Hosts, God of Israel, enthroned above the cherubim, YouyouYou alone are God of all the kingdoms of the earth; YouyouYou have made Heavenheavenheaven and EarthEarthearth. 17Incline YouryourYour ear, O the LORD, and hear; open YouryourYour eyes, O the LORD, and see; and hear all the words of Sennacherib, which he has sent to defy the living God. 18Truly, O the LORD, the kings of Assyria have laid waste all the countries and their lands, 19and have cast their gods into the fire; for they were not gods, but the work of men’s hands, wood and stone, and so they destroyed them. 20Now therefore, O the LORD our God, save us from his hand, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that YouyouYou alone are the LORD.”

The LORD’s Oracle and Jerusalem’s Deliverance

21Then Isaiah son of Amoz sent to Hezekiah, saying, “Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel:

“‘Because you have prayed to MemeMe concerning Sennacherib king of Assyria, 22this is the word that the LORD has spoken concerning him: The virgin daughter of Zion despises you and mocks you; the daughter of Jerusalem shakes her head behind you. 23Whom have you reproached and blasphemed? Against whom have you raised your voice and lifted up your eyes on high? Against the HolyholyHoly OneoneOne of Israel! 24By your servants you have reproached the Lord, and you have said,

“‘“With my many chariots I have ascended the heights of the mountains, the farthest recesses of Lebanon; I cut down its tallest cedars, its choicest cypresses; I reached its farthest height, its densest forest. 25I dug wells and drank waters; with the sole of my feet I dried up all the streams of Egypt.”

“‘26Have you not heard? Long ago I ordained it; from days of old I planned it. Now I have brought it to pass, that you should turn fortified cities into heaps of ruins. 27Their inhabitants, shorn of strength, are dismayed and put to shame; they are like grass of the field and green herb, like grass on the rooftops and grain blasted before it grows. 28But I know your sitting down and your going out and your coming in, and your raging against MemeMe. 29Because your raging against MemeMe and your arrogance have come up into MymyMy ears, therefore I will put MymyMy hook in your nose and MymyMy bridle in your lips, and I will turn you back by the way you came. 30And this shall be the sign for you: This year you shall eat what grows of itself, and in the second year what springs from that; but in the third year you shall sow and reap, and plant vineyards and eat their fruit. 31And the surviving remnant of the house of Judah shall again take root downward and bear fruit upward. 32For out of Jerusalem shall go a remnant, and out of Mount Zion a band of survivors. The zeal of the LORD of Hosts will accomplish this. 33Therefore thus says the LORD concerning the king of Assyria: He shall not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with a shield, nor cast up a siege mound against it. 34By the way that he came, by the same he shall return; and he shall not come into this city, declares the LORD. 35For I will defend this city to save it, for MymyMy own sake and for the sake of MymyMy servant David.’”

36Then the angel of the LORD went out and struck down 185,000 in the camp of the Assyrians; and when the people arose early in the morning, behold, all of them were dead bodies. 37So Sennacherib king of Assyria departed and returned home and lived at Nineveh. 38And as he was worshiping in the house of Nisroch his god, Adrammelech and Sharezer, his sons, struck him down with the sword; and they escaped into the land of Ararat. And Esarhaddon his son reigned in his place.

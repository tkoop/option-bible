Paul's Jealous Guardianship of the Corinthian Congregation

1I would that you might indulge me in a modicum of folly; indeed, do bear with me. 2For I am jealous over you with the jealousy of God, since I espoused you to one husband, that I might present you as a chaste virgin to Christ. 3But I fear lest, even as the serpent beguiled Eve through his craftiness, your minds should be corrupted from the simplicity and purity that are toward Christ. 4For if one who comes proclaims another Jesus whom we did not proclaim, or you receive a different spirit which you did not receive, or a different gospel which you did not accept, you bear with him admirably.

The Superiority of Paul's Authentic Apostleship over the So-Called Super-Apostles

5For I reckon that I am in no wise inferior to these preeminent apostles. 6Even if I am unpolished in speech, I am not so in knowledge; nay, in every respect we have been made manifest among you in all things. 7Or did I commit a sin in abasing myself that you might be exalted, in that I preached the gospel of God to you free of charge? 8I despoiled other churches, receiving support for my ministry to you. 9And when I was present with you and in want, I was not burdensome to anyone; for the brethren who came from Macedonia replenished my deficiency; and in all things I kept myself from being burdensome to you, and so I shall keep. 10As the truth of Christ is in me, this boasting of mine shall not be stopped in the regions of Achaia. 11And why? Because I do not love you? God knows that I do.

12But what I do I will also continue to do, in order to cut off occasion from those who desire occasion, that in the very matter in which they boast they may be found on equal footing with us. 13For such are false apostles, deceitful workers, disguising themselves as apostles of Christ. 14And no marvel, for Satan himself disguises himself as an angel of light. 15It is no great thing, therefore, if his ministers also disguise themselves as ministers of righteousness, whose end shall be according to their works.

Paul's Reluctant Boasting in His Sufferings and Weakness

16I say again, let no one think me foolish; but if otherwise, receive me even as a fool, that I too may boast a little. 17What I speak, I speak not according to the Lord, but as in folly, in this confidence of boasting. 18Since many boast according to the flesh, I will boast also. 19For you gladly bear with the foolish, being wise yourselves. 20For you bear with it if anyone enslaves you, if anyone devours, if anyone exploits you, if anyone exalts himself, if anyone strikes you on the face. 21I speak in terms of dishonourdishonourdishonor, as though we had been weak; but in whatsoever any other dares—I speak as a fool—I dare also.

22Are they Hebrews? So am I. Are they Israelites? So am I. Are they the seed of Abraham? So am I. 23Are they ministers of Christ? I speak as one beside himself—I am more so: in labourslabourslabors more abundant, in stripes beyond measure, in imprisonments more frequent, in deaths oft.

24Five times I received from the Jews forty stripes save one. 25Thrice was I beaten with rods, once was I stoned, thrice I suffered shipwreck, a night and a day have I been in the deep. 26In journeyings often, in perils of rivers, in perils of robbers, in perils from my own countrymen, in perils from the Gentiles, in perils in the city, in perils in the wilderness, in perils in the sea, in perils among false brethren; 27in toil and travail, in watchings often, in hunger and thirst, in fastings often, in cold and nakedness.

28Apart from the things that are without, there is that which presseth upon me daily, the care of all the churches. 29Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is made to stumble, and I do not burn with indignation? 30If I must needs boast, I will boast of the things that concern my weakness. 31The God and FatherfatherFather of the Lord Jesus, HeheHe who is blessed forever, knows that I do not lie. 32In Damascus the governor under Aretas the king guarded the city of the Damascenes, desiring to apprehend me; 33and through a window in a basket was I let down by the wall, and escaped his hands.

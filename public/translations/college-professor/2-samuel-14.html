Joab Enlists a Wise Woman from Tekoa

1Now Joab, the son of Zeruiah, discerned that the king's affections were inclined toward Absalom. 2Accordingly, Joab dispatched a messenger to Tekoa and procured from there a sagacious woman, to whom he said, "Please feign mourning: don mourning garments, do not anoint yourself with oil, and present yourself as a woman who has lamented the dead for a protracted season. Then go to the king and speak to him in this manner." 3And Joab placed the words in her mouth.

The Tekoite Woman's Plea Before the King

4When the woman of Tekoa addressed the king, she fell upon her face to the earth, prostrated herself, and cried, "Help me, O king!"

5The king asked her, "What ails you?"

She replied, "Alas, I am a widow, and my husband has perished.

6"Your maidservant had two sons; they contended in the field, and, with no one to interpose, one struck the other and slew him.

7"And behold, the entire clan has risen against your maidservant, demanding, 'Surrender the one who struck his brother, that we may put him to death for the life of his brother whom he killed, and annihilate the heir as well.' Thus they would extinguish the last ember of my household, leaving my husband neither name nor remnant upon the earth."

8The king said to the woman, "Go to your house, and I will issue a decree concerning you."

9The woman of Tekoa said to the king, "MymyMy lord the king, let the guilt rest upon me and upon my father's house; but may the king and his throne be held guiltless."

10The king said, "If anyone molests you, bring him to me, and he will not molest you again."

11She said, "Please let the king remember the LORD your God, that the avenger of blood may not destroy, so that they will not destroy my son."

The king replied, "As the LORD lives, not one hair of your son shall fall to the ground."

12Then the woman said, "Please let your maidservant speak a further word to my lord the king."

He said, "Speak."

The Parable Applied to the King's Banished Son

13The woman said, "Why then have you devised such a thing against the people of God? For by speaking this word, the king incurs guilt, inasmuch as he does not restore his banished son.

14"For we must all die, and we are like water spilled upon the ground, which cannot be gathered up again; yet God does not take away life, but devises means by which the banished one may not remain estranged from HimhimHim.

15"Now I have come to speak this word to my lord the king because the people have intimidated me; and your maidservant said, 'Let me speak to the king; perhaps the king will grant the request of his maidservant.'

16"For the king will listen, to deliver his maidservant from the hand of the man who would destroy me and my son together from the inheritance of God.

17"Your maidservant said, 'May the word of my lord the king bring repose; for my lord the king is like the angel of God in discerning good and evil; and may the the LORD your God be with you.'"

She said, "Let my lord the king speak."

She answered, "As surely as you live, my lord the king, no one can turn to the right or to the left from all that my lord the king has spoken; for your servant Joab commanded me, and he placed all these words in the mouth of your maidservant. 20Your servant Joab has done this to alter the appearance of the matter; but my lord is wise, according to the wisdom of the angel of God, to know all that is upon the earth."

21The king said to Joab, "Behold, I have granted this request; go, bring back the young man Absalom."

22Joab fell on his face to the ground, prostrated himself, and blessed the king. Then Joab said, "Today your servant knows that he has found favourfavourfavor in your sight, my lord the king, in that the king has granted the request of his servant."

23Joab arose, journeyed to Geshur, and brought Absalom to Jerusalem.

24The king said, "Let him withdraw to his own house, but let him not see my face." So Absalom withdrew to his own house and did not see the king's face.

Absalom's Appearance, Family, and Eventual Reconciliation

25Now in all Israel there was no man so handsome as Absalom; from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head there was no blemish in him.

26When he sheared the hair of his head—and at the end of every year he sheared it, because it was heavy upon him—he weighed the hair of his head at two hundred shekels by the king's standard.

27Three sons were born to Absalom, and one daughter named Tamar; she was a woman of beautiful appearance.

28Absalom resided in Jerusalem two full years without seeing the king's face.

29Absalom sent for Joab, in order to send him to the king, but he would not come; he sent again a second time, but he would not come.

30Absalom said to his servants, "See, Joab's field is next to mine, and he has barley there; go and set it ablaze." So Absalom's servants burned the field.

31Joab arose, came to Absalom's house, and said to him, "Why have your servants set my field on fire?"

32Absalom said to Joab, "Behold, I sent to you, saying, 'Come here, that I may send you to the king, to say, "Why have I come from Geshur? It would be better for me still to be there."' Now therefore let me see the king's face; and if there is iniquity in me, let him put me to death."

33Joab went to the king and reported; and the king summoned Absalom, who came to the king, bowed himself with his face to the ground before the king, and the king kissed Absalom.

Liberation from the Law through Christ’s Body

1Or are you unaware, brothers—for I am addressing those conversant with the law—that the law exercises dominion over a human being solely for the duration of his life? 2For a married woman is legally bound to her husband during his lifetime; yet should her husband die, she is emancipated from the law appertaining to her husband. 3Hence, if she becomes the wife of another man while her husband yet lives, she shall be denominated an adulteress; but if her husband dies, she is free from that law, so that she is not an adulteress upon becoming the wife of another man. 4Accordingly, my brothers, you also have been put to death with respect to the law through the body of Christ, in order that you might belong to another—to HimhimHim who was raised from the dead—so that we might bear fruit for God. 5For when we were in the flesh, the passions of sins, which were activated by the law, were operative in our members, producing fruit for death. 6But now, having died to that which held us in bondage, we have been released from the law, so that we serve in the newness of the SpiritspiritSpirit and not in the antiquity of the letter.

The Law’s Disclosure of Sin

7What then shall we say? Is the law sin? By no means! Rather, I would not have recognizedrecogniserecognized sin except through the law; for I would not have known covetous desire unless the law had declared, “You shall not covet.” 8But sin, having seized an occasion through the commandment, produced in me every species of covetous desire; for apart from the law, sin is dead. 9Once I lived apart from the law; but when the commandment arrived, sin revived, and I died. 10And this commandment, which was ordained for life, was found by me to be unto death. 11For sin, having seized an occasion through the commandment, deceived me and through it put me to death. 12So then, the law is holy, and the commandment is holy, righteous, and good. 13Did that which is good, then, become death to me? By no means! Rather, in order that sin might be manifested as sin, it wrought death in me through what is good, so that through the commandment sin might become exceedingly sinful.

The Existential Conflict between Flesh and Mind

14For we know that the law is spiritual; but I am carnal, sold under sin. 15For I do not comprehend what I am doing; for what I wish to do, I do not practisedo not practisedo not practice, but what I hate, that I do. 16But if I do what I do not wish, I assent that the law is good. 17So now it is no longer I who accomplish it, but sin dwelling in me. 18For I know that good does not dwell in me—that is, in my flesh—for the willing is present with me, but the performing of the good is not. 19For I do not do the good that I wish, but the evil that I do not wish, this I practice. 20But if I do what I do not wish, it is no longer I who accomplish it, but sin dwelling in me. 21I find then this law: that when I wish to do the good, evil is present with me. 22For I delight in the law of God according to the inner man. 23But I perceive another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind and taking me captive to the law of sin that is in my members. 24Wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me from this body of death? 25Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord! So then, I myself serve the law of God with my mind, but with my flesh the law of sin.

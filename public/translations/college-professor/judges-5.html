The Canticle of Deborah and Barak

1On that very day Deborah and Barak, son of Abinoam, intoned this canticle, declaring: 2“When the chieftains take the vanguard in Israel, and the populace freely consecrates itself to the fray, extol the LORD. 3Hear, O kings; give ear, O rulers! I myself will sing to the LORD; I will make melody to the LORD, the God of Israel.”

4“The LORD, when YouyouYou went out from Seir, when YouyouYou marched from the field of Edom, the earth trembled, the heavens dripped, and even the clouds poured down water. 5The mountains quaked before the LORD, indeed, this Sinai before the LORD, the God of Israel.”

6“In the days of Shamgar son of Anath, in the days of Jael, the highways were deserted, and travelers walked by crooked paths. 7The villages failed in Israel; they failed until I, Deborah, arose—until I arose as a mother in Israel.”

8“When they chose new gods, warfare reached the city gates, and neither shield nor spear appeared among forty thousand in Israel. 9My heart is with the commanders of Israel, who offered themselves willingly among the people. Bless the LORD!”

10“You who ride upon white donkeys, YouyouYou who repose upon saddle carpets, and YouyouYou who traverse the road—give voice! 11At the sound of archers among the watering places, there they recount the righteous acts of the LORD, the righteous acts of HishisHis rural folk in Israel. Then the people of the LORD came down to the gates.”

12“Awake, awake, Deborah! Awake, awake, utter a song! Arise, Barak, and lead away your captives, son of Abinoam. 13Then the remnant came down to the nobles; the people of the LORD came down for me against the mighty.”

The Tribal Reckoning and the Summons to Arms

14“From Ephraim came those whose root is in Amalek; after you, Benjamin, amid your clans. From Machir came down commanders, and from Zebulun those who bear the marshal's staff. 15The princes of Issachar were with Deborah; yes, Issachar was with Barak, sent into the valley at his feet. Among the clans of Reuben there were great searchings of heart. 16Why did you sit among the sheepfolds, to hear the whistling for the flocks? Among the clans of Reuben there were great searchings of heart.”

17“Gilead stayed beyond the Jordan; and Dan—why did he linger by the ships? Asher sat still at the coast of the sea, dwelling by its inlets. 18Zebulun was a people who jeopardized their lives unto death, and Naphtali upon the high places of the field.”

19“The kings came and fought; then the kings of Canaan fought at Taanach by the waters of Megiddo; they seized no silver as spoil. 20From Heavenheavenheaven the stars fought; from their courses they fought against Sisera. 21The torrent Kishon swept them away, the ancient torrent, the torrent Kishon. O my soul, march on in strength! 22Then the horses' hooves thundered from the galloping, the galloping of his chargers. 23The angel of the LORD pronounces a bitter curse upon Meroz and its inhabitants, because they did not come to the help of the LORD, to the help of the LORD among the mighty.”

Jael's Blessed Deed and Sisera's Fall

24“Most blessed among women is Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite; blessed is she in the tent above women. 25He asked for water, and she gave him milk; she brought him curds in a princely bowl. 26She put her hand to the tent peg, and her right hand to the workmen's hammer; she struck Sisera, she crushed his head, she shattered and pierced his temple. 27Between her feet he bowed, he fell, he lay; between her feet he bowed, he fell; where he bowed, there he fell down ruined.”

Sisera's Mother and the Spoils

28The song then turns to Sisera's mother, who looks out through the window and cries through the lattice: “Why is his chariot so long in coming? Why tarry the hoofbeats of his chariots?” Her wise attendants answer, and she herself replies: 2930“Are they not finding and dividing the plunder—a girl or two for every warrior; dyed garments for Sisera, embroidered garments for the necks of the captives?”

31The song concludes: “So may all YouryourYour enemies perish, O the LORD! But may those who love YouyouYou be like the sun when it rises in its might.” And the land had rest for forty years.

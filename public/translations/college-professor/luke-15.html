The Setting: Tax Collectors, Sinners, Pharisees, and Scribes

1Now all the tax collectors and sinners were converging upon HimhimHim, intent upon hearing HishisHis teaching, 2and both the Pharisees and the scribes were murmuring, saying, “This man welcomes sinners and shares table fellowship with them.”

The Parable of the Lost Sheep

3So HeheHe propounded to them this parable, saying: 4“Which man among you, possessing a hundred sheep and having lost one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine behind in the wilderness and pursue the one that is lost until he discovers it? 5And upon discovering it, he lays it upon his shoulders, rejoicing.

6“And upon arriving home, he convenes his friends and neighbors, saying to them, ‘Rejoice with me, for I have found my lost sheep.’ 7I tell you that, in like manner, there will be joy in Heavenheavenheaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous persons who have no need of repentance.

The Parable of the Lost Coin

8“Or what woman, possessing ten drachmas, if she misplaces one drachma, does not light a lamp, sweep the house, and seek with meticulous diligence until she finds it? 9And upon finding it, she convenes her friends and neighbors, saying, ‘Rejoice with me, for I have found the drachma that I lost.’

10“In the same way, I tell you, there is joy before God, in the presence of the angels, over one sinner who repents.”

The Parable of the Prodigal Son

11“A certain man had two sons. 12And the younger of them said to his father, ‘Father, give me the portion of the estate that falls to me.’ And he divided his estate between them.

13“And after not many days, the younger son gathered everything together and journeyed into a far country, and there he squandered his estate in reckless living. 14And when he had exhausted everything, a severe famine arose throughout that country, and he began to be in want.

15“And going, he hired himself out to one of the citizens of that country, and that man sent him into his fields to feed swine. 16And he longed to satisfy his hunger with the carob pods that the swine were eating, yet no one gave him anything.

17“And when he came to his senses, he said, ‘How many of my father’s hired men have bread in abundance, while I am perishing here with hunger! 18I will rise and go to my father, and I will say to him, “Father, I have sinned against Heavenheavenheaven and before you. 19I am no longer worthy to be called your son; make me as one of your hired men.”’

20“And having arisen, he came to his father. But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was moved with compassion, and running, he fell upon his neck and kissed him.

21“And the son said to him, ‘Father, I have sinned against Heavenheavenheaven and before you; I am no longer worthy to be called your son.’

22“But the father said to his servants, ‘Quickly bring out the best robe and clothe him, and put a ring on his hand and sandals on his feet. 23And bring the fattened calf; slaughter it, and let us eat and be merry. 24For this son of mine was dead and has come to life; he was lost and has been found.’ And they began to celebrate.

25“Now his older son was in the field; and as he came and drew near to the house, he heard music and dancing. 26And calling one of the servants, he inquired what these things might signify. 27And he said to him, ‘Your brother has come, and your father has slaughtered the fattened calf, because he has received him back safe and sound.’

28“But he was angry and was unwilling to go in. And his father, having come out, was pleading with him. 29But answering, he said to his father, ‘Behold, for so many years I have served you, and I never transgressed your command, and you never gave me a young goat that I might celebrate with my friends. 30But when this son of yours came, who devoured your property with prostitutes, you slaughtered the fattened calf for him.’

31“And he said to him, ‘Child, you are always with me, and all that is mine is yours. 32But it was fitting that we should celebrate and rejoice, because this brother of yours was dead and has come to life, and was lost and has been found.’”

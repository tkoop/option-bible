Timothy’s Recruitment and the Promulgation of Apostolic Decrees

1Paul likewise reached Derbe and then Lystra, where there was found a disciple named Timothy, the son of a believing Jewish woman, though his father was a Greek. 2He was well attested by the brothers in Lystra and Iconium.

3Paul desired that Timothy should accompany him; accordingly, he took him and circumcised him on account of the Jews dwelling in those regions, for all knew that his father was a Greek. 4As they journeyed through the cities, they delivered the decrees that the apostles and elders in Jerusalem had adjudicated, enjoining the churches to observe them. 5Thus the churches were fortified in the faith and increased daily in number.

The Macedonian Vision and the Voyage to Philippi

6Having been forbidden by the HolyholyHoly SpiritspiritSpirit to proclaim the word in Asia, they traversed the region of Phrygia and Galatia; 7and, upon reaching the vicinity of Mysia, they attempted to enter Bithynia, but the SpiritspiritSpirit of Jesus did not permit them. 8Passing by Mysia, they descended to Troas.

9During the night a vision appeared to Paul: a Macedonian man stood before him, imploring him and saying, “Cross over into Macedonia and help us.” 10When Paul had seen the vision, we immediately sought to set out for Macedonia, concluding that God had summoned us to preach the gospel to them.

11Setting sail from Troas, we sailed a straight course to Samothrace, and on the following day to Neapolis; 12from there we proceeded to Philippi, a leading city of the Macedonian district and a colony, where we remained for several days.

Lydia, the Pythoness, and the Disturbance at Philippi

13On the Sabbath we went outside the gate beside a river, where we supposed there was a place of prayer; and, having sat down, we spoke to the women who had gathered. 14A certain woman named Lydia—a dealer in purple cloth from Thyatira and a worshiper of God—was listening, and the Lord opened her heart to heed the things spoken by Paul.

15When she and her household had been baptized, she urged us, saying, “If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, come into my house and remain.” And she prevailed upon us.

16Now as we were going to the place of prayer, a certain slave girl possessed by a spirit of divination met us, bringing considerable profit to her masters through soothsaying. 17She followed Paul and us, crying out, “These men are servants of the MostmostMost HighhighHigh God, who proclaim to you the way of salvation.”

18She persisted in this for many days, until Paul, greatly vexed, turned and said to the spirit, “I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her.” And it departed from her that very hour.

19When her masters perceived that their hope of profit had vanished, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace before the magistrates. 20Bringing them before the magistrates, they declared, “These men, though Jews, are disturbing our city, 21and they advocate customs that it is not lawful for us Romans to adopt or practiseor practiseor practice.”

22The crowd then rose up in concert against them; and the magistrates, having stripped their garments from them, ordered them to be beaten with rods. 23After inflicting many blows, they cast them into prison, charging the jailer to guard them securely. 24He, having received such an order, thrust them into the inner prison and secured their feet in the stocks.

Imprisonment, Earthquake, and the Jailer’s Conversion

25About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, while the prisoners listened to them; 26and suddenly a great earthquake shook the foundations of the prison. Immediately all the doors flew open, and everyone's chains were unfastened.

27The jailer, awakened from sleep and seeing the prison doors open, drew his sword and was about to kill himself, supposing that the prisoners had escaped. 28But Paul cried out in a loud voice, “Do no harm to yourself, for we are all here.”

29Calling for lights, he rushed in and, trembling, fell down before Paul and Silas; 30and, having brought them out, he asked, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?”

31They replied, “Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved—you and your household.”

32Then they spoke the word of the Lord to him and to all who were in his house. 33At that hour of the night he took them and washed their wounds; and immediately he and his entire household were baptized. 34He brought them up into his house, set a table before them, and rejoiced greatly with his whole household, having believed in God.

35When daybreak came, the magistrates sent the lictors, saying, “Release those men.”

36The jailer reported these words to Paul: “The magistrates have sent to release you; therefore go forth now in peace.”

37But Paul said to them, “They beat us publicly, though we were uncondemned Roman citizens, and cast us into prison; and now would they send us away secretly? No indeed! Let them come themselves and escort us out.”

38The lictors reported these words to the magistrates, who were seized with fear when they heard that they were Romans. 39So they came and apologized to them; and, after leading them out, they requested that they depart from the city.

40After leaving the prison, they went to Lydia's house; and, having seen the brothers, they encouraged them and departed.

The Common Destiny of Mortals

1For I have taken all this to heart and sought to apprehend its full import: the righteous and the wise, together with their deeds, lie in the hand of God, and no one can foreknow whether love or hatred awaits them. 2A uniform fate befalls all alike—one destiny overtakes the righteous and the wicked, the good and the evil, the clean and the unclean, the one who sacrifices and the one who does not; as it is with the good, so with the sinner; as with the swearer, so with him who fears an oath. 3This is the grievous evil pervading all that is wrought beneath the sun: one fate overtakes everyone, and the human heart is saturated with evil; madness resides in their hearts throughout their lives, and thereafter they join the dead.

4Yet hope remains for anyone still reckoned among the living, since a living dog surpasses a dead lion. 5For the living know that they must die, but the dead know nothing; they have no further reward, for their memory is forgotten. 6Their love, their hatred, and their jealousy have already perished, and they no longer possess any share in anything done beneath the sun.

An Exhortation to Joyful and Diligent Living

7Go, eat your bread with joy, and drink your wine with a glad heart, for God has already approved your works. 8Let your garments be white at all times, and do not let oil be lacking upon your head.

9Enjoy life with the wife whom you love throughout all the days of your ephemeral life, which HeheHe has granted you beneath the sun—all your days of futility—for this is your portion in life and in the labourlabourlabor you perform beneath the sun. 10Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with all your might, for in Sheol, whither you are going, there is no work, nor reckoning, nor knowledge, nor wisdom.

The Caprice of Time and Chance

11I observed once more beneath the sun that the race belongs not to the swift, nor battle to the strong, nor bread to the wise, nor wealth to the discerning, nor favourfavourfavor to the knowing; instead, time and chance befall them all. 12For no one knows his time. Like fish caught in a cruel net and like birds seized in a snare, so the sons of men are ensnared in an evil time when it suddenly falls upon them.

The Parable of the Poor Wise Man

13I have likewise seen this wisdom beneath the sun, and it appeared momentous to me: 14there was a small city with few men in it; a great king came against it, surrounded it, and erected massive siegeworks against it. 15Now a poor wise man was found in it, and he saved the city by his wisdom; yet no one remembered that poor man.

16So I said, "Wisdom is better than strength, but the wisdom of the poor man is despised, and his words are not heeded."

17The quiet words of the wise are better heard than the shouting of a ruler among fools. 18Wisdom is better than weapons of war, but one sinner destroys much good.

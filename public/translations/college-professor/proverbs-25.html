Proverbs Preserved by Hezekiah's Scribes

1These are likewise the proverbs of Solomon, transcribed by the men of Hezekiah, king of Judah.

The Glory of God and the Investigative Prerogative of Kings

2To conceal a matter is the glory of God, but to search it out is the glory of kings. 3As the heavens are immeasurably high and the earth profoundly deep, so the heart of a king is unsearchable.

Purification, Courtly Etiquette, and Prudent Speech

4Remove the dross from silver, and a vessel emerges for the refiner. 5Remove the wicked from before the king, and his throne shall be established in righteousness. 6Do not aggrandize yourself before the king, nor stand in the place of the great. 7For it is better that one say to you, "Ascend hither," than that you be abased before a noble whose presence your eyes have beheld.

8Do not precipitously rush into contention, lest, when your neighbourneighbourneighbor has shamed you, you be left without recourse at the last. 9Argue your cause with your neighbourneighbourneighbor, yet do not disclose another's confidence. 10Lest the hearer shame you, and your disgrace prove irrevocable.

11A word aptly spoken is like golden apples set in silver filigree. 12Like a golden ring or an ornament of pure gold is a wise rebuke to an ear that hearkens. 13Like the coolness of snow in harvest season, a trustworthy envoy refreshes the soul of those who sent him; for he refreshes the soul of his masters. 14As clouds and wind without rain, so is the man who boasts of a gift he never gives. 15Through patient forbearance a ruler may be persuaded, and a gentle tongue can shatter the hardest resolve.

Moderation, Neighborly Boundaries, and Self-Mastery

16If you have found honey, consume only your sufficiency, lest you glut yourself and vomit it forth. 17Withdraw your foot from your neighbourneighbourneighbor's house, lest he grow weary of you and come to hate you. 18A false witness against his neighbourneighbourneighbor is as a maul, a sword, and a sharp arrow. 19Trusting an unfaithful man in a day of distress is like a broken tooth and a slipping foot. 20As removing a garment in cold weather, or pouring vinegar upon natron, so is singing songs to a heavy heart.

21If your enemy hungers, give him bread to eat, and if he thirsts, give him water to drink. 22For you will heap burning shame upon his head, and the LORD will reward you. 23As the north wind brings rain, so a slanderous tongue brings an angry countenance. 24Better to dwell in a corner of the roof than with a contentious woman in a spacious house. 25Like cold water to a weary soul, so is good news from a distant land.

26As a muddied spring and a polluted well, so is a righteous man who falters before the wicked. 27It is not good to eat too much honey, nor is it glorious to seek one's own glory. 28Like a city broken down and without walls is a man who lacks self-control.

The King's Troubling Dream and the Failure of Babylon's Wise Men

4I, Nebuchadnezzar, was tranquil in my house and flourishing in my palace. 5I beheld a dream that terrified me, and the cogitations of my bed, together with the visions of my head, dismayed me. 6Therefore I issued a decree summoning all the wise men of Babylon into my presence, that they might disclose to me the interpretation of the dream. 7Then the magicians, enchanters, Chaldeans, and astrologers entered, and I rehearsed the dream before them, yet they proved unable to disclose its interpretation to me. 8At last Daniel—called Belteshazzar after my god, and in whom resides the spirit of the holy gods—came into my presence, and I recounted the dream to him. 9“O Belteshazzar, chief of the magicians, since I know that the spirit of the holy gods is in you and that no secret is too arduous for you, declare to me the visions of my dream that I beheld, and their interpretation.”

The Vision of the Great Tree and Daniel's Interpretation

10The visions of my head upon my bed were these: I looked, and there stood a tree in the midst of the earth, exceedingly tall. 11The tree grew mighty and robust, its crown reaching to the heavens, and it was visible to the ends of all the earth. 12Its foliage was fair and its fruit abundant, and food for all was in it; beneath it the beasts of the field took shelter, the birds of the heavens nested in its branches, and all flesh was nourished from it.

13As I lay upon my bed, in the visions of my head, I beheld a watcher—a holy one—descending from Heavenheavenheaven. 14He cried out with a mighty voice and declared, “Cut down the tree, lop off its branches, strip away its foliage, and scatter its fruit; let the beasts flee from beneath it and the birds from its branches. 15Yet leave the stump of its roots in the earth, bound with a band of iron and bronze, amid the tender grass of the field; let him be drenched with the dew of Heavenheavenheaven, and let his lot be with the beasts among the grass of the earth. 16Let his mind be altered from that of a man, and let the mind of a beast be given to him, and let seven times pass over him. 17This sentence is decreed by the watchers, and the demand is pronounced by the holy ones, so that the living may know that the MostmostMost HighhighHigh rules over the kingdom of humanity, grants it to whomever HeheHe wills, and exalts over it the lowliest of men.”

18This dream I, King Nebuchadnezzar, beheld; and you, O Belteshazzar, declare its interpretation, for none of the wise men of my kingdom can disclose it to me, yet you are able, because the spirit of the holy gods is in you. 19Then Daniel, whose name is Belteshazzar, stood appalled for a time, and his thoughts troubled him. The king answered and said, “Belteshazzar, let neither the dream nor its interpretation dismay you.”

Belteshazzar answered and said, “My lord, would that the dream concerned your foes and its interpretation your enemies! 20The tree that you saw, which grew and became strong, whose top reached to Heavenheavenheaven and which was visible to the ends of all the earth— 21whose foliage was fair and whose fruit was abundant, and in which was food for all; beneath it the beasts of the field took shelter, and in its branches the birds of the heavens dwelt— 22it is you, O king, who have grown and become mighty: Your greatness has increased until it reaches Heavenheavenheaven, and your dominion extends to the ends of the earth. 23And inasmuch as the king beheld a watcher, a holy one, descending from Heavenheavenheaven and saying, ‘Cut down the tree and destroy it, yet leave the stump of its roots in the earth, bound with iron and bronze, amid the tender grass of the field; let him be drenched with the dew of Heavenheavenheaven, and let his lot be with the beasts of the field, until seven times pass over him’— 24this is the interpretation, O king: it is a decree of the MostmostMost HighhighHigh that has come upon my lord the king— 25that you will be driven from among men, and your dwelling will be with the beasts of the field; you will be made to eat grass like an ox, and you will be drenched with the dew of Heavenheavenheaven, and seven times will pass over you, until you know that the MostmostMost HighhighHigh rules over the kingdom of humanity and grants it to whomever HeheHe wills. 26And as it was commanded that the stump of the tree's roots be left, so your kingdom will be preserved for you once you acknowledge that Heaven rules. 27Therefore, O king, let my counsel be acceptable to you: redeem your sins by righteousness and your iniquities by showing mercy to the oppressed, if perhaps there may be a prolonging of your tranquillitytranquillitytranquility.”

28All of this befell King Nebuchadnezzar.

29Twelve months later, he was strolling upon the roof of the royal palace in Babylon, 30and the king declared, “Is this not Babylon the great, which I myself have built as a royal residence by the might of my power and for the glory of my majesty?”

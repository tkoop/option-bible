The Parable of the Dishonest Steward

1He also said to HishisHis disciples, “There was a certain affluent man who maintained a steward, and this steward was denounced for dissipating his master’s assets.

“2Accordingly, he summoned him and demanded, ‘What is this report I hear concerning you? Render an accounting of your stewardship, since you can no longer retain your stewardship.’

“3The steward said to himself, ‘What am I to do, now that my master is divesting me of my stewardship? I lack the strength to excavate, and I am too abashed to solicit alms. 4I have resolved upon a course of action: once I am dismissed from my stewardship, I may be received into their dwellings.’

“5Therefore he summoned each of his master’s debtors individually and inquired of the first, ‘How much are you indebted to my master?’

“‘6A hundred baths of olive oil,’ he replied.

“The steward told him, ‘Take your account, sit down at once, and reduce it to fifty.’

“7Then he said to another, ‘And what is your indebtedness?’

“‘A hundred measures of wheat,’ he answered.

“He instructed him, ‘Take your account and reduce it to eighty.’

The Master’s Commendation and Jesus’ Teaching on Wealth

“8The master then commended the unrighteous steward, because he had acted with circumspection; for the children of this age are more prudent in their dealings with their own kind than are the children of light.

“9I say to you, employ worldly wealth to procure friends for yourselves, so that, when it is exhausted, they may welcome you into the everlasting habitations.

“10Whoever proves faithful in the least is faithful also in much, and whoever is dishonest in the least is dishonest also in much. 11If, therefore, you have not been trustworthy with worldly wealth, who will commit to you the true riches? 12And if you have not been faithful with what belongs to another, who will bestow upon you what is your own?

“13No servant can be enslaved to two masters, for he will either hate the one and cherish the other, or cleave to the one and disdain the other. You cannot serve both God and wealth.”

14The Pharisees, being avaricious, heard all these pronouncements and derided HimhimHim.

The Rich Man and Lazarus

“19There was a certain wealthy man who arrayed himself in purple and fine linen and made merry in splendoursplendoursplendor each day. 20And a certain pauper named Lazarus lay at his gate, covered with sores. 21He longed to be satiated with the scraps falling from the rich man’s table; moreover, the dogs came and licked his ulcers.

“22In time the pauper died and was borne by angels to Abraham’s bosom. The rich man likewise died and was interred.

“23And in Hades, tormented as he was, he lifted his eyes and beheld Abraham afar off, with Lazarus in his bosom.

“24He cried out, ‘Father Abraham, have mercy upon me and dispatchdispatch, despatchdispatch Lazarus to dip the tip of his finger in water and cool my tongue, for I am tormented in this flame.’

“25But Abraham said, ‘Child, recall that you received your good things in your lifetime, whereas Lazarus received evil things; yet now he is consoled here, and you are in anguish. 26Besides, a vast chasm has been fixed between us and you, so that those desiring to cross from here to you cannot do so, nor can any pass from there to us.’

“27He answered, ‘Then I entreat you, father, to send him to my father’s house— 28for I have five brothers—that he may testify to them, lest they too arrive in this place of torment.’

“29But Abraham said, ‘They have Moses and the ProphetsprophetsProphets; let them heed them.’

“30He said, ‘No, father Abraham; but if one should go to them from the dead, they will repent.’

“31He said to him, ‘If they will not listen to Moses and the ProphetsprophetsProphets, neither will they be persuaded should one rise from the dead.’”

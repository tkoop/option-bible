The Macedonian Churches’ Example of Generosity

1Now, brothers, we are desirous of apprising you of the grace that God has lavished upon the Macedonian congregations, 2for amid a most rigorous ordeal, their superabundant joy, conjoined with their extreme indigence, has overflowed into the opulence of their liberality.

3I bear witness that they contributed in accordance with their capacity, and indeed beyond it, of their own spontaneous volition; 4they besought us with great urgency for the favourfavourfavor of participating in this ministry to the saints. 5Nor did they act merely as we had anticipated; rather, they first consecrated themselves to the Lord and thereafter to us, in conformity with the will of God.

The Exhortation to Excel in Generosity

6Consequently, we exhorted Titus that, just as he had previously initiated this endeavourendeavourendeavor, he should also bring to fruition among you this grace as well. 7But as you abound in every respect—in faith, in discourse, in knowledge, in all diligence, and in the love we have awakened within you—see to it that you abound in this grace also. 8I am not issuing a command; rather, I seek to assay the genuineness of your love by the standard of others’ earnestness.

The Example of Christ’s Self-Impoverishment

9For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ: though HeheHe was affluent, yet for your sake HeheHe became indigent, so that through HishisHis penury you might become affluent.

10In this matter I proffer my counsel, for it is expedient for you—not merely to have commenced the work last year, but to have desired it. 11Now, therefore, bring the execution to completion, so that your readiness of will may be consummated by a corresponding completion commensurate with your resources. 12For if the willingness is present, the offering is acceptable in proportion to what one possesses, not in proportion to what one lacks.

13For our purpose is not that others should find relief while you are distressed, but that equity should prevail. 14At the present juncture, your abundance should alleviate their want, so that their abundance may in turn alleviate your want, and thereby equity be established. 15As it is written, “He who gathered abundantly had no surplus, and he who gathered sparingly suffered no deficit.”

Titus and His Companions Entrusted with the Gift

16Thanks be to God, who has implanted in the heart of Titus the same solicitous concern for you. 17For he not only acceded to our exhortation, but, being already most earnest, he set out for you of his own spontaneous accord. 18Moreover, we are dispatching with him the brother whose commendation in the gospel is celebrated throughout all the churches. 19And not only so, but he has been designated by the churches as our travellingtravellingtraveling companion in administering this grace, to the glory of the Lord HimselfhimselfHimself and as a manifestation of our readiness.

20We are taking this precaution lest anyone should impugn us in connection with this munificent contribution that we administer. 21For we are solicitous to conduct ourselves honourablyhonourablyhonorably, not only before the Lord, but also before men. 22And we are sending with them our brother, whom we have repeatedly tested and found diligent in many undertakings, and who is now all the more diligent by reason of his great confidence in you.

23As for Titus, he is my associate and collaborator on your behalf; as for our brothers, they are envoys of the churches, a glory to Christ. 24Therefore, in the presence of the churches, exhibit to them the evidence of your love and of our boasting concerning you.

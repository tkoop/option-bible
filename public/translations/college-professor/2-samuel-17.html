Ahithophel’s Proposal to Strike David by Night

1Ahithophel said to Absalom, “Let me choose twelve thousand men, and I will set out tonight to pursue David. 2I will overtake him while he is weary and disheartened, throw him into panic, and make all the people with him flee; then I will strike down the king alone. 3I will restore the entire populace to your allegiance; once the man you seek is dead, the whole nation will be at peace. 4This counsel seemed sound to Absalom and to all the elders of Israel.

Absalom Seeks a Second Opinion

5Absalom said, “Summon Hushai the Archite as well, so that we may hear what he has to say.” 6When Hushai came before Absalom, Absalom said to him, “Ahithophel has proposed this plan. Should we adopt it? If not, speak your mind.”

7Hushai replied to Absalom, “The counsel Ahithophel has offered this time is not sound. 8You know your father and his men: they are formidable and desperate fighters, like a bear robbed of her cubs in the open country; and your father, a veteran of war, will not spend the night with the troops. 9Even now he is hidden in some cave or stronghold; if your forces suffer an initial reverse, whoever hears of it will say, ‘There has been a slaughter among the people following Absalom.’ 10Then even the boldest soldier, whose heart is like a lion’s, will lose courage, for all Israel knows that your father is a mighty warrior and that those with him are valiant men. 11Therefore I advise that all Israel be gathered to you—from Dan to Beersheba, as innumerable as sand on the shore—and that you yourself lead them into battle. 12We will overtake him wherever he is found and fall upon him like dew upon the ground, leaving not one of his men alive. 13And if he withdraws into a city, all Israel will drag that city into the valley with ropes until not even a pebble remains.”

14Absalom and all the men of Israel declared Hushai’s counsel superior to Ahithophel’s, for the LORD had ordained that Ahithophel’s sound advice be frustrated, bringing disaster upon Absalom.

The Warning Reaches David

15Hushai reported to the priests Zadok and Abiathar what Ahithophel had advised Absalom and the elders of Israel, and what he himself had advised in response. 16“Send word quickly to David,” he said, “and tell him, ‘Do not spend the night in the plains of the wilderness; cross the Jordan at once, lest the king and all his people be consumed.’”

17Jonathan and Ahimaaz were stationed at En-rogel, where a maidservant would come and relay messages to them; they would then go and inform King David, since they could not risk being seen entering the city. 18But a young man spotted them and reported them to Absalom, so the two fled swiftly to the house of a man at Bahurim, who had a well in his courtyard, and they climbed down into it. 19The man’s wife took a covering, spread it over the mouth of the well, and scattered grain over it, so that nothing was noticed.

20When Absalom’s servants came to the woman at the house and asked, “Where are Ahimaaz and Jonathan?”

She answered, “They crossed the brook.” The men searched but could not find them, and returned to Jerusalem.

21After they had gone, the two climbed out of the well and went to inform King David. “Rise and cross the water quickly,” they told him, “for Ahithophel has counselledcounselledcounseled thus against you.” 22So David and all the people with him arose and crossed the Jordan; by dawn not one of them remained on the near side.

Ahithophel’s Death and David’s Reception at Mahanaim

23When Ahithophel saw that his advice had not been followed, he saddled his donkey, went home to his city, set his house in order, and hanged himself. He died and was buried in his father’s tomb. 24David reached Mahanaim, while Absalom and all the men of Israel crossed the Jordan. 25Absalom appointed Amasa over the army in place of Joab; Amasa was the son of a man named Ithra the Israelite, who had married Abigail daughter of Nahash, sister of Zeruiah, Joab’s mother. 26Israel and Absalom encamped in the land of Gilead. 27When David came to Mahanaim, Shobi son of Nahash from Rabbah of the Ammonites, Machir son of Ammiel from Lo-debar, and Barzillai the Gileadite from Rogelim 28brought beds, basins, and pottery, together with wheat, barley, flour, parched grain, beans, lentils, and parched legumes, 29as well as honey, curds, sheep, and cheese from the cattle. They presented these provisions to David and to the people with him, for they said, “The people are hungry, weary, and thirsty in the wilderness.”

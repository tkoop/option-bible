Wine, Kings, and the Sluggard’s Folly

1Wine is a mocker, strong drink a brawler, and whoever is led astray by it is not wise. 2A king’s terror is like the roaring of a lion; whoever provokes him sins against his own life. 3It is an honourhonourhonor for a man to withdraw from strife, but every fool rushes into contention. 4The sluggard will not plow in winter; at harvest he will seek and find nothing. 5Counsel in the heart of a man is deep water, but a man of understanding draws it out. 6Many a man proclaims his own steadfast love, yet who can find a faithful man? 7The righteous man who walks in his integrity—blessed are his children after him. 8A king who sits on the throne of judgmentjudgementjudgment winnows all evil with his eyes.

9Who can say, “I have purified my heart; I am clean from my sin”?

Purity, Weights, and Restrained Speech

10Differing weights and differing measures are both an abomination to the LORD. 11Even a child is known by his deeds, whether his conduct is pure and upright. 12The hearing ear and the seeing eye—the LORD has made them both. 13Do not love sleep, lest you come to poverty; open your eyes, and you will be satisfied with bread.

14“Bad, bad!” says the buyer, but when he goes away, he boasts.

15There is gold and an abundance of pearls, but the lips of knowledge are a precious jewel. 16Take his garment, for he has stood surety for a stranger; and hold it in pledge for foreigners. 17Bread gained by deceit is sweet to a man, but afterward his mouth is filled with gravel. 18Plans are established by counsel, and by wise guidance one wages war. 19Whoever goes about as a talebearer reveals a secret; therefore do not associate with a babbler. 20Whoever curses his father or his mother—his lamp will be extinguished in utter darkness.

Inheritance, Vows, and the King’s Winnowing

21An inheritance gained hastily at the beginning will not be blessed in the end.

22Do not say, “I will repay evil”; wait for the LORD, and HeheHe will save you.

23Differing weights are an abomination to the LORD, and false scales are not good. 24A man’s steps are from the LORD; how then can a man understand his way? 25It is a snare for a man to say, “It is holy,” and only afterward to examine his vows.

26A wise king winnows the wicked and drives the threshing wheel over them. 27The spirit of man is the lamp of the LORD, searching all his inmost parts. 28Steadfast love and faithfulness preserve the king, and by steadfast love his throne is upheld. 29The glory of young men is their strength, but the splendoursplendoursplendor of old men is their greygreygray hair. 30Blows that wound cleanse away evil, and strokes make clean the innermost parts.

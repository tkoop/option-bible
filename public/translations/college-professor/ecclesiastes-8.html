On Wisdom, Royal Authority, and the Limits of Human Power

1Who resembles the sage, and who apprehends the elucidation of a matter? Wisdom illumines a man's visage, and the severity of his countenance is transfigured. 2Keep the king's injunction—I enjoin this on account of the oath sworn before God. 3Be not precipitate in withdrawing from his presence, nor persist in an evil affair, for he executes whatsoever he desires. 4For a king's word is sovereign; who may say to him, "What are you doing?"

5The keeper of the commandment knows no evil thing; a wise heart discerns both season and judgmentjudgementjudgment. 6For every purpose has its season and its judgmentjudgementjudgment, because the wretchedness of man is heavy upon him. 7For he knows not what shall be, and who can declare to him how it shall transpire? 8No one has dominion over the breath to restrain it, nor authority over the day of death, nor is there any discharge from war; and wickedness does not rescue those enthralled to it.

On the Vanity of Oppression, Delayed Justice, and Reversed Fortunes

9All this I have seen, and I applied my heart to every work wrought under the sun, in the time when one man tyrannized over another to his detriment. 10Thus I saw the wicked interred—those who once frequented the holy place—and forgotten in the very city where they had acted so; this too is vanity. 11Because sentence against an evil work is not executed with celerity, the heart of the sons of men is fully emboldened within them to do evil.

12Although a sinner does evil a hundred times and prolongs his days, yet I know that it shall be well with those who fear God, who stand in awe before HimhimHim. 13But it shall not be well with the wicked, nor shall he prolong his days, which are as a shadow, because he does not fear before God. 14There is a vanity wrought upon the earth: righteous men to whom it happens according to the deeds of the wicked, and wicked men to whom it happens according to the deeds of the righteous. I declared that this too is vanity.

On Joy and the Inscrutability of Divine Work

15Therefore I commended joy, for there is no good for a man under the sun save to eat, drink, and rejoice; and this shall accompany him in his toil throughout the days of life that God has given him under the sun.

16When I set my heart to know wisdom and to observe the business done on the earth—for neither by day nor by night does sleep visit his eyes— 17then I saw all the work of God, that man cannot discover the work done under the sun; however much he labourslabourslabors in seeking, he shall not find it; and even if the wise man professes to know, he cannot find it.

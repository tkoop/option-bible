The Assyrian Conquest of Judah's Fortified Cities

1In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah's reign, Sennacherib, king of Assyria, marched against every fortified city of Judah and captured them. 2The Assyrian monarch dispatched the Rabshakeh from Lachish to King Hezekiah in Jerusalem, accompanied by a formidable host; he took his station beside the conduit of the upper pool, on the road to the Fuller's FieldfieldField. 3Then Eliakim son of Hilkiah, the palace administrator, Shebna the scribe, and Joah son of Asaph the recorder, went out to meet him.

The Rabshakeh's Challenge to Hezekiah's Trust

4The Rabshakeh said to them, "Tell Hezekiah, 'Thus says the great king, the king of Assyria: What is this confidence in which you trust? 5You say—though it is merely a word of the lips—'I have counsel and strength for war.' Now in whom do you trust, that you have rebelled against me? 6Behold, you are relying upon Egypt, that splintered reed of a staff, which, if a man leans upon it, will pierce his hand. So is Pharaoh king of Egypt to all who rely upon him. 7But if you say to me, 'We trust in the LORD our God,' is it not HeheHe whose high places and altars Hezekiah has removed, saying to Judah and Jerusalem, "YouyouYou shall worship before this altar"?

8"Now therefore, strike a bargain with my master, the king of Assyria: I will give you two thousand horses, if you are able on your part to mount riders upon them. 9How then could you repel even one captain among the least of my master's servants, when you rely upon Egypt for chariots and horsemen? 10Moreover, have I ascended against this land to destroy it apart from the LORD? The LORD said to me, 'Go up against this land and destroy it.'"

The Language Dispute and the Public Summons to Surrender

11Then Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah said to the Rabshakeh, "Please address your servants in Aramaic, for we understand it; and do not speak to us in Hebrew within earshot of the people on the wall."

12But the Rabshakeh replied, "Has my master sent me to speak these words to your master and to you, and not to the men sitting on the wall, who, together with you, will eat their own excrement and drink their own urine?"

13Then the Rabshakeh stood and cried out in a loud voice in Hebrew, "Hear the words of the great king, the king of Assyria! 14Thus says the king: 'Do not let Hezekiah deceive you, for he will not be able to deliver you; 15nor let Hezekiah make you trust in the LORD, saying, "ThetheThe LORD will surely deliver us; this city will not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria."'

16"Do not listen to Hezekiah; for thus says the king of Assyria: 'Make peace with me and come out to me; then every one of you shall eat from his own vine and his own fig tree, and every one shall drink the water of his own cistern, 17until I come and take you away to a land like your own land—a land of grain and new wine, a land of bread and vineyards.' 18Beware lest Hezekiah mislead you, saying, 'The LORD will deliver us.' Has any of the gods of the nations delivered his land from the hand of the king of Assyria? 19Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad? Where are the gods of Sepharvaim? Have they delivered Samaria from my hand? 20Who among all the gods of these lands has delivered his country from my hand, that the LORD should deliver Jerusalem from my hand?"

The Envoys' Silent Return to Hezekiah

21But they remained silent and did not answer him a word, for the king's command was, "Do not answer him."

22Then Eliakim son of Hilkiah, who was over the household, Shebna the scribe, and Joah son of Asaph the recorder, came to Hezekiah with their garments torn, and reported to him the words of the Rabshakeh.

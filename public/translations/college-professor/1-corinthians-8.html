Knowledge, Love, and the Question of Idols

1Now concerning food offered to idols, we know that all of us possess knowledge; yet knowledge inflates with pride, whereas love builds up. 2If anyone presumes that he has attained knowledge, he has not yet apprehended it in the manner that one ought to apprehend it. 3But if anyone loves God, that person is known by HimhimHim.

The One God and the One Lord

4Therefore, concerning the consumption of food offered to idols, we know that an idol is nothing in the world and that there is no God except one. 5For even if there are those denominated gods, whether in Heavenheavenheaven or on EarthEarthearth—as indeed there are many gods and many lords— 6yet for us there is one God, the FatherfatherFather, from whom all things proceed and toward whom we exist, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom all things proceed and through whom we exist.

Conscience, Liberty, and the Weak Brother

7Yet not all possess this knowledge; rather, some, habituated to the idol until now, eat food as though it were offered to an idol, and their conscience, being infirm, is defiled. 8Food does not commend us to God: we are neither diminished if we abstain from eating nor augmented if we partake.

9Take heed, however, lest your liberty become a stumbling block to the weak. 10For if anyone sees you, who possess knowledge, reclining at table in an idol’s temple, will not his feeble conscience be emboldened to eat what is offered to idols? 11So the weak brother, for whom Christ died, is ruined by your knowledge. 12Thus, by sinning against your brothers and wounding their infirm conscience, you sin against Christ. 13Therefore, if food causes my brother to stumble, I will never again eat meat, lest I cause my brother to stumble.

David’s Muster and the Disposition of His Forces

1David mustered the people who were with him and appointed over them commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds. 2He dispatched the troops in three divisions: a third under Joab, a third under Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab’s brother, and a third under Ittai the Gittite. The king then said to the people, “I myself will surely go out with you.”

3But the people replied, “You must not go out, for if we flee, they will be utterly indifferent to us; and even if half of us perish, they will be utterly indifferent to us. Yet you are worth ten thousand of us, so it is better that you remain in the city to succor us.”

4The king answered them, “I will do whatever seems good in your eyes.” So the king stood beside the gate, while all the people marched out by hundreds and by thousands.

5The king had charged Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, saying, “Deal gently, for my sake, with the young man Absalom.” All the people heard the king’s charge to all the commanders concerning Absalom.

The Forest Battle and the Death of Absalom

6So the army went out into the field to meet Israel, and the battle was joined in the forest of Ephraim. 7There the people of Israel were defeated before David’s servants, and the slaughter was great that day—twenty thousand men. 8The battle spread over the whole countryside, and on that day the forest devoured more of the people than the sword did.

9Absalom happened to encounter David’s servants. He was riding on his mule, and as the mule passed beneath the thick boughs of a great oak, Absalom’s head became entangled in the tree, so that he was suspended between Heavenheavenheaven and EarthEarthearth while the mule beneath him kept going.

10When one of the men saw this, he told Joab, “Behold, I saw Absalom hanging in an oak.”

11Joab said to the man who reported it, “What? You saw him? Why did you not strike him to the ground there? I would have given you ten shekels of silver and a belt.”

12But the man replied to Joab, “Even if a thousand shekels were weighed into my hand, I would not lay my hand on the king’s son, for in our hearing the king charged you and Abishai and Ittai, saying, ‘Guard the young man Absalom.’ 13Otherwise, had I dealt treacherously with his life—and nothing is concealed from the king—you would have stood aloof from me.”

14Joab said, “I shall not linger in this fashion before you.” He took three javelins in his hand and thrust them into Absalom’s heart while he was still alive in the oak.

15Then ten young men who bore Joab’s armourarmourarmor surrounded Absalom, struck him, and killed him. 16Joab blew the trumpet, and the people ceased pursuing Israel, for Joab restrained the people. 17They took Absalom and cast him into a great pit in the forest and raised over him a very large heap of stones, while all Israel fled, each to his own tent.

18Now Absalom, in his lifetime, had taken and set up for himself a pillar in the KingkingKing’s ValleyvalleyValley, for he said, “I have no son to keep my name in remembrance.” He called the pillar after his own name, and it is called Absalom’s MonumentmonumentMonument to this day.

The Messengers Bring Tidings to the King

19Then Ahimaaz son of Zadok said, “Let me run and carry tidings to the king that the LORD has vindicated him against his enemies.”

20Joab said to him, “You are not a man of good tidings today; you may bring tidings another day, but today you shall bring no tidings, because the king’s son is dead.”

21Joab said to the Cushite, “Go, tell the king what you have seen.” The Cushite bowed to Joab and ran.

22Ahimaaz son of Zadok said yet again to Joab, “Let happen what may, I will run after the Cushite.”

Joab said, “Why would you run, my son, seeing that you will have no reward for your tidings?”

23“Let happen what may,” he said, “I will run.”

So Joab said, “Run.” Ahimaaz ran by way of the plain and passed the Cushite.

24Now David was sitting between the two gates, and the watchman went up to the roof of the gate by the wall. He looked out and saw a man running alone.

25So the watchman called out and told the king. The king said, “If he is alone, he has good news.” As the man drew nearer,

26the watchman saw another man running. So he called to the gatekeeper and said, “Look, there is another man running alone. This one must be bringing good news as well.”

“He is also a bearer of good tidings,” said the king.

27The watchman said, “I think the running of the first man is like the running of Ahimaaz son of Zadok.”

The king said, “He is a good man and comes with good news.”

28Then Ahimaaz called out to the king, “All is well!” He bowed down before the king with his face to the ground and said, “Blessed be the LORD your God, who has delivered up the men who raised their hands against my lord the king.”

29The king asked, “Is the young man Absalom safe?”

Ahimaaz answered, “When Joab sent your servant, I saw a great commotion, but I did not know what it was.”

30“Turn aside and stand here,” said the king. So he turned aside and stood still.

31Just then the Cushite arrived and said, “May my lord the king receive good news: The LORD has vindicated you today from all who rose up against you.”

32The king asked the Cushite, “Is the young man Absalom safe?”

The Cushite replied, “May the enemies of my lord the king and all who rise up against you to harm you be like that young man.”

David’s Lament over Absalom

33Then the king was deeply moved, and he went up to the chamber above the gate and wept. And as he went, he said, “O my son Absalom! My son, my son Absalom! If only I had died instead of you—O Absalom, my son, my son!”

The Ephemeral Condition of Mortal Humanity

1A mortal, begotten of woman, endures but a truncated span and is saturated with turmoil. 2He blossoms like a flower and is cut down; he flees like a shadow and leaves no enduring trace.

No one. 5If his days are predetermined, the enumeration of his months resides with YouyouYou; YouyouYou have fixed his terminus, which he cannot transgress. 6Avert YouryourYour gaze from him and let him rest, until, like a hired labourerlabourerlaborer, he completes his allotted day.

The Renewal of Trees and the Finality of Human Death

7For a tree retains hope: if it is felled, it will sprout anew, and its tender shoots will not languish. 8Though its root ages in the earth and its stump dies in the ground, 9at the scent of water it will bud and put forth branches like a newly planted shoot. 10Yet a mortal perishes and is laid low; he expires, and whither has he gone? 11As water vanishes from the sea and a river wastes and desiccates, 12so a man lies down and does not rise again; until the heavens are no more, he will not awaken or be roused from his slumber.

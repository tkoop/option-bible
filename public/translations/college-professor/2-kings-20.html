Hezekiah's Mortal Illness and Divine Reprieve

1In those days Hezekiah fell gravely ill. The prophet Isaiah son of Amoz came to him and declared, “Thus says the LORD: ‘Put your affairs in order, for you are about to die; you shall not recover.’”

2Hezekiah turned his face toward the wall and prayed to the LORD, saying, 3“Please, O the LORD, remember how I have walked before YouyouYou in truth and with an undivided heart, and have done what is pleasing in YouryourYour sight.” And Hezekiah wept bitterly.

4Before Isaiah had gone out into the middle court, the word of the LORD came to him: 5“Return and tell Hezekiah, the leader of MymyMy people, ‘Thus says the LORD, the God of your father David: I have heard your prayer and seen your tears. Behold, I will heal you; on the third day you shall ascend to the house of the LORD. 6I will add fifteen years to your life; I will deliver you and this city from the grasp of the king of Assyria; and I will shield this city for MymyMy own sake and for the sake of MymyMy servant David.’” 7Then Isaiah said, “Bring a poultice of figs.” So they brought it and applied it to the boil, and he recovered.

The Sign of the Receding Shadow

8Hezekiah asked Isaiah, “What sign will the LORD give that HeheHe will heal me and that I shall go up to the house of the LORD on the third day?”

9Isaiah replied, “This is the sign from the LORD that HeheHe will accomplish what HeheHe has promised: shall the shadow advance ten steps, or retreat ten steps?”

10Hezekiah said, “It is a trivial matter for the shadow to advance ten steps; no, let the shadow retreat ten steps.” 11Then Isaiah the prophet cried out to the LORD, and HeheHe caused the shadow to retreat ten steps along the steps by which it had descended—the stairway of Ahaz.

Babylonian Envoys and the Prophetic Reprimand

12At that time Berodach-baladan son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent envoys with letters and a gift to Hezekiah, because he had heard that Hezekiah had been ill. 13Hezekiah welcomed them and showed them his entire treasury—silver, gold, spices, precious oil, his armouryarmouryarmory, and everything found in his storehouses. There was nothing in his palace or throughout his realm that he did not display to them.

14Then the prophet Isaiah came to King Hezekiah and asked, “What did these men say, and from where did they come to you?”

Hezekiah replied, “They have come from a distant land—from Babylon.”

15Isaiah asked, “What have they seen in your house?”

Hezekiah said, “They have seen everything in my house; there is nothing among my treasures that I have not shown them.”

16Isaiah said to Hezekiah, “Hear the word of the LORD.” 17“Behold, days are coming when everything in your house, and all that your fathers have laid up until this day, shall be carried off to Babylon; nothing shall remain, says the LORD. 18Some of your own sons, born to you, shall be taken away and made eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.”

19Hezekiah said to Isaiah, “The word of the LORD that you have spoken is good,” for he thought, “Why not, if there will be peace and truth in my days?”

Hezekiah's Death and Succession

20The rest of the deeds of Hezekiah, all his might, and how he made the pool and the conduit and brought water into the city—are they not recorded in the BookbookBook of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah? 21Hezekiah passed away, joining his ancestors, and his son Manasseh reigned in his place.

The Sign of the Linen Belt

1This is what the LORD said to me: “Go and procure for yourself a linen loincloth, and place it about your waist, but do not let it touch water.”

2So I bought a loincloth in accordance with the word of the LORD, and I placed it about my waist.

3Then the word of the LORD came to me a second time:

4“Take the loincloth that you bought and are wearing, and go at once to Perath and hide it there in a crevice of the rocks.”

5So I went and hid it at Perath, exactly as the LORD had commanded me.

6Many days later the LORD said to me, “Arise, go to Perath, and take the loincloth that I commanded you to hide there.”

7So I went to Perath and dug, and I took the loincloth from the place where I had hidden it; and, behold, it was ruined—it had become altogether worthless.

The Ruin of Judah’s Pride

8Then the word of the LORD came to me:

9“This is what the LORD says: In the same way I will ruin the pride of Judah and the great pride of Jerusalem. 10These evil people, who refuse to listen to MymyMy words, who follow the stubbornness of their own hearts, and who go after other gods to serve and worship them, shall be like this loincloth—utterly useless. 11For just as a loincloth clings to a man’s waist, so I have made the whole house of Israel and the whole house of Judah cling to MemeMe, declares the LORD, so that they might be MymyMy people for MymyMy renown, praise, and glory. But they would not listen.”

The Cup of Drunkenness

12“Therefore you are to tell them that this is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: ‘Every wineskin should be filled with wine.’”

And when they reply, “Do we not surely know that every wineskin will be filled with wine?”

13“Then you are to tell them that this is what the LORD says: ‘Behold, I will fill all the inhabitants of this land—the kings who sit on David’s throne, the priests, the prophets, and all the inhabitants of Jerusalem—with drunkenness. 14I will smash them against one another, fathers and sons alike, declares the LORD. I will allow no pity, nor mercy, nor compassion to keep MemeMe from destroying them.’”

A Plea for Repentance and a Lament

15Listen and give heed. Do not be proud, for the LORD has spoken. 16Give glory to the LORD your God before HeheHe brings darkness, and before your feet stumble upon the twilight hills; while you wait for light, HeheHe turns it into the shadow of death and makes it deep gloom. 17But if you do not listen, I will weep in secret because of your pride; my eyes will overflow with tears, because the LORD’s flock has been taken captive.

JudgmentJudgementJudgment upon the Royal House

18Say to the king and to the queen mother: “Take a lowly seat, for your glorious crowns have fallen from your heads.”

19The cities of the Negev have been shut up, and there is no one to open them. All Judah has been carried into exile—wholly carried into exile. 20Raise your eyes and see those coming from the north. Where is the flock entrusted to you, the sheep that were your pride? 21What will you say when HeheHe sets over you those you have trained to be your allies? Will not pangs seize you like a woman in labourlabourlabor? 22And if you say in your heart, “Why has this happened to me?”—it is because of the greatness of your iniquity that your skirts have been stripped off and your bare feet exposed. 23Can the Cushite change his skin, or the leopard its spots? Then neither can you do good, you who are accustomed to doing evil.

Exile, Shame, and a Final Woe

24“I will scatter you like chaff driven by the wind from the desert. 25This is your lot, the portion I have measured out to you,” declares the LORD, “because you have forgotten MemeMe and trusted in falsehood. 26I will also pull up your skirts over your face, that your shame may be seen. 27I have seen your abominations on the hills and in the fields. Woe to you, O Jerusalem! How long will you remain unclean?”

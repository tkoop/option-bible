The Patrimonial Allotment of Manasseh

1The lot was assigned to the tribe of Manasseh, for he was Joseph’s firstborn—namely Machir, the firstborn of Manasseh and progenitor of Gilead, a warrior; consequently, Gilead and Bashan were conferred upon him. 2The allotment likewise pertained to the remaining posterity of Manasseh, according to their clans: the clans of Abiezer, Helek, Asriel, Shechem, Hepher, and Shemida. These were the male descendants of Manasseh son of Joseph, enumerated by their families.

The Claim of Zelophehad’s Daughters

3Now Zelophehad son of Hepher, son of Gilead, son of Machir, son of Manasseh, had no sons, but daughters only; and these are the names of his daughters: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah. 4They presented themselves before Eleazar the priest, before Joshua son of Nun, and before the leaders, saying, “The LORD commanded Moses to give us an inheritance among our brothers.” Accordingly, Joshua granted them an inheritance among their father’s brothers, in accordance with the command of the LORD.

5Thus ten portions fell to Manasseh, apart from the land of Gilead and Bashan beyond the Jordan, 6because the daughters of Manasseh received an inheritance among his sons, whereas the land of Gilead belonged to the rest of the sons of Manasseh.

The Boundaries and Cities of Manasseh

7The boundary of Manasseh extended from Asher to Michmethath, east of Shechem; it then turned southward toward the inhabitants of En-tappuah. 8The land of Tappuah belonged to Manasseh, but Tappuah itself, situated on the border of Manasseh, belonged to the Ephraimites. 9From there the boundary descended into the Kanah ValleyvalleyValley. These cities, though reckoned among the cities of Manasseh, belonged to Ephraim; and the border of Manasseh lay on the northern side of the valley, terminating at the sea. 10Ephraim’s territory lay to the south, and Manasseh’s to the north, with the sea as its western limit; and they abutted Asher on the north and Issachar on the east. 11Within Issachar and Asher, Manasseh also possessed Beth-shean and its dependent towns, Ibleam and its dependent towns, the inhabitants of Dor and its dependent towns, the inhabitants of Endor and its dependent towns, the inhabitants of Taanach and its dependent towns, and the inhabitants of Megiddo and its dependent towns—three Naphoth.

12Yet the descendants of Manasseh were unable to dispossess these cities, because the Canaanites were resolutely determined to remain in that land. 13But when the Israelites grew strong, they reduced the Canaanites to compulsory servitude and did not expel them entirely.

The Grievance of Joseph’s Descendants and Joshua’s Admonition

14Then the descendants of Joseph addressed Joshua, saying, “Why have you given us only one allotment and one portion? We are a populous people, because the LORD has blessed us abundantly.”

15Joshua answered them, “If you are a populous people, ascend into the forest and hew out a place for yourself there in the land of the Perizzites and the Rephaim, since the hill country of Ephraim is too constricted for you.”

16The descendants of Joseph replied, “The hill country is insufficient for us, and all the Canaanites who dwell in the valley possess iron chariots—both those in Beth-shean and its villages and those in the valley of Jezreel.”

17Then Joshua said to the house of Joseph—to Ephraim and Manasseh—“You are a populous and mighty people; you will have not only one allotment, 18but the hill country as well. It is a forest, and you will clear it, and its farthest borders will be yours. Although the Canaanites have iron chariots and are strong, you can drive them out.”

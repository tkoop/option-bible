The Adversaries’ Overture and the Rejection of Their Aid

1When the adversaries of Judah and Benjamin ascertained that the returned exiles were erecting a temple unto the LORD, the God of Israel, 2they drew near to Zerubbabel and the chiefs of the ancestral houses and said to them, “Let us build with you, for we seek your God even as you do, and we have been sacrificing to HimhimHim since the days of Esarhaddon, king of Assyria, who transported us hither.”

3But Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the remaining chiefs of Israel’s ancestral houses replied to them, “You have no share with us in building a house for our God; we ourselves will build it for the LORD, the God of Israel, as King Cyrus of Persia has enjoined upon us.”

Sustained Obstruction of the Temple Reconstruction

4Thereupon the people of the land weakened the hands of the people of Judah and terrified them from building, 5and they also suborned counselors against them to frustrate their design throughout the days of Cyrus, king of Persia, and until the reign of Darius, king of Persia.

6In the commencement of the reign of Ahasuerus, they composed an accusation against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem.

The Accusatory Correspondence to Artaxerxes

7And in the days of Artaxerxes, Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel, and the rest of their colleagues wrote to Artaxerxes, king of Persia; the letter was written in Aramaic and translated.

8Rehum the chancellor and Shimshai the scribe wrote a letter against Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes in these terms:

“9Rehum the chancellor, Shimshai the scribe, and the rest of their associates—the Dinaites, the Apharsathchites, the Tarpelites, the Apharsites, the Archevites, the Babylonians, the Susanchites, the Dehavites, and the Elamites— 10together with the rest of the nations whom the great and noble Asnappar deported and settled in the city of Samaria and elsewhere in the province Beyond the River—and now—

“11this is a copy of the letter that they sent to him—to King Artaxerxes—from your servants, the men of the province Beyond the River: Peace to you. And now— 12let it be known to the king that the Jews who came up from you to us have arrived at Jerusalem. They are rebuilding that rebellious and deleterious city; they are completing its walls and repairing its foundations.

“13Now let it be known to the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are finished, they will render no tribute, custom, or toll, and the royal revenues will be diminished. 14Now, because we are in the king’s service and are bound by fealty to the crown, it is not seemly that we should behold the king’s dishonourdishonourdishonor; therefore we send this notification and make it known to the king,

“15that an inquiry may be made in the book of the records of your fathers. In that record you will discover and learn that this city is a rebellious city, injurious to kings and provinces, and that sedition has been fomented within it from days of old; for this cause the city was devastated. 16We make known to the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are completed, you will have no share beyond the River.”

The Royal Decree and the Cessation of Building

“17The king sent this reply: “To Rehum the chancellor, Shimshai the scribe, and the rest of their associates dwelling in Samaria and elsewhere in the province Beyond the River: Peace to you. And now—

“18The letter that you sent to us has been read distinctly before me. 19And I issued a decree, and a search has been made, and it has been found that this city from ancient times has risen up against kings, and that rebellion and sedition have been committed in it.

“20And mighty kings have been over Jerusalem, who ruled over all the province Beyond the River, and to whom tribute, custom, and toll were paid. 21Therefore issue a decree that these men be stopped, and that this city not be rebuilt until a decree is issued by me.

“22And be diligent not to be remiss in this matter. Why should damage increase to the detriment of the kings?”

23Then, when the copy of King Artaxerxes’ letter was read before Rehum, Shimshai, and their associates, they hastened to Jerusalem against the Jews and stopped them by force and might. 24Then the work on the house of God in Jerusalem was suspended, and it remained suspended until the second year of the reign of Darius, king of Persia.

4Do not answer a fool according to his folly, lest you become like him. 5Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he regard himself as wise.

6Entrusting a message to a fool is like severing one's own feet and drinking violence. 7A proverb in a fool's mouth is as useless as the dangling legs of the lame. 8Bestowing honourhonourhonor upon a fool is as senseless as binding a stone into a sling. 9A proverb on a fool's lips is like a thorn lodged in a drunkard's hand.

10Hiring a fool—or any random passerby—is like an archer who wounds indiscriminately. 11As a dog returns to its vomit, so a fool reiterates his folly. 12Do you see a person wise in his own conceit? There is more hope for a fool than for him.

The Sluggard's Specious Excuses

13The sluggard says, "There is a lion in the road—a lion in the streets!" 14As a door turns on its hinges, so the sluggard turns upon his bed. 15The sluggard buries his hand in the dish, yet is too weary to return it to his mouth. 16The sluggard is wiser in his own eyes than seven men who answer with discernment.

Meddling, Whispering, and Hypocrisy

17He who seizes a dog by the ears is like one who passes by and meddles in a quarrel not his own. 18Like a madman shooting firebrands, arrows, and death, 19so is the person who deceives his neighbourneighbourneighbor and says, "Was I not joking?"

20Without wood the fire dies; without a whisperer, strife subsides. 21As charcoal is to burning coals and wood to fire, so is a contentious person to kindle strife. 22The words of a whisperer are like dainty morsels that descend into the innermost parts.

23Fervent lips with a wicked heart are like a silver glaze over an earthen vessel. 24The hater disguises himself with his lips, while he stores up deceit within. 25Though his speech be gracious, do not believe him, for seven abominations reside in his heart.

26His malice may be concealed by deception, but his wickedness will be exposed in the assembly. 27Whoever digs a pit will fall into it, and whoever rolls a stone will find it rolling back upon him. 28A lying tongue hates those it crushes, and a flattering mouth brings ruin.

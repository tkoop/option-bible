The Earthly Tabernacle and Its Sacred Furnishings

1Now even the first covenant possessed regulations for worship and an earthly sanctuary. 2For a tabernacle was prepared—the first section—in which were the lampstand, the table, and the bread of the PresencepresencePresence; this is called the HolyholyHoly PlaceplacePlace. 3Behind the second curtain stood a tabernacle called the MostmostMost HolyholyHoly PlaceplacePlace, 4having the golden altar of incense and the ark of the covenant, overlaid on every side with gold, in which was a golden urn containing the manna, Aaron’s staff that had budded, and the tablets of the covenant. 5Above it were the cherubim of glory, overshadowing the mercy seat; concerning these things, however, we are not now to speak in detail.

The Restricted Access of the Levitical Priests

6These arrangements having been thus completed, the priests enter the first section continually, performing the sacred services; 7but into the second section the high priest alone enters, and that only once a year, not without blood, which he offers for himself and for the people’s unwitting sins. 8The HolyholyHoly SpiritspiritSpirit thereby signified that the way into the holy places was not yet disclosed while the first tabernacle still maintained its standing, 9which constitutes a figure for the present age, according to which both gifts and sacrifices are offered that cannot perfect the conscience of the worshiper. 10They pertain only to food and drink and various washings—bodily ordinances imposed until the time of reformation.

The Superior Priesthood of Christ

11But when Christ appeared as HighhighHigh Priest of the good things that have come, HeheHe entered through the greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands—that is, not of this creation— 12and not through the blood of goats and calves, but through HishisHis own blood, HeheHe entered once for all into the holy places, having secured eternal redemption. 13For if the blood of goats and bulls, and the sprinkling of those defiled with the ashes of a heifer, sanctifies them for the purification of the flesh, 14how much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal SpiritspiritSpirit offered HimselfhimselfHimself without blemish to God, cleanse our conscience from dead works to serve the living God.

15And for this reason HeheHe is the mediator of a new covenant, so that those who have been called may receive the promised eternal inheritance, a death having occurred for the redemption of the transgressions committed under the first covenant. 16For where a covenant exists, it is necessary to establish the death of the one who made it. 17For a covenant is valid only upon death, since it is never in force while the one who made it lives.

The Ratification by Blood and the Final Salvation

18Hence not even the first covenant was ratified without blood. 19For when every commandment had been spoken by Moses to all the people according to the law, he took the blood of calves and goats, with water, scarlet wool, and hyssop, and sprinkled both the scroll itself and all the people, 20saying, “This is the blood of the covenant that God commanded for you.” 21And in the same manner he sprinkled with the blood both the tabernacle and all the vessels of the ministry. 22Indeed, according to the law, almost everything is purified with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins.

23It was therefore necessary for the copies of the heavenly realities to be purified with these rites, whereas the heavenly things themselves required better sacrifices than these. 24For Christ did not enter holy places made by hands, which are mere copies of the true ones, but into Heavenheavenheaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf. 25Nor did HeheHe offer HimselfhimselfHimself repeatedly, as the high priest enters the holy places year by year with blood not his own; 26otherwise HeheHe would have had to suffer repeatedly since the foundation of the world. But now, at the consummation of the ages, HeheHe has appeared once for all to put away sin through the sacrifice of HimselfhimselfHimself. 27And just as it is appointed for people to die once, and after that comes judgmentjudgementjudgment, 28so Christ, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time, not to deal with sin, but to save those who are eagerly awaiting HimhimHim.

Ordinances Governing Hebrew Servitude

1These are the ordinances that you shall set before them.

2If you acquire a Hebrew servant, he shall serve six years, and in the seventh year he shall go out free, without payment. 3If he entered alone, he shall go out alone; if he was married, his wife shall go out with him. 4If his master gives him a wife, and she bears him sons or daughters, the woman and her children shall belong to their master, and he shall go out alone.

5But if the servant declares, “I love my master, my wife, and my children; I do not wish to go free,” 6then his master shall bring him before the judges, and he shall bring him to the door or to the doorpost and bore his ear with an awl; thereafter he shall serve his master in perpetuity.

7If a man sells his daughter as a maidservant, she shall not go out as the male servants go out. 8If she is displeasing in the eyes of her master, who has designated her for himself, he shall let her be redeemed; he shall have no authority to sell her to a foreign people, because he has dealt treacherously with her. 9If he designates her for his son, he shall deal with her according to the ordinance for daughters. 10If he takes another wife for himself, he shall not diminish her food, her clothing, or her conjugal rights. 11If he does not provide her with these three things, she shall go out free, without money.

Statutes on Homicide, Assault, and Capital Crimes

12Whoever strikes a man so that he dies shall surely be put to death. 13But if he did not lie in wait, and God caused the man to fall into his hand, then I will appoint for you a place to which he may flee. 14But if a man acts with premeditation against his neighbourneighbourneighbor, to kill him by treachery, he shall be taken from MymyMy altar to be put to death.

15Whoever strikes his father or his mother shall surely be put to death. 16Whoever kidnaps a man and sells him, or if the kidnapped man is found in his possession, shall surely be put to death. 17Whoever curses his father or his mother shall surely be put to death.

18If men quarrel, and one strikes his neighbourneighbourneighbor with a stone or with his fist, and the man does not die but is confined to bed, 19then, if he rises again and walks about outside with his staff, the striker shall be absolved; nevertheless, he shall compensate the injured man for his lost time and provide for his healing.

20If a man strikes his male or female servant with a rod, and the servant dies under his hand, vengeance shall surely be taken. 21However, if the servant survives a day or two, the owner shall not be punished, because the servant is his property.

22If men who are fighting strike a pregnant woman and her children come forth, but there is no injury, the offender shall surely be fined, according to what the woman's husband imposes upon him and as the judges determine. 23But if there is an injury, then you shall give life for life, 24eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot, 25burn for burn, wound for wound, bruise for bruise.

26If a man strikes the eye of his male or female servant and destroys it, he shall send the servant away free in compensation for the eye. 27If he knocks out a tooth of his male or female servant, he shall send the servant away free in compensation for the tooth.

Liability for Goring Oxen and Property Damage

28If an ox gores a man or a woman to death, the ox shall surely be stoned, and its flesh shall not be eaten; but the owner of the ox shall be acquitted. 29If the ox has been accustomed to gore in the past, and its owner has been warned, yet he does not restrain it, and it kills a man or a woman, then the ox shall be stoned and its owner shall also be put to death. 30If, however, a ransom is imposed upon him, he shall pay whatever is demanded for the redemption of his life. 31Whether the ox gores a son or a daughter, it shall be done to him according to this same ordinance. 32If the ox gores a male or female servant, the owner shall give thirty shekels of silver to his master, and the ox shall be stoned.

33If a man opens or digs a pit and does not cover it, and an ox or a donkey falls into it, 34the owner of the pit shall make restitution; he shall give money to the owner of the animal, and the dead animal shall be his.

35If one man's ox injures another man's ox so that it dies, they shall sell the living ox and divide the proceeds, and they shall also divide the dead ox. 36But if it was known that the ox had a habit of goring in the past, and its owner did not restrain it, he shall pay ox for ox, and the dead ox shall be his.

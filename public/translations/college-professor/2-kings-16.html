Ahaz’s Accession and Apostasy

1In the seventeenth year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Ahaz son of Jotham ascended the throne of Judah. 2Ahaz was twenty years old at his accession and reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem; nevertheless, he did not do what was right in the sight of the LORD his God, as David his father had done. 3He emulated the conduct of the kings of Israel, and he even consigned his own son to the fire, in conformity with the abominable practices of the nations that the LORD had dispossessed before the Israelites. 4He offered sacrifices and burned incense upon the high places, upon the hills, and beneath every verdant tree.

The Syro-Ephraimite Siege and the Assyrian Appeal

5At that juncture Rezin, king of Aram, and Pekah son of Remaliah, king of Israel, marched against Jerusalem to wage war; they besieged Ahaz, yet proved unable to subdue him. 6Concurrently, Rezin king of Aram recovered Elath for Aram, expelling the Judahites from Elath; the Arameans occupied Elath and remain there to this day.

7Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, saying, “I am your servant and your son. Ascend and deliver me from the power of the king of Aram and from the power of the king of Israel, who have risen up against me.” 8Ahaz further appropriated the silver and gold discovered in the house of the LORD and in the royal treasuries, dispatching them as tribute to the king of Assyria. 9The king of Assyria hearkened to him: he ascended against Damascus, seized it, deported its inhabitants to Kir, and executed Rezin.

The Damascus Altar and the Reordering of Worship

10King Ahaz journeyed to Damascus to greet Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria; upon beholding the altar in Damascus, he transmitted to Uriah the priest a delineation of the altar and a model thereof, in accordance with all its craftsmanship. 11Uriah the priest constructed the altar precisely in accordance with all that King Ahaz had sent from Damascus, and he completed it before King Ahaz returned from Damascus. 12Upon the king’s return from Damascus, when he saw the altar, he drew near to it and ascended upon it. 13He burned his burnt offering and his grain offering, poured out his libation, and sprinkled upon the altar the blood of his peace offerings. 14He relocated the bronze altar that stood before the LORD from its position in front of the temple—between the new altar and the house of the LORD—and stationed it on the north side of the new altar.

15King Ahaz commanded Uriah the priest, “Burn upon the great altar the morning burnt offering and the evening burnt offering, the king’s burnt offering and his grain offering, the burnt offering of all the people of the land, and their grain offerings and their drink offerings; sprinkle upon it all the blood of the burnt offering and all the blood of the sacrifice. But the bronze altar shall be mine for consultation.” 16Uriah the priest executed all that King Ahaz had commanded. 17King Ahaz dismantled the frames of the stands and removed the basin from them; he took down the SeaseaSea from the bronze oxen beneath it and placed it upon a stone pavementroadwaypavement. 18He also removed from the house of the LORD the Sabbath canopy that had been erected there, together with the king’s outer entrance, because of the king of Assyria.

Ahaz’s Death and Succession

19The remainder of the deeds of Ahaz, are they not inscribed in the BookbookBook of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah? 20Ahaz lay down with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the CitycityCity of David; and his son Hezekiah reigned in his stead.

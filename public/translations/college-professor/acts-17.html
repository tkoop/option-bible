1Having traversed Amphipolis and Apollonia, they arrived at Thessalonica, where a Jewish synagogue was situated. 2In accordance with his established practice, Paul entered their assembly and, over the course of three Sabbaths, discoursed with them from the ScripturesscripturesScriptures, 3explicating and adducing that the Christ was obliged to suffer and to be raised from the dead, and declaring, “This Jesus whom I proclaim to you is the Christ.”

4A number of them were persuaded and attached themselves to Paul and Silas, together with a considerable multitude of devout Greeks and no small number of women of prominence. 5The Jews, however, moved by jealousy, conscripted certain worthless men from the marketplace, raised a tumult, and threw the city into confusion; then, assailing Jason’s house, they sought to drag Paul and Silas before the populace.

6Failing to locate them, they dragged Jason and certain brothers before the city magistrates, crying, “These men who have subverted the inhabited world have now arrived here as well, 7and Jason has given them lodging; moreover, all of them contravene the decrees of Caesar, alleging that there is another king—Jesus.” 8Upon hearing this, both the crowd and the magistrates were thrown into agitation. 9After exacting adequate security from Jason and the others, they released them.

The Berean Reception and Flight to Athens

10The brothers at once dispatched Paul and Silas by night to Berea; upon reaching that city, they repaired to the Jewish synagogue. 11These Bereans proved more noble-minded than their Thessalonian counterparts, for they welcomed the message with exemplary eagerness, scrutinizing the ScripturesscripturesScriptures daily to ascertain whether these assertions held true. 12Consequently, many of them believed, together with a considerable number of reputable Greek women and men. 13When, however, the Jews of Thessalonica learned that the word of God had been proclaimed by Paul at Berea as well, they came thither, inciting and unsettling the multitudes. 14The brothers therefore sent Paul off at once toward the sea, while Silas and Timothy remained behind. 15Paul’s escorts conveyed him to Athens and then departed, bearing instructions for Silas and Timothy to join him with all dispatchdispatch, despatchdispatch.

Paul’s Provocation amid Athenian Idolatry

16While Paul awaited them in Athens, his spirit was provoked within him as he observed the city given over to idolatry. 17Accordingly, he reasoned in the synagogue with the Jews and the devout, and in the marketplace each day with whomever he encountered. 18Certain Epicurean and StoicstoicStoic philosophers entered into disputation with him. Some derided him as a prattling seed-picker, while others remarked that he appeared to be a herald of foreign deities—this because he was proclaiming Jesus and the resurrection. 19Seizing him, they led him to the Areopagus, saying, “May we ascertain what this novel teaching is that you are presenting? 20For you are introducing strange notions into our ears, and we wish to know what these things signify.” 21All the Athenians, together with the sojourning foreigners, devoted themselves to nothing so much as to narrating or hearing some novelty.

Paul’s Areopagitic Discourse on the Unknown God

22Paul then stood in the midst of the Areopagus and said, “Men of Athens, I perceive that in all things you are exceedingly religious. 23For as I traversed the city and contemplated your devotions, I discovered an altar bearing the inscription, ‘To an Unknown God.’ What therefore you worship without knowing, that I proclaim to you.

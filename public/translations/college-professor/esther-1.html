Ahasuerus’s Imperial Banquet

1Now in the days of Ahasuerus—that is, the Ahasuerus who reigned over one hundred twenty-seven provinces stretching from India to Cush— 2in those days, when King Ahasuerus sat upon his royal throne in the citadel of Susa, 3in the third year of his reign, he convened a feast for all his officials and servants, while the military commanders of Persia and Media, together with the nobles and provincial governors, stood before him. 4For one hundred eighty days in all, he displayed the resplendent wealth of his kingdom and the magnificent splendoursplendoursplendor of his majesty.

The Banquet in the Palace Garden

5When those days had run their course, the king furnished a seven-day banquet in the garden courtyard of the royal palace for all the people, from the most humble to the most exalted, who were present in the citadel of Susa. 6White and violet linen hangings were fastened with cords of fine linen and purple to silver rings on marble pillars; gold and silver couches rested upon a pavementroadwaypavement of porphyry, marble, mother-of-pearl, and other precious stones. 7Wine was served in golden vessels, and the royal wine flowed abundantly, in keeping with the king’s munificence. 8The drinking proceeded according to ordinance: no one was compelled, for the king had instructed every steward of his household to allow each guest to drink according to his own inclination.

Queen Vashti’s Refusal

9Queen Vashti likewise hosted a banquet for the women in the royal palace of King Ahasuerus. 10On the seventh day, when the king’s heart was gladdened by wine, he commanded the seven eunuchs who attended him—Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha, Abagtha, Zethar, and Carkas— 11to bring Queen Vashti before the king, crowned with the royal diadem, so that he might exhibit her beauty to the peoples and officials, for she was of surpassing beauty. 12But Queen Vashti refused to appear at the king’s behest, conveyed through the eunuchs, whereupon the king was incensed, and his fury burned within him.

The Counsel of the Princes and the Royal Decree

13Then the king consulted the wise men who understood the times—for it was the king’s custom to seek counsel from all who knew law and justice— 14namely, Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena, and Memucan, the seven princes of Persia and Media who enjoyed access to the king and occupied the foremost rank in the kingdom. 15“What,” he asked, “does the law require concerning Queen Vashti, since she has not obeyed the command of King Ahasuerus conveyed by the eunuchs?”

16Memucan replied before the king and the princes, “Queen Vashti has not merely wronged the king, but also all the officials and all the peoples throughout the provinces of King Ahasuerus. 17For the queen’s behaviourbehaviourbehavior will become known to every woman, and it will move them to treat their husbands with contempt, saying, ‘King Ahasuerus commanded that Queen Vashti be brought before him, yet she did not come.’ 18This very day the noblewomen of Persia and Media who have heard of the queen’s conduct will repeat it to all the king’s officials, and there will be no end of contempt and indignation

19“If it please the king, let a royal decree issue from him and be inscribed among the laws of Persia and Media, so that it may not be revoked: Vashti shall never again enter the presence of King Ahasuerus, and the king shall bestow her royal dignity upon another who is more worthy than she. 20When this decree, issued throughout so vast a kingdom, is proclaimed, all women, from the most humble to the most exalted, will render honourhonourhonor to their husbands.”

21The king and his princes approved of this counsel, and the king acted in accordance with Memucan’s advice. 22He dispatched letters to every province of the kingdom, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language, decreeing that every man should be sovereign in his own household and should speak according to the tongue of his own people.

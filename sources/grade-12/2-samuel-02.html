David Anointed King over Judah

1 After this, David inquired of the LORD, “Shall I go up to one of the cities of Judah?”

The LORD said to him, “Go up.”

David asked, “Where shall I go up?”

He answered, “To Hebron.”

2 So David went up there with his two wives, Ahinoam of Jezreel and Abigail, Nabal’s widow from Carmel. 3 David brought up the men who were with him, each with his household, and they settled in the cities of Hebron.

4 The men of Judah came and anointed David king over the house of Judah. They also told David, “The men of Jabesh-gilead are the ones who buried Saul.”

5 David sent messengers to the men of Jabesh-gilead and said, “Blessed are you to the LORD, because you have shown this kindness to Saul your lord by burying him. 6 Now may the LORD show kindness and faithfulness to you; I too will reward you for this good deed, because you have done it. 7 Now therefore let your hands be strong, and be valiant, for Saul your lord is dead, and the house of Judah has anointed me king over them.”

Ish-bosheth Made King over Israel

8 But Abner son of Ner, commander of Saul’s army, took Ish-bosheth son of Saul and brought him across to Mahanaim. 9 He made him king over Gilead, the Ashurites, Jezreel, Ephraim, Benjamin, and all Israel. 10 Ish-bosheth son of Saul was forty years old when he became king over Israel, and he reigned two years. The house of Judah, however, followed David. 11 David reigned over the house of Judah in Hebron for seven years and six months.

Battle at the Pool of Gibeon

12 Abner son of Ner and the servants of Ish-bosheth son of Saul went out from Mahanaim to Gibeon. 13 Joab son of Zeruiah and David’s servants went out and met them by the pool of Gibeon; they sat down, these on one side of the pool and those on the other.

14 Abner said to Joab, “Let the young men rise and compete before us.”

Joab said, “Let them rise.”

15 Then they rose and crossed over by number: twelve for Benjamin and for Ish-bosheth son of Saul, and twelve of David’s servants. 16 Each man seized his opponent by the head and thrust his sword into his opponent’s side, so they fell together. Therefore that place was called Helkath-hazzurim, in Gibeon. 17 The battle grew very fierce that day; Abner and the men of Israel were defeated before David’s servants.

Asahel’s Pursuit and Death

18 The three sons of Zeruiah were there—Joab, Abishai, and Asahel—and Asahel was as swift of foot as a wild gazelle. 19 Asahel pursued Abner, and as he went he did not turn to the right or to the left from following him.

20 Abner looked behind him and asked, “Are you Asahel?”

He answered, “I am.”

21 Abner said to him, “Turn aside to your right or to your left, seize one of the young men, and take his armor.” But Asahel refused to turn aside from following him.

22 Abner said again to Asahel, “Turn aside from following me. Why should I strike you to the ground? How then could I lift up my face to Joab your brother?”

23 He refused to turn aside, so Abner struck him with the blunt end of his spear beneath the fifth rib; the spear came out behind him, and he fell and died there. Everyone who came to the place where Asahel fell and died stood still.

24 Joab and Abishai pursued Abner. The sun was going down when they reached the hill of Ammah, which lies before Giah on the way to the wilderness of Gibeon. 25 The Benjaminites rallied behind Abner, formed a single band, and stood on top of a hill.

26 Abner called to Joab, “Shall the sword devour forever? Do you not know that it will be bitter in the end? How long will you not tell the people to turn back from pursuing their brothers?”

27 Joab answered, “As God lives, if you had not spoken, the men would have kept pursuing their brothers until morning.”

28 Then Joab blew the trumpet, and all the people stopped; they no longer pursued Israel, and they ceased from fighting.

29 Abner and his men marched all night through the Arabah; they crossed the Jordan, went through all Bithron, and came to Mahanaim. 30 Joab returned from pursuing Abner and gathered all the people. Nineteen of David’s servants were missing, besides Asahel. 31 But David’s servants had struck down three hundred sixty men of Benjamin and Abner’s forces, and they died. 32 They took up Asahel and buried him in his father’s tomb in Bethlehem. Joab and his men marched all night and reached Hebron at daybreak.

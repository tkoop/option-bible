The Appointment of the Seven

1 In those days, as the disciples were increasing in number, a complaint arose from the Hellenists against the Hebrews, because their widows were being overlooked in the daily distribution. 2 The Twelve therefore summoned the whole multitude of disciples and said, “It is not right that we should forsake the word of God in order to serve tables. 3 Therefore, brothers, choose from among you seven men of good repute, full of the Spirit and of wisdom, whom we may appoint to this task. 4 But we will devote ourselves to prayer and to the ministry of the word.”

5 The proposal pleased the whole multitude, and they chose Stephen, a man full of faith and the Holy Spirit, together with Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolas, a proselyte from Antioch. 6 They set these men before the apostles, who prayed and laid their hands upon them. 7 So the word of God increased, and the number of disciples in Jerusalem multiplied greatly; even a large company of priests became obedient to the faith.

Stephen's Power and the Synagogue Dispute

8 Stephen, full of grace and power, performed great wonders and signs among the people. 9 Then certain men from the synagogue called the Synagogue of the Freedmen, as well as Cyrenians, Alexandrians, and men from Cilicia and Asia, rose up and disputed with Stephen. 10 Yet they could not withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by which he spoke.

11 Then they secretly induced men to say, “We have heard him speak blasphemous words against Moses and against God.” 12 They agitated the people, the elders, and the scribes; then they seized him and led him away to the Sanhedrin.

False Testimony Against Stephen

13 They set up false witnesses, who said, “This man does not cease to speak against this holy place and the law. 14 For we have heard him say that this Jesus of Nazareth will destroy this place and change the customs that Moses delivered to us.” 15 All who were seated in the Sanhedrin, looking intently at him, saw his face as if it were the face of an angel.

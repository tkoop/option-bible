David’s Kindness and the Insult

1 After this, Nahash king of the Ammonites died, and his son reigned in his place. 2 David said, “I will show kindness to Hanun son of Nahash, because his father showed kindness to me.” So David sent messengers to comfort him concerning his father.

3 But the princes of the Ammonites said to Hanun, “Do you think David is honoring your father by sending comforters to you? Haven’t his servants come to you to explore the land, spy it out, and overthrow it?”

4 So Hanun took David’s servants, shaved them, cut their garments off at the buttocks, and sent them away. 5 When David was told about the men, he sent messengers to meet them, because they were deeply humiliated. The king said, “Stay in Jericho until your beards have grown back, and then return.”

The Ammonites Hire Aramean Allies

6 When the Ammonites realized they had made themselves repugnant to David, Hanun and the Ammonites sent a thousand talents of silver to hire chariots and horsemen from Aram-naharaim, Aram-maacah, and Zobah. 7 So they hired thirty-two thousand chariots, along with the king of Maacah and his troops, who came and camped near Medeba. The Ammonites also gathered from their cities and came out for battle.

Joab’s Victory over Aram and Ammon

8 When David heard this, he sent Joab and the entire army of mighty men. 9 The Ammonites came out and arrayed for battle at the entrance of the city, while the kings who had come were alone in the open country. 10 When Joab saw that the battle was set against him both in front and behind, he chose some of the best men in Israel and deployed them against the Arameans. 11 He put the rest of the troops under the command of his brother Abishai, and they arrayed themselves against the Ammonites. 12 “If the Arameans are too strong for me,” he said, “then you are to rescue me; but if the Ammonites are too strong for you, then I will rescue you.” 13 “Be strong,” he continued, “and let us fight bravely for our people and for the cities of our God. The LORD will do what is good in His sight.” 14 So Joab and the troops with him advanced to fight the Arameans, and they fled before him. 15 When the Ammonites saw that the Arameans had fled, they too fled before Abishai and entered the city. Then Joab returned to Jerusalem.

David Defeats the Arameans

16 When the Arameans saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they sent messengers to bring out the Arameans who were beyond the Euphrates, with Shophach the commander of Hadadezer’s army leading them. 17 When David was informed, he gathered all Israel, crossed the Jordan, and advanced against them. David drew up his forces for battle, and the Arameans engaged him. 18 But the Arameans fled before Israel, and David killed seven thousand of their charioteers and forty thousand foot soldiers. He also killed Shophach, the commander of the army. 19 When Hadadezer’s servants saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they made peace with David and became subject to him. So the Arameans were unwilling to help the Ammonites anymore.

Jesus Heals on the Sabbath

1 One Sabbath, Jesus went to eat in the home of a prominent Pharisee, and those present were observing Him closely. 2 There, right in front of Him, stood a man afflicted with dropsy.

3 Jesus answered the legal scholars and the Pharisees by asking, “Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath, or not?”

4 But they remained silent. So He took hold of the man, healed him, and sent him away. 5 Then He asked them, “Which of you has a son or an ox that falls into a well on the Sabbath day and will not immediately pull him out?” 6 And they could not answer these things.

Teaching on Humility and Hospitality

7 Noticing how the invited guests were selecting the places of honor, He told those invited a parable.

8 “When you are invited by someone to a wedding feast, do not recline in the place of honor, in case someone more distinguished than you has been invited by him. 9 Then the host who invited you—and the other guest as well—will come and say to you, ‘Give this man your place,’ and you will begin, with shame, to occupy the last place. 10 But when you are invited, go and recline in the last place, so that when the host who invited you comes, he will say to you, ‘Friend, move up higher.’ Then you will have honor before everyone reclining with you. 11 For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted.”

12 He also said to the one who had invited Him, “When you give a luncheon or a dinner, do not invite your friends, your brothers, your relatives, or rich neighbors, in case they invite you in return and a repayment is made to you. 13 But when you give a banquet, invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, and the blind. 14 You will be blessed, because they cannot repay you; for you will be repaid at the resurrection of the righteous.”

The Parable of the Great Banquet

15 One of those reclining with Him heard this and said to Him, “Blessed is everyone who eats bread in the kingdom of God.”

16 “A certain man prepared a great banquet and invited many guests. 17 At the hour of the banquet, he sent his servant to tell those who had been invited, ‘Come, for everything is now ready.’ 18 But they all alike began to make excuses. The first said to him, ‘I have bought a field, and I must go see it; please excuse me.’ 19 Another said, ‘I have bought five yoke of oxen, and I am going to test them; please excuse me.’ 20 Still another said, ‘I have just married a wife, and therefore I cannot come.’ 21 The servant returned and reported these things to his master. Then the master of the house became angry and said to his servant, ‘Go out quickly into the streets and lanes of the city, and bring in the poor, the crippled, the blind, and the lame.’ 22 ‘Sir,’ the servant said, ‘what you ordered has been done, and there is still room.’ 23 Then the master said to the servant, ‘Go out to the roads and hedges and compel them to come in, so that my house may be filled. 24 For I tell you that none of those men who were invited will taste my banquet.’”

The Cost of Discipleship

25 Large crowds were traveling with Him. Turning, He said to them,

26 “If anyone comes to Me and does not hate his father, mother, wife, children, brothers, and sisters—yes, even his own life—he cannot be My disciple. 27 And whoever does not carry his own cross and follow Me cannot be My disciple.

28 “For which of you, intending to build a tower, does not first sit down and calculate the cost to see whether he has enough to complete it? 29 Otherwise, after laying the foundation and being unable to finish, everyone who sees it may begin to mock him, 30 saying, ‘This man began to build and was not able to finish.’

31 “Or what king, going out to wage war against another king, does not first sit down and deliberate whether he is able with ten thousand men to oppose the one coming against him with twenty thousand? 32 If he is not able, he sends a delegation while the other is still far away and asks for terms of peace. 33 So none of you who does not renounce all his possessions can be My disciple.

34 “Salt is good; but if salt has lost its flavor, with what can it be seasoned? 35 It is fit neither for the soil nor for the manure pile; people throw it out. Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear.”

The Danger of Drifting from Salvation

1 Therefore, we must pay far closer attention to what we have heard, lest we drift away. 2 For if the message declared through angels proved firm, and every transgression and disobedience received a just recompense, 3 how shall we escape if we neglect so great a salvation? It first began to be spoken by the Lord and was confirmed to us by those who heard, 4 while God Himself bore witness alongside them through signs, wonders, and various miracles, and through gifts of the Holy Spirit distributed according to His will.

The Coming World Subject to Christ, Not Angels

5 For it was not to angels that God subjected the world to come, concerning which we speak. 6 But someone somewhere testified, saying, “What is man, that You are mindful of him, or the son of man, that You care for him? 7 You made him a little lower than the angels; You crowned him with glory and honor. 8 You subjected all things under his feet.” For in subjecting everything to him, He left nothing outside his dominion. Yet now we do not see everything subjected to him.

Jesus Crowned Through Suffering

9 But we do see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels, now crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of death, so that by the grace of God He might taste death for everyone.

Christ’s Solidarity with His People

10 For it was fitting that He—for whom and through whom all things exist—should, in bringing many sons to glory, perfect the founder of their salvation through sufferings. 11 For both He who sanctifies and those who are sanctified derive from one source; therefore He is not ashamed to call them brothers, 12 saying, “I will declare Your name to My brothers; in the midst of the assembly I will sing praise to You.” 13 And again, “I will put My trust in Him.” And again, “Behold, I and the children whom God has given Me.”

14 Since, therefore, the children share in flesh and blood, He Himself likewise partook of the same nature, so that through death He might destroy the one who holds the power of death—that is, the devil— 15 and deliver all those who, throughout their lives, were enslaved by their fear of death.

16 For He certainly does not help angels, but He helps the descendants of Abraham. 17 Therefore, He had to be made like His brothers in every way, so that He might become a merciful and faithful High Priest in service to God, to make propitiation for the sins of the people. 18 For because He Himself suffered when tempted, He is able to help those who are being tempted.

Pharaoh's Officers Transgress Against Their King

1 After these events, the cupbearer and the baker of the king of Egypt transgressed against their master, Pharaoh. 2 Pharaoh grew furious with two of his officials—the chief cupbearer and the chief baker— 3 and delivered them into custody in the residence of the captain of the guard, the very prison where Joseph was bound. 4 The captain of the guard entrusted Joseph with their care, and he attended them; consequently, they remained incarcerated for an extended period. 5 Subsequently, both men—the cupbearer and the baker of Egypt's king, imprisoned in the dungeon—dreamed a dream during the same night, each dream possessing a distinctive interpretation.

Joseph Notices Their Distress

6 When Joseph entered their presence in the morning, he perceived their despondency. 7 So he inquired of Pharaoh's officials confined with him in his master's house, "Why do your faces appear so downcast today?"

8 They answered, "We have dreamed a dream, but no one can interpret it."

Joseph replied, "Do not interpretations belong to God? Pray recount the dreams to me."

The Cupbearer's Dream

9 Thereupon the chief cupbearer related his dream to Joseph: "In my dream, behold, a vine stood before me. 10 The vine bore three branches; as it budded, its blossoms emerged, and its clusters matured into grapes. 11 Pharaoh's cup rested in my hand; I gathered the grapes, extracted their juice into Pharaoh's cup, and presented the cup to Pharaoh."

12 Joseph said to him, "This is its interpretation: the three branches signify three days. 13 Within three days Pharaoh will restore you to favor and reinstate you in your position; then you shall place Pharaoh's cup in his hand, as you did before when you were his cupbearer."

14 "But when things go well for you, please remember me and show me kindness by mentioning me to Pharaoh, so that he may bring me out of this house. 15 For I was indeed abducted from the land of the Hebrews, and even here I have committed no offense warranting my confinement in this pit."

The Baker's Dream

16 When the chief baker perceived that the interpretation was propitious, he said to Joseph, "I too dreamed, and behold, three baskets of fine bread rested upon my head. 17 In the uppermost basket were all manner of baked delicacies for Pharaoh, the product of a baker's artistry, while birds devoured them from the basket atop my head."

18 Joseph answered, "This is its interpretation: the three baskets represent three days. 19 Within three days Pharaoh will condemn you and have you hanged on a tree, and the birds will eat your flesh from you."

The Dreams Come to Pass

20 On the third day, Pharaoh's birthday, he hosted a banquet for all his servants; in their presence he rendered judgment upon the chief cupbearer and the chief baker. 21 He reinstated the chief cupbearer in his office, and the cup was restored to Pharaoh's hand. 22 But the chief baker he hanged, precisely as Joseph had interpreted for them. 23 Nevertheless, the chief cupbearer did not remember Joseph; he forgot him.

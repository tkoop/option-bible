Jonathan Intercedes for David

1 Saul communicated to his son Jonathan and to all his courtiers his command that David be put to death; nevertheless, Jonathan, Saul’s son, cherished David with profound affection.

2 Jonathan warned David, “My father Saul is intent upon killing you. Therefore, exercise vigilance at daybreak: remain in a concealed location and hide yourself.” 3 He added, “I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where you are, and I will discourse with my father concerning you. Then I will observe the outcome and report it to you.”

4 Jonathan extolled David to Saul his father, saying, “Let not the king sin against his servant David, for he has committed no sin against you, and his deeds have redounded greatly to your benefit. 5 He staked his life and struck down the Philistine, and the LORD wrought a great deliverance for all Israel. You witnessed it and rejoiced. Why then would you sin by shedding innocent blood and slaying David without cause?”

6 Saul heeded Jonathan’s counsel and swore by the LORD: “As the LORD lives, David shall not be executed.”

7 Jonathan summoned David and rehearsed all these words to him. Then Jonathan escorted David into Saul’s presence, and David ministered before him as he had formerly.

Saul Violates His Oath

8 Once more war erupted, and David went forth and fought the Philistines. He inflicted upon them a crushing defeat, and they fled before him.

9 Moreover, an evil spirit from the LORD afflicted Saul while he sat in his house with his spear in his hand, while David played the lyre.

10 Saul sought to transfix David against the wall with the spear, but David evaded Saul; the spear struck the wall, and David fled and escaped that night.

Michal’s Subterfuge Preserves David

11 Saul dispatched messengers to David’s house to surveil him and to kill him in the morning. Michal, David’s wife, alerted him, “If you do not preserve your life tonight, tomorrow you will be executed.” 12 Accordingly, Michal lowered David through the window, and he fled and escaped.

13 Michal took the household idol and laid it in the bed, placed a goat-hair bolster at its head, and covered it with a garment.

14 When Saul sent messengers to seize David, she claimed, “He is ill.”

15 Saul then sent messengers to inspect David, commanding, “Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may put him to death.”

16 When the messengers entered, they found the household idol in the bed, with the goat-hair bolster at its head.

17 Saul said to Michal, “Why have you deceived me in this manner and released my enemy, so that he has escaped?”

Michal answered Saul, “He said to me, ‘Let me go! Why should I kill you?’”

Saul Pursues David to Naioth

18 David fled and escaped, and he went to Samuel at Ramah and recounted to him everything Saul had done to him. Then he and Samuel departed and took up residence in Naioth.

19 It was reported to Saul, “David is in Naioth in Ramah.”

20 Saul sent messengers to apprehend David, but when they observed the band of prophets prophesying, with Samuel presiding over them, the Spirit of God descended upon them, and they too prophesied.

21 When this was reported to Saul, he dispatched another contingent of messengers, and they likewise prophesied. Saul sent a third contingent of thirty men, and they also prophesied.

22 Then he himself traveled to Ramah and arrived at the great cistern in Secu. He inquired, “Where are Samuel and David?”

Someone replied, “They are in Naioth in Ramah.”

23 So he proceeded to Naioth in Ramah, and the Spirit of God came upon him as well. He continued onward, prophesying as he went, until he reached Naioth in Ramah.

24 He likewise stripped off his garments and prophesied before Samuel, and he lay naked throughout that day and that night. Consequently, people say, “Is Saul also among the prophets?”

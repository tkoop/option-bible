David Remains in Jerusalem While Israel Goes to War

1 In the spring, when kings march out to war, David sent Joab with his officers and all Israel. They ravaged the Ammonites and besieged Rabbah, but David remained in Jerusalem.

David's Adultery with Bathsheba

2 One evening David rose from his bed and walked on the roof of the royal palace. From the roof he saw a woman bathing, and she was exceedingly beautiful. 3 David sent someone to inquire about her, and he was told, "Is this not Bathsheba, daughter of Eliam and wife of Uriah the Hittite?" 4 David sent messengers to bring her; when she came to him, he slept with her. She had purified herself from her uncleanness, and then she returned home. 5 The woman conceived and sent word to David: "I am pregnant."

Uriah's Integrity and David's Deception

6 David sent word to Joab: "Send me Uriah the Hittite." So Joab sent Uriah to David. 7 When Uriah arrived, David asked how Joab was faring, how the troops were faring, and how the war was progressing.

8 Then David said to Uriah, "Go home and refresh yourself." Uriah left the palace, and a gift from the king followed him. 9 But Uriah slept at the entrance of the royal palace with all the king's servants; he did not go down to his house.

10 When David learned that Uriah had not gone down to his house, he asked Uriah, "Have you not just come from a campaign? Why did you not go home?"

11 Uriah answered David, "The ark and Israel and Judah are dwelling in shelters, my lord Joab and your servants are camping in the open field, and should I then go to my house to eat and drink and lie with my wife? As surely as you live and as your soul lives, I will not do such a thing."

12 David said to Uriah, "Stay here today, and tomorrow I will send you back." So Uriah remained in Jerusalem that day and the next. 13 David invited him to eat and drink in his presence and made him drunk. In the evening Uriah went out to lie on his bed among the king's servants, but he did not go down to his house.

Uriah's Death and David's Response

14 In the morning David wrote a letter to Joab and sent it by Uriah. 15 In the letter he wrote, "Station Uriah in the front line where the fighting is fiercest, then fall back so that he may be struck down and die."

16 While Joab was besieging the city, he assigned Uriah to a place where he knew valiant men were. 17 The men of the city came out and fought Joab; some of David's servants fell, and Uriah the Hittite died as well.

18 Joab sent a messenger to report to David everything that had happened in the battle. 19 He instructed the messenger, "When you have finished reporting to the king all the details of the battle, 20 if the king grows angry and asks, 'Why did you approach the city so closely to fight? Did you not realize they would shoot from the wall?' 21 then remind him, 'Who struck down Abimelech son of Jerubbesheth? Was it not a woman who dropped an upper millstone on him from the wall, so that he died at Thebez? Why did you go near the wall?' and then say, 'Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead also.'"

22 The messenger departed, came to David, and reported everything Joab had sent him to say. 23 The messenger said to David, "The men overpowered us and came out against us in the open country, but we drove them back to the entrance of the gate. 24 The archers shot at your servants from the wall, and some of the king's servants died; your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead as well."

25 David said to the messenger, "Tell Joab, 'Do not be distressed by this matter, for the sword devours one as well as another. Press the attack against the city and destroy it.' Encourage him."

26 When Uriah's wife heard that her husband was dead, she mourned for him. 27 After the mourning ended, David sent for her and brought her to his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son. But the thing David had done displeased the LORD.

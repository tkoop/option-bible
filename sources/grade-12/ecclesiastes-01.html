The Teacher’s Opening Reflections on Transience

1 These are the words of the Teacher, the son of David, who reigned as king in Jerusalem. 2 “Utter futility,” says the Teacher, “utter futility; everything is futile.”

The Monotony of Nature and Human Endeavor

3 What profit does a person derive from all the toil he expends on earth? 4 One generation passes away and another arises, yet the earth abides in perpetuity. 5 The sun rises and the sun sets, hastening to the place from which it rises again. 6 The wind proceeds southward and veers northward; circling, ever circling, it returns upon its circuits. 7 All the rivers run into the sea, yet the sea is never filled; to the place from which the rivers flow, they return to flow again. 8 All things are wearisome beyond utterance: the eye is never satiated with seeing, nor the ear contented with hearing. 9 What has been is what will be, and what has been accomplished is what will be accomplished; there is nothing new on earth. 10 Is there anything of which one may say, “Behold, this is new”? It has already existed in the ages that preceded us. 11 There is no remembrance of former things, and even those yet to come will leave no remembrance among those who succeed them.

The Teacher’s Royal Quest for Wisdom

12 I, the Teacher, was king over Israel in Jerusalem. 13 I devoted my heart to seek and to search out by wisdom all that is done in the world. What a burdensome occupation God has assigned to humanity, to be exercised therein! 14 I have observed all the works that are done on earth, and indeed all is futile, an empty pursuit. 15 What is crooked cannot be made straight, and what is deficient cannot be reckoned. 16 I said in my heart, “Look, I have magnified and increased wisdom beyond all who preceded me in Jerusalem, and my heart has perceived much wisdom and knowledge.” 17 Then I set my heart to know wisdom, and likewise to know madness and folly. I perceived that this also is an empty pursuit. 18 For in abundant wisdom is abundant vexation, and he who augments knowledge augments sorrow.

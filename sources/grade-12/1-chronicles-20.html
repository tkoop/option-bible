The Ammonite Campaign and the Fall of Rabbah

1 When the year had turned and the customary season for kings to go to war had arrived, Joab led forth the army and devastated the territory of the Ammonites. He advanced upon Rabbah, encircled the city, and laid siege to it, whereas David remained in Jerusalem; Joab then assaulted Rabbah and demolished it. 2 David took the crown from the head of their king—it weighed a talent of gold, was set with a precious stone, and was placed upon David’s head—and he also seized an enormous quantity of spoil from the city. 3 He brought out the people who dwelt there and subjected them to labor with saws, iron picks, and axes; he imposed an identical fate upon every Ammonite city. Thereafter David and all his forces returned to Jerusalem.

Philistine Conflicts at Gezer

4 Afterward war broke out with the Philistines at Gezer. At that time Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Sippai, a descendant of the Rephaim, and the Philistines were subdued. 5 In a further engagement with the Philistines, Elhanan son of Jair killed Lahmi, the brother of Goliath the Gittite, whose spear shaft was like a weaver’s beam.

The Giant of Gath

6 Still another battle was fought at Gath, where there was a man of prodigious stature who had six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot—twenty-four in all—and who was likewise descended from Rapha. 7 Because he taunted Israel, Jonathan, son of Shimea—David’s brother—killed him. 8 These descendants of Rapha in Gath therefore perished by the hands of David and his servants.

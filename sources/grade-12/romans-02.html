The Impartiality of God’s Judgment

1 Therefore you are without excuse, O man—every one of you who passes judgment—for in judging another you condemn yourself, since you who judge practice the very same things. 2 Now we know that God’s judgment against those who practice such things is based on truth.

3 Do you suppose, O man—you who judge those who practice such things and yet do them yourself—that you will escape God’s judgment? 4 Or do you despise the riches of His kindness, forbearance, and patience, not realizing that God’s kindness is meant to lead you to repentance?

5 But because of your stubbornness and unrepentant heart, you are storing up wrath for yourself in the day of wrath, when God’s righteous judgment will be revealed. 6 He will repay each person according to his deeds:

7 to those who by patient endurance in doing good seek glory, honor, and immortality, He will give eternal life; 8 but to those who are self-seeking and who disobey the truth while obeying unrighteousness, there will be wrath and fury.

9 There will be tribulation and distress for every human being who does evil—to the Jew first and also to the Greek— 10 but glory, honor, and peace for everyone who does good, to the Jew first and also to the Greek. 11 For God does not show favoritism.

The Law, Conscience, and the Day of Judgment

12 All who sin apart from the law will also perish apart from the law, and all who sin under the law will be judged by the law. 13 For it is not the hearers of the law who are righteous before God, but the doers of the law who will be justified.

14 Indeed, when Gentiles, who do not have the law, do by nature what the law requires, they are a law to themselves, even though they do not have the law. 15 They show that the work of the law is written on their hearts, while their conscience bears witness and their thoughts either accuse or excuse them.

16 This will take place on the day when, according to my gospel, God judges the hidden things of people through Jesus Christ.

Boasting in the Law While Breaking It

17 Now if you call yourself a Jew and rely on the law and boast in God, 18 and if you know His will and approve what is excellent because you are instructed from the law, 19 and if you are convinced that you yourself are a guide to the blind and a light to those in darkness, 20 an instructor of the foolish and a teacher of infants, possessing in the law the embodiment of knowledge and truth—

21 then you, who teach another, do you not teach yourself? You who preach against stealing, do you steal? 22 You who say, “Do not commit adultery,” do you commit adultery? You who abhor idols, do you rob temples?

23 You who boast in the law, do you dishonor God by breaking the law? 24 For, as it is written, “The name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you.”

True Circumcision of the Heart

25 Circumcision has value if you obey the law, but if you are a breaker of the law, your circumcision becomes uncircumcision. 26 If, then, an uncircumcised man keeps the righteous requirements of the law, will not his uncircumcision be regarded as circumcision?

27 The one who is physically uncircumcised yet keeps the law will condemn you who, despite having the written code and circumcision, are a transgressor of the law.

28 For a person is not a Jew because he appears to be one outwardly, nor is circumcision merely an outward, fleshly mark. 29 Rather, a person is a Jew inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart, by the Spirit and not by the letter. Such a person’s praise comes not from people but from God.

The King’s Troubling Dream

1 In the second year of Nebuchadnezzar’s reign, Nebuchadnezzar had dreams that left his spirit deeply agitated, and sleep abandoned him. 2 The king commanded that the magicians, enchanters, sorcerers, and Chaldeans be summoned to tell him what he had dreamed. When they arrived, they stood before the king. 3 The king said to them, “I have had a dream, and my spirit is troubled until I understand it.”

The Chaldeans’ Failure and the King’s Ultimatum

4 The Chaldeans answered the king in Aramaic: “Long live the king! Tell your servants the dream, and we will declare its interpretation.”

5 The king replied to the Chaldeans, “My decision is final: if you do not make the dream known to me, together with its interpretation, you will be torn to pieces and your houses will be reduced to rubble. 6 But if you declare the dream and its interpretation, you will receive from me gifts, rewards, and great honor. Therefore, tell me the dream and its interpretation.”

7 They answered a second time, “Let the king tell his servants the dream, and we will declare its interpretation.”

8 The king answered, “I know with certainty that you are deliberately stalling, because you see that my decision is final. 9 If you do not make the dream known to me, one decree applies to all of you: you have colluded to speak deceitful and corrupt words before me until circumstances change. So tell me the dream, and then I will know that you can declare its interpretation.”

10 The Chaldeans answered the king, “No mortal on earth can do what the king demands, for no great and powerful king has ever asked such a thing of any magician, enchanter, or Chaldean. 11 What the king asks is too difficult; no one can reveal it to him except the gods, whose dwelling is not with mortals.”

12 At this the king became furious and enraged, and he commanded that all the wise men of Babylon be destroyed. 13 So the decree was issued, and the wise men were about to be killed; they sought out Daniel and his companions to execute them.

Daniel Seeks God’s Revelation

14 Then Daniel replied with prudence and discretion to Arioch, the captain of the king’s guard, who had gone out to execute the wise men of Babylon. 15 He asked Arioch, the king’s captain, “Why is the king’s decree so severe?” Then Arioch explained the matter to Daniel. 16 So Daniel went in and asked the king to grant him time, promising that he would declare the interpretation to the king.

17 Then Daniel went to his house and explained the matter to his companions—Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah—and urged them to seek mercy from the God of heaven concerning this mystery, so that Daniel and his companions would not be destroyed along with the rest of Babylon’s wise men. 18 19 Then the mystery was revealed to Daniel in a night vision, and Daniel blessed the God of heaven.

20 Daniel answered and said, “Blessed be the name of God from age to age, for wisdom and power belong to Him. 21 He changes the times and seasons; He removes kings and establishes kings. He gives wisdom to the wise and knowledge to those who have understanding. 22 He reveals what is deep and hidden; He knows what lies in darkness, and light dwells with Him. 23 To You, O God of my fathers, I give thanks and praise, because You have given me wisdom and power and have now revealed to me what we requested of You—You have made known to us the king’s matter.”

24 Therefore Daniel went to Arioch, whom the king had appointed to destroy Babylon’s wise men. He went and said to him, “Do not destroy Babylon’s wise men. Bring me before the king, and I will declare the interpretation to the king.”

25 Then Arioch hurriedly brought Daniel before the king and said to him, “I have found a man among the exiles from Judah who will declare the interpretation to the king.”

26 The king answered Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, “Are you able to make known to me the dream I saw and its interpretation?”

27 Daniel answered before the king, “No wise man, enchanter, magician, or astrologer can reveal to the king the mystery he has asked about. 28 But there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries, and He has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will happen in the latter days. Your dream and the visions of your head as you lay on your bed are these: 29 As for you, O king, while you lay on your bed, your thoughts turned to what would happen after this, and the revealer of mysteries has made known to you what is to be. 30 As for me, this mystery has been revealed to me, not because I possess greater wisdom than anyone alive, but so that the interpretation may be made known to the king, and that you may understand the thoughts of your mind.”

The Dream and Its Interpretation

31 “You, O king, were watching, and behold, there was a great statue. This statue was enormous and dazzling, and it stood before you; its appearance was terrifying. 32 The head of the statue was of fine gold; its chest and arms were of silver; its belly and thighs were of bronze; 33 its legs were of iron; its feet were partly of iron and partly of clay. 34 As you watched, a stone was cut out, not by human hands, and it struck the statue on its feet of iron and clay and crushed them. 35 Then the iron, clay, bronze, silver, and gold were crushed together and became like chaff from the summer threshing floors; the wind carried them away, and no trace of them could be found. But the stone that struck the statue became a great mountain and filled the whole earth.

36 “This is the dream. Now we will tell the king its interpretation. 37 You, O king, are king of kings, to whom the God of heaven has given kingdom, power, strength, and glory. 38 Wherever people dwell, along with the beasts of the field and the birds of the heavens, He has placed them in your hand and has made you ruler over them all—you are the head of gold. 39 After you, another kingdom will arise, inferior to yours; then a third kingdom, of bronze, which will rule over all the earth. 40 Finally, a fourth kingdom will be strong as iron, because iron crushes and shatters everything; and like iron that pulverizes, it will crush and break all these.

41 “And as you saw the feet and toes—partly of potter’s clay and partly of iron—so the kingdom will be divided. Yet it will retain some of the firmness of iron, just as you saw iron mixed with clay. 42 As the toes were partly iron and partly clay, so the kingdom will be partly strong and partly brittle. 43 As you saw iron mixed with clay, so people will mingle with one another through marriage, but they will not hold together, just as iron does not mix with clay. 44 In the days of those kings, the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that will never be destroyed, and its sovereignty will not be transferred to another people. It will crush all those kingdoms and bring them to an end, and it will stand forever. 45 This is the meaning of the stone you saw cut from the mountain without human hands, which crushed the iron, bronze, clay, silver, and gold. The great God has made known to the king what will happen after this. The dream is certain, and its interpretation is trustworthy.”

46 Then King Nebuchadnezzar fell on his face and paid homage to Daniel, and he commanded that an offering and incense be presented to him.

47 The king answered Daniel, “Truly, your God is the God of gods and the Lord of kings and the revealer of mysteries, for you were able to reveal this mystery.”

48 Then the king gave Daniel great honors and many lavish gifts, and he appointed him ruler over the entire province of Babylon and chief prefect over all its wise men. 49 At Daniel’s request, the king appointed Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego to administer the province of Babylon, while Daniel remained at the king’s court.

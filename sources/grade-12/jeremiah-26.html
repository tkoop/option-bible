A Prophetic Warning in the Temple Court

1 At the commencement of the reign of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah, this message came from the LORD:

2 “This is what the LORD says: Stand in the courtyard of the LORD’s house and speak to all the cities of Judah that come to worship in the LORD’s house; deliver every word that I command you to speak to them, and do not omit a single word. 3 Perhaps they will listen and turn back—each from his wicked way—so that I may relent concerning the disaster I am planning to bring upon them because of the wickedness of their deeds. 4 And you are to say to them, ‘This is what the LORD says: If you do not listen to Me and walk in My law, which I have set before you, 5 and if you do not heed the words of My servants the prophets, whom I have been sending to you again and again—though you have not listened— 6 then I will make this house like Shiloh, and I will make this city an object of cursing among all the nations of the earth.’”

Jeremiah Seized and Accused

7 Now the priests, the prophets, and all the people heard Jeremiah speaking these words in the house of the LORD. 8 And when Jeremiah had finished speaking all that the LORD had commanded him to say to all the people, the priests, the prophets, and all the people seized him, crying, “You must die!”

9 “Why have you prophesied in the name of the LORD, declaring that this house will be like Shiloh and this city will be laid waste and uninhabited?” And all the people gathered around Jeremiah in the house of the LORD.

10 When the officials of Judah heard of these things, they came up from the king’s palace to the house of the LORD and sat at the entrance of the New Gate of the LORD’s house.

11 Then the priests and prophets said to the officials and all the people, “This man deserves to die, for he has prophesied against this city, as you have heard with your own ears.”

Jeremiah’s Defense Before the Officials

12 Then Jeremiah said to all the officials and all the people, “The LORD sent me to prophesy against this house and against this city all the words that you have heard. 13 So now, amend your ways and your deeds, and obey the voice of the LORD your God, so that He may relent concerning the disaster He has pronounced against you. 14 As for me, I am in your hands; do with me whatever you think is good and right. 15 But know for certain that if you put me to death, you will bring innocent blood upon yourselves, upon this city, and upon its inhabitants; for in truth the LORD has sent me to speak all these words in your hearing.”

16 Then the officials and all the people said to the priests and prophets, “This man does not deserve to die, for he has spoken to us in the name of the LORD our God.”

Ancient Precedent, Uriah’s Fate, and Jeremiah’s Protection

17 Some of the elders of the land then rose and said to the whole assembly, “18 Micah the Moreshite prophesied in the days of Hezekiah king of Judah, saying, ‘This is what the LORD of Hosts says: Zion will be plowed like a field, Jerusalem will become a heap of ruins, and the temple mount will become a forested hill.’ 19 Did Hezekiah king of Judah or anyone in Judah put him to death? Did they not fear the LORD and implore His favor, so that the LORD relented concerning the disaster He had pronounced against them? Yet we are bringing a great evil upon ourselves.”

20 Moreover, there was a man named Uriah son of Shemaiah from Kiriath-jearim who prophesied in the name of the LORD; he prophesied against this city and against this land in accordance with all the words of Jeremiah. 21 When King Jehoiakim and all his mighty men and officials heard his words, the king sought to put him to death; but when Uriah heard of it, he was afraid and fled to Egypt. 22 Then King Jehoiakim sent men to Egypt—Elnathan son of Achbor, along with some other men. 23 They brought Uriah out of Egypt and delivered him to King Jehoiakim, who struck him down with the sword and cast his corpse into the burial place of the common people.

24 Nevertheless, Ahikam son of Shaphan supported Jeremiah, so that he was not handed over to the people to be put to death.

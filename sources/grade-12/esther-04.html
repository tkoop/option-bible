Mordecai's Public Mourning

1 When Mordecai learned the full extent of what had transpired, he tore his garments, donned sackcloth and ashes, and proceeded into the city's central thoroughfare, uttering a loud and bitter lamentation. 2 He advanced only to the king's gate, since no one clad in sackcloth was permitted to enter it. 3 Throughout every province, wherever the royal command and decree reached, the Jews sank into profound mourning—fasting, weeping, and wailing—while many reclined in sackcloth and ashes.

Esther Learns of the Edict

4 Upon receiving this report, Queen Esther was seized by acute anguish. She dispatched garments for Mordecai to wear in place of his sackcloth, but he refused them. 5 Consequently, Esther summoned Hathach, one of the eunuchs whom the king had appointed to serve her, and directed him to ascertain from Mordecai the cause and meaning of these events. 6 Hathach then went out to Mordecai in the open square before the king's gate.

7 Mordecai disclosed to him all that had befallen him, including the precise sum of silver Haman had pledged to deposit into the royal treasuries in exchange for the annihilation of the Jews. 8 He also entrusted Hathach with a copy of the written decree promulgated in Susa for their destruction, instructing him to exhibit it to Esther, explicate its implications, and command her to approach the king, supplicate his favor, and intercede on behalf of her people. 9 Hathach returned and relayed Mordecai's message to Esther.

Esther's Reluctance to Approach the King

10 Then Esther instructed Hathach to convey this reply to Mordecai: 11 "All the king's servants and the subjects of every province recognize that any man or woman who enters the king's inner court unsummoned incurs one immutable penalty—death—unless the king extends the golden scepter to that person, thereby granting life. As for me, I have not been summoned to the king these thirty days."

12 Hathach reported these words to Mordecai.

Mordecai's Challenge and Esther's Resolve

13 Mordecai responded with this message: "Do not think to yourself that you will find sanctuary in the royal palace any more than the rest of the Jews. 14 For if you persist in silence at this critical juncture, relief and deliverance will arise for the Jews from another quarter, yet you and your father's household will perish. Moreover, who can discern whether you have attained royal authority for a moment precisely like this?"

15 Esther then directed them to answer Mordecai: 16 "Go, assemble every Jew in Susa, and observe a fast on my behalf; abstain from food and drink for three days and nights. I and my attendants will fast in the same manner. Thereafter, I shall approach the king, notwithstanding that it contravenes the law; and if it costs me my life, so be it."

17 Mordecai departed and executed all of Esther's instructions.

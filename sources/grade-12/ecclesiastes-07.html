A Good Name and the House of Mourning

1 A good name is more valuable than costly perfume, and the day of one's death surpasses the day of one's birth. 2 It is better to enter a house of mourning than a house of feasting, because death is the common destiny of all humanity, and the living should take this truth to heart.

3 Grief surpasses laughter, for a sorrowful countenance can refine the heart. 4 The wise heart gravitates toward the house of mourning, whereas the foolish heart gravitates toward the house of pleasure.

Listening to Wisdom and Living with Patience

5 It is better to heed a wise person's reproof than to listen to the songs of fools. 6 For as thorns crackle beneath a pot, so is the laughter of fools—and that too is futility.

7 Surely oppression can drive a wise person to madness, and a bribe corrupts the heart. 8 The conclusion of a matter is better than its commencement, and patience is superior to pride.

9 Do not be quick to anger in your spirit, for anger settles in the lap of fools. 10 Do not ask, "Why were former days better than these?" for such a question does not spring from wisdom.

11 Wisdom, like an inheritance, is good and advantageous to those who see the sun. 12 For wisdom is a shelter, as money is a shelter, but the advantage of knowledge is that wisdom preserves the life of its possessor.

God's Work and Human Limitations

13 Consider the work of God: who can straighten what He has bent? 14 In the day of prosperity be joyful, but in the day of adversity reflect on this: God has made one alongside the other, so that a person cannot discover what will come after him.

15 In my futile life I have seen everything: a righteous person perishing in his righteousness, and a wicked person prolonging his life in his wickedness. 16 Do not be excessively righteous, and do not make yourself too wise. Why should you bring ruin upon yourself? 17 Do not be excessively wicked, and do not be a fool. Why should you die before your time? 18 It is good to grasp the one and not let the other slip from your hand, for the one who fears God will heed both warnings.

The Search for Wisdom

19 Wisdom empowers the wise person more than ten rulers in a city. 20 Surely there is no righteous person on earth who does good and never sins.

21 Do not give heed to every word people say, lest you hear your servant cursing you. 22 For you know in your heart that you yourself have cursed others many times.

23 All this I tested by wisdom, saying, "I will be wise," but it remained beyond me. 24 What exists is far off and profoundly deep. Who can discover it?

25 I set my mind to know, to investigate, and to seek out wisdom and the scheme of things, and to understand the folly of wickedness and the madness of folly. 26 I found something more bitter than death: the woman whose heart is snares and nets, whose hands are chains. The man who pleases God will escape her, but the sinner will be ensnared by her.

27 "Behold, this is what I have found," says the Teacher, "adding one thing to another to find the sum." 28 While my soul was still searching but not finding, I found one upright man among a thousand, but not one upright woman among them all. 29 Behold, I have found only this: God made mankind upright, but they have sought out many schemes.

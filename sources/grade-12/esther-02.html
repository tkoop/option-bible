The Search for a New Queen

1 After these events, King Ahasuerus's wrath had subsided; once his anger cooled, he remembered Vashti—both what she had done and the decree issued against her. 2 Then the king's personal attendants proposed, "Let a search be made for beautiful young virgins for the king. 3 Let the king appoint commissioners in every province of his realm to gather all these beautiful young women into the harem at the citadel of Susa. Place them under the care of Hegai, the king's eunuch who oversees the women, and let beauty treatments be provided for them. 4 Then let the young woman who pleases the king be queen in Vashti's place." This counsel pleased the king, and he put it into effect.

Mordecai and Esther

5 Now in the citadel of Susa there was a Jew named Mordecai son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjamite, 6 who had been exiled from Jerusalem with the captives taken with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had carried away. 7 Mordecai had raised his cousin Hadassah—that is, Esther—because she had neither father nor mother. The young woman was lovely in form and beautiful in appearance, and when her parents died, Mordecai adopted her as his own daughter. 8 When the king's order and edict had been proclaimed, many young women were brought to the citadel of Susa and placed under Hegai's supervision. Esther, too, was taken into the king's palace and entrusted to Hegai, who was in charge of the women. 9 She pleased him and won his favor, so he promptly provided her with beauty treatments and special food. He assigned her seven attendants chosen from the king's palace, moved her and her attendants to the best quarters in the harem, and treated them better than the rest. 10 Esther did not disclose her people or her family background, because Mordecai had commanded her not to. 11 Every day Mordecai walked back and forth in front of the courtyard of the harem to learn how Esther was and what was being done to her.

Esther's Preparation and Rise

12 Before a young woman's turn came to go in to the king, she had to complete twelve months of beauty treatments prescribed for the women: six months with oil of myrrh and six months with perfumes and cosmetics. 13 When she went to the king, she was given whatever she requested to take with her from the women's quarters to the king's palace. 14 She would go in the evening and return in the morning to a second set of women's quarters, under the care of Shaashgaz, the king's eunuch in charge of the concubines. She would not return to the king unless he was pleased with her and summoned her by name. 15 When Esther's turn came—she was the daughter of Abihail, Mordecai's uncle, whom Mordecai had taken as his own daughter—she requested nothing except what Hegai, the king's eunuch in charge of the women, advised. And Esther won the favor of everyone who saw her. 16 She was brought to King Ahasuerus in his royal residence in the tenth month, the month of Tebeth, in the seventh year of his reign. 17 The king loved Esther more than all the other women, and she found greater favor and kindness in his sight than any of the other virgins, so he set the royal crown on her head and made her queen instead of Vashti. 18 Then the king gave a great banquet—Esther's banquet—for all his nobles and officials. He proclaimed a holiday throughout the provinces and distributed gifts with royal generosity.

19 When the virgins were gathered a second time, Mordecai was sitting at the king's gate. 20 Esther still had not revealed her family background or her people, just as Mordecai had instructed her; she continued to follow Mordecai's instructions as she had when he was raising her.

Mordecai at the King's Gate

21 During the days when Mordecai was sitting at the king's gate, Bigthan and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs who guarded the threshold, became enraged and conspired to assassinate King Ahasuerus. 22 The matter came to Mordecai's knowledge, and he told Queen Esther; Esther then reported it to the king in Mordecai's name. 23 When the report was investigated and found true, the two men were hanged on a gallows, and the incident was recorded in the book of the chronicles in the king's presence.

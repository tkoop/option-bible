Paul’s Second Visit to Jerusalem

1 Fourteen years later, I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, taking Titus with me. 2 I went up because of a revelation and laid before them the gospel I preach among the Gentiles. I did so privately, however, to those who seemed to be leaders, in case I was running, or had run, my race in vain. 3 Yet even Titus, who was with me, was not compelled to be circumcised, though he is a Greek.

4 This matter arose because of false brothers who had been secretly admitted; they slipped in to spy out the freedom we have in Christ Jesus, intending to enslave us. 5 We did not yield to them in submission for even an hour, so that the truth of the gospel would remain with you.

6 But as for those who seemed to be important—whatever they were makes no difference to me, since God does not show favoritism—those men added nothing to my message. 7 On the contrary, they saw that I had been entrusted with the gospel for the uncircumcised, just as Peter had been entrusted with it for the circumcised. 8 For He who worked in Peter for his apostleship to the circumcised also worked in me for my ministry to the Gentiles.

9 When James, Cephas, and John—those reputed to be pillars—recognized the grace given to me, they gave Barnabas and me the right hand of fellowship. They agreed that we should go to the Gentiles and they to the circumcised. 10 They asked only that we remember the poor, the very thing I was already eager to do.

Paul Confronts Peter in Antioch

11 When Cephas came to Antioch, I opposed him to his face, because he stood condemned. 12 Before certain men came from James, he had been eating with the Gentiles; but when they arrived, he drew back and separated himself, fearing those who belonged to the circumcision. 13 The other Jews joined him in this hypocrisy, so that even Barnabas was led astray by their hypocrisy.

14 But when I saw that they were not walking in line with the truth of the gospel, I said to Cephas in front of them all, “If you, though a Jew, live like a Gentile and not like a Jew, how can you compel the Gentiles to live like Jews?”

Justification by Faith Rather Than Law

15 We are Jews by birth, not Gentile sinners. 16 Yet we know that a person is not justified by works of the law but through faith in Jesus Christ. So we too have believed in Christ Jesus, so that we may be justified by faith in Christ and not by works of the law, because by works of the law no one will be justified.

17 But if, while seeking to be justified in Christ, we ourselves are found to be sinners, does that make Christ a servant of sin? Absolutely not! 18 For if I rebuild what I tore down, I prove myself to be a transgressor.

19 For through the law I died to the law, so that I might live to God. 20 I have been crucified with Christ. I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. The life I now live in the flesh, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself for me. 21 I do not nullify the grace of God, for if righteousness comes through the law, Christ died for nothing.

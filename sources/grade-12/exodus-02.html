The Birth and Preservation of Moses

1 A man of the Levitical house took a Levite woman in marriage; 2 she conceived and bore a son. Perceiving that he was an unusually fine child, she concealed him for three months.

3 When concealment was no longer possible, she prepared a basket of papyrus reeds, coated it with tar and pitch, laid the infant within, and set it among the reeds along the bank of the Nile.

4 His sister stood at a remove to see what would become of him.

5 Pharaoh’s daughter went down to bathe in the Nile, attended by her maidens walking along the riverbank. Observing the basket among the reeds, she dispatched her handmaid to fetch it.

6 She opened it and saw the child; the boy was weeping, and she pitied him. “This is surely one of the Hebrew children,” she remarked.

7 His sister then inquired of Pharaoh’s daughter, “Shall I go and summon a Hebrew woman to nurse the child for you?”

8 “Go,” she answered. The girl accordingly went and brought the child’s mother.

9 Pharaoh’s daughter instructed her, “Take this child and nurse him for me, and I will compensate you.” The woman therefore took the child and nursed him.

10 When the child had matured, she presented him to Pharaoh’s daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, declaring, “I drew him out of the water.”

Moses Intervenes for His People

11 In those days, after Moses had attained manhood, he went out among his fellow Israelites and witnessed their forced labor. He observed an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his own kinsmen.

12 After glancing in every direction and seeing no one, he struck the Egyptian down and hid his body in the sand.

13 The following day he went out again and discovered two Hebrews in contention. He challenged the aggressor, “Why are you striking your fellow Hebrew?”

14 The man retorted, “Who made you a ruler and judge over us? Do you mean to kill me as you killed the Egyptian?”

Moses then grew afraid and thought, “Surely my act is now known.”

15 When Pharaoh learned of the affair, he attempted to have Moses killed. Moses, however, fled from Pharaoh and took up residence in Midian, where he seated himself beside a well.

Moses Finds Refuge in Midian

16 The priest of Midian had seven daughters, who came to draw water and fill the troughs to water their father’s flock.

17 Some shepherds came and drove them away, but Moses rose, defended them, and watered their flock.

18 When the young women returned to Reuel, their father, he asked, “Why have you returned so early today?”

19 They replied, “An Egyptian rescued us from the shepherds; he even drew water for us and watered the flock.”

20 He asked his daughters, “Where is he? Why did you abandon him? Invite him to eat with us.”

21 Moses agreed to stay with the man, who gave his daughter Zipporah to Moses in marriage.

22 She bore a son, and Moses named him Gershom, saying, “I have been a sojourner in a foreign land.”

God Hears Israel’s Cry

23 In the course of those many days, the king of Egypt died. The Israelites groaned under their bondage, cried out, and their cry for deliverance from their slavery ascended to God.

24 God heard their groaning and remembered His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

25 God regarded the Israelites and was mindful of them.

Reuben’s Birthright Transferred

1 The sons of Reuben, Israel’s firstborn, were recorded as follows: although Reuben was the firstborn, his birthright was given to the sons of Joseph son of Israel, because Reuben defiled his father’s bed; consequently, he was not reckoned as the firstborn in the genealogical record. 2 For Judah grew powerful among his brothers, and a ruler arose from him, yet the birthright belonged to Joseph.

The Clans of Reuben and Gad

3 The sons of Reuben, Israel’s firstborn, were Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi. 4 The sons of Joel were Shemaiah, whose line continued through Gog and Shimei; 5 it then continued through Micah, Reaiah, and Baal. 6 Beerah, his son, was carried into exile by Tilgath-pilneser, king of Assyria; he had been a leader among the Reubenites. 7 His relatives were enrolled by their clans in the genealogical records: Jeiel the chief, and Zechariah. 8 Bela son of Azaz, son of Shema, son of Joel, lived in Aroer as far as Nebo and Baal-meon. 9 They extended eastward to the edge of the wilderness and the Euphrates, because their herds had multiplied in the land of Gilead. 10 During Saul’s reign they waged war against the Hagrites, who fell into their hands; they then occupied the Hagrites’ tents throughout the entire region east of Gilead.

11 The Gadites lived opposite them in Bashan as far as Salecah. 12 Joel was chief, Shapham second, and Janai and Shaphat served in Bashan. 13 Their kinsmen, by ancestral houses, were Michael, Meshullam, Sheba, Jorai, Jacan, Zia, and Eber—seven in all. 14 These were the sons of Abihail son of Huri, son of Jaroah, son of Gilead, son of Michael, son of Jeshishai, son of Jahdo, son of Buz. 15 Ahi son of Abdiel, son of Guni, was chief of their ancestral house. 16 They settled in Gilead, in Bashan and its towns, and in all the pasturelands of Sharon to their borders. 17 All were enrolled during the reigns of Jotham king of Judah and Jeroboam king of Israel.

The Eastern Tribes at War

18 The Reubenites, Gadites, and half the tribe of Manasseh mustered 44,760 men for military service—valiant men who carried shield and sword, drew the bow, and were trained for war. 19 They waged war against the Hagrites, Jetur, Naphish, and Nodab. 20 In battle they cried out to God, and He answered them because they trusted in Him; so the Hagrites and all who were with them were delivered into their hands. 21 They captured their livestock: 50,000 camels, 250,000 sheep, 2,000 donkeys, and 100,000 people. 22 Many fell slain, for the battle was God’s; and they occupied the Hagrites’ territory until the exile.

Exile of the Eastern Tribes

23 The half-tribe of Manasseh was numerous; its people settled in the land from Bashan to Baal-hermon, Senir, and Mount Hermon. 24 These were the heads of their ancestral houses: Epher, Ishi, Eliel, Azriel, Jeremiah, Hodaviah, and Jahdiel—mighty men of valor, renowned men, and heads of their fathers’ houses. 25 But they were unfaithful to the God of their fathers and prostituted themselves to the gods of the peoples of the land, whom God had destroyed before them. 26 Therefore the God of Israel stirred the spirit of Pul, king of Assyria, and of Tilgath-pilneser, king of Assyria, and they carried the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh into exile, settling them in Halah, Habor, Hara, and by the river Gozan, where they remain to this day.

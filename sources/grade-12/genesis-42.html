Jacob Sends His Sons to Egypt

1 When Jacob perceived that grain was available in Egypt, he said to his sons, "Why do you merely gaze at one another? 2 I have heard that grain is to be had in Egypt. Descend there and procure grain for us, that we may survive and not perish."

3 So ten of Joseph's brothers went down to Egypt to purchase grain. 4 Yet Jacob did not send Benjamin, Joseph's brother, along with them, reasoning, "Lest some calamity befall him." 5 Thus the sons of Israel came to buy grain among the throngs who came, for the famine had gripped the land of Canaan.

Joseph Recognizes His Brothers

6 Now Joseph was the governor of the land, the one who dispensed grain to all its inhabitants. When Joseph's brothers arrived, they prostrated themselves before him with their faces to the earth.

7 Joseph recognized his brothers the moment he saw them, yet he concealed his identity and addressed them harshly. "Where do you come from?" he demanded.

"From the land of Canaan," they replied, "to purchase food." 8 Although Joseph recognized his brothers, they did not recognize him.

9 Joseph recalled the dreams he had dreamed concerning them, and he accused them: "You are spies! You have come to observe the vulnerability of the land."

10 "No, my lord," they protested. "Your servants have come merely to buy food. 11 We are all sons of a single father. We are honest men; your servants are not spies."

12 "On the contrary," he retorted, "you have come to spy out the land's vulnerability."

13 "Your servants," they explained, "are twelve brothers, sons of one man in Canaan. The youngest remains with our father today, and one is no longer living."

Joseph Tests His Brothers

14 Joseph declared, "As I have stated, you are spies. 15 Here is how you will be tested: as surely as Pharaoh lives, you shall not depart from this place until your youngest brother arrives. 16 Dispatch one of you to retrieve your brother, while the rest remain confined, so that your claims may be verified. Otherwise, as surely as Pharaoh lives, you are indeed spies."

17 He then confined them all for three days.

18 On the third day, Joseph addressed them: "Do this and live, for I fear God. 19 If you are honest men, let one of your brothers remain imprisoned while the rest carry grain to relieve the famine in your households. 20 Bring your youngest brother to me, so your words may be confirmed and you may live." They consented.

21 They said to one another, "Surely we are guilty concerning our brother. We witnessed his anguish when he pleaded with us, yet we refused to listen. That is why this calamity has befallen us."

22 Reuben replied, "Did I not warn you, 'Do not sin against the boy'? You refused to listen, and now his blood is demanded of us."

23 They were unaware that Joseph comprehended their words, since an interpreter mediated between them. 24 He turned aside from them and wept. Then he returned and spoke to them, and he seized Simeon and bound him in their presence.

25 Joseph commanded that their containers be filled with grain, that each man's money be returned in his sack, and that provisions be given them for the journey. So it was done. 26 They loaded their grain onto their donkeys and departed.

The Brothers Return to Jacob

27 At the lodging place, one opened his sack to feed his donkey and discovered his money at the mouth of his sack. 28 He cried to his brothers, "My money has been returned—indeed, it is in my sack!" They were gripped by dread, and trembling, they turned to one another, asking, "What has God done to us?"

29 They returned to their father Jacob in Canaan and recounted all that had befallen them, saying, 30 "The man, the lord of the land, spoke harshly to us and treated us as spies. 31 We told him, 'We are honest men, not spies.' 32 We are twelve brothers, sons of one father. One is no more, and the youngest remains with our father in Canaan. 33 The man, the lord of the land, said to us, 'By this I will know you are honest men: leave one brother with me, take grain for your households, and depart.' 34 'Bring your youngest brother to me, and then I will know you are not spies but honest men. I will release your brother to you, and you may trade freely in the land.'"

35 As they emptied their sacks, each man's bundle of money appeared in his sack. When they and their father saw the money, they were terrified.

36 Their father Jacob said to them, "You have bereaved me of my children! Joseph is gone, Simeon is gone, and now you would take Benjamin. Everything is against me."

37 Reuben said to his father, "You may put my two sons to death if I do not return him to you. Entrust him to my care, and I will bring him back to you."

38 He replied, "My son will not go down with you, for his brother is dead and he alone remains. If any harm befalls him on the journey you are taking, you will bring my gray head down to Sheol in grief."

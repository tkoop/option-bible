Solomon and Hiram Form an Alliance

1 Hiram, king of Tyre, heard that Solomon had been anointed king in place of his father, and Hiram had always been a loyal friend to David. 2 Solomon sent messengers to Hiram with this message:

“3 You know that my father David could not build a house for the name of the the LORD his God because wars surrounded him, until the the LORD placed his enemies under the soles of his feet. 4 Now the the LORD my God has granted me rest on every side; I have no adversary and no calamity. 5 So I intend to build a house for the name of the the LORD my God, just as the the LORD promised my father David: ‘Your son, whom I will set on your throne in your place, will build the house for My name.’ 6 Therefore command that cedar be cut for me from Lebanon. My servants will work alongside your servants, and I will pay your servants whatever wages you set, because you know that none of us can cut timber like the Sidonians.”

7 When Hiram heard Solomon’s words, he rejoiced greatly and said, “Blessed be the the LORD today, for He has given David a wise son to rule over this great people.”

Hiram Supplies Cedar and Cypress Timber

8 Hiram sent word to Solomon: “I have heard what you sent to me; I will do all your desire concerning cedar and cypress timber. 9 My servants will bring the logs down from Lebanon to the sea, and I will lash them into rafts and float them to whatever place you designate. There I will break them apart, and you can take them away. In return, you will provide food for my household, as I desire.”

10 So Hiram gave Solomon all the cedar and cypress timber he desired. 11 In return, Solomon gave Hiram 20,000 cors of wheat as food for his household and 20 cors of pressed olive oil; Solomon provided this to Hiram year after year. 12 The LORD gave Solomon wisdom, just as He had promised him, and there was peace between Hiram and Solomon; the two of them made a covenant.

Solomon’s Labor Force and Building Preparations

13 King Solomon conscripted a forced labor of 30,000 men from all Israel. 14 He sent them to Lebanon in monthly shifts: 10,000 men worked in Lebanon each month, and they spent two months at home. Adoniram was in charge of the forced labor. 15 Solomon also had 70,000 men who carried burdens and 80,000 stonecutters in the mountains. 16 Besides Solomon’s chief officials who supervised the work, there were 3,300 foremen who ruled over the people doing the work. 17 At the king’s command, they quarried large, costly stones—dressed stones—to lay the foundation of the temple. 18 Solomon’s builders and Hiram’s builders, along with the Gebalites, cut and prepared the timber and stones for building the temple.

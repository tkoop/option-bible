Reverent Worship and Wise Restraint

1 Guard your steps when you enter the house of God. Draw near to listen rather than to offer the sacrifice of fools, for they do not realize that they are doing wrong. 2 Do not be hasty with your mouth, and do not let your heart rush to utter a word before God, for God is in heaven and you are on earth; therefore let your words be few. 3 For a dream comes with many cares, and a fool's voice with many words.

4 When you make a vow to God, do not delay to fulfill it, for He has no pleasure in fools. Fulfill what you have vowed. 5 It is better not to vow than to make a vow and not fulfill it. 6 Do not let your mouth cause you to sin, and do not tell the messenger that your vow was a mistake. Why should God be angry with your words and destroy the work of your hands? 7 For in a multitude of dreams and words there is emptiness; therefore fear God.

The Futility of Oppression and Hoarded Wealth

8 If you see the poor oppressed in a district and justice and righteousness denied, do not be astonished at the matter, for one official is watched by a higher one, and higher ones still are over them. 9 The profit of the land is for all; the king himself is served by the field. 10 Whoever loves money is never satisfied with money, and whoever loves wealth never has enough income. This too is meaningless.

11 When possessions increase, those who consume them increase as well; and what benefit is there to the owner except to see them with his own eyes? 12 The sleep of a laborer is sweet, whether he eats little or much, but the abundance of a rich person permits no sleep. 13 I have seen a grievous evil under the sun: wealth stored up to the harm of its owner, 14 or wealth lost through misfortune, so that when its owner has a son, nothing remains in his hand.

15 As a person comes from his mother's womb, so he returns naked; he takes nothing from his toil that he can carry away in his hand. 16 This too is a grievous evil: exactly as he came, so he goes, and what profit does he have from toiling for the wind? 17 All his days he eats in darkness, with much sorrow, sickness, and anger.

Receiving God's Gifts with Joy

18 This is what I have observed to be good and fitting: to eat and drink and to enjoy the good from all one's labor under the sun during the few days of life God has given, for this is one's portion. 19 Furthermore, when God gives a person wealth and possessions and enables him to enjoy them, to accept his lot, and to rejoice in his labor, this is a gift from God. 20 For he will not dwell much on the days of his life, because God keeps him occupied with joy in his heart.

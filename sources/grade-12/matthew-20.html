Parable of the Laborers in the Vineyard

1 "For the kingdom of heaven is like a landowner who went out at daybreak to hire laborers for his vineyard. 2 He reached an agreement with them for a denarius a day and dispatched them into his vineyard.

3 "About the third hour he went out and observed others standing idle in the marketplace. 4 He said to them, 'You also go into my vineyard, and I will pay you whatever is just.' 5 So they departed. He went out again about the sixth and ninth hours and acted likewise.

6 "'Why have you stood here the entire day, doing nothing?' he asked.

7 "'Because no one has hired us,' they replied.

"He told them, 'You also go into my vineyard.'

8 "When evening arrived, the proprietor of the vineyard instructed his steward, 'Summon the laborers and disburse their wages, beginning with the last hired and proceeding to the first.'

9 "Those hired about the eleventh hour came and received a denarius each. 10 Consequently, when the first hired presented themselves, they anticipated receiving more, yet each of them likewise received a denarius.

11 "Upon receiving it, they murmured against the landowner.

12 "'These last worked a single hour,' they said, 'and you have made them equal to us, who have borne the burden of the day and the scorching heat.'

13 "He answered one of them, 'Friend, I do you no injustice. Did you not agree with me for a denarius?'

14 "'Take what is yours and go. I choose to give this last one the same as I gave you.'

15 "'Is it not lawful for me to do as I wish with my own possessions? Or does your eye grow envious because I am generous?'

16 "'Thus the last will be first, and the first last.'"

Jesus Predicts His Death and Resurrection

17 As Jesus was ascending toward Jerusalem, He took the twelve disciples aside privately and said, 18 "Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and the Son of Man will be delivered over to the chief priests and scribes. They will condemn Him to death 19 and deliver Him to the Gentiles to be mocked, scourged, and crucified; yet on the third day He will be raised."

The Request of Zebedee’s Sons

20 Then the mother of Zebedee’s sons approached Jesus with her sons, prostrating herself before Him to request something.

21 "What do you want?" He inquired.

She answered, "Grant that in Your kingdom these two sons of mine may sit, one at Your right hand and the other at Your left."

22 "You do not know what you are requesting. Are you able to drink the cup that I am about to drink?" Jesus replied.

"We are able," they answered.

23 "You will indeed drink My cup," Jesus said. "But to sit at My right hand or at My left is not Mine to bestow; rather, those places belong to those for whom My Father has prepared them."

24 When the ten heard of this, they became indignant with the two brothers.

25 "You know that the rulers of the Gentiles dominate them, and their great ones exercise authority over them. 26 It shall not be so among you. Instead, whoever desires to become great among you must be your servant, 27 and whoever desires to be first must be your slave— 28 just as the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life as a ransom for many."

Two Blind Men Receive Their Sight

29 As they were departing from Jericho, a sizable crowd followed Him.

30 Two blind men were seated beside the road. Hearing that Jesus was passing by, they cried out, "Lord, Son of David, have mercy on us!"

31 The crowd rebuked them, ordering them to be silent,

but they cried out all the more, "Lord, Son of David, have mercy on us!"

32 Jesus halted and summoned them. "What do you want Me to do for you?" He asked.

33 "Lord," they answered, "let our eyes be opened."

34 Moved with compassion, Jesus touched their eyes, and immediately they regained their sight and followed Him.

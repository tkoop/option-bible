The King's Sleepless Night

1 That very night, sleep eluded the king, so he commanded that the chronicle of memorable deeds—the book recording the days' events—be brought and read aloud in his presence. 2 There it was found recorded that Mordecai had disclosed the plot of Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs who guarded the threshold and who had sought to assassinate King Ahasuerus. 3 The king asked, "What honor or distinction has been conferred upon Mordecai for this?" His servants replied, "Nothing has been done for him."

Haman's Arrival and the King's Question

4 The king inquired, "Who is in the court?" Now Haman had entered the outer court of the royal palace, intending to petition the king to hang Mordecai on the gallows he had erected for him. 5 The king's servants answered, "Haman stands in the court." The king said, "Let him enter."

The Honor Decreed for Mordecai

6 When Haman entered, the king asked him, "What should be done for the man whom the king delights to honor?" Haman thought to himself, "Whom would the king delight to honor more than me?" 7 So Haman said to the king, "For the man whom the king delights to honor, 8 let royal robes be brought that the king himself has worn, and a horse on which the king has ridden, with a royal crown placed upon its head. 9 Let the robe and horse be entrusted to one of the king's most noble officials, and let them array the man whom the king delights to honor; then let them lead him on horseback through the city square and proclaim before him, 'Thus shall it be done for the man whom the king delights to honor.'"

10 The king then said to Haman, "Make haste; take the robe and the horse, exactly as you have spoken, and do this for Mordecai the Jew, who sits at the king's gate. Let nothing of all that you have spoken fail." 11 So Haman took the robe and the horse, clothed Mordecai, led him on horseback through the city square, and proclaimed before him, "Thus shall it be done for the man whom the king delights to honor."

12 Afterward Mordecai returned to the king's gate, but Haman hurried home, mourning, with his head covered.

13 Haman recounted to his wife Zeresh and to all his friends everything that had befallen him. Then his wise men and his wife Zeresh said to him, "If Mordecai, before whom your downfall has already begun, is of Jewish descent, you will not prevail against him; rather, you will certainly fall before him." 14 While they were still speaking with him, the king's eunuchs arrived and hastened to bring Haman to the banquet that Esther had prepared.

The LORD's Challenge Concerning Leviathan

"1 Can you draw Leviathan with a fishhook, or bind his tongue with a rope? 2 Can you put a reed through his nose, or pierce his jaw with a hook? 3 Will he plead with you for mercy, or speak softly to you? 4 Will he make a covenant with you, to serve you forever? 5 Can you play with him as with a bird, or bind him for your maidens? 6 Will merchants barter for him, or divide him among the traders? 7 Can you fill his skin with harpoons, or his head with fishing spears? 8 If you lay a hand on him, you will remember the battle and never attempt it again. 9 Behold, the hope of subduing him is a delusion; one is overwhelmed at the mere sight of him.

"10 No one is so fierce as to rouse him; who then can stand before Me? 11 Who has first given to Me, that I should repay him? Everything under heaven is Mine. 12 I will not keep silent about his limbs, his mighty strength, or his graceful form. 13 Who can strip off his outer coat? Who can approach him with a bridle? 14 Who can open the doors of his face? His teeth are a terror all around.

Leviathan's Armored Scales

"15 His back is rows of shields, shut tight as a sealed closure. 16 One scale draws near to another, so that no breath can pass between them. 17 They cleave to one another; they hold fast and cannot be separated.

Leviathan's Fiery Breath and Strength

"18 His snorting flashes forth light; his eyes are like the eyelids of dawn. 19 Firebrands stream from his mouth; sparks of fire shoot out. 20 Smoke billows from his nostrils as from a boiling pot over burning reeds. 21 His breath sets coals ablaze, and a flame pours from his mouth. 22 Strength abides in his neck, and dismay leaps before him. 23 The folds of his flesh are tightly joined; they are firm and immovable. 24 His heart is as hard as stone, even as hard as a lower millstone.

Leviathan's Invincible Might

"25 When he rises up, the mighty are terrified; they retreat before his thrashing. 26 The sword that reaches him cannot avail; neither spear, nor dart, nor javelin. 27 He regards iron as straw, bronze as rotten wood. 28 The bow cannot make him flee; slingstones become like chaff to him. 29 A club is regarded as stubble; he laughs at the rattling of spears. 30 His undersides are jagged potsherds, leaving a trail in the mud like a threshing sledge. 31 He makes the depths churn like a cauldron; he makes the sea like a pot of ointment. 32 He leaves a glistening wake behind him; one would think the deep had white hair. 33 Nothing on earth is his equal—a creature devoid of fear. 34 He looks down on all that is lofty; he is king over every proud creature."

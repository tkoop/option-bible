Joab’s Scheme to Restore Absalom

1 Joab son of Zeruiah perceived that the king’s heart was fixed upon Absalom.

2 So Joab sent to Tekoa and brought back a wise woman, and he said to her, “Please mourn; put on mourning garments, and do not anoint yourself with oil. Be like a woman who has mourned the dead for many days.”

3 “Then go to the king and speak to him in this manner,” Joab said, putting these words in her mouth.

4 The woman from Tekoa went to the king, fell on her face to the ground, bowed down, and cried, “Help me, O king!”

5 The king asked her, “What troubles you?”

She answered, “Truly I am a widow, and my husband is dead.”

6 “Your maidservant had two sons, and they quarreled in the field. With no one to intervene, one struck the other and killed him. 7 Now the whole clan has risen against your maidservant and says, ‘Surrender the man who struck his brother, so that we may put him to death for the life of his brother whom he killed and destroy the heir as well.’ Thus they would extinguish the last ember of my husband’s lineage, leaving my husband neither name nor remnant on the earth.”

8 The king said to the woman, “Go to your home, and I will issue an order concerning you.”

9 The woman said to the king, “My lord the king, let the guilt rest upon me and upon my father’s house, while the king and his throne remain innocent.”

10 The king said, “If anyone speaks to you, bring him to me, and he will not trouble you again.”

11 She said, “Please let my lord the king remember the LORD your God, so that the avenger of blood does not destroy my son.”

The king replied, “As surely as the LORD lives, not one hair of your son shall fall to the ground.”

12 Then the woman said, “Please let your servant speak a word to my lord the king.”

“Speak,” he said.

13 The woman said, “Why then have you devised such a thing against the people of God? For in pronouncing this judgment, the king condemns himself, since he has not restored his own banished son. 14 For we will surely die and are like water spilled upon the ground, which cannot be gathered up again; yet God does not take life away, but He devises means so that the banished one is not cast out from Him.”

15 “Now I have come to speak this word to my lord the king because the people have intimidated me. Your servant said, ‘I will speak to the king; perhaps the king will grant his maidservant’s request.’”

16 “For the king will hear and deliver his maidservant from the hand of the man who would destroy me and my son together from God’s inheritance.”

17 “Your servant said, ‘May the word of my lord the king bring me rest,’ for my lord the king is like the angel of God in discerning good and evil. May the LORD your God be with you.”

18 The king answered the woman, “Please do not conceal from me anything I ask you.”

“Let my lord the king speak,” the woman said.

19 The king said, “Is Joab’s hand in all this?”

The woman answered, “As your soul lives, my lord the king, no one can deviate from anything my lord the king has spoken. Yes, your servant Joab commanded me; he put all these words in the mouth of your servant. 20 To bring about this change of affairs, your servant Joab has done this thing. But my lord is wise, with the wisdom of the angel of God, to know everything that happens in the land.”

21 The king said to Joab, “Very well, I have done this. Go and bring back the young man Absalom.”

22 Joab fell on his face to the ground, bowed down, and blessed the king. Then Joab said, “Today your servant knows that he has found favor in your eyes, my lord the king, because the king has granted his servant’s request.”

Absalom’s Return to Jerusalem

23 Joab rose, went to Geshur, and brought Absalom to Jerusalem.

24 The king said, “Let him go to his own house, but he must not see my face.” So Absalom went to his own house and did not see the king’s face.

25 In all Israel there was no man so handsome as Absalom; from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head he was without blemish. 26 When he cut the hair of his head—and he cut it at the end of each year because its weight burdened him—he would weigh it out at two hundred shekels by the king’s standard. 27 Three sons and a daughter were born to Absalom. His daughter’s name was Tamar, and she was a beautiful woman.

28 Absalom lived in Jerusalem for two full years without seeing the king’s face.

Absalom’s Demand for an Audience

29 Then Absalom sent for Joab, to send him to the king, but Joab would not come. He sent a second time, but Joab would not come.

30 So Absalom said to his servants, “Look, Joab’s field is next to mine, and he has barley there. Go and set it ablaze.” So Absalom’s servants set the field on fire.

31 Joab got up, went to Absalom’s house, and said, “Why have your servants set my field ablaze?”

32 Absalom answered Joab, “Look, I sent to you and said, ‘Come here, so that I may send you to the king to ask why I came from Geshur. It would have been better for me to remain there.’ Now let me see the king’s face, and if there is guilt in me, let him put me to death.”

33 Joab went and told the king. Then the king summoned Absalom, and he came to the king and bowed with his face to the ground before him. The king kissed Absalom.

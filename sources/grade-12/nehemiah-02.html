Nehemiah Seeks Permission to Rebuild Jerusalem

1 In the month of Nisan, in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes, wine was set before him. I took the wine and presented it to the king, for I had never before appeared downcast in his presence. 2 The king said to me, "Why does your countenance appear so dejected when you are not ill? This can only be anguish of heart." I was exceedingly afraid.

3 I said to the king, "O king, live forever! Why should my face not be downcast when the city, the place of my fathers' graves, lies in ruins and its gates have been consumed by fire?"

4 The king said to me, "What is your request?" So I prayed to the God of heaven.

5 I said to the king, "If it pleases the king, and if your servant has found favor in your sight, send me to Judah, to the city of my fathers' tombs, that I may rebuild it."

6 The king, with the queen seated beside him, asked me, "How long will your journey take, and when will you return?" It pleased the king to send me, and I set him an appointed time.

7 I said to the king, "If it pleases the king, let letters be given to me for the governors beyond the River, that they may grant me safe passage until I reach Judah."

8 And a letter was given to Asaph, keeper of the king's forest, that he may give me timber to make beams for the gates of the fortress by the temple, for the city wall, and for the house into which I am to go. The king granted my request, for the gracious hand of my God was upon me. 9 I went to the governors beyond the River and delivered the king's letters. The king had also sent army officers and cavalry with me.

10 When Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite official heard of this, they were deeply displeased that someone had come to seek the welfare of the Israelites.

Nehemiah Inspects the Ruined Walls by Night

11 I arrived in Jerusalem and remained there three days. 12 Then I set out by night with a few men. I had told no one what my God had put into my heart to do for Jerusalem, and there was no animal with me except the beast on which I rode.

13 I went out by night through the Valley Gate toward the Dragon Spring and on to the Dung Gate, inspecting the walls of Jerusalem, which had been broken down, and its gates, which had been destroyed by fire. 14 Then I proceeded to the Fountain Gate and to the King's Pool, but there was not enough room for the animal under me to pass. 15 So I went up by night along the valley and inspected the wall. Then I turned back and reentered through the Valley Gate.

16 The officials did not know where I had gone or what I was doing, for I had not yet told the Jews, the priests, the nobles, the officials, or the rest of those who were doing the work.

The People Join the Rebuilding Work

17 Then I said to them, "You see the trouble we are in: Jerusalem lies in ruins, and its gates have been burned with fire. Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, so that we will no longer be an object of scorn."

18 I also told them about the gracious hand of my God upon me, and about what the king had said to me. They replied, "Let us start rebuilding." So they began the good work.

19 But when Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite official, and Geshem the Arab heard about it, they mocked and despised us, saying, "What is this you are doing? Are you rebelling against the king?"

20 I answered them by saying, "The God of heaven will grant us success. We His servants will start rebuilding, but you have no share, right, or memorial in Jerusalem."

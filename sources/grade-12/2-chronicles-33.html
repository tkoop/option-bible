Manasseh's Wicked Reign and Idolatry

1 Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king, and he reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem. 2 He acted wickedly in the sight of the LORD, following the abominations of the nations that the LORD had dispossessed before the Israelites. 3 He reversed course and rebuilt the high places his father Hezekiah had torn down; he erected altars to the Baals, fashioned Asherah poles, and bowed down to and served the entire host of heaven.

4 He built altars in the temple of the LORD, the very house of which the LORD had declared, “My Name will remain in Jerusalem forever.” 5 In the two courts of the house of the LORD, he built altars to all the host of heaven.

6 He made his sons pass through the fire in the Valley of Ben Hinnom; he practiced soothsaying, divination, and sorcery; he consulted mediums and spiritists; and he did much evil in the sight of the LORD, provoking Him to anger.

7 He set up the carved image of the idol he had made in the house of God, the very place of which God had said to David and to Solomon his son, “In this house and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen from all the tribes of Israel, I will put My Name forever. 8 I will never again banish Israel from the land I appointed for your fathers, provided they take care to do all that I commanded them through Moses—all the law, statutes, and ordinances.” 9 Yet Manasseh led Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem astray, so that they committed more evil than the nations the LORD had destroyed before the Israelites.

The LORD's Judgment and Manasseh's Repentance

10 The LORD spoke to Manasseh and his people, but they paid no heed. 11 Therefore the LORD brought against them the commanders of the Assyrian king’s army, who captured Manasseh with hooks, bound him with bronze chains, and carried him off to Babylon.

12 In his distress he entreated the LORD his God and humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers. 13 Manasseh prayed to Him, and God was moved by his entreaty and heard his supplication; He restored Manasseh to Jerusalem and to his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that the LORD is God.

Manasseh's Reforms and Death

14 Afterward he built an outer wall for the City of David on the west side of Gihon in the valley, extending to the entrance of the Fish Gate; he encircled the Ophel and raised it to a great height. He also stationed military commanders in all the fortified cities of Judah.

15 He removed the foreign gods and the carved image from the house of the LORD, along with all the altars he had built on the mountain of the house of the LORD and in Jerusalem, and he cast them outside the city. 16 He restored the altar of the LORD and sacrificed peace offerings and thank offerings on it; he commanded Judah to serve the LORD, the God of Israel. 17 Nevertheless, the people continued to sacrifice on the high places, but only to the LORD their God.

18 The rest of the acts of Manasseh, his prayer to his God, and the words of the seers who spoke to him in the name of the LORD God of Israel are recorded in the annals of the kings of Israel. 19 His prayer and how he was heard, along with all his sin and trespass, and the sites where he built high places and set up Asherah poles and carved images before he humbled himself, are written in the records of the seers.

20 Manasseh died and was buried in his palace; his son Amon succeeded him.

The Reign and Assassination of Amon

21 Amon was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned two years in Jerusalem. 22 He did evil in the sight of the LORD, as his father Manasseh had done; Amon sacrificed to all the carved images that Manasseh his father had made, and served them. 23 He did not humble himself before the LORD, as his father Manasseh had humbled himself; instead, Amon increased his guilt.

24 His servants conspired against him and put him to death in his house. 25 But the people of the land struck down all those who had conspired against King Amon, and they made his son Josiah king in his place.

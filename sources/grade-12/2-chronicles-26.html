Uzziah's Accession and Early Prosperity

1 The whole people of Judah took Uzziah, who was sixteen years old, and made him king in place of his father Amaziah. 2 He rebuilt Eloth and restored it to Judah after Amaziah had died. 3 Uzziah was sixteen years old when he became king, and he reigned fifty-two years in Jerusalem; his mother's name was Jecoliah of Jerusalem. 4 He did what was right in the eyes of the the LORD, according to all that his father Amaziah had done. 5 He devoted himself to seeking God in the days of Zechariah, who was adept in the visions of God; and as long as he sought the the LORD, God granted him prosperity.

6 He campaigned against the Philistines, dismantling the walls of Gath, Jabneh, and Ashdod, and he founded cities in Ashdod and throughout Philistine territory. 7 God sustained him against the Philistines, against the Arabs dwelling in Gur-baal, and against the Meunites. 8 The Ammonites paid tribute to Uzziah, and his renown extended to the approaches of Egypt, for his power had grown immense. 9 Moreover, Uzziah erected towers in Jerusalem at the Corner Gate, the Valley Gate, and the Angle, reinforcing them. 10 He built towers in the wilderness and excavated numerous cisterns, because he owned extensive herds in the Shephelah and on the plain; he employed farmers and vinedressers in the highlands and in Carmel, for he cherished the soil.

11 Uzziah maintained an army of warriors who marched out in divisions, tallied by Jeiel the scribe and Maaseiah the officer under the authority of Hananiah, one of the king's commanders. 12 The aggregate of the ancestral chieftains, valiant men, numbered 2,600. 13 At their disposal stood an army of 307,500 men, exercising formidable martial power to assist the king against his adversaries. 14 Uzziah outfitted them—the entire force—with shields, spears, helmets, coats of mail, bows, and sling stones. 15 He also devised in Jerusalem ingenious war machines, crafted by skilled artisans, to be mounted on the towers and corners for discharging arrows and hurling massive stones. His renown reached distant lands, for he received extraordinary assistance until he had grown powerful.

Pride, Presumption, and Divine Judgment

16 Yet once he had become powerful, pride swelled in his heart to his ruin; he acted unfaithfully against the the LORD his God by entering the the LORD's temple to burn incense on the altar of incense.

17 Azariah the priest followed him in, accompanied by eighty priests of the the LORD—men of valor. 18 They confronted King Uzziah and declared, “It is not your prerogative, Uzziah, to burn incense to the the LORD; that privilege belongs to the priests, the sons of Aaron, who have been consecrated for this rite. Leave the sanctuary at once, for you have trespassed; nor will this bring you honor from the the LORD God.”

19 Then Uzziah grew furious, and while his anger raged against the priests, leprosy erupted on his forehead in the presence of the priests within the the LORD's house, beside the altar of incense. 20 When Azariah the chief priest and all the priests turned toward him and saw that he was leprous on his forehead, they expelled him from the premises; he himself hastened to depart, because the the LORD had afflicted him. 21 King Uzziah remained a leper until his death. He lived in an isolated dwelling, barred from the the LORD's house, and his son Jotham oversaw the royal palace, governing the people of the land.

Uzziah's Death and Succession

22 The remaining deeds of Uzziah, from first to last, were recorded by the prophet Isaiah son of Amoz. 23 Uzziah died and was buried with his ancestors in the royal burial field, because, as they acknowledged, he was a leper. His son Jotham succeeded him.

Job's Character and Prosperity

1 There was a man in the land of Uz whose name was Job. That man was blameless and upright; he feared God and turned away from evil. 2 Seven sons and three daughters were born to him. 3 His livestock included seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen, and five hundred female donkeys, along with very many servants. He was the greatest of all the men of the East. 4 His sons used to go and hold a feast in the house of each one on his own day, and they would send and invite their three sisters to eat and drink with them. 5 When the days of feasting had run their course, Job would send for them and consecrate them; rising early in the morning, he would offer burnt offerings for each of them, for Job said, "Perhaps my sons have sinned and cursed God in their hearts." Job did this continually.

Satan's Challenge to Job's Integrity

6 One day the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan also came among them. 7 The LORD said to Satan, "Where have you come from?" Satan answered the LORD, "From roaming through the earth and walking back and forth in it." 8 Then the LORD said to Satan, "Have you considered My servant Job? There is no one on earth like him—blameless and upright, one who fears God and turns away from evil."

9 Satan answered the LORD, "Is Job's reverence for God really selfless? 10 Have You not put a hedge around him, his household, and everything he has, on every side? You have blessed the work of his hands, and his livestock have multiplied in the land. 11 But stretch out Your hand and touch everything he owns, and he will surely curse You to Your face."

12 The LORD said to Satan, "Behold, all that he has is in your power; only do not stretch out your hand against him." Then Satan went out from the presence of the LORD.

Job's First Great Losses

13 Now on a day when his sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their eldest brother, 14 a messenger came to Job and said, "The oxen were plowing and the donkeys were feeding beside them, 15 when the Sabeans attacked and carried them off. They put the servants to the sword, and I alone have escaped to tell you."

16 While he was still speaking, another messenger came and said, "The fire of God fell from heaven and burned up the sheep and the servants. I alone have escaped to tell you."

17 While he was still speaking, another came and said, "The Chaldeans formed three raiding parties and swept down on the camels, carrying them off. They put the servants to the sword, and I alone have escaped to tell you."

18 While he was still speaking, yet another messenger came and said, "Your sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their eldest brother, 19 when a mighty wind swept in from the wilderness and struck the four corners of the house. It collapsed on the young people, and they died. I alone have escaped to tell you."

Job's Response to Tragedy

20 Then Job arose, tore his robe, shaved his head, and fell to the ground in worship. 21 He said, "Naked I came from my mother's womb, and naked I will return there. The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD." 22 In all this Job did not sin, nor did he charge God with wrongdoing.

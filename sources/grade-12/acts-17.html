Paul and Silas in Thessalonica

1 After Paul and Silas had passed through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they came to Thessalonica, where a Jewish synagogue stood. 2 As was his custom, Paul entered the synagogue and, over three Sabbaths, reasoned with them from the Scriptures, 3 explaining and proving that the Christ had to suffer and rise from the dead, and declaring, "This Jesus, whom I proclaim to you, is the Christ." 4 Some of them were persuaded and joined Paul and Silas, along with a great multitude of God-fearing Greeks and quite a few of the leading women.

5 But the Jews, moved by jealousy, recruited certain worthless men from the marketplace, formed a mob, and threw the city into turmoil. They assaulted Jason's house, seeking to bring Paul and Silas out to the crowd. 6 When they could not find them, they dragged Jason and some brothers before the city magistrates, shouting, "These men who have thrown the whole world into disorder have now come here as well, 7 and Jason has received them into his home. They all act contrary to Caesar's decrees, claiming that there is another king—Jesus." 8 Hearing this, the crowd and the magistrates were thrown into confusion. 9 After taking bond from Jason and the others, they released them.

The Bereans Receive the Word

10 As soon as night fell, the brothers sent Paul and Silas away to Berea; upon arriving, they went into the Jewish synagogue. 11 Now these Bereans were more noble-minded than those in Thessalonica, for they received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to determine whether these things were so. 12 Consequently, many of them believed, along with a number of prominent Greek women and men. 13 But when the Jews of Thessalonica learned that the word of God was also being proclaimed by Paul in Berea, they went there too, inciting and agitating the crowds. 14 The brothers then immediately sent Paul away toward the sea, while Silas and Timothy remained behind. 15 Those who escorted Paul brought him to Athens and returned with instructions for Silas and Timothy to join him as soon as possible.

Paul in Athens

16 While Paul waited for them in Athens, his spirit was provoked within him as he observed that the city was full of idols. 17 So he reasoned in the synagogue with the Jews and the God-fearing Gentiles, and in the marketplace with those who happened to be there each day. 18 Some of the Epicurean and Stoic philosophers also engaged him in discussion. Some asked, "What is this babbler trying to say?"

Others said, "He seems to be proclaiming foreign deities," because he was preaching Jesus and the resurrection. 19 They took hold of Paul and brought him to the Areopagus, saying, "May we know what this new teaching is that you are presenting? 20 For you are bringing some strange things to our ears, and we want to know what they mean." 21 Now all the Athenians and the foreigners residing there spent their time doing nothing but telling or hearing something new.

Paul's Address on the Areopagus

22 Then Paul stood in the midst of the Areopagus and said: "Men of Athens, I observe that you are extremely religious in every respect. 23 For as I passed along and examined your objects of worship, I found an altar inscribed, 'TO AN UNKNOWN GOD.' Therefore, what you worship in ignorance, I now proclaim to you.

"24 The God who made the world and everything in it is the Lord of heaven and earth and does not dwell in temples made by hands, 25 nor is He served by human hands, as though He needed anything, since He Himself gives to all life and breath and all things. 26 From one man He made every nation of mankind to dwell on the entire face of the earth, having determined their appointed seasons and the boundaries of their habitation, 27 so that they would seek God, and perhaps reach out for Him and find Him, though He is not far from each one of us. 28 For in Him we live and move and have our being, as even some of your own poets have said, 'We are His offspring.' 29 Therefore, since we are God's offspring, we ought not to think that the Divine Being is like gold or silver or stone, an image carved by human skill and imagination. 30 Although God overlooked the times of ignorance, He now commands all people everywhere to repent, 31 because He has set a day on which He will judge the world in righteousness by the Man whom He has appointed, having furnished proof to all by raising Him from the dead."

32 When they heard of the resurrection of the dead, some scoffed, but others said, "We want to hear you again about this matter." 33 So Paul went out from their midst. 34 But some men joined him and believed, including Dionysius the Areopagite, a woman named Damaris, and others with them.

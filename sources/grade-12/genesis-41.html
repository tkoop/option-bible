Pharaoh's Troubling Dreams

1 Two full years later, Pharaoh dreamed that he was standing beside the Nile. 2 He saw seven cows, beautiful and well fed, come up out of the river and graze among the reeds. 3 After them, seven other cows—ugly and gaunt—came up from the Nile and stood beside the first cows on the riverbank. 4 The ugly, gaunt cows devoured the seven beautiful, fat cows, whereupon Pharaoh awoke.

5 He fell asleep again and dreamed a second time: seven heads of grain, plump and good, grew on a single stalk. 6 After them, seven heads of grain, thin and scorched by the east wind, sprouted up. 7 The thin heads swallowed the seven plump, full heads. Pharaoh awoke and realized it had been a dream.

8 In the morning his spirit was troubled, so he sent for all the magicians and wise men of Egypt. Pharaoh told them his dream, but none could interpret it for him.

Joseph Is Summoned to Interpret the Dreams

9 Then the chief cupbearer said to Pharaoh, "Today I remember my offenses. 10 When Pharaoh was angry with his servants, he imprisoned me and the chief baker in the house of the captain of the guard. 11 One night we both dreamed—each of us a dream with its own meaning. 12 A young Hebrew was there with us, a servant of the captain of the guard. We told him our dreams, and he interpreted each one according to its meaning. 13 It happened just as he said: I was restored to my position, and the baker was hanged."

14 Pharaoh then sent for Joseph, and they hurried him out of the prison. He shaved, changed his clothes, and went in to Pharaoh.

15 Pharaoh said to Joseph, "I have dreamed a dream, and no one can interpret it. I have heard that you can understand a dream and interpret it."

16 Joseph answered Pharaoh, "I cannot do that on my own; God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace."

17 Pharaoh said to Joseph, "In my dream I was standing on the bank of the Nile. 18 Seven cows, fat and beautiful, came up out of the river and grazed among the reeds. 19 After them, seven other cows came up—scrawny, very ugly, and lean. I have never seen such miserable cattle in all Egypt. 20 The lean, ugly cows devoured the first seven fat cows. 21 Yet after swallowing them, they looked just as thin and ugly as before. Then I awoke. 22 In my dream I also saw seven heads of grain, full and good, growing on one stalk. 23 After them, seven heads of grain, withered, thin, and scorched by the east wind, sprouted up. 24 The thin heads swallowed the seven good heads. I told this to the magicians, but no one could explain it to me."

25 Joseph said to Pharaoh, "Pharaoh's dream is one dream. God has shown Pharaoh what He is about to do. 26 The seven good cows are seven years, and the seven good heads of grain are seven years; it is the same dream. 27 The seven lean, ugly cows that came up afterward are seven years, and the seven empty heads scorched by the east wind will be seven years of famine. 28 This is what I told Pharaoh: God has shown Pharaoh what He is about to do. 29 Seven years of great abundance are coming throughout all Egypt. 30 After them will come seven years of famine, and all the abundance will be forgotten in Egypt; the famine will ravage the land. 31 The abundance will not be remembered because the famine that follows will be so severe. 32 The dream was repeated to Pharaoh twice because God has established the matter, and He will bring it about soon."

33 Joseph continued, "Now let Pharaoh look for a discerning and wise man and put him in charge of the land of Egypt. 34 Let Pharaoh appoint officials over the land and collect a fifth of Egypt's harvest during the seven years of abundance. 35 Let them gather all the food of those good years and store grain under Pharaoh's authority, keeping it in the cities. 36 That food will be a reserve for the land during the seven years of famine in Egypt, so that the country will not be destroyed by famine."

Joseph Is Elevated to Power

37 The plan pleased Pharaoh and all his servants.

38 Pharaoh said to his servants, "Can we find anyone like this man, in whom is the Spirit of God?"

39 Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Since God has shown you all this, there is no one so discerning and wise as you. 40 You are to be over my house, and all my people will be governed by your word. Only the throne will I keep higher than you."

41 Pharaoh said to Joseph, "See, I have placed you over all the land of Egypt."

42 Pharaoh took his signet ring from his hand and put it on Joseph's hand; he dressed him in fine linen and placed a gold chain around his neck. 43 He had him ride in his second chariot, and men cried out before him, "Bow the knee!" Thus he put him in charge of all Egypt.

44 Pharaoh said to Joseph, "I am Pharaoh, and without you no one in all Egypt will lift a hand or a foot."

45 Pharaoh named him Zaphenath-paneah and gave him Asenath, daughter of Potiphera priest of On, as his wife. Joseph then went out over all the land of Egypt. 46 Joseph was thirty years old when he entered the service of Pharaoh king of Egypt. He left Pharaoh's presence and traveled throughout Egypt.

47 During the seven years of plenty, the land produced abundantly. 48 Joseph gathered all the food of those seven years in Egypt and stored it in the cities; in each city he stored the produce of the surrounding fields. 49 Joseph stored up grain in such abundance—like the sand of the sea—that he stopped keeping count, because it was beyond measure.

50 Before the years of famine came, Joseph had two sons by Asenath, daughter of Potiphera priest of On. 51 He named his firstborn Manasseh, saying, "God has made me forget all my hardship and my father's household." 52 He named the second Ephraim, saying, "God has made me fruitful in the land of my affliction."

The Famine Begins

53 The seven years of plenty in Egypt came to an end. 54 Then the seven years of famine began, just as Joseph had said. There was famine in all lands, but throughout Egypt there was bread.

55 When all Egypt was famished, the people cried to Pharaoh for food. Pharaoh told all the Egyptians, "Go to Joseph and do whatever he tells you."

56 The famine was over all the face of the earth. Joseph opened all the storehouses and sold grain to the Egyptians, as the famine grew severe in Egypt. 57 All the earth came to Egypt to buy grain from Joseph, because the famine was severe everywhere.

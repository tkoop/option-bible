Jesus Departs from Judea for Galilee

1 Upon discerning that the Pharisees had heard He was making and baptizing more disciples than John— 2 although Jesus Himself was not baptizing, but His disciples were— 3 He left Judea and returned to Galilee. 4 It was necessary for Him to traverse Samaria. 5 Consequently, He arrived at a Samaritan settlement called Sychar, adjacent to the parcel Jacob had bequeathed to his son Joseph. 6 Jacob’s well was situated there, and Jesus, fatigued by His itinerary, seated Himself beside the well. The time was approximately the sixth hour.

The Samaritan Woman at the Well

7 A woman from Samaria approached to draw water, and Jesus said to her, “Give Me a drink.” 8 (His disciples had gone into the town to buy food.) 9 The Samaritan woman said to Him, “How is it that You, a Jew, solicit a drink from me, a Samaritan woman?” (For Jews have no dealings with Samaritans.)

10 Jesus answered, “If you recognized the gift of God and who it is that asks you for a drink, you would have asked Him, and He would have given you living water.”

11 “Sir,” the woman said, “You possess no vessel with which to draw, and the well is deep. Whence, then, can You obtain this living water? 12 Are You greater than our patriarch Jacob, who gave us this well and drank from it himself, as did his sons and his livestock?”

13 Jesus answered, “Everyone who drinks of this water will thirst again, 14 but whoever drinks the water I shall give him will never thirst; rather, the water I give him will become within him a spring welling up to eternal life.”

15 The woman said to Him, “Sir, grant me this water, that I may not thirst nor continue coming here to draw.”

16 He told her, “Go, summon your husband and return here.”

17 “I have no husband,” she replied. Jesus said to her, “You have spoken aptly in saying that you have no husband. 18 Indeed, you have had five husbands, and the man you presently have is not your husband; You have spoken truthfully.”

19 “Sir,” the woman said, “I perceive that You are a prophet. 20 Our ancestors worshiped on this mountain, yet you Jews assert that the obligatory place of worship is Jerusalem.”

21 “Believe Me, woman,” Jesus replied, “an hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem. 22 You worship what you do not know; we worship what we know, because salvation originates from the Jews. 23 Yet an hour is coming, and indeed is now here, when true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth, for the Father seeks such people to worship Him. 24 God is Spirit, and those who worship Him must worship in spirit and truth.”

25 The woman said, “I know that the Messiah (that is, the Christ) is coming; when He comes, He will declare all things to us.”

26 Then Jesus declared, “I who speak to you am He.”

27 At that moment His disciples returned, and they marveled that He was conversing with a woman; nevertheless, no one inquired, “What do You seek?” or “Why do You converse with her?”

28 Then the woman abandoned her water jar, returned to the town, and said to the people, 29 “Come, behold a man who told me all that I ever did. Could this be the Christ?”

30 Accordingly, they departed from the town and went out to meet Him.

The Fields Are White for Harvest

31 Meanwhile, the disciples entreated Him, “Rabbi, eat.”

32 But He said to them, “I have sustenance to eat of which you are unaware.”

33 Thus the disciples said to one another, “Has someone brought Him food?”

34 Jesus said to them, “My nourishment is to accomplish the will of Him who sent Me and to complete His work.”

35 “Do you not say, ‘Four months yet remain until the harvest’? I tell you, lift up your eyes and observe the fields, for they are already ripe for harvest.”

36 “Already the reaper receives his wages and gathers fruit unto eternal life, so that the sower and the reaper may rejoice in unison.”

37 “For herein the adage proves true: ‘One sows while another reaps.’”

38 “I commissioned you to reap that for which you did not labor; others have toiled, and you now harvest the fruit of their labor.”

39 Many Samaritans from that town believed in Him on account of the woman’s testimony, “He told me all that I have done.” 40 So, when the Samaritans came to Him, they requested that He remain with them, and He abode there two days.

41 Moreover, many more believed because of His word. 42 They said to the woman, “We no longer believe on account of your words, for we have heard for ourselves and know that this Man is indeed the Savior of the world.”

43 After those two days, He departed for Galilee. 44 (For Jesus Himself had borne witness that a prophet receives no honor in his own country.) 45 When He arrived in Galilee, the Galileans received Him, having seen all that He had done in Jerusalem at the feast, for they too had gone up to the feast.

The Royal Official’s Son Is Healed

46 Thus He returned to Cana of Galilee, where He had transformed water into wine. Now a certain royal official had a son who was ill in Capernaum. 47 When this man heard that Jesus had come from Judea to Galilee, he went to Him and implored Him to descend and heal his son, who was at the point of death.

48 Jesus said to him, “Unless you people behold signs and wonders, you will never believe.”

49 The official entreated, “Sir, come down before my child dies.”

50 Jesus replied, “Go. Your son will live.” The man believed the word Jesus had spoken to him and departed.

51 As he was descending, his servants met him, announcing that his boy was alive. 52 He inquired of them the hour at which his son had begun to recover, and they replied, “Yesterday at the seventh hour the fever abated.”

53 Then the father recognized that this was the very hour in which Jesus had told him, “Your son will live”; and he himself believed, together with his whole household.

54 This was the second sign Jesus performed after coming from Judea into Galilee.

Solomon Constructs His Royal Palace

1 It required thirteen years for Solomon to complete the construction of his palace. 2 He built the House of the Forest of Lebanon one hundred cubits in length, fifty cubits in width, and thirty cubits in height; it rested upon four rows of cedar columns that supported cedar beams.

3 Cedar paneling covered the structure above the beams resting on the columns—forty-five beams, fifteen in each row. 4 Three rows of high windows faced one another in three tiers. 5 Every doorway and rectangular window had stone frames, with the windows aligned opposite one another in three tiers.

6 He constructed the colonnade fifty cubits long and thirty cubits wide, with a portico in front, together with a canopy and pillars before them. 7 He also built the hall for the throne—the Hall of Justice—where he would judge; it was paneled with cedar from floor to ceiling. 8 The palace in which he would reside, set farther back, was built in the same manner. Solomon likewise made a palace for Pharaoh’s daughter, whom he had married, comparable to this hall.

9 All these buildings were constructed from costly stones, cut to size and trimmed with saws, both inside and outside, from the foundation to the coping, including the great courtyard. 10 The foundations were laid with large, costly stones, some ten cubits long and others eight cubits long. 11 Above them were high-grade stones, cut to size, together with cedar beams. 12 The great courtyard was surrounded by a wall of three courses of dressed stone and one course of trimmed cedar beams, as were the inner courtyard and the portico of the house of the LORD.

Hiram of Tyre Fashioned the Temple’s Bronze Work

13 Now King Solomon sent to bring Hiram from Tyre. 14 He was the son of a widow from the tribe of Naphtali, and his father was a man of Tyre, a craftsman in bronze. Hiram was filled with wisdom, understanding, and skill for every kind of bronze work, so he came to King Solomon and performed all his work.

15 He cast two bronze pillars, each eighteen cubits high and twelve cubits in circumference. 16 He also made two capitals of cast bronze to set upon the tops of the pillars, each five cubits high. 17 For the capitals on top of the pillars he fashioned a network of lattice work and wreaths of chain work, seven for each capital. 18 He made the pillars with two rows of pomegranates around each network to cover the capitals atop the pillars; he did the same for the second capital.

19 The capitals atop the pillars in the portico were shaped like lilies and were four cubits high. 20 The capitals on the two pillars also had pomegranates on the upper part, beside the rounded projection next to the network; two hundred pomegranates were arranged in rows around both capitals. 21 Thus he erected the pillars at the portico of the temple. He named the right pillar Jachin and the left pillar Boaz. 22 The capitals on top were shaped like lilies, so the work on the pillars was completed.

23 He also made the Sea of cast bronze—ten cubits from brim to brim, circular in shape, and five cubits high. A line of thirty cubits measured around it. 24 Below the rim, ornamental gourds encircled it, ten per cubit, encircling the Sea all around; the gourds were cast in two rows as part of the Sea. 25 It stood on twelve oxen—three facing north, three facing west, three facing south, and three facing east—with all their hindquarters toward the center. 26 It was a handbreadth thick, and its rim was like the rim of a cup, like a lily blossom. It held two thousand baths.

The Ten Bronze Stands and Their Basins

27 In addition, he made ten bronze stands; each was four cubits long, four cubits wide, and three cubits high. 28 This was the design of the stands: they had side panels, and the panels were between the crossbars. 29 On the panels between the crossbars were lions, oxen, and cherubim; on the crossbars above and below the lions and oxen were wreathlike decorations. 30 Each stand had four bronze wheels and bronze axles, and its four corners had supports. Under the basin were cast supports, each bearing wreaths on either side. 31 The opening inside each stand’s rim was one cubit deep, with a round opening like a pedestal one and a half cubits wide. The rim bore carvings, and its frames were square, not round.

32 The four wheels were under the panels, and the axles of the wheels were attached to the stand; each wheel was one and a half cubits in diameter. 33 The wheels were made like chariot wheels: their axles, rims, spokes, and hubs were all cast metal. 34 Each stand had four handles, one on each corner, projecting from the stand. 35 At the top of each stand was a circular band half a cubit high, with supports and panels cast as part of the stand. 36 He engraved cherubim, lions, and palm trees on the surfaces of the supports and panels wherever there was space, with wreaths all around. 37 This is how he made the ten stands, each cast from the same mold, of the same size and shape.

38 He also made ten bronze basins, each holding forty baths; each basin was four cubits across and was placed on one of the ten stands. 39 He set five stands on the south side of the temple and five on the north, and he set the Sea on the southeast corner of the temple.

Completion of the Temple Furnishings

40 Additionally, Hiram made the pots, shovels, and sprinkling bowls. So Hiram completed all the work he had undertaken for King Solomon in the house of the LORD: the two pillars; the two bowl-shaped capitals atop the pillars; the two sets of network to cover the two bowl-shaped capitals atop the pillars; the four hundred pomegranates for the two networks—two rows of pomegranates for each network to cover the two bowl-shaped capitals atop the pillars; the ten stands; the ten basins on the stands; the Sea; the twelve oxen under the Sea; and the pots, shovels, and sprinkling bowls. All these articles that Hiram made for the house of the LORD were of polished bronze.

46 The king had them cast in clay in the plain of the Jordan between Succoth and Zarethan. 47 Solomon left all these vessels unweighed, because there were so many; the weight of the bronze could not be measured.

48 Solomon also made all the furnishings for the house of the LORD: the golden altar; the golden table for the Bread of the Presence; 49 the lampstands of pure gold—five on the right side and five on the left before the inner sanctuary; the flowers, lamps, and tongs of gold; 50 the basins, wick trimmers, sprinkling bowls, ladles, and censers of pure gold; and the gold hinges for the doors of the inner sanctuary and for the doors of the main hall. 51 So all the work that King Solomon had done for the house of the LORD was completed. Then Solomon brought in the things his father David had dedicated—the silver, gold, and furnishings—and he put them in the treasuries of the house of the LORD.

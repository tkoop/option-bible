Daniel's Rise in Darius's Court

1 It pleased Darius to appoint one hundred twenty satraps to govern the entire kingdom. 2 Over them he placed three administrators, among whom Daniel was one, so that these satraps might render account to them and the king suffer no loss. 3 Because an extraordinary spirit was in Daniel, he distinguished himself above the administrators and satraps, and the king considered setting him over the whole realm.

The Conspiracy Against Daniel

4 Then the administrators and satraps sought to find grounds for a charge against Daniel in connection with the kingdom, but they could discover no corruption or negligence, for he was faithful, and no error or fault was found in him. 5 Consequently, these men concluded, "We will find no grounds for a charge against this Daniel unless it concerns the law of his God."

6 Thereupon these administrators and satraps assembled before the king and addressed him, "O King Darius, live forever!"

7 "All the kingdom's administrators, governors, satraps, counselors, and provincial officials have consulted together to establish a royal statute and to enforce a decree that for thirty days anyone who petitions any god or man except you, O king, shall be cast into the lions' den. 8 Now, O king, establish the decree and sign the document, so that it cannot be altered, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be revoked."

9 Therefore King Darius signed the written decree.

Daniel's Defiance and the King's Anguish

10 When Daniel learned that the document had been signed, he went to his house, whose upper chamber had windows open toward Jerusalem; three times each day he knelt, prayed, and gave thanks to his God, just as he had done previously. 11 Then these men assembled and found Daniel petitioning and pleading before his God.

12 They then approached the king and asked, "Did you not sign a decree that for thirty days anyone who petitions any god or man except you, O king, shall be thrown into the lions' den?"

The king answered, "The matter is established, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be revoked."

13 They answered the king, "Daniel, one of the exiles from Judah, pays no attention to you, O king, or to the decree you have signed, but petitions his God three times a day."

14 When the king heard these words, he was deeply distressed and set his mind on rescuing Daniel; until sunset he strove to save him.

15 Then these men went as a group to the king and said, "Know, O king, that it is the law of the Medes and Persians that no decree or ordinance the king establishes may be changed."

16 So the king gave the order, and Daniel was brought and cast into the lions' den. The king said to Daniel, "May your God, whom you serve continually, deliver you!"

The Lions' Den and Its Aftermath

17 A stone was brought and placed over the mouth of the den, and the king sealed it with his signet ring and with the signet rings of his nobles, so that nothing might be changed concerning Daniel. 18 Then the king went to his palace and spent the night fasting; no entertainment was brought before him, and sleep fled from him. 19 At dawn, at first light, the king arose and hurried to the lions' den.

20 As he approached the den, he called out to Daniel in an anguished voice, "Daniel, servant of the living God, has your God, whom you serve continually, been able to rescue you from the lions?"

21 Daniel replied to the king, "O king, live forever! 22 My God sent His angel and shut the lions' mouths, so that they have not harmed me, because I was found innocent before Him; and also before you, O king, I have done no wrong."

23 The king was exceedingly glad and commanded that Daniel be taken up from the den. So Daniel was taken up, and no injury was found on him, because he had trusted in his God. 24 Then the king commanded that the men who had accused Daniel be brought, and they, their children, and their wives were thrown into the lions' den; before they reached the bottom, the lions overpowered them and crushed all their bones.

25 Then King Darius wrote to all peoples, nations, and languages dwelling in all the earth, "May your peace abound!

26 "I issue a decree that in every dominion of my kingdom people are to tremble and fear before the God of Daniel, for He is the living God, enduring forever; His kingdom will not be destroyed, and His dominion will never end. 27 He delivers and rescues; He performs signs and wonders in heaven and on earth; He has delivered Daniel from the power of the lions."

28 So Daniel prospered during the reign of Darius and during the reign of Cyrus the Persian.

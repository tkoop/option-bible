Timothy Joins Paul’s Mission

1 Paul came to Derbe and Lystra, where he found a disciple named Timothy, the son of a Jewish woman who believed and a Greek father. 2 The brothers in Lystra and Iconium spoke well of him. 3 Paul wanted Timothy to accompany him, so he took him and circumcised him because of the Jews in those regions, for everyone knew his father was Greek. 4 As they traveled from city to city, they delivered the decisions reached by the apostles and elders in Jerusalem for the believers to observe. 5 So the churches were strengthened in faith and grew in number each day.

The Holy Spirit Redirects the Mission

6 They passed through the Phrygian and Galatian region because the Holy Spirit had forbidden them to speak the word in Asia. 7 When they reached Mysia, they attempted to enter Bithynia, but the Spirit of Jesus did not permit them. 8 So they passed by Mysia and descended to Troas. 9 During the night Paul had a vision: a Macedonian man stood before him, urging him, “Cross over to Macedonia and help us.” 10 After Paul saw the vision, we immediately sought to leave for Macedonia, concluding that God had called us to proclaim the gospel to them.

11 We sailed from Troas on a straight course to Samothrace, and the next day we reached Neapolis. 12 From there we went to Philippi, the leading city of that district of Macedonia, and we stayed there several days.

Lydia’s Household and the Slave Girl’s Deliverance

13 On the Sabbath we went outside the gate to the river, where we supposed there was a place of prayer, and we sat down and spoke to the women who had gathered. 14 One of them was Lydia, a seller of purple cloth from Thyatira, a worshiper of God. The Lord opened her heart to attend to what Paul was saying. 15 After she and her household were baptized, she urged us, saying, “If you have judged me faithful to the Lord, come and stay at my house.” And she persuaded us.

16 As we were going to the place of prayer, a slave girl met us. She had a spirit of divination and brought her masters great profit by fortune-telling. 17 She followed Paul and us, crying out, “These men are servants of the Most High God, who proclaim to you the way of salvation.” 18 She kept doing this for many days. Finally Paul, greatly annoyed, turned and said to the spirit, “I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her.” And it left her at that very hour.

Imprisonment and the Jailer’s Conversion

19 When her masters saw that their hope of profit had gone, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace before the rulers. 20 They brought them before the magistrates and said, “These men are Jews, and they are disturbing our city. 21 They advocate customs that are not lawful for us Romans to accept or practice.” 22 The crowd joined in the attack against them, and the magistrates ordered them stripped and beaten with rods. 23 After inflicting many blows, they threw them into prison and commanded the jailer to guard them securely. 24 Receiving such an order, the jailer put them in the inner prison and fastened their feet in the stocks.

25 At midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them. 26 Suddenly a great earthquake shook the prison so violently that its foundations were loosened; immediately all the doors opened, and everyone’s chains fell off. 27 The jailer woke, saw the prison doors open, drew his sword, and was about to kill himself, thinking the prisoners had escaped.

28 But Paul shouted in a loud voice, “Do not harm yourself, for we are all here.”

29 The jailer called for lights, rushed in, and trembling fell down before Paul and Silas. 30 He brought them out and asked, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?”

31 They answered, “Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved—you and your household.” 32 Then they spoke the word of the Lord to him and to all who were in his house. 33 At that hour of the night he took them and washed their wounds; immediately he and all his family were baptized. 34 He brought them into his house, set food before them, and rejoiced greatly with his entire household because they had believed in God.

35 When day came, the magistrates sent the officers with the order, “Release those men.”

36 The jailer reported these words to Paul: “The magistrates have sent orders to release you. Now come out and go in peace.”

37 But Paul said to them, “They beat us publicly, though we are uncondemned Roman citizens, and now they want to send us away secretly? No indeed! Let them come themselves and escort us out.”

38 The officers reported these words to the magistrates, and they were afraid when they heard that Paul and Silas were Roman citizens. 39 So they came and apologized to them, brought them out, and asked them to leave the city. 40 After leaving the prison, Paul and Silas went to Lydia’s house, and when they saw the brothers, they encouraged them and departed.

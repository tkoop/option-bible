Job Replies to Eliphaz

1 Then Job answered: 2 “If only my anguish were weighed, and my calamity laid together in the balances, 3 for then it would outweigh the sand of the seas—no wonder my words have been rash.

4 “For the arrows of the Almighty are in me, and my spirit drinks their venom; the terrors of God are arrayed against me. 5 Does a wild donkey bray over grass, or an ox bellow over its fodder? 6 Is tasteless food eaten without salt, or is there flavor in the white of an egg? 7 My soul refuses to touch them; they are like loathsome food to me.

Job Longs for Release from His Suffering

8 “Oh, that I might have my request, and that God would grant my desire! 9 That it would please God to crush me, to loose His hand and cut me off! 10 Then I would still have this consolation—my joy in unrelenting pain—that I have not denied the words of the Holy One.

Job Questions His Strength and His Friends’ Faithfulness

11 “What is my strength, that I should wait? And what is my end, that I should prolong my life? 12 Is my strength the strength of stones, or is my flesh bronze? 13 Is my help not within me, and has sound wisdom been driven from me? 14 A despairing man should have the kindness of his friend, even if he forsakes the fear of the Almighty. 15 My brothers have proved as treacherous as a brook, like torrents of brooks that vanish— 16 darkened with ice, in which snow hides itself; 17 but when they are scorched, they vanish, and in the heat they are consumed from their place. 18 The caravans turn aside from their path; they wander into the wasteland and perish. 19 The caravans of Tema looked for them; the traveling companies of Sheba waited for them. 20 They were confounded because they had trusted; they came there and were ashamed.

Job Confronts His Friends’ Accusations

21 “For now you have become nothing; you see a terror and are afraid. 22 Did I ever say, ‘Give me something,’ or, ‘Pay a ransom for me from your wealth’? 23 Or, ‘Deliver me from the hand of the adversary, or redeem me from the hand of the ruthless’? 24 Teach me, and I will be silent; make me understand wherein I have erred. 25 How forceful are honest words! But what does your reproof actually prove? 26 Do you intend to reprove mere words, the speeches of a desperate man, which are only wind? 27 You would even cast lots for the fatherless and barter away your friend. 28 But now, be pleased to look at me, for I will not lie to your face. 29 Turn back, I beg you; let there be no injustice; turn back, for my righteousness still stands. 30 Is there injustice on my tongue? Can my palate not discern mischief?”

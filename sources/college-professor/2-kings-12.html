The Reign of Joash and the Reform of the Temple

1 In the seventh year of Jehu, Joash became king, and he reigned forty years in Jerusalem; his mother was Zibiah of Beersheba. 2 Joash did what was right in the eyes of the LORD throughout all his days, because Jehoiada the priest instructed him. 3 Nevertheless, the high places were not removed; the people continued to sacrifice and burn incense on the high places.

The Funding and Repair of the Temple

4 Joash said to the priests, “All the money brought into the house of the LORD as sacred offerings—the money collected in the census, each man’s assessed valuation, and every voluntary contribution that anyone decides to bring into the house of the LORD— 5 let each priest receive it from his acquaintances and apply it to repairing every defect found in the house.”

6 Yet by the twenty-third year of King Joash, the priests had not repaired the damage to the house.

7 So King Joash summoned Jehoiada the priest and his fellow priests and said to them, “Why have you not repaired the damage to the house? Now, therefore, take no more money from your acquaintances, but hand it over for the repair of the house.” 8 The priests agreed that they would collect no more money from the people and would no longer repair the damage to the house.

9 Then Jehoiada the priest took a chest, bored a hole in its lid, and set it beside the altar on the right side, as one enters the house of the LORD. The priests who guarded the threshold put into it all the money brought into the house of the LORD.

10 Whenever they saw that there was a great deal of money in the chest, the king’s secretary and the high priest would come up, count the money found in the house of the LORD, and tie it up in bags. 11 They then gave the counted money to those supervising the work on the house of the LORD, who disbursed it to the carpenters and builders working on the house. 12 It went to the masons and stonecutters, and for the purchase of timber and hewn stone to repair the damage to the house, and for every other expense incurred in restoring the house.

13 However, no silver basins, wick trimmers, sprinkling bowls, trumpets, or any articles of gold or silver were made from the money brought into the house of the LORD. 14 Instead, it was given to the workmen, and they used it to repair the house of the LORD. 15 Moreover, no accounting was demanded from the men into whose hands the money was entrusted for disbursement to the workmen, because they acted faithfully.

16 The money from guilt offerings and sin offerings was not brought into the house of the LORD; it belonged to the priests.

Hazael’s Threat and Joash’s Tribute

17 At that time Hazael king of Aram went up and fought against Gath and captured it; then Hazael set his face to go up against Jerusalem. 18 Joash king of Judah took all the sacred objects that his ancestors Jehoshaphat, Jehoram, and Ahaziah, kings of Judah, had consecrated, together with his own sacred objects and all the gold found in the treasuries of the house of the LORD and of the royal palace, and sent them to Hazael king of Aram, who then withdrew from Jerusalem.

19 The rest of the acts of Joash, and all that he did, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah?

The Assassination of Joash

20 His servants conspired against him and struck Joash down at Beth-millo, on the road that descends to Silla. 21 His servants Jozabad son of Shimeath and Jehozabad son of Shomer struck him, and he died; they buried him with his ancestors in the City of David, and his son Amaziah reigned in his place.

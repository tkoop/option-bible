Paul and Barnabas Proclaim the Gospel in Iconium

1 In Iconium, they entered the synagogue of the Jews together and spoke so persuasively that a great multitude of both Jews and Greeks believed. 2 The Jews who refused to believe, however, stirred up the Gentiles and embittered their minds against the brethren. 3 So they remained there for a considerable time, speaking boldly in reliance upon the Lord, who bore witness to the word of His grace by granting that signs and wonders be performed through their hands. 4 The populace of the city was divided: some aligned themselves with the Jews, while others sided with the apostles. 5 When both the Gentiles and the Jews, together with their rulers, made a concerted attempt to mistreat them and stone them,

Flight to Lycaonia and the Healing at Lystra

6 they perceived the danger and fled to the Lycaonian cities of Lystra and Derbe, together with the surrounding countryside, 7 and there they continued to proclaim the good news. 8 In Lystra sat a man powerless in his feet, lame from his mother's womb, who had never walked. 9 He listened to Paul as he spoke; and Paul, fixing his gaze upon him and perceiving that he possessed faith to be healed, 10 cried out in a loud voice, "Stand upright on your feet!" And he sprang up and began to walk.

The Crowd Mistakes the Apostles for Gods

11 When the crowds saw what Paul had done, they raised their voices in the Lycaonian language, crying out, "The gods have descended to us in human form." 12 They designated Barnabas as Zeus and Paul as Hermes, because he was the principal spokesman. 13 The priest of Zeus, whose shrine stood before the city, brought oxen and garlands to the gates and intended to offer sacrifice together with the crowds.

14 Upon hearing this, the apostles Barnabas and Paul tore their garments and rushed into the crowd, crying aloud, "Men, why are you doing these things? We too are men of like nature with you, bringing you good news so that you may turn from these futile things to the living God, who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and all that is in them. 16 In past generations He allowed all the nations to walk in their own ways; 17 yet He did not leave Himself without witness, for He did good, giving you rains from heaven and fruitful seasons, filling your hearts with food and gladness."

18 Even with these words, they scarcely restrained the crowds from offering sacrifice to them.

Paul Is Stoned and the Churches Are Strengthened

19 Then Jews arrived from Antioch and Iconium; after persuading the crowds, they stoned Paul and dragged him outside the city, presuming him to be dead. 20 Yet while the disciples stood around him, he rose and re-entered the city; and on the following day he departed with Barnabas for Derbe. 21 After proclaiming the good news in that city and making many disciples, they returned to Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch, 22 strengthening the souls of the disciples, encouraging them to remain steadfast in the faith, and declaring that it is through many afflictions that we must enter the kingdom of God. 23 Having appointed elders for them in every church, and having prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord in whom they had believed.

24 Passing through Pisidia, they came to Pamphylia. 25 After speaking the word in Perga, they descended to Attalia. 26 From there they sailed to Antioch, the place from which they had been commended to the grace of God for the work they had now completed. 27 Upon their arrival, they assembled the church and reported all that God had accomplished through them, and how He had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles. 28 And they remained there for a considerable time with the disciples.

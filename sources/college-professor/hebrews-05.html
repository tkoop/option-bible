The Human High Priesthood and Its Limitations

1 Indeed, every high priest is appointed from among human beings and acts on their behalf in matters relating to God, so that he may present both gifts and sacrifices for sins. 2 He is able to deal gently with those who are ignorant and astray, since he himself is likewise encompassed by weakness. 3 Consequently, he is obligated to offer sacrifices for sins on his own behalf as well as on behalf of the people. 4 Moreover, no one arrogates this honor to himself; rather, one receives it only when summoned by God, just as Aaron was.

Christ’s Appointment as High Priest after the Order of Melchizedek

5 In like manner, Christ did not arrogate to Himself the glory of becoming High Priest; instead, He was so designated by the One who declared to Him, "You are My Son; today I have begotten You." 6 And, as He likewise declares in another place, "You are a Priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek." 7 During the days of His earthly flesh, He offered up prayers and entreaties, accompanied by vehement cries and tears, to the One who was able to save Him from death; and He was heard on account of His reverence. 8 Although He was the Son, He learned obedience through the things He suffered. 9 And, having been made perfect, He became the source of eternal salvation for all who obey Him, 10 having been designated by God a High Priest according to the order of Melchizedek.

The Call to Spiritual Maturity

11 Concerning Him we have a great deal to say, and it is difficult to articulate, since you have become sluggish in hearing. 12 For though by this time you ought to be teachers, you have need again for someone to teach you the elementary principles of the oracles of God, and you have come to need milk and not solid food. 13 For everyone who partakes of milk is unskilled in the word of righteousness, for he is an infant. 14 But solid food belongs to the mature, whose faculties, through habitual exercise, have been trained to distinguish good from evil.

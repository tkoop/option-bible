Jesus Withdraws from Judea and Journeys through Samaria

1 Now, upon Jesus’ discernment that the Pharisees had been apprised of His making and baptizing a greater multitude of disciples than John— 2 although Jesus Himself did not baptize, but His disciples did— 3 He withdrew from Judea and set out once more for Galilee. 4 It was incumbent upon Him to traverse Samaria.

The Samaritan Woman at Jacob’s Well

5 Thus He arrived at a Samaritan town called Sychar, contiguous to the tract of land that Jacob had bequeathed to his son Joseph. 6 Jacob’s well was situated there; and Jesus, fatigued by the journey, seated Himself beside the well. It was about the sixth hour.

7 A Samaritan woman came to draw water. Jesus said to her, “Give Me a draught.” 8 (His disciples, meanwhile, had gone into the city to procure provisions.)

9 The Samaritan woman said to Him, “How is it that you, a Jew, solicit a drink from me, a Samaritan woman?” (For Jews do not consort with Samaritans.)

10 Jesus answered her, “If you apprehended the gift of God and the identity of Him who says to you, ‘Give Me a drink,’ you would have petitioned Him, and He would have bestowed upon you living water.”

11 The woman said to Him, “Sir, you possess no vessel with which to draw, and the well is deep. Whence, then, do you obtain this living water? 12 Are you greater than our father Jacob, who gave us the well and drank from it himself, together with his sons and his cattle?”

13 Jesus said to her, “Everyone who drinks of this water shall thirst again, 14 but whoever drinks of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst again; rather, the water that I shall give him shall become within him a spring of water leaping up to eternal life.”

15 The woman said to Him, “Sir, bestow upon me this water, so that I may no longer thirst nor come hither to draw.”

16 Jesus said to her, “Go, summon your husband, and return hither.”

17 The woman answered Him, “I have no husband.”

Jesus said to her, “You have spoken aptly in saying, ‘I have no husband’; 18 for you have had five husbands, and the man you now have is not your husband. What you have uttered is true.”

19 The woman said to Him, “Sir, I discern that you are a prophet. 20 Our fathers worshiped upon this mountain, yet you affirm that in Jerusalem is the locus where one ought to worship.”

21 Jesus said to her, “Woman, believe Me, an hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem. 22 You worship what you do not know; we worship what we know, for salvation is of the Jews. 23 But an hour is coming, and now is, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth; for indeed the Father seeks such worshipers. 24 God is Spirit, and those who worship Him must worship in spirit and truth.”

25 The woman said to Him, “I know that Messiah is coming (the One called Christ). When He comes, He will declare all things to us.”

26 Jesus said to her, “I who speak to you am He.”

27 At that moment His disciples returned. They were astonished that He was conversing with a woman, yet no one said, “What do you seek?” or, “Why are you speaking with her?”

28 So the woman left her water jar, went into the city, and said to the people, 29 “Come, see a man who told me everything I ever did. Could this be the Christ?” 30 They left the city and were coming to Him.

The Harvest and the Samaritan Confession

31 Meanwhile the disciples urged Him, saying, “Rabbi, eat.”

32 But He said to them, “I have nourishment to eat of which you are unaware.”

33 So the disciples said to one another, “Has anyone brought Him something to eat?”

34 Jesus said to them, “My nourishment is to do the will of Him who sent Me and to complete His work. 35 Do you not say, ‘Four months remain, and then the harvest comes’? Behold, I say to you, lift up your eyes and observe that the fields are already white for harvest. 36 Even now the reaper receives wages and gathers fruit unto eternal life, so that sower and reaper may rejoice together. 37 For in this instance the proverb proves true: ‘One sows, and another reaps.’ 38 I dispatched you to reap that for which you did not toil. Others have toiled, and you have entered into their toil.”

39 Many Samaritans from that city believed in Him on account of the woman’s testimony: “He told me everything I ever did.”

40 So when the Samaritans came to Him, they entreated Him to remain with them, and He abode there two days. 41 And many more believed on account of His word.

42 They said to the woman, “It is no longer on account of your words that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves and know that this is indeed the Savior of the world.”

43 After the two days He departed for Galilee. 44 (For Jesus Himself had attested that a prophet is without honor in his own country.) 45 So when He came to Galilee, the Galileans received Him, having witnessed all that He had done in Jerusalem during the festival; for they themselves had gone up to the festival.

The Healing of the Royal Official’s Son

46 Thus He came again to Cana in Galilee, where He had turned the water into wine. Now at Capernaum there was a certain royal official whose son was ailing. 47 When this man heard that Jesus had come from Judea to Galilee, he went to Him and implored Him to descend and heal his son, for he was at the brink of death.

48 So Jesus said to him, “Unless you see signs and wonders, you will by no means believe.”

49 The official said to Him, “Sir, descend before my child dies.”

50 Jesus said to him, “Go; your son will live.” The man believed the word that Jesus spoke to him and went his way.

51 As he was descending, his servants met him and reported that his son was recovering. 52 So he inquired of them the hour at which he had begun to amend, and they told him, “Yesterday at the seventh hour the fever abated.”

53 The father recognized that this was the very hour in which Jesus had said to him, “Your son will live.” And he believed, together with his whole household. 54 This was the second sign that Jesus performed after coming from Judea into Galilee.

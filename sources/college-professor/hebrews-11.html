The Ontological Ground of Faith and the Testimony of the Ancients

1 Now faith is the hypostatic reality of things hoped for and the rigorous demonstration of realities unseen. 2 For by it the elders received their attestation. 3 By faith we apprehend that the ages were divinely framed by the utterance of God, so that what is visible did not come into being from things apparent.

4 By faith Abel offered to God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain, through which he was attested to be righteous, God Himself bearing witness to his gifts; and through that faith he still speaks, although he is dead. 5 By faith Enoch was translated so that he should not see death, and he was not found, because God had translated him; for before his translation he had been attested to have pleased God. 6 And without faith it is impossible to please Him, for whoever approaches God must believe that He exists and that He becomes a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him. 7 By faith Noah, having been warned concerning things not yet seen, moved with reverent fear, prepared an ark for the salvation of his household, by which he condemned the world and became heir of the righteousness that is according to faith.

The Pilgrimage of the Patriarchs and the Heavenly City

8 By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out to a place that he was to receive as an inheritance, obeyed and went out, not knowing where he was going. 9 By faith he sojourned in the land of promise as in a foreign country, dwelling in tents with Isaac and Jacob, fellow heirs of the same promise; 10 for he was looking forward to the city that has foundations, whose architect and builder is God. 11 By faith Sarah herself, though barren and past the season of age, received power to conceive seed, because she judged Him faithful who had promised. 12 Therefore from one man—and he, moreover, as good as dead—there were born descendants as numerous as the stars of heaven and as innumerable as the sand upon the seashore.

13 All these died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them and greeted them from afar, and having confessed that they were strangers and sojourners upon the earth. 14 For those who speak in this manner make it manifest that they are seeking a homeland. 15 And if indeed they had been mindful of that country from which they had gone out, they would have had opportunity to return; 16 but now they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed to be called their God, for He has prepared for them a city.

Faith in Testing, Deliverance, and Prophetic Hope

17 By faith Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac, and he who had received the promises offered up his only begotten son, 18 concerning whom it had been spoken, "In Isaac shall your seed be called," 19 having reasoned that God is able to raise up even from the dead, from which he also received him back in a figure. 20 By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau concerning things to come. 21 By faith Jacob, as he was dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph and worshipped, leaning upon the top of his staff. 22 By faith Joseph, when he was dying, made mention of the exodus of the sons of Israel and gave commandment concerning his bones.

23 By faith Moses, when he was born, was hidden for three months by his parents, because they saw that the child was beautiful, and they were not afraid of the king's decree. 24 By faith Moses, when he had grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter, 25 choosing rather to suffer affliction with the people of God than to enjoy the passing pleasures of sin, 26 esteeming the reproach borne for Christ greater riches than the treasures of Egypt; for he looked to the recompense. 27 By faith he left Egypt, not fearing the wrath of the king, for he endured as seeing Him who is invisible. 28 By faith he kept the Passover and the sprinkling of blood, lest the destroyer of the firstborn should touch them. 29 By faith they passed through the Red Sea as through dry land, but the Egyptians, when they attempted to do so, were swallowed up. 30 By faith the walls of Jericho fell down, after they had been encircled for seven days. 31 By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish with those who were disobedient, having received the spies with peace.

The Catalogue of Faith's Triumphs and Torments

32 And what more shall I say? For time will fail me if I recount Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, and the prophets, 33 who through faith subdued kingdoms, wrought righteousness, obtained promises, stopped the mouths of lions, 34 quenched the power of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, were strengthened out of weakness, became mighty in war, and put to flight foreign armies. 35 Women received back their dead by resurrection; and others were tortured, not accepting deliverance, that they might obtain a better resurrection. 36 Others experienced trial of mockings and scourgings, yes, moreover of bonds and imprisonment. 37 They were stoned, they were sawn in two, they were slain with the sword; they went about in sheepskins and goatskins, being destitute, afflicted, and mistreated—of whom the world was not worthy—wandering in deserts and mountains and in dens and caves of the earth.

38 of whom the world was not worthy—wandering in deserts and mountains and in dens and caves of the earth. 39 And all these, having been attested through faith, did not receive the promise, 40 since God had foreseen something better concerning us, that they should not be made perfect apart from us.

Jacob's Settlement in Canaan and the Preferential Love for Joseph

1 Jacob took up residence in the land of his father's sojournings, within Canaan. 2 These are the generations of Jacob: Joseph, at seventeen years of age, was tending the flock alongside his brothers; while still an adolescent, he was in the company of the sons of Bilhah and Zilpah, his father's wives; and Joseph conveyed an unfavorable report concerning them to their father.

3 Now Israel loved Joseph above all his sons, for he was the son of his old age, and he fashioned for him a robe of many colors. 4 When his brothers perceived that their father loved him more than any of them, they conceived a hatred for him and could not address him with civility.

Joseph's Dream-Visions and the Brothers' Intensifying Animosity

5 Joseph dreamed a dream and recounted it to his brothers, whereupon they hated him still more. 6 He said, “Hear, I pray you, this dream that I have dreamed: behold, we were binding sheaves in the field, and, lo, my sheaf arose and stood upright; and, behold, your sheaves gathered round about and bowed down to my sheaf.”

7 His brothers said to him, “Shall you indeed reign over us? Or shall you indeed exercise dominion over us?” And they hated him yet more on account of his dreams and his words.

8 He dreamed yet another dream and told it to his brothers, saying, “Behold, I have dreamed a further dream: behold, the sun and the moon and eleven stars bowed down to me.”

9 He told it to his father and to his brothers; and his father rebuked him and said to him, “What is this dream that you have dreamed? Shall I and your mother and your brothers indeed come to bow ourselves down to you to the earth?”

10 His brothers envied him, but his father kept the saying in mind.

Joseph Dispatched to His Brothers in Shechem

11 His brothers went to pasture their father's flock in Shechem.

12 Israel said to Joseph, “Are not your brothers pasturing in Shechem? Come now, and I will send you to them.”

And he said, “Here I am.”

13 He said to him, “Go now, see whether it is well with your brothers and with the flock, and bring me back word.” So he sent him out of the valley of Hebron, and he came to Shechem.

14 A man found him, and, behold, he was wandering in the field; and the man asked him, “What are you seeking?”

15 He said, “I am seeking my brothers. Please tell me where they are pasturing.”

16 The man said, “They have departed from here, for I heard them say, ‘Let us go to Dothan.’” So Joseph went after his brothers and found them at Dothan.

The Conspiracy, the Sale, and Jacob's Grief

17 They saw him from afar, and before he drew near to them they conspired against him to put him to death.

18 They said to one another, “Behold, this dreamer is coming. 19 Come now, let us kill him and cast him into one of the pits, and say, ‘A wild beast has devoured him’; then we shall see what becomes of his dreams.”

20 Reuben heard it and rescued him from their hand, saying, “Let us not take his life.” 21 Reuben said to them, “Shed no blood; cast him into this pit in the wilderness, but lay no hand on him”—intending to rescue him from their hand and restore him to his father.

22 When Joseph came to his brothers, they stripped him of his robe, the robe of many colors that was upon him. 23 They seized him and cast him into the pit; the pit was empty, and there was no water in it.

24 They sat down to eat bread; and they lifted up their eyes and looked, and, behold, a caravan of Ishmaelites was coming from Gilead, with their camels bearing spices, balm, and myrrh, on their way to carry them down to Egypt.

25 Judah said to his brothers, “What profit is there if we slay our brother and conceal his blood? 26 Come, let us sell him to the Ishmaelites, and let not our hand be upon him; for he is our brother, our own flesh.” And his brothers listened.

27 Midianite traders passed by; they drew Joseph up out of the pit and sold him for twenty shekels of silver to the Ishmaelites, and they brought Joseph into Egypt.

28 Reuben returned to the pit, and, behold, Joseph was not in the pit; and he tore his garments.

29 He returned to his brothers and said, “The boy is gone; and I—where shall I go?”

30 They took Joseph's robe and slaughtered a young goat and dipped the robe in the blood. 31 They sent the robe of many colors and brought it to their father, and said, “We have found this; know now whether it is your son's robe or not.”

32 He recognized it and said, “It is my son's robe; an evil beast has devoured him; Joseph is without doubt torn in pieces.”

33 Jacob tore his garments and put sackcloth upon his loins and mourned for his son many days. 34 All his sons and all his daughters rose up to comfort him, but he refused to be comforted, and said, “I will go down to Sheol mourning for my son.” And his father wept for him.

35 The Midianites sold him in Egypt to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh, the captain of the guard.

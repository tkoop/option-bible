From Slavery to Sonship in the Fullness of Time

1 Now I say that for as long as the heir remains a minor, he differs in no respect from a slave, although he is lord of all; 2 rather, he is under guardians and stewards until the date appointed by the father; 3 so likewise we, when we were minors, were enslaved beneath the elemental principles of the world.

4 Yet when the fullness of time had come, God sent forth His Son, born of a woman, born under the law, 5 in order that He might redeem those under the law, in order that we might receive adoption as sons. 6 And because you are sons, God sent forth the Spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying, “Father, O Father!” 7 Consequently, you are no longer a slave, but a son; and if a son, then an heir through God.

The Peril of Reverting to Elemental Bondage

8 But formerly, not knowing God, you were enslaved to beings that by nature are no gods; 9 but now, having come to know God—or rather, having been known by God—how is it that you turn back again to the weak and beggarly elemental principles, unto which you desire to be enslaved anew? 10 You observe days and months and seasons and years. 11 I fear for you, lest somehow I have labored among you in vain.

Paul’s Affectionate Remonstrance and Remembrance

12 I entreat you, brothers, become as I am, because I also became as you are. You did me no wrong. 13 And you know that because of weakness of the flesh I formerly proclaimed the gospel to you. 14 And your ordeal occasioned by my bodily weakness you did not despise nor spit out, but received me as an angel of God, as Christ Jesus. 15 Where, then, is your blessedness? For I testify to you that, if possible, having plucked out your eyes, you would have given them to me. 16 So have I become your enemy by speaking truth to you?

17 They zealously court you, yet not honorably; rather, they desire to shut you out, that you may be zealous for them. 18 But it is commendable to be zealous in a good cause always, and not only when I am present with you. 19 My little children, for whom I am again in travail until Christ is formed in you, 20 I could wish to be present with you now and to alter my voice, because I am perplexed concerning you.

The Allegory of the Two Covenants

21 Tell me, you who desire to be under the law: do you not hear the law? 22 For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one by the bondwoman and one by the free woman. 23 But the one by the bondwoman was born according to the flesh, whereas the one by the free woman was born through promise. 24 These things are to be taken allegorically: for these women are two covenants, one from Mount Sinai, bearing children unto slavery, which is Hagar. 25 Now Hagar is Mount Sinai in Arabia, and she corresponds to the present Jerusalem, for she serves together with her children. 26 But the Jerusalem above is free, and she is our mother. 27 For it is written: “Rejoice, barren woman who does not bear; break forth and cry aloud, you who do not travail; for more are the children of the desolate than of her who has the husband.” 28 And you, brothers, are children of promise according to Isaac. 29 But just as then the one born according to the flesh persecuted the one according to the Spirit, so also now. 30 But what does the Scripture say? “Cast out the bondwoman and her son, for the son of the bondwoman will not inherit with the son of the free woman.” 31 Therefore, brothers, we are not children of the bondwoman, but of the free woman.

The People’s Murmuring and the Fire at Taberah

1 Now the people were like those who complain bitterly, acting wickedly in the hearing of the LORD; and when the LORD heard, His anger was kindled, and fire from the LORD burned among them and consumed the outskirts of the camp. 2 Then the people cried out to Moses, and Moses prayed to the LORD, and the fire subsided. 3 So he named that place Taberah, because the fire of the LORD had burned among them.

The Craving for Meat and Moses’ Anguish

4 Meanwhile, the rabble among them craved a craving; the Israelites also turned back and wept, saying, “Who will feed us meat? 5 We remember the fish that we ate in Egypt for nothing—the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions, and the garlic. 6 But now our appetite is dried up; there is nothing at all except this manna before our eyes.”

7 Now the manna was like coriander seed, and its appearance was like the appearance of bdellium. 8 The people went about and gathered it, ground it in hand mills or crushed it in mortars, boiled it in pots, and made cakes of it; and its taste was like the taste of cakes baked with oil. 9 When the dew fell upon the camp by night, the manna fell with it.

10 Moses heard the people weeping throughout their families, each man at the entrance of his tent; and the anger of the LORD burned fiercely, and Moses was also displeased.

11 So Moses said to the LORD, “Why have You treated Your servant so badly? Why have I not found favor in Your eyes, that You have laid the burden of all this people upon me?

12 “Did I conceive all this people? Did I give birth to them, that You should say to me, ‘Carry them in your bosom, as a nurse carries a nursing child, to the land You swore to their fathers’?

13 “Where am I to get meat to give to all this people? For they weep before me, saying, ‘Give us meat, that we may eat.’

14 “I am not able to carry all this people alone, because it is too heavy for me.

15 “If You are going to deal with me like this, please kill me now—if I have found favor in Your eyes—and do not let me see my own ruin.”

The Seventy Elders and the Spirit’s Distribution

16 Then the LORD said to Moses, “Gather to Me seventy men from the elders of Israel whom you know to be elders and officers of the people; bring them to the Tent of Meeting, and let them take their stand there with you.

17 “I will come down and speak with you there, and I will take some of the Spirit that is upon you and put it upon them; and they shall bear the burden of the people with you, so that you will not bear it alone.

18 “And say to the people, ‘Consecrate yourselves for tomorrow, for you shall eat meat, because you have wept in the hearing of the LORD, saying, “Who will feed us meat? For it was better for us in Egypt!” Therefore the LORD will give you meat, and you shall eat.’

19 “You shall eat not one day, nor two days, nor five days, nor ten days, nor twenty days,

20 “but a whole month—until it comes out of your nostrils and becomes loathsome to you—because you have rejected the LORD, who is among you, and have wept before Him, saying, ‘Why did we ever leave Egypt?’”

21 But Moses said, “I am among six hundred thousand men on foot, and You have said, ‘I will give them meat, that they may eat for a whole month!’

22 “Are all the flocks and herds to be slaughtered for them, or are all the fish of the sea to be gathered for them, that it might suffice for them?”

23 Then the LORD said to Moses, “Is the LORD’s power insufficient? Now you shall see whether My word will come to pass for you or not.”

24 So Moses went out and spoke the words of the LORD to the people; and he gathered the seventy elders of the people and stationed them around the tent.

25 Then the LORD came down in the cloud and spoke to him, and He took some of the Spirit that was upon Moses and put it upon the seventy elders; and when the Spirit rested upon them, they prophesied, but they did not do so again.

26 Now two men had remained in the camp—one named Eldad and the other Medad—and the Spirit rested upon them; they were among those enrolled, but they had not gone out to the tent, and they prophesied in the camp.

27 And a young man ran and told Moses, saying, “Eldad and Medad are prophesying in the camp.”

28 Then Joshua son of Nun, the attendant of Moses from his youth, answered and said, “My lord Moses, restrain them!”

29 But Moses said to him, “Are you jealous on my behalf? Would that all the LORD’s people were prophets, that the LORD would put His Spirit upon them!”

30 Then Moses returned to the camp, he and the elders of Israel.

The Quail and the Plague at Kibroth-hattaavah

31 Then a wind went out from the LORD and drove quail in from the sea, and scattered them over the camp to a depth of about two cubits on the ground, for about a day’s journey in every direction around the camp.

32 And the people rose up all that day and all that night and all the next day and gathered the quail; the one who gathered least gathered ten homers; and they spread them out for themselves all around the camp.

33 While the meat was still between their teeth, before it was consumed, the anger of the LORD burned against the people, and the LORD struck the people with a very great plague.

34 So the name of that place was called Kibroth-hattaavah, because there they buried the people who had craved.

35 From Kibroth-hattaavah the people journeyed to Hazeroth, and they remained at Hazeroth.

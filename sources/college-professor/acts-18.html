Paul's Arrival and Ministry in Corinth

1 After these events, having departed from Athens, Paul arrived in Corinth. 2 There he encountered a certain Jew named Aquila, a native of Pontus, who had recently arrived from Italy with his wife Priscilla, because Claudius had commanded all the Jews to withdraw from Rome; and he attached himself to them. 3 Because they were of the same trade, he remained with them and labored, for they were tentmakers by profession. 4 On every Sabbath he reasoned in the synagogue, seeking to persuade both Jews and Greeks.

5 When Silas and Timothy descended from Macedonia, Paul was constrained by the word, testifying to the Jews that Jesus is the Christ. 6 But when they opposed him and reviled him, he shook out his garments as a symbolic repudiation of them and said to them, "The responsibility for your own destruction rests upon your own heads; I am innocent; from this moment forward I shall go to the Gentiles."

7 Departing from there, he entered the house of a certain man named Titus Justus, whose house adjoined the synagogue and who worshiped God. 8 Crispus, the ruler of the synagogue, believed in the Lord together with his entire household; and many of the Corinthians, upon hearing, believed and were baptized.

9 During the night the Lord said to Paul in a vision, "Do not fear, but speak and do not be silent, 10 for I am with you, and no one will assault you to injure you, for I have many people in this city." 11 And he settled there for a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.

Paul Before the Proconsul Gallio

12 While Gallio was proconsul of Achaia, the Jews rose up unanimously against Paul and brought him before the judgment seat, 13 saying, "This man persuades people to worship God contrary to the law."

14 But as Paul was about to open his mouth, Gallio said to the Jews, "If indeed there were some injustice or wicked villainy, O Jews, I would reasonably bear with you; 15 but if these are disputes about doctrine and nomenclature and your own law, see to them yourselves; I do not wish to be judge of such matters." 16 And he drove them from the judgment seat. 17 Then all seized Sosthenes, the ruler of the synagogue, and beat him before the judgment seat; and none of these things concerned Gallio.

Paul's Departure, Vow, and Return to Antioch

18 Paul, having remained for a considerable span of additional days, took leave of the brothers, and, having shaved his head at Cenchreae because he was under a vow, sailed for Syria, accompanied by Priscilla and Aquila. 19 They reached Ephesus, where he left them; and he himself entered the synagogue and reasoned with the Jews. 20 When they urged him to remain for a longer time, he did not consent, 21 but took leave, saying, "I will return to you again, if God wills," and set sail from Ephesus.

22 Landing at Caesarea, he went up and greeted the church, then went down to Antioch. 23 After spending some time there, he departed, journeying successively through the Galatian country and Phrygia, strengthening all the disciples.

Apollos's Instruction and Ministry

24 A certain Jew named Apollos, an Alexandrian by birth, came to Ephesus; he was an eloquent man, mighty in the Scriptures. 25 He had been instructed in the way of the Lord, and, being fervent in spirit, he spoke and taught accurately the things concerning Jesus, though he knew only the baptism of John. 26 He began to speak boldly in the synagogue; but when Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and expounded to him the way of God more accurately. 27 When he desired to cross into Achaia, the brothers encouraged him and wrote to the disciples to receive him; and upon arriving, he greatly assisted those who had believed through grace. 28 For he vigorously refuted the Jews in public, demonstrating through the Scriptures that Jesus is the Christ.

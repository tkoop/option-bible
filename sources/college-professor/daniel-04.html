Nebuchadnezzar's Proclamation to All Peoples

1 King Nebuchadnezzar to all peoples, nations, and languages dwelling throughout the whole earth: may your peace be multiplied. 2 It has seemed good to me to declare the signs and wonders that the Most High God has wrought toward me. 3 How great are His signs, and how mighty His wonders! His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and His dominion endures from generation to generation.

The King's Troubling Dream and the Failure of Babylon's Wise Men

4 I, Nebuchadnezzar, was tranquil in my house and flourishing in my palace. 5 I beheld a dream that terrified me, and the cogitations of my bed, together with the visions of my head, dismayed me. 6 Therefore I issued a decree summoning all the wise men of Babylon into my presence, that they might disclose to me the interpretation of the dream. 7 Then the magicians, enchanters, Chaldeans, and astrologers entered, and I rehearsed the dream before them, yet they proved unable to disclose its interpretation to me. 8 At last Daniel—called Belteshazzar after my god, and in whom resides the spirit of the holy gods—came into my presence, and I recounted the dream to him. 9 “O Belteshazzar, chief of the magicians, since I know that the spirit of the holy gods is in you and that no secret is too arduous for you, declare to me the visions of my dream that I beheld, and their interpretation.”

The Vision of the Great Tree and Daniel's Interpretation

10 The visions of my head upon my bed were these: I looked, and there stood a tree in the midst of the earth, exceedingly tall. 11 The tree grew mighty and robust, its crown reaching to the heavens, and it was visible to the ends of all the earth. 12 Its foliage was fair and its fruit abundant, and food for all was in it; beneath it the beasts of the field took shelter, the birds of the heavens nested in its branches, and all flesh was nourished from it.

13 As I lay upon my bed, in the visions of my head, I beheld a watcher—a holy one—descending from heaven. 14 He cried out with a mighty voice and declared, “Cut down the tree, lop off its branches, strip away its foliage, and scatter its fruit; let the beasts flee from beneath it and the birds from its branches. 15 Yet leave the stump of its roots in the earth, bound with a band of iron and bronze, amid the tender grass of the field; let him be drenched with the dew of heaven, and let his lot be with the beasts among the grass of the earth. 16 Let his mind be altered from that of a man, and let the mind of a beast be given to him, and let seven times pass over him. 17 This sentence is decreed by the watchers, and the demand is pronounced by the holy ones, so that the living may know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of humanity, grants it to whomever He wills, and exalts over it the lowliest of men.”

18 This dream I, King Nebuchadnezzar, beheld; and you, O Belteshazzar, declare its interpretation, for none of the wise men of my kingdom can disclose it to me, yet you are able, because the spirit of the holy gods is in you. 19 Then Daniel, whose name is Belteshazzar, stood appalled for a time, and his thoughts troubled him. The king answered and said, “Belteshazzar, let neither the dream nor its interpretation dismay you.”

Belteshazzar answered and said, “My lord, would that the dream concerned your foes and its interpretation your enemies! 20 The tree that you saw, which grew and became strong, whose top reached to heaven and which was visible to the ends of all the earth— 21 whose foliage was fair and whose fruit was abundant, and in which was food for all; beneath it the beasts of the field took shelter, and in its branches the birds of the heavens dwelt— 22 it is you, O king, who have grown and become mighty: Your greatness has increased until it reaches heaven, and your dominion extends to the ends of the earth. 23 And inasmuch as the king beheld a watcher, a holy one, descending from heaven and saying, ‘Cut down the tree and destroy it, yet leave the stump of its roots in the earth, bound with iron and bronze, amid the tender grass of the field; let him be drenched with the dew of heaven, and let his lot be with the beasts of the field, until seven times pass over him’— 24 this is the interpretation, O king: it is a decree of the Most High that has come upon my lord the king— 25 that you will be driven from among men, and your dwelling will be with the beasts of the field; you will be made to eat grass like an ox, and you will be drenched with the dew of heaven, and seven times will pass over you, until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of humanity and grants it to whomever He wills. 26 And as it was commanded that the stump of the tree's roots be left, so your kingdom will be preserved for you once you acknowledge that Heaven rules. 27 Therefore, O king, let my counsel be acceptable to you: redeem your sins by righteousness and your iniquities by showing mercy to the oppressed, if perhaps there may be a prolonging of your tranquility.”

The Fulfillment of the Decree and the King's Restoration

28 All of this befell King Nebuchadnezzar.

29 Twelve months later, he was strolling upon the roof of the royal palace in Babylon, 30 and the king declared, “Is this not Babylon the great, which I myself have built as a royal residence by the might of my power and for the glory of my majesty?”

31 While the word was yet upon the king's lips, a voice fell from heaven: “O King Nebuchadnezzar, to you it is declared: the kingdom has been taken from you, 32 and you will be driven from among men, and your dwelling will be with the beasts of the field; you will be made to eat grass like an ox, and seven times will pass over you, until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of humanity and grants it to whomever He wills.”

33 Immediately the word was fulfilled upon Nebuchadnezzar: he was driven from among men, ate grass like an ox, and his body was drenched with the dew of heaven until his hair grew as long as eagles' feathers and his nails became like birds' claws.

34 At the end of those days I, Nebuchadnezzar, lifted my eyes to heaven, and my understanding returned to me; I blessed the Most High, and praised and honored Him who lives forever; His dominion is an everlasting dominion, and His kingdom endures from generation to generation. 35 All the inhabitants of the earth are reckoned as nothing; He does according to His will among the host of heaven and among the inhabitants of the earth, and none can restrain His hand or say to Him, “What have You done?” 36 At that very time my understanding returned to me, and for the glory of my kingdom my majesty and splendor were restored to me; my counselors and nobles sought me out, and I was re-established in my kingdom, with even greater greatness added to me. 37 Now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise, exalt, and honor the King of heaven, for all His works are truth and His ways are justice; and those who walk in pride He is able to humble.

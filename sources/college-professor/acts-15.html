The Question of Gentile Circumcision

1 Certain men who had descended from Judea were instructing the brothers that, unless you are circumcised according to the Mosaic rite, you cannot be saved. 2 When a considerable dissension and disputation arose between Paul and Barnabas and these men, the community appointed Paul and Barnabas, along with several others from their number, to ascend to Jerusalem to consult the apostles and elders concerning this question. 3 Having been commended by the church, they traversed Phoenicia and Samaria, recounting the conversion of the Gentiles and bringing great joy to all the brothers. 4 Upon their arrival in Jerusalem, they were received by the church, the apostles, and the elders, and they reported all that God had accomplished through them. 5 Yet certain believers from the Pharisaic party arose, insisting that it is incumbent upon the Gentiles to be circumcised and to be charged with observing the law of Moses.

Peter's Testimony and James's Judgment

6 The apostles and elders therefore convened to deliberate upon this matter. 7 After extensive debate, Peter rose and addressed them: "Brothers, you know that from ancient days God selected among you that the Gentiles should hear the word of the gospel from my lips and believe. 8 And God, the Knower of hearts, bore witness by giving the Holy Spirit to them just as He did to us. 9 He made no discrimination between us and them, having purified their hearts by faith. 10 Now then, why do you test God by imposing upon the disciples' necks a yoke that neither we nor our fathers were able to bear? 11 Rather, we believe that we are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, in precisely the same manner as they are." 12 The entire assembly then fell silent and attended to Barnabas and Paul as they narrated the signs and wonders that God had wrought among the Gentiles through them.

13 When they had concluded, James responded: "Brothers, attend to me. 14 Simon has explained how God first concerned Himself to take from the Gentiles a people for His name. 15 And the words of the prophets concur with this, as it is written: '16 After these things I will return and rebuild the fallen tent of David; I will restore its ruins and erect it again, 17 so that the remnant of humanity may seek the Lord, together with all the Gentiles upon whom My name has been invoked, says the Lord, who accomplishes these things.' 18 Known to God from eternity are all His works. 19 Therefore I judge that we ought not to harass the Gentiles who are turning to God, 20 but rather to write to them, directing them to abstain from pollutions associated with idols, from sexual immorality, from what is strangled, and from blood. 21 For Moses has had, from ancient generations, those who proclaim him in every city, since he is read in the synagogues every Sabbath."

The Apostolic Letter to the Gentiles

22 Then it seemed good to the apostles and elders, together with the whole church, to select men from among themselves and send them to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas—namely Judas called Barsabbas, and Silas, men of prominence among the brothers. 23 They dispatched the following epistle: "The apostles and elders, your brothers, to the Gentile believers in Antioch, Syria, and Cilicia: Greetings. 24 Since we have heard that certain persons who went out from us, though we gave them no mandate, have disturbed you and unsettled your minds with their words, 25 it has seemed good to us, having reached unanimity, to select men and send them to you together with our beloved Barnabas and Paul— 26 men who have hazarded their lives for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. 27 We have therefore sent Judas and Silas, who will also communicate the same things by word of mouth. 28 For it seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to impose upon you no further burden beyond these essentials: 29 that you abstain from things offered to idols, from blood, from strangled meat, and from sexual immorality. You will do well to keep yourselves from these. Farewell."

30 Thus the men were dismissed and went down to Antioch; and having assembled the congregation, they delivered the letter. 31 When the people had read it, they rejoiced over the encouragement. 32 Judas and Silas, being prophets themselves, exhorted the brothers at length and strengthened them. 33 After spending some time there, they were sent away in peace by the brothers to return to those who had commissioned them. 35 But Paul and Barnabas remained in Antioch, teaching and proclaiming the word of the Lord together with many others.

Paul and Barnabas Part Company

36 After some days Paul said to Barnabas, "Let us return and visit the brothers in every city where we proclaimed the word of the Lord, to see how they are faring." 37 Barnabas desired to take with them John called Mark; 38 but Paul judged it inexpedient to take along the one who had withdrawn from them in Pamphylia and had not accompanied them in the work. 39 A sharp provocation arose, so that they separated from one another; Barnabas took Mark and sailed to Cyprus, 40 whereas Paul selected Silas and departed, having been commended by the brothers to the grace of the Lord. 41 He passed through Syria and Cilicia, strengthening the churches.

The Imperative of Heeding the Salvation Proclaimed

1 Consequently, it behooves us to attend with exceptional vigilance to the tidings we have received, lest we should drift away from them. 2 For if the discourse promulgated through angels attained firm validity, and every transgression and act of disobedience received a commensurate retribution, 3 how shall we possibly escape, having neglected so great a salvation, a salvation that, having first been announced through the Lord, was confirmed to us by those who heard Him, 4 while God Himself bore corroborative witness through signs, portents, and manifold miracles, together with distributions of the Holy Spirit apportioned according to His sovereign volition?

The Subjection of the Coming World and the Psalmist's Testimony

5 For it was not to angels that He subjected the inhabited world that is yet to come, the realm concerning which we are discoursing. 6 But somewhere a witness attests, saying, 7 "What is man, that You should be mindful of him? Or the son of man, that You should visit him with Your solicitude? You have made him a little lower than the angels; You have crowned him with glory and honor. 8 You have subjected all things beneath his feet." For in subjecting everything to him, He left nothing outside his dominion; yet at present we do not behold all things as having been subjected to him.

Jesus, the Pioneer of Salvation, Perfected Through Suffering

9 But we do behold Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels, now crowned with glory and honor on account of His having suffered death, so that, by the grace of God, He might taste death on behalf of all. 10 For it was eminently fitting for Him—for whom all things exist and through whom all things are accomplished—in bringing many sons to glory, to perfect through sufferings the pioneer of their salvation. 11 For both the One who sanctifies and those who are sanctified are all of one origin; for which cause He is not ashamed to call them brothers, 12 saying, "I will proclaim Your name to My brothers; in the midst of the assembly I will sing hymns of praise to You."

13 And again, "I will place My confidence in Him." And again, "Behold, I and the children whom God has bestowed upon Me."

The Incarnation as the Means of Deliverance from Death's Bondage

14 Since, then, the children are participants in flesh and blood, He Himself in like manner partook of the same nature, in order that through death He might render powerless the one who holds the dominion of death—that is, the devil— 15 and might emancipate those who, through dread of death, had been held in bondage throughout the entirety of their lives. 16 For assuredly He does not assist angels, but He assists the offspring of Abraham. 17 Therefore it was incumbent upon Him to be made like His brothers in every respect, so that He might become a merciful and faithful high priest in the things pertaining to God, to make propitiation for the sins of the people. 18 For because He Himself has suffered, having been subjected to temptation, He is able to succor those who are being tempted.

A Sabbath Healing and the Question of the Law

1 It came to pass on a Sabbath that He went to break bread in the residence of one of the principal men among the Pharisees, while they scrutinized Him. 2 And behold, before Him stood a certain man suffering from dropsy. 3 And Jesus, in response, said to the jurists and Pharisees, “Is it permissible to heal on the Sabbath, or not?” 4 Yet they held their peace; and taking hold of the man, He healed him and dismissed him.

5 And He said to them, “Which of you, if his son or ox should fall into a well on the Sabbath day, would not straightway draw him out?” 6 And they were unable to answer Him regarding these things.

Humility and the Ethics of Hospitality

7 Now He observed how the invited guests were selecting the chief seats, and He addressed them in a parable, saying:

8 “When you are invited by someone to a wedding feast, do not recline in the place of honor, lest a guest more distinguished than you has been invited by him, 9 and the one who invited both you and him approaches and says to you, ‘Give this man your place,’ whereupon you begin, with shame, to occupy the lowest seat. 10 But when you are invited, go and recline in the lowest place, so that when your host comes he may say to you, ‘Friend, ascend higher’; then you will be honored before all who recline at table with you. 11 For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted.”

12 And He said also to the one who had invited Him, “When you host a luncheon or dinner, do not summon your friends, nor your brothers, nor your kinsmen, nor affluent neighbors, lest they likewise invite you in return, and you thereby receive your recompense. 13 But when you host a banquet, invite the indigent, the maimed, the lame, and the blind; 14 and you will be blessed, because they cannot repay you; for you shall be repaid at the resurrection of the righteous.”

15 When one of those reclining at table with Him heard these things, he said to Him, “Blessed is everyone who shall eat bread in the kingdom of God.”

The Parable of the Great Banquet

16 But He said to him, “A certain man was giving a great banquet and invited many. 17 And at the hour of the banquet he sent his servant to say to those who had been invited, ‘Come, for all things are now ready.’ 18 Yet they all, with one accord, began to excuse themselves. The first said to him, ‘I have purchased a field, and I must go out and inspect it; I beg you to excuse me.’ 19 And another said, ‘I have purchased five yoke of oxen, and I am going to test them; I beg you to excuse me.’ 20 And another said, ‘I have married a wife, and therefore I cannot come.’ 21 And the servant, returning, reported these things to his master. Then the master of the house, incensed, said to his servant, ‘Go out swiftly into the streets and lanes of the city, and bring in here the indigent, the maimed, the blind, and the lame.’ 22 And the servant said, ‘Sir, what you commanded has been done, and still there is room.’ 23 And the master said to the servant, ‘Go out into the highways and hedges and compel them to enter, so that my house may be filled.’ 24 For I say to you, none of those men who were invited shall taste my banquet.’”

The Cost of Discipleship

25 Now great multitudes were journeying with Him, and turning He said to them,

26 “If anyone comes to Me and does not hate his own father and mother and wife and children and brothers and sisters—yes, and even his own life—he cannot be My disciple. 27 Whoever does not bear his own cross and follow after Me cannot be My disciple. 28 For which of you, intending to build a tower, does not first sit down and reckon the cost, whether he has sufficient to complete it? 29 Otherwise, when he has laid a foundation and is unable to finish, all who behold it begin to deride him, 30 saying, ‘This man began to build and was not able to finish.’ 31 Or what king, marching out to engage another king in battle, does not first sit down and deliberate whether he is able, with ten thousand, to withstand him who comes against him with twenty thousand? 32 And if not, while the other is yet far off, he sends an embassy and requests terms of peace. 33 So therefore, none of you who does not renounce all that he possesses can be My disciple. 34 Salt is good; but if salt has lost its savor, with what shall it be seasoned? 35 It is fit neither for the soil nor for the dunghill; they cast it out. He who has ears to hear, let him hear.”

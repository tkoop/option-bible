Ben-Hadad Besieges Samaria

1 Ben-Hadad, king of Aram, mustered his entire host and, accompanied by thirty-two kings together with cavalry and chariots, ascended and besieged Samaria, waging war against it. 2 He dispatched messengers into the city to Ahab, king of Israel, 3 and they declared, “Thus says Ben-Hadad: ‘Your silver and gold are mine; the finest of your wives and children are mine as well.’”

4 The king of Israel answered, “As you have said, my lord the king, I am yours, and all that I have is yours.”

5 The messengers returned and said, “Thus says Ben-Hadad: ‘I did send to you, saying, “Give me your silver and gold, your wives and your children.”’ 6 Yet about this time tomorrow I will send my servants to you, and they shall search your house and the houses of your servants; they shall lay hands on whatever is precious in your eyes and carry it away.’”

7 Then the king of Israel summoned all the elders of the land and said, “Consider and see how this man seeks mischief, for he sent to me for my wives, my children, my silver, and my gold, and I did not refuse him.”

8 All the elders and the people said to him, “Do not listen to him, and do not consent.”

9 So he said to Ben-Hadad’s messengers, “Tell my lord the king, ‘All that you first sent for I will do, but this I cannot do.’” The messengers departed and brought back his answer.

10 Ben-Hadad sent to him and said, “May the gods do so to me, and more also, if the dust of Samaria suffice for handfuls for all the people who follow me.”

11 The king of Israel answered, “Tell him, ‘Let not the one who girds on his armor boast as though he were taking it off.’”

12 When Ben-Hadad heard this message, while he and the kings were drinking in the booths, he said to his servants, “Take your positions.” So they took their positions against the city.

The LORD Promises Victory through the Provincial Commanders

13 Then a prophet drew near to Ahab, king of Israel, and said, “Thus says the the LORD: ‘Have you seen all this great multitude? Behold, I will give it into your hand today, and you shall know that I am the the LORD.’”

14 Ahab said, “By whom?”

He said, “Thus says the the LORD: ‘By the young men of the provincial commanders.’”

Ahab said, “Who shall open the battle?”

He said, “You.”

15 So he mustered the young men of the provincial commanders, and they numbered two hundred and thirty-two; after them he mustered all the people of Israel, seven thousand.

16 They went out at noon, while Ben-Hadad was drinking himself drunk in the booths, he and the thirty-two kings who were helping him.

17 The young men of the provincial commanders went out first. Ben-Hadad sent out scouts, and they reported to him, “Men have come out from Samaria.”

18 He said, “Whether they have come out for peace or for war, seize them alive.”

19 So these went out from the city—the commanders at the head and the army behind them.

20 Each struck down his man, and Aram fled while Israel pursued them; Ben-Hadad, king of Aram, escaped on horseback with the horsemen.

21 The king of Israel went out and struck the horses and chariots, inflicting a great slaughter upon Aram.

The Second Campaign in the Plain

22 Then the prophet drew near to the king of Israel and said to him, “Go, strengthen yourself; consider and see what you must do, for at the return of the year the king of Aram will come up against you.”

23 The servants of the king of Aram said to him, “Their god is a god of the hills; therefore they were stronger than we. But let us fight against them in the plain, and surely we shall be stronger than they.”

24 “Do this,” they advised: “remove the kings from their commands and appoint governors in their place. 25 Muster for yourself an army like the army that you lost, horse for horse and chariot for chariot, and fight against them in the plain; surely then you will be stronger than they.” He listened to their counsel and did so.

26 At the return of the year Ben-Hadad mustered Aram and went up to Aphek to fight against Israel.

27 The Israelites were mustered and provisioned, and they went to meet them; the Israelites encamped before them like two little flocks of goats, while Aram filled the land.

28 Then the man of God drew near to the king of Israel and said, “Thus says the the LORD: ‘Because Aram has said, “The the LORD is a god of the hills and not a god of the valleys,” I will give all this great multitude into your hand, and you shall know that I am the the LORD.’”

29 They encamped opposite one another for seven days, and on the seventh day the battle was joined; the Israelites struck down one hundred thousand foot soldiers of Aram in a single day.

30 The rest fled into the city of Aphek, and the wall fell upon twenty-seven thousand of those who remained. Ben-Hadad fled into the city and hid himself in an inner chamber.

Ben-Hadad’s Surrender and Ahab’s Fatal Mercy

31 His servants said to him, “Behold, we have heard that the kings of the house of Israel are merciful kings. Let us put sackcloth on our loins and ropes on our heads and go out to the king of Israel; perhaps he will spare your life.”

32 So they girded sackcloth on their loins and put ropes on their heads, and they came to the king of Israel and said, “Your servant Ben-Hadad says, ‘Please let me live.’”

He said, “Is he still alive? He is my brother.”

33 The men took it as a favorable omen and quickly seized upon his words, saying, “Yes, your brother Ben-Hadad.”

Then he said, “Go, bring him.” Ben-Hadad came out to him, and Ahab had him mount the chariot.

34 Ben-Hadad said to him, “I will restore the cities that my father took from your father, and you shall have streets for yourself in Damascus, as my father had in Samaria; and I will let you go upon this covenant.” So Ahab made a covenant with him and let him go.

35 By the word of the the LORD, one of the sons of the prophets said to his companion, “Strike me, please.” But the man refused to strike him.

36 Then he said to him, “Because you did not obey the voice of the the LORD, behold, as soon as you depart from me a lion shall strike you down.” As soon as he departed from him, a lion found him and struck him down.

37 He found another man and said, “Strike me, please.” The man struck him and wounded him.

38 Then the prophet went and stood by the road, waiting for the king, having disguised himself with a bandage over his eyes.

39 As the king passed by, he cried out to the king, “Your servant went out into the midst of the battle, and behold, a man turned aside and brought a man to me and said, ‘Guard this man; if he is missing, your life shall be for his life, or else you shall pay a talent of silver.’”

40 “While your servant was busy here and there, he was gone.”

The king of Israel said to him, “So shall your judgment be; you yourself have decided it.”

41 Then he hastily removed the bandage from his eyes, and the king of Israel recognized him as one of the prophets.

42 He said to him, “Thus says the the LORD: ‘Because you have let go out of your hand the man whom I devoted to destruction, your life shall be for his life, and your people for his people.’”

43 The king of Israel went to his house sullen and vexed, and he came to Samaria.

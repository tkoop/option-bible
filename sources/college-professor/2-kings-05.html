Naaman's Affliction and the Captive Maiden's Counsel

1 Naaman, commander of the army of the king of Aram, was a man of great consequence before his master and highly honored, because through him the the LORD had granted deliverance to Aram; he was a mighty man of valor, yet the man was leprous. 2 Now the Arameans had gone out in raiding bands and had taken captive a little girl from the land of Israel, and she served before Naaman's wife.

3 She said to her mistress, “Oh, that my master might stand before the prophet who is in Samaria; then he would recover him from his leprosy.”

4 He went in and told his master, saying, “Thus and thus has the girl from the land of Israel spoken.”

5 The king of Aram said, “Go now, and I will send a letter to the king of Israel.” So he departed, taking in his hand ten talents of silver, six thousand pieces of gold, and ten changes of garments. 6 He brought the letter to the king of Israel, which read, “Now, when this letter reaches you, behold, I have sent to you Naaman my servant, that you may cure him of his leprosy.”

7 When the king of Israel read the letter, he tore his garments and said, “Am I God, to kill and to make alive, that this man sends to me to cure a man of leprosy? Consider now, and see that he seeks an occasion against me.”

The Prophet's Summons and the Commander's Healing

8 When Elisha, the man of God, heard that the king of Israel had torn his garments, he sent to the king, saying, “Why have you torn your garments? Let him come now to me, that he may know that there is a prophet in Israel.” 9 So Naaman came with his horses and chariot and stood at the door of Elisha's house.

10 Elisha sent a messenger to him, saying, “Go and wash in the Jordan seven times, and your flesh shall be restored to you, and you shall be clean.”

11 But Naaman was wroth and went away, saying, “Behold, I thought that he would surely come out to me, and stand, and call on the name of the the LORD his God, and wave his hand over the place, and cure the leper.” 12 “Are not Abanah and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? Could I not wash in them and be clean?” So he turned and went away in rage.

13 His servants came near and spoke to him, “My father, if the prophet had spoken to you a great thing, would you not have done it? How much more, then, when he says to you, ‘Wash, and be clean’?”

14 So he went down and dipped himself seven times in the Jordan, according to the word of the man of God; his flesh returned like the flesh of a little child, and he was clean. 15 Then he returned to the man of God, he and all his company, and stood before him and said, “Behold, now I know that there is no God in all the earth but in Israel; now, therefore, please accept a blessing from your servant.”

16 Elisha said, “As the the LORD lives, before whom I stand, I will take nothing.” And he urged him to take it, but he refused.

17 Naaman said, “If not, then please let two mule-loads of earth be given to your servant, for your servant will no longer offer burnt offering or sacrifice to other gods, but to the the LORD. 18 In this matter may the the LORD pardon your servant: when my master goes into the house of Rimmon to worship there, and he leans on my hand, and I bow myself in the house of Rimmon—when I bow myself in the house of Rimmon, may the the LORD pardon your servant in this matter.”

19 He said to him, “Go in peace.” So he departed from him a little way.

Gehazi's Covetousness and Its Leprous Recompense

20 Gehazi, the servant of Elisha the man of God, said, “Behold, my master has spared this Aramean, Naaman, by not receiving from his hand what he brought. As the the LORD lives, I will run after him and take something from him.”

21 Gehazi pursued after Naaman. When Naaman saw him running after him, he alighted from the chariot to meet him and said, “Is all well?”

22 Gehazi said, “All is well. My master has sent me, saying, ‘Behold, now two young men of the sons of the prophets have come to me from the hill country of Ephraim. Please give them a talent of silver and two changes of garments.’”

23 Naaman said, “Be pleased to take two talents.” He urged him, and bound two talents of silver in two bags, with two changes of garments, and gave them to two of his servants; and they carried them before him.

24 When he came to the hill, he took them from their hand and deposited them in the house, and sent the men away, and they departed.

25 When he had come in and stood before his master, Elisha said to him, “Where have you been, Gehazi?”

And he said, “Your servant has not gone anywhere.”

26 Elisha said, “Did not my heart go with you when the man turned from his chariot to meet you? Is it now a time to take silver, and to take garments, and olive groves, and vineyards, and sheep, and oxen, and male servants, and female servants?”

27 “Therefore the leprosy of Naaman shall cling to you and to your seed forever.” And he went out from his presence a leper, white as snow.

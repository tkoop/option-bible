Pilate's Examination and the Crowd's Demand for Crucifixion

1 Thereupon Pilate took Jesus into custody and ordered Him to be flogged. 2 The soldiers plaited a crown of thorns, set it upon His head, and arrayed Him in a purple robe. 3 They approached Him, crying, "Hail, King of the Jews!" and struck Him with their palms.

4 Pilate went out again and said to them, "Observe that I am bringing Him forth to you, so that you may ascertain that I discover no ground for accusation against Him." 5 Then Jesus came out, wearing the crown of thorns and the purple robe, and Pilate said to them, "Behold the man!"

6 When the chief priests and their officers beheld Him, they shouted, "Crucify Him! Crucify Him! Take Him and crucify yourselves," for I find no basis for a charge against Him.

Pilate said to them, "I find no basis for a charge against Him."

7 The Jews answered him, "We possess a law, and by that law He is bound to die, because He has made Himself the Son of God."

8 When Pilate heard this statement, he was seized by an even deeper fear. 9 He went back into the praetorium and said to Jesus, "Where are You from?" But Jesus gave him no answer.

10 So Pilate said to Him, "Do You refuse to speak to me? Are You unaware that I possess authority to release You and authority to crucify You?"

11 Jesus answered him, "You would possess no authority over Me at all were it not granted to you from above; consequently, the one who handed Me over to you bears the greater sin."

12 From that moment Pilate endeavored to release Him, but the Jews cried out, "If you release this man, you are no friend of Caesar; everyone who sets himself up as a king is an adversary of Caesar."

13 When Pilate heard these words, he brought Jesus out and took his seat upon the judgment tribunal at a place called the Stone Pavement, which in Hebrew is Gabbatha.

14 Now it was the Preparation Day of the Passover, and it was about the sixth hour. He said to the Jews, "Behold your King!"

15 They shouted, "Take Him away! Take Him away! Crucify Him!"

Pilate said to them, "Am I to crucify your King?"

The chief priests answered, "We have no king but Caesar."

16 Then he handed Him over to them to be crucified, and they led Jesus away.

The Crucifixion at Golgotha

17 Bearing His own cross, He went forth to the place called the Place of the Skull, which in Hebrew is Golgotha. 18 There they crucified Him, together with two others, one on each side, Jesus occupying the middle.

19 Pilate also wrote an inscription and affixed it to the cross. It read, "JESUS OF NAZARETH, THE KING OF THE JEWS." 20 Many of the Jews read this inscription, since the place where Jesus was crucified lay near the city, and it had been written in Hebrew, Latin, and Greek.

21 So the chief priests of the Jews said to Pilate, "Do not write, 'The King of the Jews,' but rather, 'This fellow said, I am King of the Jews.'"

22 Pilate answered, "What I have written, I have written."

23 After crucifying Jesus, the soldiers took His garments and divided them into four shares, one for each soldier, together with the tunic; and the tunic was seamless, woven as a single piece from top to bottom. 24 So they said to one another, "Let us not rend it, but let us cast lots for it, to determine whose it shall be"—this, so that the Scripture might be fulfilled that says, "They divided My garments among themselves and cast lots for My clothing." Thus the soldiers carried out these actions.

25 Standing by the cross of Jesus were His mother, His mother's sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene. 26 When Jesus beheld His mother and the disciple whom He loved standing near, He said to His mother, "Woman, behold your son." 27 Then He said to the disciple, "Behold your mother." And from that very hour the disciple received her into his own household.

The Death of Jesus and the Fulfillment of Scripture

28 Afterward, Jesus, knowing that all things were now accomplished, said—so that the Scripture might be fulfilled—"I thirst." 29 A vessel full of sour wine stood there; so they filled a sponge with the sour wine, affixed it to a hyssop branch, and presented it to His mouth. 30 When Jesus had received the sour wine, He said, "It is finished." Then, bowing His head, He surrendered His spirit.

31 Because it was the Preparation Day, and in order that the bodies might not remain upon the cross during the Sabbath—for that Sabbath was an especially sacred day—the Jews petitioned Pilate to have their legs broken and their bodies removed. 32 Accordingly, the soldiers came and fractured the legs of the first man and of the other crucified with Him. 33 But upon coming to Jesus and perceiving that He was already dead, they did not fracture His legs. 34 Rather, one of the soldiers pierced His side with a spear, and immediately blood and water issued forth.

35 The eyewitness has testified to this, and his testimony is true; he knows that he speaks truthfully, so that you too may believe. 36 For these events occurred so that Scripture might be fulfilled: "None of His bones shall be broken." 37 And again another Scripture declares, "They shall look upon Him whom they have pierced."

The Burial of Jesus

38 Afterward, Joseph of Arimathea, who was a disciple of Jesus—though covertly, out of fear of the Jews—petitioned Pilate for leave to remove the body of Jesus; Pilate granted permission, and Joseph came and took away His body. 39 Nicodemus, who had earlier visited Him by night, came as well, bearing a mixture of myrrh and aloes weighing about a hundred pounds.

40 They therefore took the body of Jesus and wrapped it in linen cloths with the spices, in accordance with the burial custom of the Jews. 41 Now in the place where He had been crucified there was a garden, and in that garden a new tomb in which no one had yet been entombed. 42 Because it was the Jewish Preparation Day and the tomb lay close at hand, they laid Jesus there.

The Gospel and the Witnesses to the Resurrection

1 I make known to you, brothers, the gospel I proclaimed to you, which you received and in which you stand firm, 2 through which you are being saved, provided that you retain the message I preached to you—unless, indeed, you believed in vain. 3 For I transmitted to you, as of primary importance, what I myself received: that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, 4 that He was entombed, and that He was raised on the third day, again in fulfillment of the Scriptures,

5 and that He appeared to Cephas, and subsequently to the Twelve. 6 Thereafter He appeared to more than five hundred brothers at one time, the majority of whom remain alive to this day, though some have died. 7 Then He appeared to James, and thereafter to all the apostles. 8 Last of all, as to one untimely born, He appeared to me as well.

9 For I am the least of the apostles, unworthy even to bear the apostolic name, inasmuch as I persecuted the church of God. 10 Yet by the grace of God I am what I am, and His grace toward me has not proven futile; rather, I toiled more abundantly than all of them—though it was not I, but the grace of God operative alongside me. 11 Whether, then, it be I or they, this is our proclamation, and this is the faith in which you have believed.

The Necessity and Order of the Resurrection

12 Now if it is proclaimed that Christ has been raised from the dead, how is it that some among you maintain that there is no resurrection of the dead? 13 For if the dead are not raised, then Christ Himself has not been raised either; 14 and if Christ is not raised, our proclamation is vacuous, and your faith is equally vacuous.

15 Moreover, we are discovered to be false witnesses concerning God, for we have testified that God raised Christ—whom He did not raise, if indeed the dead are not raised. 16 For if the dead remain unraised, Christ has not been raised. 17 And if Christ has not been raised, your faith is nugatory, and you remain entangled in your sins. 18 Then those who have died in Christ have perished as well. 19 If our hope in Christ is confined to this life alone, we are, of all human beings, the most pitiable.

20 But as it is, Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have died. 21 For since death came through a human being, the resurrection of the dead likewise comes through a human being. 22 For as in Adam all die, so in Christ all shall be made alive. 23 Yet each in his proper order: Christ as firstfruits; then those who belong to Christ at His advent,

24 then the consummation, when He delivers the kingdom to God the Father, having nullified every rule, authority, and power. 25 For He must reign until He has placed all enemies beneath His feet. 26 The final enemy to be annihilated is death. 27 For, as it is written, “He has subjected all things beneath His feet.” But when the Scripture says, “All things are subjected,” it is manifest that the One who subjected all things to Him is excepted. 28 When all things have been subjected to Him, then the Son Himself will also be subjected to the One who subjected all things to Him, so that God may be all in all.

The Practical Consequences of Resurrection Hope

29 Otherwise, what purpose is served by those who are baptized on behalf of the dead? If the dead are not raised at all, why are they baptized on their behalf? 30 And why do we ourselves imperil ourselves every hour? 31 I protest, brothers, by the pride I take in you in Christ Jesus our Lord: I face death daily.

32 If, from a merely human standpoint, I fought with beasts at Ephesus, what advantage accrues to me? If the dead are not raised, “Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die.” 33 Do not be misled: “Bad company corrupts good morals.” 34 Awaken to righteousness, as is your obligation, and cease sinning; for some are devoid of the knowledge of God—I say this to your shame.

The Resurrection Body and the Victory over Death

35 But someone will object, “How are the dead raised? And with what sort of body do they come?” 36 You foolish one: what you sow does not come to life unless it dies. 37 And what you sow is not the body that shall be, but a naked kernel—perhaps of wheat, or of some other grain. 38 But God bestows upon it a body as He pleases, and to each seed its own distinctive body.

39 Not all flesh is identical; rather, there is one flesh of human beings, another of beasts, another of birds, and another of fish. 40 There are heavenly bodies and earthly bodies; yet the splendor of the heavenly differs from that of the earthly. 41 The sun possesses one glory, the moon another, and the stars another; for one star differs from another in glory.

42 Thus it is with the resurrection of the dead: what is sown in corruption is raised in incorruption; 43 what is sown in dishonor is raised in glory; what is sown in weakness is raised in power; 44 what is sown a natural body is raised a spiritual body. If there is a natural body, there is also a spiritual body.

45 So too it is written, “The first man Adam became a living being”; the last Adam became a life-giving spirit. 46 Yet the spiritual is not first, but the natural; afterward comes the spiritual. 47 The first man is from the earth, earthy; the second man is from heaven. 48 As is the earthy, so are those who are earthy; and as is the heavenly, so are those who are heavenly. 49 And just as we have borne the image of the earthy, we shall also bear the image of the heavenly.

50 Now this I declare, brothers: mortal human nature cannot inherit the kingdom of God, nor does the perishable inherit the imperishable. 51 Behold, I disclose to you a mystery: we shall not all die, but we shall all be transformed, 52 in an instant, in a flash, at the last trumpet; for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised imperishable, and we shall be transformed. 53 For this perishable must be clothed with imperishability, and this mortal must be clothed with immortality.

54 But when this perishable has been clothed with imperishability, and this mortal has been clothed with immortality, then shall be fulfilled the saying that is written: “Death is swallowed up in victory.” 55 “O death, where is your victory? O death, where is your sting?” 56 The sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law; 57 but thanks be to God, who grants us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ. 58 Therefore, my beloved brothers, be steadfast and immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that your labor is not futile in the Lord.

Jacob Dispatches His Sons to Egypt for Provisions

1 Jacob perceived that grain was available in Egypt, and he said to his sons, “Why do you gaze at one another? 2 Behold, I have heard that there is grain in Egypt; go down there and procure grain for us, that we may live and not perish.”

3 So ten of Joseph’s brothers descended to purchase grain from Egypt. 4 Yet Jacob did not send Benjamin, Joseph’s brother, with his brothers, for he said, “Lest some calamity befall him.” 5 Thus the sons of Israel came to buy among the throng of arrivals, for the famine prevailed in the land of Canaan.

Joseph Recognizes His Brothers and Puts Them to the Test

6 Joseph, who served as viceroy over the land and purveyor of grain to all its inhabitants, received his brothers, and they prostrated themselves before him with their faces to the ground.

7 Joseph recognized his brothers and, concealing his identity, addressed them with severity: “Whence have you come?”

They answered, “From the land of Canaan, to purchase food.”

8 Although Joseph recognized his brothers, they did not recognize him.

9 Joseph remembered the dreams he had dreamed concerning them and said, “You are spies; you have come to discern the land’s vulnerability.”

10 They replied, “No, my lord; your servants have come to buy food.” 11 “We are all sons of one man; we are honest men, not spies.”

12 He said, “No, you have come to discern the land’s vulnerability.”

13 They said, “Your servants are twelve brothers, sons of one man in Canaan; the youngest is with our father today, and one is no more.”

14 Joseph said, “It is just as I told you: you are spies.”

15 “By this you shall be tested: as Pharaoh lives, you shall not depart from here unless your youngest brother comes here. 16 Send one of you to fetch your brother, while the rest of you remain imprisoned, so that your words may be tested and truth found in you; otherwise, as Pharaoh lives, you are spies.”

17 So he confined them in custody for three days.

18 On the third day Joseph said to them, “Do this and live, for I fear God. 19 If you are honest men, let one of your brothers be confined in prison, while the rest of you go and carry grain for the famine of your households. 20 Bring your youngest brother to me, so that your words may be verified and you may not die.” And they did so.

21 They said to one another, “Truly we are guilty concerning our brother, for we saw the anguish of his soul when he pleaded with us, and we did not listen; therefore this distress has come upon us.”

22 Reuben answered them, “Did I not say to you, ‘Do not sin against the boy’? But you would not listen. Now his blood is required of us.”

23 They did not know that Joseph understood them, for an interpreter stood between them.

24 He turned away from them and wept; then he returned and spoke to them. He took Simeon from among them and bound him before their eyes.

25 Joseph then commanded that their sacks be filled with grain, that each man’s silver be restored to his sack, and that provisions be given them for the journey; and so it was done. 26 They loaded their grain upon their donkeys and departed from there.

27 At the lodging place, one of them opened his sack to give fodder to his donkey and saw his silver in the mouth of the sack. 28 He said to his brothers, “My silver has been returned; here it is in my sack!”

Their hearts sank, and they trembled, saying to one another, “What is this that God has done to us?”

The Brothers Return to Canaan with Troubling News

29 They came to Jacob their father in the land of Canaan and recounted to him all that had happened to them. 30 “The man who is lord of the land addressed us harshly and treated us as spies of the land. 31 We replied, ‘We are honest men; we are not spies.’ 32 ‘We are twelve brothers, sons of our father; one is no more, and the youngest remains with our father in Canaan.’ 33 But the man, lord of the land, said that by this he would know whether we were honest: we were to leave one brother with him, take grain for the famine of our households, and go. 34 He further said that we must bring our youngest brother to him, so that he might know we were not spies but honest men; then he would return our brother to us and permit us to trade in the land.”

35 As they emptied their sacks, behold, each man’s bundle of silver was in his sack; and when they and their father saw the bundles of silver, they were afraid.

36 Their father Jacob said to them, “You have bereaved me: Joseph is no more, Simeon is no more, and now you would take Benjamin. All these things are against me.”

37 Reuben said to his father, “You may put my two sons to death if I do not bring him back to you. Entrust him to my care, and I will return him to you.”

38 But Jacob said, “My son shall not go down with you, for his brother is dead and he alone is left. If harm should befall him on the journey you are to take, then you would bring down my gray hair with sorrow to Sheol.”

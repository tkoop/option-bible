The Primogeniture and Lineage of Reuben

1 The sons of Reuben, Israel's firstborn—for he indeed was the firstborn—suffered the forfeiture of his primogeniture because he defiled his father's conjugal couch; consequently, his birthright was transferred to the sons of Joseph, son of Israel, and his lineage was not reckoned according to the rights of the firstborn. 2 For Judah prevailed over his brethren and produced the ruler, although the birthright itself remained Joseph's.

3 The sons of Reuben, Israel's firstborn, were Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi.

4 The descendants of Joel were Shemaiah his son, Gog his son, and Shimei his son; 5 his son was Micah, his son was Reaiah, his son was Baal; 6 his son was Beerah, whom Tiglath-pileser, king of Assyria, carried into exile, and he was a chieftain of the Reubenites.

7 His kinsmen, according to their clans and as registered in their genealogies, were Jeiel the chief and Zechariah.

8 Bela, son of Azaz, son of Shema, son of Joel, dwelt in Aroer, extending as far as Nebo and Baal-meon. 9 He also settled eastward to the entrance of the wilderness, from the Euphrates River, because their herds had multiplied in the land of Gilead.

10 In the days of Saul they waged war against the Hagrites, who fell into their power; and they occupied their tents throughout the whole eastern expanse of Gilead.

The Clans and Territory of Gad

11 The sons of Gad dwelt opposite them in the land of Bashan, as far as Salecah. 12 Joel was the chief, Shapham the second, and Janai and Shaphat were in Bashan. 13 Their kinsmen according to their fathers' houses were Michael, Meshullam, Sheba, Jorai, Jacan, Zia, and Eber—seven in all.

14 These were the sons of Abihail, son of Huri, son of Jaroah, son of Gilead, son of Michael, son of Jeshishai, son of Jahdo, son of Buz. 15 Ahi, son of Abdiel, son of Guni, was chief of their fathers' houses. 16 They settled in Gilead, in Bashan and its towns, and throughout all the pasturelands of Sharon, to their outer limits.

17 All of these were enrolled in the days of Jotham, king of Judah, and in the days of Jeroboam, king of Israel.

Martial Strength and Victory over the Hagrites

18 The Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh had valiant warriors—men bearing shield and sword, drawing the bow, and trained in war—forty-four thousand seven hundred sixty, who went out to battle.

19 They waged war against the Hagrites, Jetur, Naphish, and Nodab. 20 They were helped against them, and the Hagrites and all who were with them were delivered into their hand, because they cried out to God in the battle; and He was entreated by them, because they trusted in Him. 21 They seized their livestock—fifty thousand camels, two hundred fifty thousand sheep, two thousand donkeys—and one hundred thousand captives. 22 For many fell slain, because the warfare was of God; and they dwelt in their place until the exile.

The Half-Tribe of Manasseh: From Prosperity to Exile

23 The sons of the half-tribe of Manasseh dwelt in the land; they were numerous from Bashan to Baal-hermon, Senir, and Mount Hermon. 24 These were the heads of their fathers' houses: Epher, Ishi, Eliel, Azriel, Jeremiah, Hodaviah, and Jahdiel—mighty men of valor, men of renown, heads of their fathers' houses.

25 But they transgressed against the God of their fathers, and gave themselves to the gods of the peoples of the land, whom God had destroyed before them. 26 So the God of Israel stirred up the spirit of Pul, king of Assyria, and the spirit of Tiglath-pileser, king of Assyria, and he carried them away—the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh—and brought them to Halah, Habor, Hara, and the river Gozan, to this day.

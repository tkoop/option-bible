The Congregation Murmurs in the Wilderness of Sin

1 On the fifteenth day of the second month following their exodus from Egypt, the entire congregation of Israel journeyed from Elim and entered the wilderness of Sin, situated between Elim and Sinai. 2 There, in the desert, the whole congregation murmured against Moses and Aaron, 3 and the Israelites said to them, "If only we had perished by the hand of the the LORD in Egypt, where we sat beside the fleshpots and ate bread to satiety; for you have brought us into this wilderness to exterminate this whole assembly by famine."

The LORD's Promised Provision from Heaven

4 Then the the LORD said to Moses, "Behold, I am about to rain bread from heaven for you, and the people shall go out and gather a day's portion each day, that I may test them, whether they will walk in My law or not. 5 On the sixth day, when they prepare what they bring in, it shall be twice as much as they gather daily." 6 So Moses and Aaron said to all the Israelites, "This evening you will know that the the LORD brought you out of the land of Egypt, 7 and in the morning you will behold the glory of the the LORD, because He has heard your grumbling against Him. For what are we, that you should grumble against us?" 8 Moses added, "When the the LORD gives you meat to eat in the evening and bread to satiety in the morning, because He has heard your grumbling against Him—what are we? Your grumbling is not against us but against the the LORD." 9 Then Moses said to Aaron, "Say to the whole congregation of the Israelites, 'Draw near before the the LORD, for He has heard your grumbling.'" 10 As Aaron addressed the whole congregation, they turned toward the wilderness, and behold, the glory of the the LORD appeared in the cloud. 11 And the the LORD spoke to Moses, saying, "I have heard the grumbling of the Israelites. Say to them, 'At twilight you will eat meat, and in the morning you will be filled with bread. Then you will know that I am the the LORD your God.'" 12

Quail and Manna Descend upon the Camp

13 In the evening quail ascended and covered the camp, and in the morning a layer of dew lay around the camp. 14 When the layer of dew evaporated, there upon the surface of the wilderness was a fine, flaky substance, as fine as frost upon the ground. 15 When the Israelites saw it, they said to one another, "What is it?"—for they did not know what it was. Moses said to them, "It is the bread that the the LORD has given you to eat." 16 Moses continued, "This is what the the LORD has commanded: 'Gather of it, each according to his eating—an omer for each person, according to the number of your people; let each take for those in his tent.'" 17 The Israelites did so, and they gathered, some more and some less. 18 When they measured it by the omer, the one who gathered much had no surplus, and the one who gathered little had no deficit; each had gathered according to his eating. 19 Moses said to them, "No one is to leave any of it until morning." 20 But they did not listen to Moses; some left part of it until morning, and it bred worms and became malodorous. So Moses was incensed with them. 21 Morning by morning they gathered it, each according to his eating; and when the sun grew hot, it melted.

The Sabbath Provision and the Preserved Manna

22 On the sixth day they gathered twice as much bread—two omers for each person—and all the leaders of the congregation came and reported to Moses. 23 He said to them, "This is what the the LORD has said: 'Tomorrow is a Sabbath rest, a holy Sabbath to the the LORD. Bake what you will bake and boil what you will boil, and lay aside all that remains to be kept until morning.'" 24 So they laid it aside until morning, as Moses had commanded, and it did not become malodorous, nor were there worms in it. 25 Moses said, "Eat it today, for today is a Sabbath to the the LORD; today you will not find it in the field. 26 Six days you shall gather it, but on the seventh day, the Sabbath, there will be none." 27 Nevertheless, some of the people went out on the seventh day to gather, but they found none. 28 And the the LORD said to Moses, "How long will you refuse to keep My commandments and My laws? 29 See, the the LORD has given you the Sabbath; therefore on the sixth day He gives you bread for two days. Let each of you remain in his place; let no one go out of his place on the seventh day." 30 So the people rested on the seventh day. 31 The house of Israel called its name manna. It was like coriander seed, white, and its taste was like wafers made with honey. 32 Moses said, "This is what the the LORD has commanded: 'Keep an omer of it throughout your generations, so that they may see the bread with which I fed you in the wilderness, when I brought you out of the land of Egypt.'" 33 And Moses said to Aaron, "Take a jar, and put an omer of manna in it, and place it before the the LORD to be kept throughout your generations." 34 As the the LORD commanded Moses, so Aaron placed it before the testimony to be kept. 35 The Israelites ate the manna forty years, until they came to an inhabited land; they ate the manna until they reached the border of the land of Canaan. 36 Now an omer is a tenth of an ephah.

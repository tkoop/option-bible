Paul’s Itinerary Through Macedonia and Greece

1 Once the tumult had subsided, Paul summoned the disciples, exhorted them, took his leave with an embrace, and departed for Macedonia. 2 Having traversed those regions and encouraged the believers with copious discourse, he entered Greece. 3 Having spent three months there, and because the Jews had conspired against him as he was about to sail for Syria, he resolved to return through Macedonia.

4 Sopater son of Pyrrhus, from Berea, accompanied him, together with Aristarchus and Secundus of Thessalonica, Gaius of Derbe, Timothy, and the Asians Tychicus and Trophimus. 5 These men had gone on ahead and were awaiting us in Troas. 6 After the days of Unleavened Bread, we sailed from Philippi and, within five days, reached them in Troas, where we sojourned seven days.

A Midnight Restoration in Troas

7 On the first day of the week, when we had assembled to break bread, Paul, intending to depart the next day, discoursed with them and prolonged his address until midnight. 8 There were numerous lamps in the upper chamber where we were gathered. 9 A young man named Eutychus, seated at the window, was overpowered by profound slumber while Paul continued speaking at length; succumbing to sleep, he fell from the third story and was borne away dead.

10 Paul descended, fell upon him, embraced him, and said, “Do not be alarmed, for his life is in him.” 11 Having returned upstairs, broken bread, and partaken, and having conversed with them at considerable length until dawn, he departed. 12 They brought the boy away alive and were exceedingly comforted.

Voyage Toward Miletus

13 We went on ahead to the ship and sailed for Assos, intending to take Paul aboard there, for he had arranged matters thus, intending to travel on foot himself. 14 When he met us at Assos, we took him aboard and came to Mitylene. 15 Sailing thence, we arrived the next day opposite Chios; the following day we touched at Samos; and the day after that we reached Miletus. 16 For Paul had determined to sail past Ephesus, lest he be detained in Asia, since he was hastening to reach Jerusalem, if possible, by the day of Pentecost.

Paul’s Valedictory to the Ephesian Elders

17 From Miletus he sent to Ephesus and summoned the elders of the church. 18 When they had come to him, he said to them, “19 You know how I conducted myself among you during the entire time from the first day I set foot in Asia: I served the Lord with all humility, with tears, and amid the trials that befell me through the plots of the Jews. 20 I did not shrink from declaring to you anything profitable, nor from teaching you publicly and from house to house. 21 I testified to both Jews and Greeks that they must turn to God in repentance and have faith in our Lord Jesus Christ.”

22 “And now, behold, bound by the Spirit, I am going to Jerusalem, not knowing what will befall me there. 23 Except that the Holy Spirit testifies to me in every city that chains and afflictions await me. 24 But I do not count my life as precious to myself, if only I may finish my course and the ministry I received from the Lord Jesus, to testify to the gospel of the grace of God.”

25 “And now, behold, I know that none of you among whom I went about proclaiming the kingdom will see my face again. 26 Therefore I testify to you this day that I am innocent of the blood of all. 27 For I did not shrink from declaring to you the whole counsel of God.”

28 “Pay careful attention to yourselves and to all the flock, in which the Holy Spirit has appointed you overseers, to shepherd the church of God, which He purchased with His own blood. 29 I know that after my departure savage wolves will come in among you, not sparing the flock. 30 And from among your own number men will arise, speaking distorted things, to draw the disciples away after themselves. 31 Therefore be watchful, remembering that for three years I did not cease to admonish each one of you, night and day, with tears.”

32 “And now I commend you to God and to the word of His grace, which is able to build you up and to give you the inheritance among all those who have been sanctified. 33 I coveted no one’s silver or gold or clothing. 34 You yourselves know that these hands ministered to my needs and to those who were with me. 35 In all things I showed you that by laboring thus we must help the weak, and remember the words of the Lord Jesus, for He Himself said, ‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’”

36 When he had said these things, he knelt down with them all and prayed. 37 There was much weeping among them all; they fell upon Paul’s neck and kissed him. 38 Sorrowing most of all because of the word he had spoken, that they should see his face no more, they accompanied him to the ship.

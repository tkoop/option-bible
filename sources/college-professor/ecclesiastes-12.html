The Summons to Remember the Creator in Youth

1 Remember your Creator in the days of your youth, before the evil days arrive and the years draw near when you will say, “I have no pleasure in them”; and before the sun, the light, the moon, and the stars are darkened and the clouds return after the rain; 2 3 in the day when the keepers of the house tremble, the men of strength are bent, the grinders cease because they are few, and those who look through the windows grow dim.

4 When the doors to the street are shut and the sound of grinding is low, when one rises at the sound of a bird, and all the daughters of song are brought low; 5 when men are afraid of heights and terrors are in the road; when the almond tree blossoms, the grasshopper drags itself along, and desire fails—for man goes to his eternal home, and mourners walk about in the street; 6 before the silver cord is severed, or the golden bowl is crushed, or the pitcher is shattered at the spring, or the wheel is broken at the well; 7 and the dust returns to the earth as it was, and the spirit returns to God who gave it.

The Teacher’s Final Appraisal of Wisdom

8 “Futility of futilities,” says the Teacher, “all is futility.” 9 Not only was the Teacher wise, but he also taught the people knowledge; he pondered, searched out, and arranged many proverbs. 10 The Teacher sought to find pleasing words, and he wrote words of truth with uprightness. 11 The words of the wise are like goads, and like nails firmly fixed are the collected sayings; they are given by one Shepherd. 12 My son, be warned by these: of making many books there is no end, and much study is a weariness of the flesh.

The Final Verdict on Human Existence

13 When all has been heard, the conclusion of the matter is this: Fear God and keep His commandments, for this is the whole duty of man. 14 For God will bring every deed into judgment, along with every hidden thing, whether good or evil.

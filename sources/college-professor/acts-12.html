Herod’s Persecution of the Church

1 About that time, King Herod laid violent hands upon certain members of the church, meaning to afflict them; 2 and he put James, the brother of John, to the sword. 3 Perceiving that this gratified the Jews, he proceeded likewise to seize Peter—this during the days of Unleavened Bread. 4 Having arrested him, he consigned him to prison, committing him to four squads of soldiers for safekeeping, intending to bring him before the people after Passover. 5 Thus Peter was kept in custody, while the church unceasingly and earnestly interceded with God on his behalf.

Peter’s Miraculous Deliverance

6 On that very night, when Herod was about to produce him, Peter slept between two soldiers, bound with two chains, while sentinels guarded the prison before the door. 7 And behold, an angel of the Lord stood by, and a light illumined the cell; striking Peter’s side, he roused him, saying, “Arise quickly!” and the chains fell from his hands. 8 The angel said to him, “Gird yourself and bind on your sandals.” Peter did so. Then he said to him, “Wrap your cloak about you and follow me.” 9 Peter followed and went out, not knowing that what was being accomplished through the angel was real; he supposed he was beholding a vision. 10 Passing the first and second guard, they came to the iron gate leading into the city, which opened to them of its own accord; and going out, they advanced one street, whereupon the angel immediately departed from him. 11 When Peter recovered himself, he said, “Now I know truly that the Lord has sent His angel and rescued me from the hand of Herod and from all that the Jewish people expected.”

The Church’s Reception of Peter

12 Having considered the matter, he went to the house of Mary, the mother of John who was called Mark, where many were gathered and praying. 13 When he knocked at the door of the gateway, a servant girl named Rhoda came to answer. 14 Recognizing Peter’s voice, she, in her joy, did not open the gate, but ran in and announced that Peter was standing before the gateway. 15 They said to her, “You are out of your mind.” But she vehemently affirmed that it was so, and they said, “It is his angel.” 16 Meanwhile Peter persisted in knocking; and when they opened, they saw him and were astounded. 17 Motioning to them with his hand to be silent, he related how the Lord had led him out of prison, and said, “Report these things to James and to the brothers.” Then, having spoken thus, he departed and went to another place.

Herod’s Judgment and the Growth of the Word

18 When day came, there was no small commotion among the soldiers concerning what had become of Peter. 19 Herod, having sought him in vain and examined the guards, ordered them put to death; then he went down from Judea to Caesarea and took up residence there. 20 Now he was in a state of bitter hostility with the Tyrians and Sidonians; so they came to him with one accord, and having won over Blastus, the king’s chamberlain, they sought peace, because their country drew its sustenance from the royal provisions. 21 On an appointed day, Herod, arrayed in royal apparel and seated upon the judgment seat, delivered a public address to them. 22 The people shouted, “The voice of a god, and not of a man!” 23 Immediately, because he did not give glory to God, an angel of the Lord struck him down, and he was eaten by worms and breathed his last. 24 But the word of God increased and multiplied. 25 As for Barnabas and Saul, having fulfilled their ministry in Jerusalem, they returned, taking with them John, who was called Mark.

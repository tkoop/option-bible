The Ephraimite Grievance and Gideon's Diplomatic Rejoinder

1 The men of Ephraim then said to him, “What is this deed that you have done to us, that you did not summon us when you went forth to wage war against Midian?” And they contended with him vehemently.

2 He replied to them, “What have I now accomplished in comparison with you? Are not the gleanings of Ephraim superior to the vintage of Abiezer? 3 God has delivered into your hands the princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb; what, then, could I have done to rival you?” When he spoke thus, their wrath subsided toward him.

The Weary Pursuit and the Refusal of Succoth and Penuel

4 Gideon, together with the three hundred men who accompanied him, came to the Jordan and crossed it, though they were weary, yet still in pursuit.

5 He said to the men of Succoth, “Please give loaves of bread to the people who are following me, for they are exhausted, and I am pursuing Zebah and Zalmunna, the kings of Midian.”

6 But the officials of Succoth answered, “Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna already in your possession, that we should give bread to your army?”

7 Gideon replied, “Very well; when the LORD has given Zebah and Zalmunna into my hand, I will thresh your flesh with the thorns of the wilderness and with briers.”

8 From there he ascended to Penuel and spoke to them in the same manner; the men of Penuel answered him just as the men of Succoth had answered.

9 So he said to the men of Penuel, “When I return in peace, I will demolish this tower.”

The Final Rout, the Capture of the Kings, and Retribution Against the Cities

10 Now Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor with their camp—about fifteen thousand men, all that remained of the entire host of the people of the East, for a hundred and twenty thousand men who drew the sword had already fallen.

11 Gideon went up by the route of the tent-dwellers east of Nobah and Jogbehah, and struck the camp, for the camp was unsuspecting.

12 Zebah and Zalmunna fled, and he pursued them and captured the two kings of Midian, Zebah and Zalmunna, and threw the whole camp into consternation.

13 Gideon, the son of Joash, then returned from the battle by the ascent of Heres.

14 He captured a young man of Succoth and interrogated him; and the youth wrote down for him the officials and elders of Succoth—seventy-seven men.

15 Then he came to the men of Succoth and said, “Behold Zebah and Zalmunna, concerning whom you taunted me, saying, ‘Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna now in your hand, that we should give bread to your men?’”

16 He then took the elders of the city, together with thorns of the wilderness and briers, and with them he chastised the men of Succoth.

17 He also demolished the tower of Penuel and slew the men of the city.

The Execution of the Kings, the Refusal of Rule, and Israel's Subsequent Relapse

18 He said to Zebah and Zalmunna, “Where are the men whom you killed at Tabor?”

They answered, “As you are, so were they—each one resembling the son of a king.”

19 He said, “They were my brothers, the sons of my mother. As the LORD lives, if you had spared them alive, I would not have killed you.”

20 Then he said to Jether, his firstborn, “Arise and kill them.” But the youth did not draw his sword, for he was afraid, because he was still a youth.

21 Then Zebah and Zalmunna said, “Arise yourself and fall upon us; for as the man is, so is his strength.”

Gideon arose and killed Zebah and Zalmunna, and took the crescent ornaments that were on the necks of their camels.

22 Then the men of Israel said to Gideon, “Rule over us—you and your son and your grandson as well—for you have delivered us from the hand of Midian.”

23 Gideon said to them, “I will not rule over you, nor will my son rule over you; the LORD will rule over you.”

24 Gideon also said to them, “Let me make one request of you: each of you give me the earrings from his spoil.” (For they had golden earrings, because they were Ishmaelites.)

25 They answered, “We will willingly give them.” And they spread out a cloak, and each man cast into it the earrings of his spoil.

26 The weight of the gold earrings that he requested came to seventeen hundred shekels, besides the crescent ornaments, the pendants, the purple garments worn by the kings of Midian, and the chains that were on their camels’ necks.

27 Gideon fashioned an ephod from it and set it up in his city, Ophrah; and all Israel gave themselves to idolatry in following it, so that it became a snare to Gideon and to his household.

28 Thus Midian was subdued before the people of Israel, and they did not again lift up their heads; and the land enjoyed rest for forty years in the days of Gideon.

29 Then Jerubbaal, the son of Joash, went and dwelt in his own house.

30 Now Gideon had seventy sons of his own issue, for he had many wives.

31 His concubine, who was in Shechem, also bore him a son, and he named him Abimelech.

32 Gideon, the son of Joash, died at a good old age and was buried in the tomb of Joash his father, at Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

33 As soon as Gideon was dead, the people of Israel turned back and gave themselves to the Baals in idolatrous devotion, and made Baal-berith their god.

34 The people of Israel did not remember the LORD their God, who had delivered them from the hand of all their enemies round about.

35 Neither did they show steadfast love to the house of Jerubbaal—that is, Gideon—in proportion to all the good that he had done for Israel.

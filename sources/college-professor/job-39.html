The Mountain Goats and the Wild Donkey

“1 Do you know the season in which the mountain goats bring forth their young, and do you keep watch while the hinds are in labor? 2 Can you reckon the months they carry to term, and are you aware of the hour of their delivery? 3 They crouch down to calve, they bring forth their offspring, and they cast off their birth pangs. 4 Their young grow robust and flourish in the open country; they go forth and never return to them.

“5 Who has granted liberty to the wild donkey, and who has loosed the ropes that held it? 6 I have appointed the wasteland as its dwelling and the salt flats as its habitation. 7 It mocks the uproar of the city and is deaf to the driver's shout. 8 It roams the mountain ranges in search of pasture and seeks out every green blade.

The Wild Ox and the Limits of Human Dominion

“9 Will the wild ox consent to be your servant, or will it lodge by your manger through the night? 10 Can you bind it to the furrow with a tether, or will it plow the valleys in your wake? 11 Will you rely upon it for its prodigious strength, and entrust your heavy labor to it? 12 Can you trust it to bring home your seed and gather the harvest to your threshing floor?

The Ostrich and the Mystery of Instinct

“13 The ostrich's wings beat exultantly, but are they the pinions and plumage of the stork? 14 For she abandons her eggs to the earth and lets them be warmed in the dust, 15 forgetful that a foot may crush them or that a beast of the field may trample them. 16 She is harsh toward her young, as though they were not her own; her labor is for naught, and she is without fear, 17 because God has deprived her of wisdom and has not imparted understanding to her. 18 Yet when she rouses herself to flee, she mocks horse and rider.

The War Horse and the Soaring Birds of Prey

“19 Do you impart strength to the horse, or clothe its neck with a flowing mane? 20 Do you make it bound like a locust, so that the majesty of its snorting inspires terror? 21 It paws violently in the valley and exults in its might; it charges forth to meet the clash of arms. 22 It scoffs at terror and is not daunted; it does not recoil before the sword. 23 The quiver rattles against it, along with the gleaming spear and javelin. 24 With quaking and fury it devours the ground, nor can it believe that the sound is merely the trumpet. 25 At every trumpet blast it snorts, 'Aha!' and from afar it scents the battle—the thunder of commanders and the war cry.

“26 Does the hawk take wing by your wisdom, spreading its pinions toward the south? 27 Does the eagle soar at your bidding and build its nest on high? 28 It inhabits the cliff and spends the night there; the rocky crag is its fortress. 29 From there it spies out prey, and its eyes discern it from afar. 30 Its fledglings drink in blood, and wherever the slain lie, there it is.”

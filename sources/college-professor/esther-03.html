Haman’s Elevation and Mordecai’s Defiance

1 After these events, King Ahasuerus exalted Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, promoting him and setting his throne above all the officials who were with him.

2 All the king’s servants who were at the king’s gate bowed down and paid homage to Haman, for the king had so commanded concerning him; yet Mordecai neither bowed down nor paid homage.

3 Then the king’s servants who were at the king’s gate said to Mordecai, “Why do you transgress the king’s command?”

4 When they spoke to him day after day and he would not listen, they reported the matter to Haman, to see whether Mordecai’s refusal would be tolerated, for he had disclosed to them that he was a Jew.

5 When Haman saw that Mordecai neither bowed down nor paid homage to him, he was filled with rage.

6 But Haman disdained to lay hands on Mordecai alone, for they had made known to him Mordecai’s people; instead, Haman sought to destroy all the Jews throughout the kingdom of Ahasuerus, the people of Mordecai.

The Casting of the Lot and Haman’s Accusation

7 In the first month, that is Nisan, in the twelfth year of King Ahasuerus, Pur—that is, the lot—was cast before Haman, day by day and month by month, until the twelfth month, that is Adar.

8 Haman said to King Ahasuerus, “There is a certain people scattered and dispersed among the peoples in all the provinces of your kingdom; their laws differ from those of every people, and they do not observe the king’s laws, so it is not in the king’s interest to tolerate them. 9 If it pleases the king, let it be decreed that they be destroyed, and I will remit ten thousand talents of silver into the royal treasuries, entrusting it to those charged with the king’s affairs.”

10 So the king removed his signet ring from his hand and gave it to Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of the Jews.

11 The king said to Haman, “The silver is given to you, and the people as well; do with them as it seems good to you.”

The Decree of Annihilation

12 Then on the thirteenth day of the first month the king’s scribes were summoned, and it was written according to all that Haman commanded to the satraps, the governors of each province, and the officials of each people, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language; it was written in the name of King Ahasuerus and sealed with the king’s signet ring.

13 Letters were sent by couriers to all the king’s provinces, to destroy, to kill, and to annihilate all the Jews, young and old, women and children, in a single day—the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, that is Adar—and to plunder their possessions.

14 A copy of the document was to be issued as law in every province, published to all peoples, so that they might be ready for that day.

15 The couriers went out, hastened by the king’s command, and the decree was promulgated in Susa the citadel; the king and Haman sat down to drink, but the city of Susa was thrown into confusion.

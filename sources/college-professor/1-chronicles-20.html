The Ammonite Campaign and the Fall of Rabbah

1 In the springtime, when monarchs customarily take the field, Joab led forth the army, laid waste the territory of the Ammonites, advanced upon Rabbah, and besieged it, while David remained in Jerusalem; Joab then struck Rabbah and overthrew it.

Spoils, Captives, and the Subjugation of Ammon

2 David seized the crown of their king from his head; finding it to weigh a talent of gold and to be set with a precious stone, he placed it upon his own head, and he carried away from the city an exceedingly great quantity of spoil. 3 He brought out the inhabitants and consigned them to compulsory labor with saws, iron picks, and axes; he dealt thus with every city of the Ammonites, and then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

Philistine Conflicts and the Defeat of Giant Descendants

4 Afterward war arose with the Philistines at Gezer, and Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Sippai, one of the descendants of the giants, so that the Philistines were subdued. 5 War flared again with the Philistines, and Elhanan son of Jair struck down Lahmi, brother of Goliath the Gittite, whose spear shaft resembled a weaver's beam. 6 Another battle took place at Gath, where a man of prodigious stature—bearing six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot, twenty-four in all—also descended from the giants. 7 When he reviled Israel, Jonathan, son of Shimea (David's brother), struck him down. 8 These men, descendants of the giants in Gath, fell by the hand of David and by the hand of his servants.

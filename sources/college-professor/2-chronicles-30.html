Hezekiah Summons All Israel to the Passover

1 Hezekiah dispatched messengers throughout all Israel and Judah, and he likewise inscribed letters to Ephraim and Manasseh, summoning them to the house of the LORD in Jerusalem, there to celebrate the Passover unto the LORD, the God of Israel. 2 For the king, together with his officials and the whole assembly in Jerusalem, had resolved to observe the Passover in the second month— 3 because they could not keep it at the appointed time, the priests not having consecrated themselves in sufficient numbers, nor had the people assembled in Jerusalem. 4 The plan commended itself to the king and to the entire assembly. 5 They therefore decreed that a proclamation be carried throughout all Israel, from Beersheba to Dan, bidding the people come and keep the Passover to the LORD, the God of Israel, at Jerusalem, since they had not observed it in the prescribed fullness.

The Couriers' Proclamation and the Nation's Response

6 So, in accordance with the king's command, couriers traversed all Israel and Judah bearing letters from the king and his officials, proclaiming: “People of Israel, return to the LORD, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, that He may turn in mercy to the remnant of you who have escaped the hand of the kings of Assyria. 7 Do not resemble your fathers and your brothers, who acted treacherously toward the LORD, the God of their fathers, so that He consigned them to desolation, as you see. 8 Do not now stiffen your necks, as your fathers did, but yield yourselves to the LORD and enter His sanctuary, which He has consecrated forever, and serve the LORD your God, that His fierce anger may turn away from you. 9 For if you return to the LORD, your brothers and your children will find compassion in the sight of their captors and return to this land; for the LORD your God is gracious and merciful, and He will not turn His face away from you, if you return to Him.”

10 Thus the couriers passed from city to city through the country of Ephraim and Manasseh, even as far as Zebulun; yet the people there ridiculed and mocked them. 11 Nevertheless, certain men of Asher, Manasseh, and Zebulun humbled themselves and came to Jerusalem. 12 Moreover, the hand of God was upon Judah, granting them one heart to execute the command of the king and of the officials according to the word of the LORD.

The Second-Month Passover and Its Purification

13 A very great assembly gathered in Jerusalem in the second month to keep the Feast of Unleavened Bread. 14 They rose up and removed the altars that were in Jerusalem, and every incense altar they carried away and cast into the Kidron Valley.

15 On the fourteenth day of the second month they slaughtered the Passover, while the priests and the Levites, ashamed of their unreadiness, consecrated themselves and brought burnt offerings into the house of the LORD. 16 They took their stations according to their appointed order, in accordance with the Law of Moses, the man of God; the priests sprinkled the blood received from the hand of the Levites. 17 For many in the assembly had not consecrated themselves, so the Levites were charged with slaughtering the Passover lambs for everyone who was not clean, thereby consecrating them to the LORD.

18 For a great multitude of the people—many from Ephraim, Manasseh, Issachar, and Zebulun—had not purified themselves, yet they ate the Passover otherwise than prescribed. But Hezekiah prayed for them, saying, “May the LORD, who is good, pardon every one 19 who sets his heart to seek God, the LORD God of his fathers, even though he is not cleansed according to the sanctuary's purity.” 20 And the LORD heard Hezekiah and healed the people.

A Prolonged Festival of Rejoicing

21 The Israelites who were present in Jerusalem kept the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days with great gladness; and the Levites and the priests praised the LORD day by day, singing with all their might to the LORD. 22 Hezekiah encouraged all the Levites who showed skillful understanding in the service of the LORD. Thus for seven days they ate the festival food, offering peace offerings and giving thanks to the LORD, the God of their fathers. 23 Then the whole assembly resolved to keep the feast for another seven days, and they did so with gladness. 24 For Hezekiah king of Judah contributed to the assembly one thousand bulls and seven thousand sheep, and the officials contributed one thousand bulls and ten thousand sheep; and a great number of priests consecrated themselves. 25 The whole assembly of Judah, together with the priests, the Levites, the whole assembly that had come from Israel, the sojourners from the land of Israel, and the inhabitants of Judah, rejoiced. 26 So there was great joy in Jerusalem, for since the days of Solomon the son of David, king of Israel, there had been nothing like this in Jerusalem. 27 Then the priests and the Levites arose and blessed the people, and their voice was heard, and their prayer ascended to His holy dwelling in heaven.

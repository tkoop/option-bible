Qualifications for the Office of Overseer

1 This is a trustworthy saying: if anyone aspires to oversight, he covets a noble work. 2 Consequently, an overseer must be irreproachable, devoted to a single wife, temperate, prudent, respectable, hospitable, and didactically competent; 3 he must not be addicted to wine, nor pugnacious, but forbearing, peaceable, and averse to avarice; 4 he must manage his own household admirably, keeping his children in submission with all gravity. 5 For if a man cannot preside over his own household, how shall he superintend the church of God? 6 He must not be a neophyte, lest, inflated with conceit, he fall into the devil's condemnation; 7 moreover, he must possess an estimable testimony from outsiders, lest he fall into reproach and the snare of the devil.

Qualifications for Deacons

8 Likewise, deacons must be venerable, not duplicitous in speech, not devoted to copious wine, not sordidly avaricious; 9 they must hold the mystery of the faith with a pure conscience. 10 And let these too be examined first; then, being found blameless, let them minister. 11 Likewise, women must be venerable, not calumnious, sober, and faithful in all respects. 12 Deacons must be men of one wife, managing their children and their own households commendably. 13 For those who have ministered well acquire for themselves a noble rank and much boldness in the faith that is in Christ Jesus.

The Household of God and the Mystery of Godliness

14 Hoping to come to you soon, I write these things to you. 15 But if I am delayed, you may know how one must comport oneself in the household of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and foundation of the truth. 16 And, by common confession, great is the mystery of godliness: He was revealed in flesh, vindicated by the Spirit, seen by angels, heralded among the nations, believed upon in the world, and received up in glory.

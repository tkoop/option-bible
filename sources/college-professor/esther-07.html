Esther's Petition at the Second Banquet

1 So the king and Haman came to drink with Queen Esther. 2 On the second day, while they were drinking wine, the king said again to Queen Esther, "What is your petition, Queen Esther? It shall be granted to you. What is your request? Even to half the kingdom, it shall be done."

3 Then Queen Esther answered, "If I have found favor in your sight, O king, and if it pleases the king, let my life be granted me at my petition, and my people at my request. 4 For I and my people have been sold to be destroyed, to be killed, and to be annihilated. Had we been sold merely into servitude, both men and women, I should have held my peace, for the calamity would not have been worth troubling the king."

The King's Wrath and Haman's Destruction

5 Then King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther, "Who is he, and where is he—the one whose heart has emboldened him to do this?"

6 Esther said, "The adversary and enemy is this wicked Haman!" Then Haman was terrified before the king and the queen.

7 The king arose in his wrath from the wine banquet and went into the palace garden, while Haman stood to beg Queen Esther for his life, perceiving that the king had resolved to bring ruin upon him.

8 When the king returned from the palace garden to the banquet hall, Haman was falling upon the couch where Esther reclined. The king exclaimed, "Will he even violate the queen with me in the house?" As soon as the word left the king's mouth, they covered Haman's face.

9 Then Harbona, one of the eunuchs before the king, said, "Behold, the gallows fifty cubits high stands by Haman's house; he erected it for Mordecai, who spoke in defense of the king."

The king said, "Hang him on it!" 10 So they hanged Haman on the gallows he had prepared for Mordecai, and the king's wrath subsided.

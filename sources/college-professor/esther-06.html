A Sleepless Night and a Forgotten Service

1 That night sleep eluded the monarch, so he commanded that the book of records—the chronicles of the days—be brought and read before him. 2 There was found written that Mordecai had disclosed the conspiracy of Bigthana and Teresh, two eunuchs among the king’s doorkeepers, who had sought to assassinate King Ahasuerus.

3 The king asked, “What honor and advancement have been conferred upon Mordecai for this?”

His attendants replied, “Nothing whatsoever has been done for him.”

Haman’s Unwitting Prescription for Royal Honor

4 The king inquired, “Who is in the court?” Now Haman had just entered the outer court of the royal palace to petition the king to hang Mordecai upon the gallows he had prepared.

5 The king’s servants answered, “Behold, Haman stands in the court.”

The king said, “Let him enter.”

6 When Haman came in, the king asked him, “What should be done for the man whom the king delights to honor?” Haman reasoned within himself that the king could scarcely wish to honor anyone more than him.

7 So Haman said to the king, “For the man whom the king delights to honor, 8 let royal apparel be brought—the very robe the king has worn—and a horse upon which the king has ridden, with a royal diadem placed upon his head. 9 Let the apparel and the horse be entrusted to one of the king’s most noble officials, and let them array the man whom the king delights to honor, set him upon the horse, and lead him through the city square, proclaiming before him, ‘Thus it shall be done for the man whom the king delights to honor.’”

The King’s Command and Haman’s Humiliation

10 The king said to Haman, “Make haste; take the robe and the horse, exactly as you have spoken, and do so for Mordecai the Jew who sits at the king’s gate. Omit not a single word of all that you have said.”

11 So Haman took the robe and the horse, arrayed Mordecai, and led him through the city square, proclaiming before him, “Thus it shall be done for the man whom the king delights to honor.”

12 Then Mordecai returned to the king’s gate, while Haman hurried to his house, mourning, his head veiled. 13 Haman recounted to Zeresh his wife and all his friends everything that had befallen him. His wise men and Zeresh his wife said to him, “If Mordecai, before whom your downfall has begun, is of Jewish descent, you will not prevail against him; rather, you will surely fall before him.”

14 While they were still speaking with him, the king’s eunuchs arrived and hurriedly brought Haman to the banquet that Esther had prepared.

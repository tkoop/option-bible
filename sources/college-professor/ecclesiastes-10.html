The Corrosive Power of Folly and the Imperative of Composure

1 Dead flies cause a perfumer’s ointment to reek; likewise, a modicum of folly outweighs wisdom and honor in its capacity to corrupt. 2 The heart of the wise inclines toward the right, whereas the heart of the fool inclines toward the left. 3 Even as the fool traverses the road, his heart remains deficient, and he proclaims to everyone, “He is a fool.” 4 Should a ruler’s wrath surge against you, do not abandon your post, for composure pacifies even grave transgressions. 5 I have observed an evil beneath the sun, akin to an inadvertent misjudgment emanating from a sovereign. 6 Folly is elevated to many exalted stations, while the affluent are relegated to humble positions. 7 I have seen slaves mounted on horses, and princes proceeding on foot like slaves.

Prudence in Labor, Speech, and Governance

8 He who excavates a pit falls into it, and he who breaches a wall is bitten by a serpent. 9 He who quarries stones is injured by them, and he who cleaves wood is endangered by them. 10 If an iron blade grows dull and its edge remains unsharpened, one must marshal greater force; yet wisdom confers advantage by directing one’s efforts. 11 If a serpent bites before it is charmed, then the charmer derives no advantage. 12 The words of a wise man’s mouth are gracious, but the lips of a fool consume him. 13 The beginning of his mouth’s words is folly, and the conclusion of his speech is wicked madness. 14 The fool multiplies words, though no one knows what is coming; and who can declare to him what will transpire thereafter? 15 The toil of fools exhausts each of them, for he does not know the way to the city.

The Blessing and Burden of Rule

16 Woe to you, O land, when your king is a child and your princes feast in the morning. 17 Blessed are you, O land, when your king is a son of nobles and your princes feast at the proper time—for strength and not for drunkenness. 18 Through sloth the rafters sag, and through idleness of hands the house leaks. 19 Bread is made for laughter, wine gladdens the living, and money answers all things. 20 Even in your thoughts, do not curse the king, nor in your bedroom curse the rich, for a bird of the heavens will carry the voice, and a winged creature will report the matter.

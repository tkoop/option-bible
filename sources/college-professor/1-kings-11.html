Solomon’s Foreign Marriages and Spiritual Decline

1 King Solomon, for his part, loved many foreign women in addition to Pharaoh’s daughter—Moabite, Ammonite, Edomite, Sidonian, and Hittite women— 2 drawn from the nations concerning which the LORD had declared to the Israelites, “You must not intermarry with them, nor they with you, for they will assuredly incline your hearts after their gods.” Nevertheless, Solomon clung to them in love. 3 He had seven hundred wives of royal birth and three hundred concubines, and these women led his heart astray. 4 When Solomon grew old, his wives turned his heart toward other gods, so that his heart was no longer wholly devoted to the LORD his God, as the heart of his father David had been.

5 Solomon pursued Ashtoreth, the goddess of the Sidonians, and Milcom, the abomination of the Ammonites. 6 Thus Solomon perpetrated evil in the eyes of the LORD, and, unlike David his father, he did not remain wholly faithful to the LORD. 7 At that time, on the hill east of Jerusalem, Solomon erected a high place for Chemosh, the abomination of Moab, and for Milcom, the abomination of the Ammonites. 8 He did the same for all his foreign wives, who burned incense and sacrificed to their gods.

9 The LORD was therefore incensed with Solomon, because his heart had turned away from the LORD, the God of Israel, who had appeared to him twice. 10 Although He had expressly commanded him not to follow other gods, Solomon did not observe what the LORD had commanded.

The LORD’s Judgment and the Division of the Kingdom

11 The LORD said to Solomon, “Because this has befallen you, and because you have not kept My covenant and My statutes, which I enjoined upon you, I will surely tear the kingdom from you and give it to your servant. 12 Yet, for the sake of your father David, I will not do so in your days; I will tear it from the hand of your son. 13 Nevertheless, I will not tear away the entire kingdom; I will give one tribe to your son, for the sake of My servant David and for the sake of Jerusalem, which I have chosen.”

Adversaries Raised Against Solomon

14 Then the LORD raised up an adversary against Solomon: Hadad the Edomite, of the royal line of Edom. 15 For when David was in Edom, and Joab, commander of the army, went up to bury the slain, he struck down every male in Edom. 16 Joab and all Israel remained there six months, until he had exterminated every male in Edom. 17 Hadad, however, was still a young boy when he fled to Egypt with certain Edomites who were his father’s servants.

18 They set out from Midian and came to Paran; then, taking men from Paran with them, they entered Egypt, to Pharaoh king of Egypt, who gave Hadad a house, allotted him land, and furnished him food. 19 Pharaoh looked with great favor upon Hadad, so he gave him in marriage the sister of his own wife, Queen Tahpenes. 20 The sister of Tahpenes bore him a son, Genubath, whom Tahpenes weaned in Pharaoh’s palace; Genubath lived in Pharaoh’s palace among the sons of Pharaoh.

21 When Hadad heard in Egypt that David had died and that Joab, commander of the army, was dead, he said to Pharaoh, “Let me depart, that I may return to my own country.”

22 But Pharaoh said to him, “What are you lacking here with me, that you seek to go to your own country?”

“Nothing,” Hadad replied, “only grant me leave to depart.”

23 God also raised up another adversary against Solomon: Rezon son of Eliada, who had fled from his master, Hadadezer king of Zobah. 24 He gathered men about him and became leader of a marauding band after David’s slaughter of them; then he went to Damascus, settled there, and reigned over Damascus. 25 Rezon was an adversary to Israel throughout all the days of Solomon, adding to the distress caused by Hadad; he abhorred Israel and reigned over Aram.

Jeroboam’s Rebellion and Prophetic Commission

26 Jeroboam son of Nebat, an Ephraimite from Zeredah, whose mother was Zeruah, a widow, rebelled against the king; he was Solomon’s servant. 27 This was the occasion of his rebellion against the king: Solomon built the Millo and closed the breach in the wall of the city of his father David. 28 Jeroboam was a man of great strength and valor; when Solomon saw that the young man was industrious, he appointed him over all the forced labor of the house of Joseph. 29 At that time, when Jeroboam went out from Jerusalem, the prophet Ahijah the Shilonite met him on the road; Ahijah was wrapped in a new cloak, and the two of them were alone in the field. 30 Ahijah seized the new cloak upon him and tore it into twelve pieces.

31 He said to Jeroboam, “Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: ‘See, I am about to tear the kingdom from the hand of Solomon, and I will give you ten tribes. 32 But I will leave him one tribe for the sake of My servant David and for the sake of Jerusalem, the city I have chosen from all the tribes of Israel. 33 For they have forsaken Me and have bowed down to Ashtoreth, goddess of the Sidonians, to Chemosh, god of Moab, and to Milcom, god of the Ammonites; they have not walked in My ways, nor done what is right in My eyes, nor kept My statutes and My ordinances, as David his father did. 34 Nevertheless, I will not take the whole kingdom from his hand; I will make him ruler all the days of his life for the sake of My servant David, whom I chose, because he kept My commandments and My statutes. 35 But I will take the kingdom from his son and give it to you—ten tribes. 36 To his son I will give one tribe, so that My servant David will always have a descendant before Me in Jerusalem, the city I have chosen to put My Name. 37 I will take you, and you shall rule over all that your soul desires; you shall be king over Israel. 38 If you listen to all that I command you, walk in My ways, and do what is right in My eyes, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as My servant David did, then I will be with you. I will build you an enduring house, as I built for David, and I will give Israel to you. 39 I will afflict the house of David because of this, but not forever.’”

40 Solomon sought therefore to put Jeroboam to death; but Jeroboam arose and fled to Egypt, to Shishak king of Egypt, and he remained in Egypt until Solomon’s death. 41 Now the rest of the acts of Solomon, and all that he did, and his wisdom—are they not written in the Book of the Acts of Solomon? 42 The time that Solomon reigned over Israel in Jerusalem was forty years. 43 Solomon died and was buried in the city of his father David; his son Rehoboam reigned in his place.

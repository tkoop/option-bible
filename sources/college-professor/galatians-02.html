Paul's Vindication of His Gospel and Ministry in Jerusalem

1 After fourteen years had elapsed, I went up again to Jerusalem, accompanied by Barnabas and taking Titus with me. 2 I ascended in accordance with a revelation and laid before them the gospel that I proclaim among the Gentiles; yet I did so privately to those who seemed to be of consequence, lest somehow I should be running, or have run, in vain. 3 But not even Titus, who was with me and is a Greek, was compelled to be circumcised. 4 This was owing to certain false brothers who had insinuated themselves, slipping in by stealth to spy upon the liberty we possess in Christ Jesus, with the intent of reducing us to bondage. 5 To these intruders we did not yield in subjection for so much as an hour, in order that the truth of the gospel might abide with you.

The Recognition of Paul's Apostleship by the Jerusalem Pillars

6 As for those who appeared to be persons of consequence—whatever they once were is immaterial to me, for God shows no partiality—these reputed leaders imparted nothing to me. 7 On the contrary, perceiving that I had been entrusted with the gospel for the uncircumcised, just as Peter had been for the circumcised, 8 they recognized that He who wrought in Peter unto an apostleship to the circumcised wrought in me also toward the Gentiles. 9 And James, Cephas, and John—those reputed pillars—recognizing the grace bestowed upon me, extended to Barnabas and me the right hand of fellowship, that we should labor among the Gentiles while they did so among the circumcised. 10 They asked only that we remember the poor, a charge I was already zealous to fulfill.

The Antioch Confrontation and the Uprightness of the Gospel

11 But when Cephas came to Antioch, I withstood him to his face, for he stood condemned. 12 For before the arrival of certain men from James, he dined with the Gentiles; yet when they appeared, he withdrew and set himself apart, apprehensive of those of the circumcision. 13 The other Jews joined him in this dissimulation, so that even Barnabas was swept along by their hypocrisy. 14 When I perceived that they were not walking uprightly in accordance with the truth of the gospel, I said to Cephas in the presence of all, "If you, though a Jew, live as the Gentiles do and not as the Jews, how can you compel the Gentiles to live as Jews?"

Justification by Faith and Life in the Crucified Christ

15 We are Jews by birth, not sinners from among the Gentiles. 16 Yet knowing that a person is not justified by works of the law, except through faith in Jesus Christ, we also believed in Christ Jesus, that we might be justified by faith in Christ and not by works of the law; for by works of the law no flesh shall be justified. 17 But if, while seeking to be justified in Christ, we ourselves are discovered to be sinners, is Christ then an agent of sin? By no means! 18 For if I reconstruct what I once dismantled, I constitute myself a transgressor. 19 For through the law I died to the law, that I might live unto God. 20 I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me; and the life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and delivered Himself up for me. 21 I do not nullify the grace of God; for if righteousness comes through the law, then Christ died in vain.

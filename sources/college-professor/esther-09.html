The Reversal and the Jews’ Vindication

1 On the thirteenth day of the twelfth month—that is, Adar—the royal decree and edict were to be executed; and on that very day, when the enemies of the Jews had anticipated subjugating them, the design was inverted, so that the Jews themselves exercised dominion over their adversaries. 2 Throughout every province of King Ahasuerus, the Jews assembled in their cities to assail those who sought their ruin; no one could withstand them, for a pervasive dread of them had descended upon all peoples. 3 Moreover, all the provincial officials, the satraps, the governors, and the king’s administrators sustained the Jews, because the fear of Mordecai had fallen upon them. 4 For Mordecai had become preeminent within the royal household, his renown traversing every province, and the man Mordecai continued to wax ever more illustrious.

5 Thus the Jews smote all their foes with the sword, with slaughter and devastation, and they dealt with their adversaries according to their own sovereign pleasure.

6 In the citadel of Susa, the Jews slew and destroyed five hundred men, 7 together with Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha, 8 Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha, 9 Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai, and Vaizatha, 10 the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the adversary of the Jews; yet they did not stretch forth their hand against the spoil.

The Royal Exchange and the Hanging of Haman’s Sons

11 On that day the tally of those slain in the citadel of Susa was reported to the king.

12 The king said to Queen Esther, “In the citadel of Susa the Jews have slain and destroyed five hundred men, along with the ten sons of Haman. What, then, must they have accomplished in the remaining provinces of the king! What is your request? It shall be granted to you. And what further petition do you proffer? It shall be fulfilled.”

13 Esther replied, “If it please the king, let the Jews in Susa be permitted to act tomorrow also in accordance with today’s decree, and let the ten sons of Haman be hanged upon the gallows.”

14 So the king commanded that it be done; a decree was promulgated in Susa, and the ten sons of Haman were hanged.

15 The Jews in Susa assembled on the fourteenth day of Adar and slew three hundred men in Susa, but they did not lay their hand upon the spoil.

Rest, Rejoicing, and the First Purim Feasts

16 The rest of the Jews who were in the king’s provinces assembled and defended their lives, obtaining respite from their enemies; they slew seventy-five thousand of their haters, but they did not lay their hands upon the spoil.

17 This occurred on the thirteenth day of Adar, and on the fourteenth they rested and made it a day of feasting and gladness. 18 But the Jews in Susa had assembled on the thirteenth and the fourteenth, and rested on the fifteenth, making it a day of feasting and gladness. 19 Therefore the rural Jews, those dwelling in unwalled towns, observe the fourteenth day of Adar as a day of gladness and feasting, a propitious day, and as a day for exchanging portions with one another.

Mordecai’s Letters and the Establishment of Purim

20 Mordecai recorded these events and dispatched letters to all the Jews in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, both near and far, 21 enjoining them to keep, year after year, the fourteenth and fifteenth days of Adar, 22 as the days on which the Jews had obtained rest from their enemies, and as the month that had been transmuted for them from grief into joy, and from mourning into a propitious day; they were to make them days of feasting and gladness, of exchanging portions with one another, and of gifts to the poor.

23 The Jews undertook what they had begun and what Mordecai had written to them. 24 For Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had devised a plot against the Jews to destroy them, and had cast Pur—that is, the lot—to confound and annihilate them. 25 But when Esther came before the king, he commanded by letters that the wicked scheme Haman had devised against the Jews should recoil upon his own head, and that he and his sons should be hanged on the gallows.

26 Therefore these days were called Purim, after the name Pur. For this reason, because of all the words of this letter, and because of what they had seen and what had befallen them, 27 the Jews established and accepted for themselves, for their descendants, and for all who joined themselves to them, that it should not fail that they keep these two days according to their writing and their appointed time in every year. 28 These days are to be remembered and kept in every generation, in every family, province, and city; the days of Purim shall not pass away from among the Jews, nor shall the remembrance of them perish from their descendants.

29 Then Queen Esther daughter of Abihail, together with Mordecai the Jew, wrote with all authority to confirm this second letter concerning Purim. 30 He sent letters to all the Jews, to the one hundred and twenty-seven provinces of the kingdom of Ahasuerus, words of peace and truth, 31 to establish the days of Purim at their appointed times, as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had enjoined them, and as they had established for themselves and for their descendants, in the matters of their fasts and their lamentation. 32 The command of Esther confirmed these words of Purim, and it was written in the book.

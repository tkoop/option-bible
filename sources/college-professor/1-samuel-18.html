The Bond Between David and Jonathan

1 After David had concluded his discourse to Saul, the soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul. 2 From that day forward Saul retained David in his service and would not permit him to return to his father's household.

3 Jonathan then concluded a covenant with David, because he loved him as himself. 4 Jonathan stripped off the robe he was wearing and gave it to David, together with his tunic, his sword, his bow, and his belt.

David's Advancement and Saul's Mounting Envy

5 Wherever Saul dispatched him, David conducted himself with such sagacity that Saul elevated him to command over the men of war; this found favor in the eyes of all the people and in the eyes of Saul's servants as well.

6 When the troops returned from striking down the Philistine, the women emerged from every city of Israel to meet King Saul, celebrating with tambourines, songs of joy, and stringed instruments. 7 And as the women danced, they sang, "Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his tens of thousands."

8 Saul was exceedingly incensed, for this saying was grievous in his sight; he declared, "They have ascribed to David tens of thousands, but to me they have ascribed only thousands. What more is there for him but the kingdom?" 9 And from that day onward Saul regarded David with a jealous and hostile eye.

An Evil Spirit and Saul's Fearful Retaliation

10 On the following day an evil spirit from God rushed upon Saul, and he prophesied in the midst of the house while David played the harp, as he did day by day; Saul, meanwhile, held a spear in his hand. 11 Saul hurled the spear, saying, "I will pin David to the wall," but David eluded him on two occasions.

12 Saul feared David, because the LORD was with David but had departed from Saul. 13 Therefore Saul removed David from his presence and appointed him commander of a thousand; and David went out and came in before the people. 14 David prospered in all his undertakings, for the LORD was with him. 15 When Saul perceived how exceedingly prosperous he was, he grew fearful of him. 16 Yet all Israel and Judah loved David, because he went out and came in before them.

The Betrothals of Merab and Michal

17 Then Saul said to David, "Here is my elder daughter Merab; I will give her to you in marriage. Only prove yourself valiant on my behalf and fight the battles of the LORD." For Saul thought, 'I will not raise my hand against him; let the Philistines do that.'

18 David answered Saul, "Who am I, and what is my family—indeed, my father's clan—in Israel, that I should become the king's son-in-law?" 19 Yet when the time arrived to give Saul's daughter Merab to David, she was given in marriage to Adriel the Meholathite.

20 Now Saul's daughter Michal loved David; when this was reported to Saul, it pleased him, 21 for he thought, 'I will give her to him, that she may become a snare to him, and that the hand of the Philistines may be against him.' Therefore Saul said to David, "Today you shall become my son-in-law through one of the two."

22 Then Saul commanded his servants, "Speak to David privately and say, 'Look, the king delights in you, and all his servants love you; now therefore become his son-in-law.'" 23 So Saul's servants conveyed these words to David, and David asked, "Does it seem a trivial thing in your eyes to become the son-in-law of the king, when I am a poor man and lightly esteemed?"

24 The servants reported to Saul exactly what David had said. 25 Saul replied, "Tell David, 'The king desires no dowry except a hundred Philistine foreskins, to avenge himself upon his enemies.'" Now Saul was scheming to cause David's death by the hand of the Philistines.

26 When the servants reported these terms to David, it pleased him to become the king's son-in-law; and before the allotted time had elapsed, 27 David and his men went out and struck down two hundred Philistines. David brought their foreskins and presented them in full number to the king, so as to become the king's son-in-law; whereupon Saul gave him his daughter Michal as wife.

28 When Saul recognized that the LORD was with David, and that his daughter Michal loved David, 29 Saul grew still more afraid of David; and Saul was David's enemy continually. 30 Whenever the Philistine commanders went out to battle, David achieved greater success than all of Saul's servants, so that his name became highly esteemed.

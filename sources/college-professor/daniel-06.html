Darius Organizes the Realm and Elevates Daniel

1 It pleased Darius to appoint one hundred twenty satraps over the kingdom, stationed throughout the entire realm, 2 and over them three chief administrators, among whom Daniel was one, so that these satraps might render account to them and the king suffer no loss. 3 Then this Daniel distinguished himself above the administrators and satraps, because an extraordinary spirit was in him, and the king contemplated establishing him over the whole kingdom.

4 Thereupon the administrators and satraps sought to discover a pretext against Daniel in the affairs of the kingdom, yet they could find neither corruption nor negligence, since he was faithful and no fault or corruption was found in him. 5 Consequently these men said, "We shall find no pretext against this Daniel unless we find it in the law of his God."

The Conspirators' Irrevocable Ordinance

6 Then these administrators and satraps thronged to the king and addressed him thus: "O King Darius, live forever! 7 All the administrators of the kingdom, the prefects and satraps, the counselors and governors, have conferred together to establish a royal statute and to enforce an interdict: for thirty days, whoever petitions any god or human being except you, O King, shall be cast into the den of lions. 8 Now, O King, establish the interdict and sign the document, so that it cannot be altered, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which does not pass away." 9 Therefore King Darius signed the document and the interdict.

Daniel's Defiance and the King's Agony

10 When Daniel learned that the document had been signed, he went to his house, where, in his upper chamber, with windows opened toward Jerusalem, he knelt three times each day, prayed, and gave thanks before his God, precisely as he had done before. 11 Then these men assembled and found Daniel petitioning and supplicating before his God.

12 They approached the king and said concerning the interdict, "Did you not sign an interdict, O King, that for thirty days any person who petitions any god or human being except you, O King, shall be cast into the den of lions?"

The king answered, "The matter is established, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which does not pass away."

13 Then they answered before the king, "Daniel, one of the exiles from Judah, pays no heed to you, O King, nor to the interdict that you signed, but petitions his God three times a day."

14 When the king heard the matter, he was deeply distressed concerning Daniel, and he resolved to deliver him; he labored until sunset to rescue him. 15 Then these men thronged to the king and said, "Know, O King, that it is a law of the Medes and Persians that no interdict or statute which the king establishes may be changed."

Daniel Cast into the Lions' Den

16 Then the king commanded, and they brought Daniel and cast him into the den of lions. The king said to Daniel, "May your God, whom you serve continually, deliver you!" 17 A stone was brought and placed over the mouth of the den, and the king sealed it with his own signet and with the signets of his nobles, so that nothing might be changed concerning Daniel. 18 Then the king went to his palace and spent the night fasting; no entertainments were brought before him, and sleep fled from him.

Deliverance at Dawn and the Accusers' Fate

19 Then at daybreak, as soon as it was light, the king arose and went in haste to the den of lions. 20 As he drew near to the den, he cried out to Daniel with an anguished voice; the king spoke and said, "Daniel, servant of the Living God, has your God, whom you serve continually, been able to deliver you from the lions?"

21 Then Daniel spoke with the king, "O King, live forever! 22 My God sent His angel and shut the mouths of the lions, so that they have not harmed me, because I was found innocent before Him; and also before you, O King, I have done no wrong."

23 Then the king was exceedingly glad concerning him, and commanded that Daniel be taken up out of the den. So Daniel was taken up out of the den, and no injury was found on him, because he had trusted in his God. 24 The king then commanded, and those men who had maliciously accused Daniel were brought and cast into the den of lions—they, their children, and their wives; and before they reached the bottom of the den, the lions overpowered them and crushed all their bones.

Darius's Proclamation and Daniel's Prosperity

25 Then King Darius wrote to all peoples, nations, and languages dwelling in all the earth: "May your peace be multiplied. 26 I issue a decree that in every dominion of my kingdom people shall fear and tremble before the God of Daniel, for He is the Living God and endures forever; His kingdom shall not be destroyed, and His dominion shall be to the end. 27 He delivers and rescues; He performs signs and wonders in heaven and on earth; He has delivered Daniel from the power of the lions." 28 So this Daniel prospered during the reign of Darius and during the reign of Cyrus the Persian.

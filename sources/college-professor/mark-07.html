The Conflict over Ancestral Traditions and Ritual Purity

1 The Pharisees, together with certain scribes who had come from Jerusalem, assembled around Him. 2 Observing that some of His disciples were eating bread with common—that is, unwashed—hands, 3 they took offense, for the Pharisees and all the Jews, maintaining the tradition of the elders, do not eat unless they have washed their hands in the prescribed manner; 4 and when they return from the marketplace they do not eat without first undergoing ritual ablution, while they have received and hold many other traditions, such as the washing of cups, pitchers, bronze vessels, and couches.

5 Accordingly, the Pharisees and the scribes asked Him, “Why do your disciples not conduct themselves according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with common hands?”

6 He replied, “Well did Isaiah prophesy concerning you hypocrites, as it is written: ‘This people honors Me with their lips, but their heart is far from Me. 7 In vain do they worship Me, teaching as doctrines the commandments of men.’ 8 You have abandoned the commandment of God and hold fast to the tradition of men.”

9 He continued, “You do well to set aside the commandment of God so that you may keep your tradition. 10 For Moses said, ‘Honor your father and your mother,’ and, ‘Whoever reviles father or mother must surely be put to death.’ 11 But you say that if a man declares to his father or mother, ‘Whatever assistance you might have received from me is Corban’—that is, a dedicated gift— 12 you no longer permit him to do anything for his father or mother, 13 thus nullifying the word of God by your tradition, which you have handed down; and you do many things like this.”

True Defilement and the Condition of the Heart

14 Calling the crowd to Him again, He said to them, “Listen to Me, all of you, and understand: 15 nothing that enters a person from outside can defile him; rather, it is what proceeds from within a person that defiles him.”

17 When He had left the crowd and entered the house, His disciples asked Him about the parable. 18 He said to them, “Are you also so devoid of understanding? Do you not perceive that whatever enters a person from outside cannot defile him, 19 because it does not enter his heart but his stomach, and is expelled into the latrine, thereby purging all foods?” 20 He went on, “What comes out of a person—that is what defiles him. 21 For from within, out of the human heart, proceed evil thoughts, sexual immorality, theft, murder, adultery, 22 greed, malice, deceit, sensuality, envy, slander, pride, and folly. 23 All these evils come from within and defile a person.”

Faith of the Syrophoenician Woman

24 Rising from there, He departed into the region of Tyre. He wished no one to know, yet, having entered a house, He could not remain concealed. 25 Immediately a woman whose little daughter was possessed by an unclean spirit heard of Him, came, and fell at His feet. 26 The woman was a Gentile, a Syrophoenician by birth, and she begged Him to cast the demon out of her daughter.

27 He said to her, “Let the children be satisfied first, for it is not right to take the children’s bread and throw it to the dogs.”

28 She answered, “Yes, Lord; yet even the dogs beneath the table eat the children’s crumbs.”

29 He said to her, “Because of this answer, go; the demon has left your daughter.” 30 Returning to her house, she found the child lying on the bed and the demon gone.

Healing of a Deaf and Mute Man in the Decapolis

31 Again leaving the region of Tyre, He passed through Sidon to the Sea of Galilee, through the midst of the region of the Decapolis. 32 They brought to Him a man who was deaf and had an impediment in his speech, and they implored Him to lay His hand on him. 33 Taking him aside from the crowd privately, He put His fingers into his ears, and after spitting, touched his tongue. 34 Looking up to heaven, He sighed and said to him, “Ephphatha,” that is, “Be opened.”

35 Immediately his ears were opened, his tongue’s bond was released, and he spoke plainly. 36 He charged them to tell no one; but the more He charged them, the more abundantly they proclaimed it. 37 They were exceedingly astonished and said, “He has done all things well; He makes the deaf hear and the mute speak.”

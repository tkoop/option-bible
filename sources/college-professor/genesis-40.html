The Royal Offense and Imprisonment

1 After these events, the cupbearer and the baker of the king of Egypt incurred the wrath of their lord, Pharaoh, by offending him. 2 Pharaoh's anger was kindled against his two courtiers, the chief cupbearer and the chief baker. 3 Consequently, he remanded them to custody in the house of the captain of the guard, within the very prison where Joseph was confined.

Shared Dreams in Confinement

4 The captain of the guard entrusted them to Joseph's supervision; Joseph attended to their needs, and they remained in custody. 5 After a considerable interval had elapsed, both the cupbearer and the baker of the king of Egypt—those imprisoned there—dreamed during the same night, each dream bearing its own distinctive significance.

6 When Joseph came to them in the morning and observed their demeanor, he found them disconsolate. 7 He therefore inquired of Pharaoh's officers who were confined with him in his master's house, "Why do your countenances appear so dejected this day?" 8 They answered, "We have both dreamed dreams, yet no interpreter is at hand." Joseph then said to them, "Do not interpretations belong to God? Please recount your dreams to me."

Dreams Disclosed and Interpreted

9 The chief cupbearer thereupon related his dream to Joseph, saying, "In my dream, behold, a vine stood before me. 10 Upon the vine were three branches; and as it budded, its blossoms unfolded, and its clusters ripened into grapes. 11 Now Pharaoh's cup was in my hand, and I took the grapes, pressed them into Pharaoh's cup, and placed the cup into Pharaoh's hand."

12 Joseph said to them, "This is the interpretation: the three branches are three days. 13 Within three days, Pharaoh will restore you to favor and reinstate you in your office, and you shall once again place Pharaoh's cup into his hand, according to the former custom when you served as his cupbearer. 14 Yet when prosperity attends you, remember me and deal graciously with me; speak of me to Pharaoh, and secure my deliverance from this dungeon. 15 For I was stolen away from the land of the Hebrews, and here also I have committed no offense warranting my consignment to this pit."

16 When the chief baker perceived that the interpretation was auspicious, he said to Joseph, "I too dreamed a dream: three baskets of white bread were upon my head. 17 In the uppermost basket were all manner of baked goods for Pharaoh, but the birds were devouring them from the basket atop my head."

18 Joseph answered, "This is the interpretation: the three baskets are three days. 19 Within three days, Pharaoh will remove you from life and hang you upon a tree, where the birds will consume your flesh."

The Birthday Judgment and Its Aftermath

20 On the third day, which was Pharaoh's birthday, he prepared a banquet for all his servants and, in their midst, rendered judgment upon the chief cupbearer and the chief baker. 21 He restored the chief cupbearer to his office, so that he once more placed the cup in Pharaoh's hand. 22 But the chief baker he hanged, precisely as Joseph had interpreted for them. 23 Yet the chief cupbearer did not remember Joseph; he forgot him.

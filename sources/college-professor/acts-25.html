Festus’s Arrival in Judea and the Jewish Leaders’ Accusation

1 Three days after Festus had entered the province, he ascended from Caesarea to Jerusalem. 2 There the high priests and the leading men of the Jews laid before him their case against Paul and importuned him, 3 requesting as a favor that he send for Paul in Jerusalem, since they were contriving an ambush to kill him along the road. 4 Festus, however, replied that Paul was being kept in Caesarea and that he himself was about to depart there shortly. 5 “Therefore,” he said, “let those among you who are influential go down with me and accuse him, if there is anything amiss in the man.”

Paul’s Defense and Appeal to Caesar

6 After remaining among them no more than eight or ten days, he descended to Caesarea; and on the following day, having taken his seat on the judgment seat, he ordered Paul to be brought in. 7 When Paul had arrived, the Jews who had come down from Jerusalem stood around him, bringing many and grave accusations that they could not substantiate. 8 Paul offered his defense, declaring that he had sinned neither against the law of the Jews, nor against the temple, nor against Caesar. 9 Festus, wishing to ingratiate himself with the Jews, answered Paul, “Do you wish to go up to Jerusalem and be judged there before me concerning these matters?” 10 But Paul said, “I am standing before Caesar’s judgment seat, where I ought to be judged; I have wronged the Jews in nothing, as you yourself know far better. 11 If, then, I am a wrongdoer and have done anything deserving of death, I do not refuse to die; but if none of the charges that these men bring against me is true, no one can hand me over to them. I appeal to Caesar.” 12 Then Festus, after conferring with his council, replied, “You have appealed to Caesar; to Caesar you shall go.”

Festus Lays Paul’s Case Before King Agrippa

13 Now after some days had elapsed, King Agrippa and Bernice arrived at Caesarea to pay their respects to Festus. 14 While they were staying there for many days, Festus laid Paul’s case before the king, saying, “There is a certain man left as a prisoner by Felix; 15 and when I was in Jerusalem, the high priests and elders of the Jews informed me about him, asking for a condemnation against him. 16 I answered them that it is not the custom of Romans to surrender any man before the accused has had an opportunity to face his accusers and to make his defense concerning the charge.”

17 “So,” he continued, “when they had assembled here, I made no delay, but on the next day, having taken my seat on the judgment seat, I ordered the man to be brought in. 18 When his accusers stood up, they brought no charge of the wicked things I had suspected. 19 Instead, they had certain disputes with him about their own religious observances and about a certain Jesus who had died, whom Paul asserted to be alive. 20 Being at a loss as to how to investigate such matters, I asked whether he would be willing to go to Jerusalem and be tried there concerning them. 21 But when Paul appealed to be kept for the decision of the Emperor, I ordered him to be kept until I could send him to Caesar.” 22 Agrippa said to Festus, “I should like to hear the man myself.” “Tomorrow,” he replied, “you shall hear him.”

Paul Presented Before Agrippa

23 So on the next day Agrippa and Bernice came with great pomp and entered the auditorium, together with the military tribunes and the principal men of the city; and at Festus’s command Paul was brought in. 24 And Festus said, “King Agrippa and all the men present with us, you behold this man concerning whom the whole multitude of the Jews petitioned me, both here and in Jerusalem, crying out that he ought no longer to live.

25 “I, however, have found that he has done nothing deserving of death; and since he himself appealed to the Emperor, I have decided to send him. 26 Yet I have nothing certain to write to our sovereign concerning him. Therefore I have brought him before all of you, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that, after this examination, I may have something to write. 27 For it seems to me unreasonable to send a prisoner without also indicating the charges against him.”

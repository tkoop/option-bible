The Realization of the Promised Heir

1 The LORD attended to Sarah, precisely as He had declared, and the LORD accomplished for Sarah that which He had spoken. 2 Sarah conceived and bore a son to Abraham in his advanced age, at the designated season of which God had spoken. 3 Abraham bestowed upon the son born to him by Sarah the name Isaac. 4 Abraham circumcised his son Isaac when he had reached eight days of age, in accordance with the divine command. 5 Abraham was one hundred years old when his son Isaac was born to him.

6 Sarah said, “God has made me to laugh; everyone who hears of this will laugh with me.” 7 And she said, “Who would have declared to Abraham that Sarah would nurse children? Yet I have borne him a son in his old age.”

8 The child grew and was weaned, and Abraham prepared a great feast on the day Isaac was weaned.

Domestic Strife and the Dismissal of Hagar

9 Sarah observed that the son whom Hagar the Egyptian had borne to Abraham was mocking. 10 So she said to Abraham, “Expel this slave woman and her son, for the son of this slave woman shall not inherit alongside my son Isaac.”

11 The matter proved exceedingly grievous to Abraham on account of his son. 12 God said to Abraham, “Let it not be grievous in your sight because of the boy and your slave woman. Heed Sarah’s voice in all that she says to you, for through Isaac your offspring shall be named.” 13 “I shall also constitute the son of the slave woman a nation, because he is your offspring.”

14 Abraham rose early in the morning, took bread and a skin of water, gave them to Hagar, placing them upon her shoulder, and gave her the boy, and sent her away. She departed and strayed in the wilderness of Beersheba. 15 When the water in the skin was spent, she placed the child beneath one of the bushes. 16 She went and sat down opposite him at a distance, about a bowshot away, for she said, “Let me not behold the death of the child.” She sat opposite him and raised her voice and wept.

Providence for Hagar and Ishmael in the Wilderness

17 God heard the voice of the boy, and the angel of God called to Hagar from heaven and said to her, “What ails you, Hagar? Do not fear, for God has heard the voice of the boy in the place where he is.” 18 “Arise, lift up the boy, and sustain him by the hand, for I will make of him a great nation.”

19 Then God opened her eyes, and she beheld a well of water. She went and filled the skin with water and gave the boy a drink. 20 God was with the boy, and he grew up. He dwelt in the wilderness and became an archer. 21 He dwelt in the wilderness of Paran, and his mother took for him a wife from the land of Egypt.

The Pact at Beersheba

22 At that time Abimelech and Phicol, commander of his army, said to Abraham, “God is with you in all that you do.” 23 “Now therefore swear to me here by God that you will not deal deceitfully with me, or with my offspring, or with my descendants; according to the kindness I have shown to you, you shall do to me and to the land in which you have sojourned.”

24 Abraham said, “I will swear.”

25 Then Abraham reproved Abimelech concerning a well of water that Abimelech’s servants had seized.

26 Abimelech said, “I did not know who perpetrated this deed; you did not inform me, nor did I hear of it until today.”

27 Abraham took sheep and oxen and gave them to Abimelech, and the two of them ratified a covenant.

28 Abraham set apart seven ewe lambs from the flock.

29 Abimelech said to Abraham, “What is the significance of these seven ewe lambs that you have set apart?”

30 He said, “You shall take these seven ewe lambs from my hand, that they may serve as witness for me that I dug this well.”

31 Therefore that place was called Beersheba, because there the two of them swore an oath. 32 Thus they ratified a covenant at Beersheba. Then Abimelech and Phicol, commander of his army, arose and returned to the land of the Philistines. 33 Abraham planted a tamarisk in Beersheba and invoked there the name of the LORD, the Everlasting God. 34 Abraham sojourned in the land of the Philistines for many days.

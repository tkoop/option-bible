Jesus Brought Before Pilate and Herod

1 The entire multitude arose and led Him to Pilate. 2 They commenced accusing Him, saying,

“We have discovered this man subverting our nation, prohibiting the remittance of tribute to Caesar, and proclaiming himself to be the Christ, a king.”

3 Pilate interrogated Him, asking, “Are You the King of the Jews?”

He replied, “You say so.”

4 Pilate declared to the chief priests and the crowds, “I discern no culpability in this man.”

5 Yet they persisted, insisting, “He agitates the populace, teaching throughout all Judea, from Galilee even to this place.”

6 Upon hearing this, Pilate inquired whether the man was a Galilean. 7 Recognizing that He fell under Herod’s jurisdiction, he remanded Him to Herod, who happened to be in Jerusalem during those days.

8 Herod, upon seeing Jesus, was exceedingly gratified, for he had long desired an audience with Him, having heard reports concerning Him, and he anticipated witnessing some miracle performed by Him. 9 He interrogated Him with many words, yet He offered no reply. 10 The chief priests and scribes stood by, vehemently accusing Him. 11 Herod, together with his soldiers, treated Him with contempt and mocked Him; then, clothing Him in resplendent apparel, he sent Him back to Pilate. 12 That very day Herod and Pilate became friends, for previously they had been estranged.

Pilate’s Acquittal and the Crowd’s Demand for Crucifixion

13 Pilate summoned the chief priests, the rulers, and the people, 14 and addressed them, “You have brought this man before me as one who perverts the people; and behold, having examined Him before you, I find no culpability in this man concerning the matters of which you accuse Him. 15 Moreover, Herod has remanded Him to us; and behold, nothing worthy of death has been perpetrated by Him. 16 I will therefore chastise Him and release Him.”

18 But they cried out with one accord, saying, “Away with this man, and release Barabbas to us!” 19 (He had been incarcerated for a certain insurrection that had erupted in the city, and for murder.)

20 Pilate, desiring to release Jesus, addressed them again. 21 But they shouted, saying, “Crucify Him! Crucify Him!”

22 A third time he said to them, “Why? What evil has this man done? I have found no capital guilt in Him. I will therefore chastise Him and release Him.”

23 But they persisted, demanding with vociferous cries that He be crucified; and their voices prevailed. 24 So Pilate adjudicated that their petition be granted. 25 He released the one who had been imprisoned for insurrection and murder, whom they requested, but surrendered Jesus to their will.

The Procession to Calvary and the Crucifixion

26 As they led Him away, they apprehended Simon of Cyrene, coming in from the countryside, and compelled him to bear the cross behind Jesus. 27 A vast throng of the people followed Him, including women who mourned and lamented Him.

28 Jesus turned to them and said, “Daughters of Jerusalem, do not weep for Me; rather, weep for yourselves and for your children. 29 For behold, days are approaching in which they will declare, ‘Blessed are the barren, and the wombs that never bore, and the breasts that never nursed.’ 30 Then they will begin to say to the mountains, ‘Fall upon us!’ and to the hills, ‘Enshroud us!’ 31 For if they perpetrate these things in the green wood, what shall be done in the dry?”

32 Two other malefactors were also led forth to be executed with Him. 33 When they reached the place called the Skull, they crucified Him there, together with the malefactors—one at His right hand and the other at His left.

34 Jesus said, “Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do.” And they divided His garments and cast lots.

35 The people stood beholding; but the rulers derided Him, saying, “He saved others; let Him save Himself, if He is the Christ of God, His Chosen One.”

36 The soldiers likewise mocked Him, approaching and offering Him sour wine, 37 and saying, “If You are the King of the Jews, save Yourself!”

38 There was also a superscription over Him: “This is the King of the Jews.”

39 One of the malefactors who were hanged reviled Him, saying, “Are You not the Christ? Save Yourself and us!”

40 But the other answered, rebuking him, and said, “Do you not fear God, since you are under the same condemnation? 41 And we indeed justly, for we receive the just recompense of our deeds; but this man has done nothing amiss.”

42 And he said, “Jesus, remember me when You come into Your kingdom.”

43 He said to him, “Truly, I say to you, today you will be with Me in Paradise.”

The Death and Entombment of Jesus

44 It was now about the sixth hour, and darkness overspread the whole land until the ninth hour. 45 The sun was eclipsed, and the veil of the temple was rent in twain. 46 Jesus, crying out with a loud voice, said, “Father, into Your hands I commend My spirit.” Having said this, He expired.

47 When the centurion saw what had transpired, he glorified God, saying, “Certainly this man was righteous.” 48 All the crowds who had assembled for that spectacle, beholding the things that were done, returned beating their breasts. 49 But all His acquaintances, and the women who had followed Him from Galilee, stood afar off, observing these things.

50 Now there was a man named Joseph, a member of the Council, a good and righteous man—he had not acquiesced in their counsel and deed—from Arimathea, a city of the Jews, who was awaiting the kingdom of God. 51 He went to Pilate and requested the body of Jesus. 52 Then he took it down, wrapped it in linen, and deposited it in a sepulcher hewn from rock, where no one had yet been laid. 53 It was the day of Preparation, and the Sabbath was dawning.

54 The women who had accompanied Him from Galilee followed and observed the sepulcher and how His body was placed. 55 Then they returned and prepared spices and ointments. 56 And on the Sabbath they rested according to the commandment.

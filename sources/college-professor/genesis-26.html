Famine and God’s Promise to Isaac

1 Now a famine afflicted the land, distinct from the earlier famine that had occurred in Abraham’s days. And Isaac went to Abimelech, king of the Philistines, at Gerar. 2 And the LORD appeared to him and said, “Do not descend to Egypt; abide in the land that I will show you. 3 Sojourn in this land, and I will be with you and will bless you, for to you and to your posterity I will give all these lands, and I will establish the oath that I swore to Abraham, your father. 4 I will multiply your posterity as the stars of heaven and will give to your posterity all these lands, and in your posterity all the nations of the earth shall be blessed, 5 because Abraham hearkened to My voice and kept My charge, My commandments, My statutes, and My laws.”

Isaac’s Deception in Gerar

6 Thus Isaac settled in Gerar. 7 When the men of the place inquired concerning his wife, he said, “She is my sister,” for he feared to say, “My wife,” lest the men of the place should slay him, since Rebekah was exceedingly beautiful. 8 After he had resided there for a protracted interval, Abimelech, king of the Philistines, gazed through a window and beheld Isaac caressing Rebekah, his wife.

9 Abimelech summoned Isaac and said, “Behold, she is your wife! How then did you say, ‘She is my sister’?”

Isaac replied, “Because I said, ‘Lest I perish on account of her.’”

10 Abimelech said, “What have you done to us? One of the people might easily have lain with your wife, and you would have incurred guilt upon us.”

11 Abimelech charged all the people, saying, “Whoever touches this man or his wife shall surely be put to death.”

Prosperity, Envy, and the Struggle for Wells

12 Isaac sowed in that land and reaped in that same year a hundredfold, because the LORD blessed him. 13 The man prospered and continued to increase in wealth until he became exceedingly affluent. 14 He acquired flocks, herds, and a substantial household, so that the Philistines envied him. 15 Now all the wells that his father’s servants had excavated in Abraham’s days, the Philistines had stopped up and filled with earth.

16 Abimelech said to Isaac, “Depart from us, for you have become far mightier than we.”

17 So Isaac departed thence and encamped in the Valley of Gerar and dwelt there. 18 Isaac reopened the wells of water that had been excavated in the days of Abraham his father, which the Philistines had stopped up after Abraham’s death, and he restored to them the names his father had bestowed. 19 When Isaac’s servants dug in the valley, they discovered a well of living water.

20 But the herdsmen of Gerar contended with Isaac’s herdsmen, saying, “The water belongs to us.” Therefore he named the well Esek, because they had quarreled with him.

21 Then they excavated another well, and they quarreled over it as well; so he named it Sitnah.

22 He moved from there and dug yet another well, over which they did not quarrel. He named it Rehoboth, saying, “For now the LORD has made ample space for us, and we shall flourish in the land.”

Isaac at Beersheba and the Philistine Covenant

23 From there he ascended to Beersheba. 24 The LORD appeared to him that very night and said, “I am the God of Abraham, your father. Fear not, for I am with you and will bless you and multiply your posterity for the sake of My servant Abraham.” 25 So he erected an altar there and invoked the name of the LORD; he pitched his tent there, and Isaac’s servants excavated a well.

26 Abimelech came to him from Gerar, accompanied by Ahuzzath, his counselor, and Phicol, commander of his army. 27 Isaac said to them, “Why have you come to me, seeing that you hate me and have expelled me from your presence?”

28 They said, “We have clearly perceived that the LORD has been with you. Therefore we said, ‘Let there be an oath between us, between us and you, and let us establish a covenant with you, 29 that you will do us no harm, just as we have not molested you but have dealt with you only in kindness and have sent you away in peace. You are now blessed by the LORD.’”

30 So he prepared a feast for them, and they ate and drank. 31 They rose early the next morning and swore an oath to one another. Then Isaac dismissed them, and they departed from him in peace.

32 That same day, Isaac’s servants came and reported to him concerning the well they had dug, saying, “We have found water.” 33 He named it Shibah; hence the city is called Beersheba to this day.

34 When Esau was forty years old, he married Judith, daughter of Beeri the Hittite, and Basemath, daughter of Elon the Hittite. 35 And they proved a source of bitterness to Isaac and Rebekah.

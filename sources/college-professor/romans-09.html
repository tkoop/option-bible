Paul's Anguish for Israel and Its Covenant Prerogatives

1 I speak the truth in Christ—I do not prevaricate—while my conscience corroborates my testimony in the Holy Spirit, 2 that I am afflicted by profound sorrow and incessant anguish of heart. 3 For I could wish myself anathematized and severed from Christ on behalf of my brothers, my kinsmen according to the flesh— 4 Israelites, to whom pertain adoption, glory, covenants, legislation, liturgy, and promises; 5 whose are the patriarchs, and from whom, according to the flesh, comes the Christ, who is God over all, eternally blessed. Amen.

The Integrity of God's Word and the True Israel

6 Yet it is not as though the word of God has miscarried. For not all who descend from Israel constitute Israel, 7 nor are all Abraham's seed automatically his children; rather, “In Isaac shall your seed be called.” 8 That is, the children of the flesh are not ipso facto children of God; rather, the children of the promise are reckoned as offspring. 9 For this is the word of promise: “At this appointed time I will come, and Sarah shall have a son.”

10 Moreover, Rebekah conceived by one man, our father Isaac, 11 before the twins had been born or had perpetrated any good or evil, so that God's elective purpose might remain inviolable, 12 not on the basis of works but on the basis of Him who calls—she was told, “The elder shall serve the younger.” 13 As it is written, “Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated.”

Divine Righteousness, Mercy, and the Potter's Authority

14 What then shall we conclude? Is there unrighteousness with God? By no means! 15 For He says to Moses, “I will have mercy on whom I have mercy, and I will have compassion on whom I have compassion.” 16 Consequently, it depends not on the one who wills or the one who runs, but on God who shows mercy. 17 For Scripture says to Pharaoh, “For this very purpose I have raised you up, that I might exhibit My power in you, and that My name might be proclaimed throughout all the earth.” 18 Thus He has mercy on whomever He wills, and He hardens whomever He wills.

19 You will say to me, then, “Why does He still impute fault? For who has withstood His will?”

20 But who are you, O man, to retort against God? Will what is molded say to its molder, “Why have you made me thus?” 21 Does not the potter possess authority over the clay, to fashion from the same lump one vessel for honor and another for dishonor?

22 What if God, though willing to manifest His wrath and make known His power, has endured with abundant longsuffering vessels of wrath fitted for destruction, 23 in order to disclose the riches of His glory upon vessels of mercy, which He prepared beforehand for glory— 24 even us, whom He has called, not from the Jews only but also from the Gentiles?

Prophetic Fulfillment and Righteousness by Faith

25 As He also says in Hosea, “I will call those who were not My people, ‘My people,’ and her who was not beloved, ‘Beloved.’” 26 “And in the very place where it was said to them, ‘You are not My people,’ there they shall be called ‘sons of the living God.’”

27 And Isaiah cries out concerning Israel: “Though the number of the sons of Israel be as the sand of the sea, a remnant shall be saved; 28 for the Lord will execute His word upon the earth, bringing it to consummation and cutting it short.” 29 And as Isaiah predicted, “If the Lord of Hosts had not left us offspring, we would have become like Sodom and been made like Gomorrah.”

30 What shall we say, then? That Gentiles who did not pursue righteousness have attained righteousness—even the righteousness that is by faith— 31 but Israel, pursuing a law of righteousness, did not attain to that law. 32 Why? Because they pursued it not by faith but as though by works; they stumbled against the stone of stumbling, 33 as it is written, “Behold, I am laying in Zion a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense; and whoever believes in Him will not be put to shame.”

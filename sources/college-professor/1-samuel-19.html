Jonathan’s Protection of David

1 Saul enjoined his son Jonathan, together with all his attendants, to bring about David’s death; nevertheless, Jonathan, Saul’s son, was deeply devoted to David.

2 Jonathan warned David, saying, “My father Saul is intent upon killing you; therefore, exercise vigilance at daybreak, remain in a secluded place, and conceal yourself. 3 I shall go forth and stand beside my father in the field where you are, and I shall speak concerning you to my father; then, having ascertained his disposition, I shall report it to you.”

4 Jonathan interceded favorably for David before Saul his father, saying, “Let not the king transgress against his servant David, for he has committed no offense against you, and his conduct has been exceedingly beneficial to you. 5 He hazarded his life, struck down the Philistine, and the LORD wrought a great deliverance for all Israel; you beheld it and rejoiced. Why, then, would you incur guilt by shedding innocent blood, putting David to death without justification?”

6 Saul heeded Jonathan’s entreaty and swore by the LORD, “As the LORD lives, he shall not be put to death.”

7 Jonathan then summoned David and recounted all these things to him; he brought David before Saul, and David resumed his former station in the king’s presence.

Renewed Warfare and Saul’s Renewed Hostility

8 War erupted once more; David sallied forth, engaged the Philistines, and inflicted upon them a devastating defeat, so that they fled before him. 9 An evil spirit from the LORD seized Saul while he sat in his house, spear in hand, as David was playing the lyre. 10 Saul endeavored to transfix David against the wall with the spear, but David eluded him; the spear struck the wall, and David fled and escaped during the night.

Michal’s Stratagem and David’s Flight

11 Saul dispatched messengers to David’s house to keep watch over him and to kill him at daybreak; Michal, David’s wife, warned him, “If you do not contrive to save your life this night, tomorrow you shall be slain.”

12 Michal lowered David through the window, whereupon he departed, fled, and made good his escape. 13 Michal then took the teraphim, placed it in the bed, arranged a goat-hair bolster at its head, and covered it with a garment.

14 When Saul sent messengers to seize David, she replied, “He is ill.”

15 Saul then sent the messengers back to inspect David, with the command, “Bring him up to me in the bed, so that I may put him to death.”

16 The messengers entered and discovered the teraphim in the bed, the goat-hair bolster at its head.

17 Saul said to Michal, “Why have you deceived me in this manner, sending away my enemy so that he escaped?”

Michal answered Saul, “He said to me, ‘Let me go, or I shall kill you.’”

David’s Refuge at Naioth and the Overpowering of Saul’s Messengers

18 David fled and made his escape, coming to Samuel at Ramah, where he recounted all that Saul had done to him; then the two of them went and took up residence at Naioth. 19 Saul was informed, “David is at Naioth in Ramah.”

20 Saul sent messengers to seize David, but when they beheld the assembly of prophets prophesying, with Samuel presiding over them, the Spirit of God came upon the messengers, and they too prophesied. 21 When this was reported to Saul, he dispatched a second contingent of messengers, who likewise prophesied; then he sent a third, and they too prophesied.

22 He himself then set out for Ramah and arrived at the great cistern in Secu, where he inquired, “Where are Samuel and David?”

He was told, “They are at Naioth in Ramah.”

23 He proceeded to Naioth in Ramah; there the Spirit of God came upon him as well, and he continued prophesying as he went until he reached Naioth in Ramah.

24 He even stripped off his garments and prophesied before Samuel, lying naked throughout that day and night. Hence the proverb, “Is Saul also among the prophets?”

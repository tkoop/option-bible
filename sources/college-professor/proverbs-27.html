Prudential Maxims on Boasting, Friendship, and Human Frailty

1 Do not vaunt yourself concerning the morrow, for you cannot foretell what the day itself may engender. 2 Let a stranger extol you, and not your own mouth; let a foreigner commend you, and not your own lips. 3 A stone is heavy, and sand is weighty, yet the provocation of a fool outweighs them both. 4 Wrath is cruel, and anger is a torrent, but who can stand before jealousy? 5 Candid reproof surpasses affection kept secret. 6 The wounds inflicted by a friend are trustworthy, whereas an enemy's kisses are treacherous. 7 A glutted appetite spurns honey, yet to the famished even bitterness is sweet. 8 As a bird that strays from its nest, so is a man who strays from his dwelling. 9 Oil and incense gladden the heart, and a friend's counsel is sweet to the soul.

On Fidelity, Rebuke, and Domestic Diligence

10 Do not forsake your friend or your father's friend, and do not repair to your brother's house in the day of your calamity; a neighbor nearby is better than a brother far away. 11 Be wise, my son, and gladden my heart, that I may answer the one who reproaches me. 12 The shrewd man discerns calamity and conceals himself, whereas the naive proceed and incur punishment. 13 Take his garment, since he has pledged himself for a stranger, and obtain security from him because of a foreign woman. 14 He who loudly blesses his neighbor at daybreak will have it counted as a curse. 15 A persistent drip on a rainy day and a contentious wife are comparable. 16 To restrain her is to restrain the wind, or to grasp oil with one's right hand.

On Character, Industry, and Household Prosperity

17 Iron sharpens iron, and one man whets another. 18 He who tends a fig tree will eat its fruit, and he who guards his master will be honored. 19 As water reflects face to face, so the heart of man reflects the man. 20 Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied, and the eyes of man are never satisfied. 21 The crucible refines silver, the furnace gold, and a man is assayed by the praise he receives. 22 Though you grind a fool in a mortar with a pestle along with crushed grain, yet his folly will not depart from him. 23 Know diligently the condition of your flocks, and give attention to your herds; 24 for wealth is not perpetual, nor does a diadem endure through every generation. 25 When the grass disappears, the tender grass appears, and the herbs of the mountains are gathered, 26 the lambs will provide your clothing, and the goats the price of a field; 27 and there will be enough goats' milk for your food, for the food of your household, and for the sustenance of your maidens.

The Conspiracy Against Jesus, the Anointing at Bethany, and the Bargain of Betrayal

1 When Jesus had brought all these discourses to their consummation, He addressed His disciples, 2 “You know that in two days’ time the Passover arrives, and the Son of Man is to be betrayed unto crucifixion.”

3 Thereupon the chief priests and the elders of the people assembled in the courtyard of the high priest, who was called Caiaphas, 4 and took counsel how they might seize Jesus by stratagem and put Him to death; 5 yet they said, “Not during the festival, lest a tumult arise among the people.”

6 While Jesus was in Bethany, in the house of Simon the leper, 7 a woman approached Him bearing an alabaster vessel of exceedingly precious ointment, and she poured it upon His head as He reclined at table.

8 When the disciples beheld this, they were indignant, 9 and said, “Why this prodigal waste? For this ointment might have been sold for a great price and the proceeds bestowed upon the poor.”

10 Jesus, perceiving their consternation, said to them, “Why do you vex the woman? For she has wrought a noble deed toward Me. 11 The poor you have always with you, but Me you do not always have. 12 In anointing My body with this perfume, she has prepared Me for burial. 13 Amen, I say to you, wherever this gospel is proclaimed throughout the whole world, what she has done shall be spoken of in memorial of her.”

14 Then one of the Twelve, Judas Iscariot, went to the chief priests 15 and said, “What are you willing to give me if I deliver Him to you?” And they weighed out to him thirty pieces of silver. 16 From that hour he sought an opportune moment to betray Him.

The Passover Meal, the Institution of the New Covenant, and the Foretelling of Betrayal

17 On the first day of Unleavened Bread the disciples came to Jesus and asked, “Where do you wish us to prepare for you the Passover meal?”

18 He replied, “Go into the city to a certain man and say to him, ‘The Teacher says, “My hour is near; I am to celebrate the Passover with My disciples at your house.”’” 19 So the disciples did as Jesus had appointed them, and they prepared the Passover.

20 When evening had come, He reclined at table with the Twelve; 21 and while they were eating, He said, “Amen, I tell you, one of you will betray Me.”

22 They were exceedingly grieved and began to say to Him, one after another, “Surely it is not I, Lord?”

23 He answered, “He who has dipped his hand in the bowl with Me is the one who will betray Me. 24 The Son of Man goes forth as it is written concerning Him; yet woe to that man through whom the Son of Man is betrayed! It would have been better for that man had he never been born.”

25 Judas, who was about to betray Him, answered, “Surely not I, Rabbi?”

Jesus said to him, “You have said it.”

26 While they were eating, Jesus took bread, blessed it, broke it, and gave it to the disciples, saying, “Take and eat; this is My body.”

27 Then He took a cup, and having given thanks, He gave it to them, saying, “Drink from it, all of you, 28 for this is My blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the remission of sins.”

29 “I tell you, I shall not drink again of this fruit of the vine until that day when I drink it anew with you in My Father’s kingdom.”

30 And after singing a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives.

31 Then Jesus said to them, “This night you will all stumble because of Me; for it is written, ‘I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock will be scattered.’ 32 But after I am raised, I will go before you to Galilee.”

33 Peter answered Him, “Even if all the rest stumble because of You, I will never stumble.”

34 Jesus said to him, “Amen, I tell you, this very night, before the cock crows, you will deny Me three times.”

35 Peter said to Him, “Even if I must die with You, I will not deny You.” And all the disciples said likewise.

Gethsemane: Anguish, Prayer, and Arrest

36 Then Jesus came with them to a place called Gethsemane, and He said to His disciples, “Sit here while I go yonder and pray.”

37 Taking with Him Peter and the two sons of Zebedee, He began to be grieved and sorely distressed. 38 Then He said to them, “My soul is exceedingly sorrowful, even unto death; remain here and keep watch with Me.”

39 Going a little farther, He fell on His face and prayed, saying, “My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from Me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as You will.”

40 He came to the disciples and found them sleeping, and He said to Peter, “So you could not keep watch with Me for one hour? 41 Keep watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation. The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak.”

42 Again, a second time, He went away and prayed, “My Father, if this cup cannot pass unless I drink it, Your will be done.”

43 Again He came and found them sleeping, for their eyes were heavy with slumber.

44 Leaving them again, He went away and prayed a third time, uttering the same words.

45 Then He came to the disciples and said to them, “Sleep on, then, and take your rest. Behold, the hour is at hand, and the Son of Man is betrayed into the hands of sinners. 46 Rise, let us be going; behold, My betrayer is at hand.”

47 While He was yet speaking, behold, Judas, one of the Twelve, arrived, and with him a great multitude bearing swords and clubs, sent from the chief priests and elders of the people.

48 Now the betrayer had given them a sign, saying, “The One whom I kiss is He; seize Him.” 49 And immediately he drew near to Jesus and said, “Hail, Rabbi!” and kissed Him.

50 Jesus said to him, “Friend, do that for which you have come.” Then they came and laid hands on Jesus and seized Him.

51 And behold, one of those with Jesus stretched out his hand, drew his sword, and struck the servant of the high priest, cutting off his ear.

52 Then Jesus said to him, “Return your sword to its place; for all who take up the sword shall perish by the sword. 53 Do you suppose that I cannot entreat My Father, and He will presently furnish Me with more than twelve legions of angels? 54 But how then would the Scriptures be fulfilled, that it must be thus?”

55 At that hour Jesus said to the crowds, “Have you come out as against a bandit, with swords and clubs, to apprehend Me? Day by day I sat teaching in the temple, and you did not seize Me. 56 But all this has come to pass so that the Scriptures of the prophets may be fulfilled.” Then all the disciples abandoned Him and fled.

The Trial Before Caiaphas and Peter’s Threefold Denial

57 Those who had seized Jesus led Him away to Caiaphas the high priest, where the scribes and elders were assembled. 58 Peter followed Him from afar, even to the courtyard of the high priest; and entering within, he sat down with the attendants to see the outcome.

59 The chief priests and the whole Sanhedrin sought false testimony against Jesus, that they might put Him to death; but they found none, though many false witnesses came forward. 60 At last two came forward 61 and said, “This fellow declared, ‘I am able to destroy the temple of God and to rebuild it in three days.’”

62 The high priest arose and said to Him, “Have you no answer? What is it that these men testify against you?” 63 But Jesus kept silent. The high priest said to Him, “I adjure you by the living God, tell us whether you are the Christ, the Son of God.”

64 Jesus said to him, “You have said it. Nevertheless, I tell you, from now on you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power and coming upon the clouds of heaven.”

65 Then the high priest tore his garments and said, “He has spoken blasphemy! What further need have we of witnesses? Behold, you have now heard the blasphemy. What do you think?”

66 They answered, “He is deserving of death.”

67 Then they spat in His face and struck Him; and some slapped Him, 68 saying, “Prophesy to us, Christ! Who is it that struck you?”

69 Now Peter was sitting outside in the courtyard, and a servant girl came to him and said, “You too were with Jesus the Galilean.”

70 But he denied it before them all, saying, “I do not know what you are talking about.”

71 When he had gone out to the gateway, another servant girl saw him and said to the bystanders, “This man was with Jesus of Nazareth.”

72 And again he denied it with an oath: “I do not know the man.”

73 After a little while the bystanders came up and said to Peter, “Certainly you too are one of them, for your speech gives you away.”

74 Then he began to curse and swear, “I do not know the man.” And immediately a cock crowed.

75 Then Peter remembered the word of Jesus: “Before the cock crows, you will deny Me three times.” And he went out and wept bitterly.

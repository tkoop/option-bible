Jonathan’s Audacious Sortie and the Philistine Rout

1 Now it happened one day that Jonathan, the son of Saul, said to the young man who bore his armor, “Come, let us cross over to the Philistine garrison on the far side,” though he did not disclose this to his father. 2 Saul was sitting beneath the pomegranate tree at Migron, near the edge of Gibeah, with about six hundred men. 3 Ahijah, who wore the ephod—Ahitub’s son, Ichabod’s brother, Phinehas’s son, Eli’s son, priest of the LORD at Shiloh—was with them, and the people did not know that Jonathan had gone. 4 Between the passes by which Jonathan sought to reach the Philistine encampment lay a rocky crag on one side and a rocky crag on the other; one was called Bozez, and the other Seneh. 5 One crag rose steeply to the north toward Michmash, and the other to the south toward Geba.

6 Jonathan said to the young man bearing his armor, “Come, let us cross over to the garrison of these uncircumcised men; perhaps the LORD will act on our behalf, for nothing hinders the LORD from saving, whether by many or by few.” 7 His armor-bearer replied, “Do all that is in your heart. Proceed; I am with you as your heart desires.”

8 Jonathan said, “Behold, we will cross over to the men and reveal ourselves to them. 9 If they say to us, ‘Wait until we come to you,’ then we will stand our ground and not go up to them. 10 But if they say, ‘Come up to us,’ then we will go up, for that will be our sign that the LORD has delivered them into our hands.”

11 So the two of them showed themselves to the Philistine garrison, and the Philistines said, “Look, the Hebrews are coming out of the holes where they have been hiding!” 12 The men of the garrison called to Jonathan and his armor-bearer, “Come up to us, and we will teach you a lesson.” Jonathan said to his armor-bearer, “Follow me, for the LORD has delivered them into the hand of Israel.” 13 Jonathan climbed up on his hands and feet, with his armor-bearer behind him. The Philistines fell before Jonathan, and his armor-bearer followed and killed them. 14 In that first assault Jonathan and his armor-bearer struck down about twenty men within an area of roughly half an acre.

15 Then terror struck the camp and the field, as well as all the people; the garrison and the raiders also trembled, and the earth quaked, so that it became a great panic from God. 16 Saul’s lookouts at Gibeah in Benjamin saw the multitude melting away in every direction.

17 Saul said to the men with him, “Muster the troops and see who has gone out from among us.” When they mustered them, behold, Jonathan and his armor-bearer were missing. 18 Saul said to Ahijah, “Bring the ark of God,” for at that time the ark of God was with the Israelites. 19 While Saul was speaking to the priest, the tumult in the Philistine camp grew greater and greater, so Saul said to the priest, “Withdraw your hand.” 20 Then Saul and all the people with him assembled and went to the battle, and behold, every man’s sword was against his fellow, so that there was very great confusion.

21 Moreover, the Hebrews who had formerly been with the Philistines and had gone up with them into the camp turned and joined the Israelites who were with Saul and Jonathan. 22 Likewise, all the Israelites who had hidden themselves in the hill country of Ephraim, when they heard that the Philistines were fleeing, pursued them closely in the battle. 23 So the LORD saved Israel that day, and the battle passed beyond Beth-aven.

Saul’s Rash Oath and the People’s Transgression Over the Plunder

24 The men of Israel were hard pressed that day, for Saul had bound the people by an oath, saying, “Cursed be the man who eats food before evening and before I have avenged myself on my enemies.” So none of the people tasted food. 25 Now all the land entered the forest, and there was honey on the ground. 26 When the people entered the forest, behold, the honey was oozing, yet no one put his hand to his mouth, for the people feared the oath.

27 But Jonathan had not heard when his father charged the people with the oath, so he put out the end of the staff that was in his hand, dipped it into the honeycomb, and put his hand to his mouth, and his eyes brightened. 28 Then one of the people answered and said, “Your father strictly charged the people with an oath, saying, ‘Cursed be the man who eats food today,’ and the people are faint.” 29 Jonathan said, “My father has brought trouble upon the land. See how my eyes have brightened because I tasted a little of this honey. 30 How much more if the people had freely eaten today of the spoil of their enemies! For then the slaughter of the Philistines would have been much greater.”

31 They struck down the Philistines that day from Michmash to Aijalon, and the people were very faint. 32 The people rushed upon the spoil and took sheep, oxen, and calves, slaughtered them on the ground, and ate them with the blood. 33 They told Saul, “Behold, the people are sinning against the LORD by eating with the blood.” He said, “You have dealt treacherously. Roll a great stone here to me today.” 34 Saul said, “Disperse among the people and tell them, ‘Each of you bring me his ox or his sheep, slaughter them here, and eat; do not sin against the LORD by eating with the blood.’” So all the people brought his ox that night and slaughtered it there. 35 Saul built an altar to the LORD; it was the first altar that he built to the LORD.

Inquiry by Lot and the People’s Deliverance of Jonathan

36 Saul said, “Let us go down after the Philistines by night, plunder them until morning, and leave not one of them.” They said, “Do whatever seems best to you.” But the priest said, “Let us draw near to God here.” 37 Saul inquired of God, “Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will You give them into the hand of Israel?” But He did not answer him that day. 38 Saul said, “Come here, all you leaders of the people, and know and see how this sin came about today. 39 For as the LORD lives, who saves Israel, though it be in Jonathan my son, he shall surely die.” But no one answered him from all the people. 40 Then he said to all Israel, “You shall be on one side, and I and Jonathan my son will be on the other side.” And the people said, “Do what seems best to you.” 41 Therefore Saul said to the LORD, the God of Israel, “Give a perfect answer.” Jonathan and Saul were taken, but the people went free. 42 Saul said, “Cast the lot between me and Jonathan my son.” And Jonathan was taken.

43 Saul said to Jonathan, “Tell me what you have done.” Jonathan told him, “I tasted a little honey with the end of the staff that was in my hand. Here I am; I must die.” 44 Saul said, “God do so to me, and more also, for you shall surely die, Jonathan.” 45 But the people said to Saul, “Shall Jonathan die, who has worked this great salvation in Israel? Far be it! As the LORD lives, not one hair of his head shall fall to the ground, for he has worked with God this day.” So the people rescued Jonathan, and he did not die.

Saul’s Consolidated Kingship and Relentless Campaigns

46 Then Saul ceased pursuing the Philistines, and the Philistines withdrew to their own land. 47 After Saul had consolidated his kingship over Israel, he fought against all his enemies on every side—against Moab, against the Ammonites, against Edom, against the kings of Zobah, and against the Philistines—and wherever he turned, he inflicted punishment on them. 48 He fought valiantly and struck down Amalek, and delivered Israel from the hand of those who plundered them. 49 The sons of Saul were Jonathan, Ishvi, and Malchi-shua; the name of his firstborn daughter was Merab, and the name of the younger was Michal. 50 The name of Saul’s wife was Ahinoam, the daughter of Ahimaaz. The name of the commander of his army was Abner, the son of Ner, who was Saul’s uncle. 51 Kish, Saul’s father, and Ner, Abner’s father, were sons of Abiel. 52 There was fierce war against the Philistines all the days of Saul, and whenever Saul saw any mighty man or any valiant man, he took him into his service.

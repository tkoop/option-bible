Samson's Vengeance and the Philistines' Reprisal

1 After an interval of days, in the season of the wheat harvest, Samson took a kid of the goats and went to visit his wife; he declared, "I shall enter the chamber to my wife." Yet her father refused to grant him admittance.

2 Her father rejoined, "I was persuaded that you had come to abhor her beyond measure, and therefore I bestowed her upon your companion. Is not her younger sister more comely than she? I entreat you, let her be yours instead."

3 Samson said to them, "On this occasion I shall be held guiltless when I bring calamity upon the Philistines."

4 Samson went forth and captured three hundred foxes; he procured torches, turned the animals tail to tail, and placed a single torch between each pair of tails. 5 He ignited the torches and released the foxes into the standing grain of the Philistines, consuming the shocks and the standing crop, and extending the conflagration to the vineyards and olive orchards.

6 The Philistines demanded, "Who perpetrated this outrage?"

They were informed, "Samson, the son-in-law of the Timnite, because his wife was transferred to his companion." Thereupon the Philistines ascended and burned both the woman and her father with fire.

7 Samson said to them, "If you act in this manner, I shall not desist until I have exacted vengeance upon you."

8 He fell upon them with ferocious violence and slew them in great numbers; thereafter he descended and took refuge in the cleft of the rock of Etam.

Philistine Encroachment, Judah's Submission, and the Spirit's Deliverance

9 The Philistines marched up and encamped in Judah, deploying their forces in Lehi.

10 The men of Judah asked, "Why have you ascended against us?"

They replied, "We have come to bind Samson, that we may repay him in kind for what he has done to us."

11 Three thousand men of Judah descended to the cleft of the rock of Etam and said to Samson, "Do you not perceive that the Philistines rule over us? What, then, is this that you have wrought against us?"

He answered them, "I have merely rendered to them what they first rendered to me."

12 They said to him, "We have come to bind you and deliver you into the hands of the Philistines."

Samson said to them, "Swear to me that you yourselves will not attack me."

13 They assured him, "No, we shall only bind you securely and hand you over to them; we will not put you to death." Accordingly, they bound him with two new cords and led him up from the rock.

14 As he approached Lehi, the Philistines raised a shout to meet him. Then the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him with power, and the ropes upon his arms became like flax consumed by fire, so that the bonds melted from his hands.

15 Finding a fresh jawbone of a donkey, he reached out his hand, seized it, and with it struck down a thousand men.

16 Samson said, "With the jawbone of a donkey I have heaped them in piles; with the jawbone of a donkey I have slain a thousand men."

17 When he had finished speaking, he cast the jawbone from his hand and named that place Ramath-lehi.

Thirst, Divine Provision, and Samson's Sustained Judgeship

18 Being exceedingly thirsty, he cried out to the LORD, saying, "You have granted this great deliverance by the hand of Your servant; must I now die of thirst and fall into the hands of the uncircumcised?"

19 Then God split open the hollow place that is in Lehi, and water issued forth; when Samson drank, his spirit returned, and he was revived. Therefore he named it En-hakkore, which remains in Lehi to this day.

20 Samson judged Israel for twenty years during the days of the Philistines.

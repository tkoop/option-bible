Paul Before the Sanhedrin

1 Paul, fixing his gaze intently upon the Sanhedrin, declared, "Brothers, I have conducted myself before God with an entirely good conscience up to this very day."

2 But Ananias the high priest ordered those standing beside him to strike him on the mouth.

3 Then Paul said to him, "God will strike you, you whitewashed wall! Do you sit there judging me according to the law, while you yourself transgress the law by commanding that I be struck?"

4 Those standing nearby said, "Do you revile the high priest of God?"

5 And Paul said, "I did not know, brothers, that he was high priest; for it is written, 'You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people.'"

6 But Paul, perceiving that one faction consisted of Sadducees and the other of Pharisees, cried out in the Sanhedrin, "Brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees. It is concerning the hope and the resurrection of the dead that I am on trial."

7 When he had spoken this, a dissension arose between the Pharisees and the Sadducees, and the assembly was divided.

8 For the Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, nor angel, nor spirit, but the Pharisees acknowledge both.

9 Then a great clamor arose, and some of the scribes belonging to the Pharisees' party stood up and contended, saying, "We find nothing wrong in this man. What if a spirit or an angel has spoken to him?"

10 And when the dissension became great, the commander, fearing that Paul would be torn apart by them, ordered the troops to go down and seize him from their midst and bring him into the barracks.

11 The following night the Lord stood by him and said, "Take courage, for as you have testified to the things concerning Me in Jerusalem, so you must also testify in Rome."

The Conspiracy Against Paul

12 When day came, the Jews formed a conspiracy and bound themselves under a curse, saying that they would neither eat nor drink until they had killed Paul.

13 There were more than forty men who had made this plot.

14 They came to the chief priests and elders and said, "We have bound ourselves under a great curse to taste nothing until we have killed Paul. 15 Now therefore, you, together with the council, inform the commander to bring him down to you, as though you were going to investigate his case more accurately; and we are ready to kill him before he comes near."

16 But when Paul's sister's son heard of the ambush, he came and entered the barracks and reported it to Paul.

17 Paul called one of the centurions and said, "Take this young man to the commander, for he has something to report to him."

18 So he took him and brought him to the commander and said, "Paul the prisoner called me and asked me to bring this young man to you, because he has something to say to you."

19 The commander took him by the hand, withdrew privately, and asked, "What is it that you have to report to me?"

20 And he said, "The Jews have agreed to ask you to bring Paul down to the council tomorrow, as though they were going to inquire more accurately concerning him. 21 Do not be persuaded by them, for more than forty of their men lie in wait for him, having bound themselves under a curse neither to eat nor to drink until they have killed him; and now they are ready, waiting for your promise."

22 So the commander dismissed the young man, charging him, "Tell no one that you have disclosed these things to me."

Paul Escorted to Caesarea

23 Calling two of the centurions, he said, "Prepare two hundred soldiers, seventy horsemen, and two hundred spearmen to march as far as Caesarea at the third hour of the night, 24 and provide mounts so that they may set Paul on them and bring him safely to Governor Felix."

25 He wrote a letter in this form:

26 "Claudius Lysias, to the most excellent Governor Felix: Greetings. 27 This man was seized by the Jews and was about to be killed by them when I came upon them with the troops and rescued him, having learned that he is a Roman citizen. 28 Desiring to know the charge for which they were accusing him, I brought him down to their council. 29 I found that he was being accused about questions of their law, but had no charge against him deserving death or imprisonment. 30 When it was disclosed to me that a plot would be made against the man, I sent him to you at once, commanding his accusers also to state before you what they have against him."

31 So the soldiers, according to their orders, took Paul and brought him by night to Antipatris.

32 On the next day they returned to the barracks, leaving the horsemen to go on with him.

33 When they entered Caesarea and delivered the letter to the governor, they also presented Paul to him.

34 Having read it, he asked what province he was from; and when he learned that he was from Cilicia, 35 he said, "I will give you a hearing when your accusers arrive." And he commanded that he be kept under guard in Herod's praetorium.

The Parable of the Wicked Tenants

1 And He began to speak to them in parables: “A certain proprietor planted a vineyard, enclosed it with a hedge, dug a winepress, built a watchtower, leased it to husbandmen, and journeyed abroad.”

2 “At the appointed season he dispatched a servant to the tenants to receive from them a share of the vineyard’s produce. 3 They seized him, scourged him, and sent him away empty-handed. 4 He sent another servant; they struck him on the head and treated him ignominiously. 5 He sent yet another, whom they slew; and of many others, some they beat and some they put to death.”

6 “He still had one beloved son, and as a last resort he sent him to them, saying, ‘They will reverence my son.’ 7 But those tenants said among themselves, ‘This is the heir; come, let us kill him, and the inheritance shall be ours.’ 8 So they seized him, slew him, and cast him outside the vineyard.”

9 “What, then, will the lord of the vineyard do? He will come and destroy those tenants and allot the vineyard to others. 10 Have you not read this Scripture: ‘The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone; 11 this was the Lord’s doing, and it is wondrous in our sight’?”

12 And they sought to arrest Him, for they perceived that He had spoken the parable against them; yet, fearing the crowd, they left Him and went away.

The Question of Tribute to Caesar

13 And they send to Him some of the Pharisees and Herodians, that they might entrap Him in discourse.

14 And coming, they say to Him, “Teacher, we know that You are veracious and defer to no one; for You do not show partiality to men, but teach the way of God in truth. Is it lawful to remit tribute to Caesar, or not? Shall we give, or shall we withhold?”

15 But He, discerning their hypocrisy, said to them, “Why do you put Me to the test? Bring Me a denarius, that I may examine it.”

16 And they brought one. He then asks them, “Whose image is this, and whose inscription?”

They answered Him, “Caesar’s.”

17 And Jesus said to them, “Render unto Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and unto God the things that are God’s.” And they marveled at Him.

The Resurrection and the Great Commandment

18 And Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, come to Him and question Him, saying, 19 “Teacher, Moses wrote to us that if a man’s brother die and leave a wife but no children, his brother should take the wife and raise up offspring for his brother. 20 Now there were seven brothers: the first took a wife and died, leaving no offspring. 21 The second took her and died, leaving no offspring; and the third likewise. 22 And the seven left no offspring. Last of all the woman died also. 23 In the resurrection, therefore, when they rise, whose wife shall she be? For the seven had her as wife.”

24 Jesus said to them, “Are you not mistaken, because you know neither the Scriptures nor the power of God? 25 For when they rise from the dead, they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but are like angels in heaven. 26 And concerning the dead, that they rise—have you not read in the book of Moses, in the passage of the bush, how God spoke to him, saying, ‘I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob’? 27 He is not the God of the dead, but of the living. You are greatly mistaken.”

28 And one of the scribes came, and having heard them disputing, and seeing that He had answered them well, asked Him, “Which commandment is preeminent among all?”

29 Jesus answered him, “The first is, ‘Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God, the Lord is one. 30 And you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind, and with all your strength.’ 31 The second is this, ‘You shall love your neighbor as yourself.’ There is no other commandment greater than these.”

32 And the scribe said to Him, “Well said, Teacher; You have spoken truthfully, that He is one and there is no other besides Him. 33 And to love Him with all one’s heart and with all one’s understanding and with all one’s strength, and to love one’s neighbor as oneself, is far more than all burnt offerings and sacrifices.”

34 And Jesus, seeing that he had answered thoughtfully, said to him, “You are not far from the kingdom of God.” And no one dared to question Him any longer.

David’s Son and the Widow’s Offering

35 And while teaching in the temple, Jesus answered and said, “How is it that the scribes say that the Christ is the son of David? 36 David himself, speaking by the Holy Spirit, declared, ‘The Lord said to my Lord, “Sit at My right hand, until I put Your enemies beneath Your feet.”’ 37 David himself calls Him ‘Lord’; how then is He his son?” And the great crowd heard Him gladly.

38 And in His teaching He said, “Beware of the scribes, who desire to walk in long robes and to receive salutations in the marketplaces, 39 and the chief seats in the synagogues and the places of honor at banquets— 40 those who devour widows’ houses and, for a pretense, make long prayers. These shall receive a more severe condemnation.”

41 And having sat down opposite the treasury, He observed how the crowd cast money into the treasury; and many rich persons cast in much. 42 And a certain poor widow came and cast in two lepta, that is, a quadrans.

43 And having called His disciples to Him, He said to them, “Truly I say to you that this poor widow has cast in more than all those who are casting into the treasury. 44 For they all cast in out of their abundance, but she out of her want has cast in all that she had, her whole livelihood.”

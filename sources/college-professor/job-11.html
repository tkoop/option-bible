Zophar Rebukes Job’s Loquacity

1 Then Zophar the Naamathite answered and said:

2 “Shall an abundance of words go unanswered, or shall a man of mere lips be vindicated? 3 Your empty talk silences men, and you scoff, and no one puts you to shame. 4 You say, ‘My teaching is pure, and I am clean in Your eyes.’

5 “But oh, that God would speak, and open His lips with you! 6 And that He would show you the secrets of wisdom—for sound wisdom is twofold—know then that God exacts from you less than your iniquity deserves.

The Incomprehensibility of Divine Wisdom

7 “Can you fathom the deep things of God? Can you discover the limit of the Almighty? 8 It is higher than heaven—what can you do? Deeper than Sheol—what can you know? 9 Its measure is longer than the earth and broader than the sea. 10 If He passes by, shuts up, and summons to judgment, who can turn Him back? 11 For He knows worthless men; He sees iniquity as well, and will He not take note? 12 But a man empty of understanding will never truly become wise—just as a wild donkey’s colt cannot be born human.

The Conditions and Rewards of Repentance

13 “If you prepare your heart and stretch out your hands to Him, 14 if you put away the iniquity in your hand and do not let unrighteousness dwell in your tents, 15 then surely you will lift up your face without blemish; you will be secure and not fear. 16 For you will forget your misery; you will remember it as waters that have passed away. 17 And your life will be brighter than noonday; darkness will be like the morning. 18 You will be secure, because there is hope; you will dig, and lie down in safety. 19 You will lie down, and no one will make you afraid; many will entreat your favor. 20 But the eyes of the wicked will fail, and they will find no escape; their hope will end in a dying breath.”

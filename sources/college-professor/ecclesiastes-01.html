The Teacher’s Discourse

1 These are the words of the Teacher, son of David, king in Jerusalem. 2 “Supreme futility,” says the Teacher, “supreme futility! All is futile.” 3 What gain does a person derive from all his labor, the toil with which he toils in this earthly life?

The Recurring Patterns of the World

4 A generation departs and a generation arrives, but the earth endures forever. 5 The sun rises and the sun sets; then, hastening back to the place from which it rises, it rises there again. 6 The wind proceeds southward and veers northward; circling round and round, it returns upon its circuits. 7 All rivers run to the sea, yet the sea is not full; to the place where the rivers flow, thither they return to flow again. 8 All things are wearisome—more wearisome than one can utter. The eye is never satiated with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing.

9 What has been is what will be, and what has been done is what will be done; there is nothing new in this world. 10 If there exists anything concerning which one may say, “Behold, this is new,” it has already existed in the ages before us. 11 There is no remembrance of former things, and even the things that are yet to come will not be remembered by those who follow.

The Teacher’s Royal Investigation

12 I, the Teacher, was king over Israel in Jerusalem. 13 I devoted my heart to seek and to search out by wisdom all that is done on earth. It is a grievous business that God has imposed upon the children of Adam, that they may be busied with it. 14 I have observed all the deeds done on earth, and indeed, all is futility and a fruitless pursuit. 15 What is crooked cannot be straightened, and what is lacking cannot be reckoned.

The Burden of Wisdom

16 I said to myself, “Behold, I have grown great and increased in wisdom beyond all who were before me in Jerusalem, and my mind has observed much wisdom and knowledge.” 17 I devoted my mind to knowing wisdom, and to knowing madness and folly; I perceived that this too is a fruitless pursuit. 18 For in much wisdom is much vexation, and he who increases knowledge increases sorrow.

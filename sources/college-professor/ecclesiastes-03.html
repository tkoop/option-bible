The Appointed Seasons of Human Existence

1 For everything there exists a designated season, and beneath the heavens an appointed hour attends every purpose. 2 There is a time to be born and a time to die, a time to plant and a time to uproot what has been planted; 3 a time to kill and a time to heal, a time to tear down and a time to build; 4 a time to weep and a time to laugh, a time to mourn and a time to dance; 5 a time to cast stones away and a time to gather stones, a time to embrace and a time to refrain from embracing; 6 a time to seek and a time to forfeit, a time to retain and a time to relinquish; 7 a time to tear and a time to mend, a time to keep silence and a time to speak; 8 a time to love and a time to hate, a time for war and a time for peace.

Divine Sovereignty and the Limits of Human Comprehension

9 What substantive benefit, then, accrues to the laborer from his incessant toil? 10 I have discerned the grievous occupation that God has imposed upon humanity, whereby they might be preoccupied with toil. 11 He has rendered every undertaking fitting in its appointed moment; moreover, He has implanted an intimation of eternity within their consciousness, although no human being can fully apprehend the totality of God’s work from inception to consummation. 12 I have concluded that there is no superior good for them than to rejoice and to perform virtuous deeds throughout the span of their lives. 13 Furthermore, that every person should eat and drink, and discern felicity in the fruit of all his labor, constitutes a divine endowment. 14 I am persuaded that whatever God accomplishes possesses eternal permanence; nothing may be augmented thereto, nor anything excised therefrom; and God ordains it thus in order that humanity might revere Him. 15 That which is has already been, and that which is to be has already occurred; and God retrieves what has gone by.

Injustice Under the Sun and the Finality of Death

16 Furthermore, I observed beneath the sun that in the very seat of justice, iniquity presided, and in the locus of righteousness, wickedness likewise held sway. 17 I reasoned within my heart that God will adjudicate between the righteous and the wicked, because an appointed season exists for every purpose and for every deed. 18 I further reflected in my heart regarding the lot of humanity, namely that God puts them to the test so that they might recognize themselves to be, in their mortal condition, akin to beasts. 19 For the destiny that befalls humanity and the destiny that befalls the beasts is identical: as the one perishes, so perishes the other, and both share one breath; consequently, humanity possesses no enduring preeminence over the beasts, for all is vanity. 20 All proceed to a single destination; all derive from dust, and all revert to dust. 21 Who can ascertain whether the spirit of man ascends on high while the spirit of the beast descends into the earth? 22 Therefore, I perceived that there is nothing superior to a person’s rejoicing in his own labor, for that constitutes his allotted portion; for who shall enable him to behold what shall transpire after him?

The Birth and Rivalry of Cain and Abel

1Adam knew his wife Eve; she conceived and bore Cain, declaring, “With the help of the LORD I have acquired a man.” 2She bore again, this time his brother Abel. Abel became a shepherd, whereas Cain tilled the soil.

3After some time had elapsed, Cain brought an offering to the LORD from the fruit of the ground. 4Abel, for his part, brought some of the firstlings of his flock, along with their fat portions. The LORD regarded Abel and his offering with favor, 5but He did not regard Cain and his offering. Cain therefore grew intensely wrathful, and his countenance fell.

6The LORD said to Cain, “Why are you incensed, and why has your countenance fallen? 7If you act rightly, will you not be accepted? Yet if you do not act rightly, sin is crouching at the door; its desire is directed toward you, but you must master it.”

The First Murder and Its Aftermath

8Cain spoke to his brother Abel. And when they were in the field, Cain rose up against his brother Abel and slew him.

9The LORD said to Cain, “Where is your brother Abel?”

Cain replied, “I do not know. Am I my brother’s keeper?”

10The LORD said, “What have you done? The voice of your brother’s blood cries out to Me from the ground.”

11The LORD continued, “Now you are cursed and banished from the ground, which opened its mouth to receive your brother’s blood from your hand. 12When you cultivate the ground, it will no longer yield its vigor to you; you will become a restless fugitive upon the earth.”

13Cain said to the LORD, “My punishment exceeds what I can endure. 14Behold, You have driven me this day from the land, and from Your presence I shall be concealed. I will be a restless fugitive upon the earth, and anyone who encounters me will kill me.”

15The LORD said to him, “No; if anyone slays Cain, vengeance shall be exacted upon him sevenfold.” And the LORD placed a mark upon Cain, so that whoever encountered him might not strike him down.

16So Cain departed from the LORD’s presence and settled in the land of Nod, east of Eden.

The Line of Cain and the Rise of Human Culture

17Cain was intimate with his wife; she conceived and bore Enoch. Cain built a city and named it after his son Enoch. 18To Enoch was born Irad; Irad fathered Mehujael; Mehujael fathered Methushael; and Methushael fathered Lamech.

19Lamech took two wives: one named Adah and the other Zillah. 20Adah bore Jabal, the progenitor of those who dwell in tents and tend livestock. 21His brother was named Jubal, the progenitor of all who play the lyre and the pipe. 22Zillah likewise bore Tubal-cain, a forger of every implement of bronze and iron; the sister of Tubal-cain was Naamah.

Lamech’s Boast and the Escalation of Violence

23Lamech said to his wives, “Adah and Zillah, hear my voice; wives of Lamech, attend to my words: I have slain a man for wounding me, and a young man for bruising me. 24If Cain is avenged sevenfold, then Lamech shall be avenged seventy-sevenfold.”

The Birth of Seth and the Renewal of Worship

25Adam was intimate with his wife once more; she bore a son and named him Seth, saying, “God has granted me another offspring in place of Abel, because Cain killed him.” 26To Seth also a son was born, and he named him Enosh. At that time people began to invoke the name of the LORD.

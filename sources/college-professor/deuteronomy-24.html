Regulations on Divorce, Defilement, and the Sanctity of Marriage

1 When a man marries a woman and she becomes his wife, yet she fails to find favor in his eyes because he has discovered in her some shameful matter, he may write her a certificate of divorce, place it in her hand, and dismiss her from his house. 2 After she has left his house, she may go and become another man's wife. 3 If the subsequent husband likewise comes to despise her and writes her a bill of divorce, placing it in her hand and sending her away from his house—or if that latter husband dies, the one who took her as wife— 4 then her first husband, who dismissed her, may not take her back as his wife after she has been defiled, for such a reunion is an abomination before the LORD; nor shall you bring sin upon the land that the LORD your God gives you as an inheritance.

Newlywed Exemptions, Kidnapping, and Ritual Safeguards

5 When a man marries a new wife, he shall not march out with the army, nor shall any civic burden be imposed upon him; for one year he shall be exempt for his household, and he shall bring joy to the wife he has taken. 6 No one shall take a pair of millstones, nor even the upper stone, as security, for that would be to take a life in pledge. 7 If a man is found to have kidnapped one of his brothers among the Israelites, whether to enslave him or to sell him, that thief shall die, and you shall purge the evil from your midst. 8 In a case of leprous disease, be exceedingly careful to observe and do all that the Levitical priests instruct you, as I have commanded them; so you shall be careful to do. 9 Remember what the LORD your God did to Miriam on the way, when you came out of Egypt.

Justice in Pledges, Wages, and Judicial Equity

10 When you make a loan of any kind to your neighbor, you shall not enter his house to take his pledge. 11 You shall stand outside, and the man to whom you lend shall bring the pledge out to you. 12 If he is a poor man, you shall not go to sleep while his pledge remains in your possession. 13 You shall surely return his pledge to him by sunset, so that he may sleep in his garment and bless you; and it shall be righteousness for you before the LORD your God. 14 You shall not oppress a hired servant who is poor and needy, whether he is one of your brothers or a sojourner residing in your land within your towns. 15 You shall give him his wages on the day they are due, before sunset, because he is poor and sets his heart upon them, lest he cry out against you to the LORD, and it be sin to you. 16 Fathers shall not be put to death for their sons, nor sons for their fathers; each man shall be put to death for his own sin. 17 You shall not pervert justice for the sojourner or the orphan, nor take a widow's garment as a pledge. 18 You shall remember that you were a slave in Egypt, and the LORD your God redeemed you from there; therefore I command you to do this thing.

Harvest Gleanings and the Remembrance of Slavery

19 When you reap your harvest in your field and forget a sheaf in the field, you shall not return to take it; it shall belong to the sojourner, the orphan, and the widow, so that the LORD your God may bless you in all the work of your hands. 20 When you beat your olive tree, you shall not go over the boughs again; it shall be for the sojourner, the orphan, and the widow. 21 When you gather the grapes of your vineyard, you shall not glean after you; it shall be for the sojourner, the orphan, and the widow. 22 You shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt; therefore I command you to do this thing.

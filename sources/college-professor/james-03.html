The Weighty Office of Teaching

1 Let not many of you aspire to the office of teacher, my brethren, for you are well aware that we who teach shall incur a more stringent judgment. 2 For we all lapse in a multitude of ways; yet if a man never stumbles in speech, he is a consummate human being, endowed with the capacity to bridle his entire corporeal frame.

The Untamable Tongue

3 When we insert bits into the mouths of horses in order to secure their obedience, we thereby redirect their entire bodily frame. 4 Consider, moreover, the ships: though they be of prodigious magnitude and driven by tempestuous gales, they are steered by a rudder of the most diminutive scale, whithersoever the helmsman's volition directs. 5 Even so, the tongue, though a diminutive member, vaunts itself with grandiose pretensions; behold how a mere spark may ignite an immense expanse of timber. 6 The tongue, moreover, is a fire—a veritable world of iniquity situated among our members; it sullies the entire body, sets ablaze the wheel of nativity, and is itself ignited by Gehenna. 7 For every species of beast and bird, of reptile and marine creature, is subdued and has been subdued by the human constitution. 8 Yet no human being can subdue the tongue; it is an unruly malefactor, replete with lethal venom. 9 By means of it we bless the Lord and Father, while by the same instrument we invoke curses upon human beings who have been fashioned after the likeness of God. 10 From one and the same mouth proceed benediction and malediction. My brethren, such incongruity ought not to obtain. 11 Can a spring yield from a single aperture both the sweet and the bitter? 12 My brethren, can a fig tree bear olives, or a vine bear figs? Neither is saline water capable of yielding sweetness.

Earthly and Heavenly Wisdom

13 Who among you is wise and discerning? Let such a one evince, through an honorable mode of life, his deeds in the meekness of wisdom. 14 But if you harbor bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your hearts, do not vaunt yourselves, nor prevaricate against the truth. 15 Such wisdom is not that which descends from above; rather, it is terrestrial, naturalistic, and demoniacal. 16 For wherever jealousy and selfish ambition obtain, there one finds tumult and every species of depraved practice. 17 But the wisdom that is from above is, in the first instance, pure; thereafter peaceable, gentle, tractable, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial and unfeigned. 18 Moreover, the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace by those who are peacemakers.

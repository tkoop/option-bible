Mordecai's Public Lamentation and the Jews' Mourning

1When Mordecai learned of all that had been done, he tore his garments, clothed himself in sackcloth and ashes, and went out into the midst of the city, crying out with a great and bitter lamentation. 2He advanced only as far as the king's gate, for no one arrayed in sackcloth was permitted to enter the king's gate. 3And in every province, wherever the king's decree and edict reached, there was great mourning among the Jews, accompanied by fasting, weeping, and lamentation; multitudes reclined upon sackcloth and ashes.

Esther's Distress and the Commissioning of Hathach

4When Esther's maids and eunuchs came and reported these things to the queen, she was exceedingly distressed; she sent garments to clothe Mordecai and to remove his sackcloth from him, but he would not receive them.

5Then Esther summoned Hathach, one of the eunuchs whom the king had appointed to attend her, and commanded him concerning Mordecai, to ascertain what this was and why it was.

6So Hathach went out to Mordecai in the city square before the king's gate.

7Mordecai disclosed to him all that had befallen him, together with the precise sum of money that Haman had promised to weigh out into the king's treasuries for the annihilation of the Jews. 8He also gave him a copy of the written decree that had been issued in Susa for their destruction, in order that he might show it to Esther and explain it to her, and he charged him to command her to enter the king's presence, to entreat his favor and to petition before him on behalf of her people.

9Hathach returned and reported Mordecai's words to Esther.

Esther's Hesitation and Mordecai's Challenge

10Then Esther commanded him to answer Mordecai, saying, 11"All the king's servants and the people of the king's provinces know that for every man or woman who comes to the king in the inner court without being summoned there is one law—to be put to death—except for the one to whom the king extends the golden scepter, that he may live; and I have not been summoned to come to the king these thirty days."

12When Esther's words were reported to Mordecai, 13he sent back this reply: "Do not imagine that you will escape in the king's house, alone among all the Jews. 14For if you keep silent at this time, relief and deliverance will arise for the Jews from another place, but you and your father's house will perish; and who knows whether you have not attained to the kingdom for such a time as this?"

The Collective Fast and Esther's Resolve

15Then Esther sent this reply to Mordecai: 16"Go, gather all the Jews who are found in Susa, and fast on my behalf; do not eat and do not drink for three days, night and day. I and my maidens will likewise fast; and thus I will go to the king, though it is not according to the law; and if I perish, I perish."

17So Mordecai departed and did according to all that Esther had commanded him.

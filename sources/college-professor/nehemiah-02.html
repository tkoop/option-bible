Nehemiah’s Petition Before Artaxerxes

1 In the month of Nisan, in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes, wine was set before him; I took up the wine and presented it to the king, for I had never before appeared sorrowful in his presence.

2 The king said to me, “Why does your face look so distressed, when you are not ill? This can be nothing other than sorrow of spirit.” I was seized with very great fear.

3 I replied to the king, “May the king live forever! Why should not my countenance be downcast, when the city of my fathers’ sepulchers lies in ruins and its gates have been consumed by fire?”

4 The king said to me, “What is it you are requesting?” Then I prayed to the God of heaven.

5 I said to the king, “If it please the king, and if your servant has found favor in your sight, send me to Judah, to the city of my fathers’ sepulchers, that I may rebuild it.”

6 The king said to me—the queen being seated beside him—“How long will your journey be, and when will you return?” It pleased the king to send me, and I appointed a time for my return.

7 I said to the king, “If it please the king, let letters be given to me for the governors beyond the River, that they may grant me passage until I reach Judah, 8 and a letter to Asaph, keeper of the king’s forest, that he may give me timber to make beams for the gates of the citadel by the temple, for the wall of the city, and for the house in which I shall dwell.” The king granted these requests, for the gracious favor of my God was upon me.

The Journey to Jerusalem and a Nocturnal Survey of the Walls

9 I came to the governors beyond the River and presented them with the king’s letters. The king had also sent with me officers of the army and cavalry.

10 When Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite, the servant, heard of it, they were deeply vexed that a man had come to seek the welfare of the Israelites.

11 I came to Jerusalem and remained there three days.

12 I arose during the night, I and a few men with me. I had told no one what my God had put into my heart to do for Jerusalem, and no animal was with me except the one on which I rode. 13 I went out by night through the Valley Gate toward the Dragon Spring and the Dung Gate, surveying the walls of Jerusalem, which were broken down, and its gates, which had been consumed by fire. 14 Then I passed on to the Fountain Gate and to the King’s Pool, but there was not room enough for the animal under me to pass. 15 So I went up by night through the valley and inspected the wall; then I turned back and reentered by the Valley Gate, and so returned. 16 The officials did not know where I had gone or what I was doing, for I had not yet disclosed anything to the Jews, the priests, the nobles, the officials, or the rest of those who did the work.

The People Resolve to Rebuild

17 Then I said to them, “You see the distress in which we find ourselves, how Jerusalem lies in ruins and its gates have been burned. Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, so that we may no longer be an object of reproach.”

18 I recounted to them how the favor of my God had been upon me, and also what the king had said to me. They replied, “Let us rise up and build.” So they strengthened their resolve to undertake the good work.

19 But when Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite, the servant, and Geshem the Arab heard of it, they mocked us and held us in contempt, saying, “What is this thing that you are doing? Are you rebelling against the king?”

20 I answered them, “The God of heaven will prosper us, and we, His servants, will arise and build; but you have no share, right, or memorial in Jerusalem.”

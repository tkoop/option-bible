Love as the Indispensable Endowment

1 If I should discourse in the tongues of human beings and of angels, yet be bereft of love, I have become a sounding bronze or a clanging cymbal. 2 And if I possess prophetic utterance, and apprehend all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I hold all faith sufficient to remove mountains, yet lack love, I am nothing. 3 And if I give away all my possessions to feed the poor, and if I surrender my body in order to boast, yet am devoid of love, I derive no benefit whatsoever.

The Ethical Profile of Love

4 Love is long-suffering; love is kindly disposed; love is not envious; love does not vaunt itself; it is not inflated with pride. 5 It does not act indecorously; it does not pursue its own interests; it is not readily exasperated; it does not tally wrongs. 6 It takes no pleasure in injustice, but exults in concert with the truth. 7 It shelters all things, credits all things, anticipates all things, and perseveres through all things.

The Indefectibility of Love and the Passing of Partial Gifts

8 Love is never brought to ruin. Whether there be prophecies, they shall be rendered void; whether there be tongues, they shall fall silent; whether there be knowledge, it shall be superseded. 9 For our knowledge is partial, and our prophesying is partial. 10 Yet when the consummate arrives, the partial shall be superseded.

From Partial Knowledge to Face-to-Face Communion

11 When I was a child, I spoke as a child, I apprehended as a child, I reasoned as a child; upon becoming a man, I relinquished the ways of childhood. 12 For at present we perceive through a mirror, obscurely; but then, face to face. At present I know partially; but then I shall know fully, even as I have been fully known. 13 So now abide faith, hope, and love—these three; but the greatest among them is love.

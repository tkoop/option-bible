Jacob Purifies His Household and Returns to Bethel

1 God said to Jacob, “Arise, ascend to Bethel and abide there, and erect an altar there to the God who manifested Himself to you when you fled from your brother Esau.”

2 Jacob said to his household and to all who accompanied him, “Expunge the foreign deities that dwell in your midst, purify yourselves, and exchange your garments. 3 Let us arise and go up to Bethel, that I may make there an altar to the God who answered me in the day of my distress and has been with me in the way that I have gone.”

4 Accordingly, they surrendered to Jacob all the foreign deities in their possession, together with the rings in their ears; and Jacob interred them beneath the oak near Shechem. 5 As they departed, a divinely instilled terror descended upon the surrounding cities, so that none pursued the sons of Jacob. 6 Jacob, together with all the people accompanying him, arrived at Luz—that is, Bethel—in the land of Canaan.

7 There he erected an altar and designated the place El-bethel, for it was there that God had disclosed Himself to him as he fled from his brother. 8 Deborah, Rebekah's nurse, died and was interred beneath the oak below Bethel; hence the place was called Allon-bacuth.

God Renews the Covenant and Renames Jacob

9 God appeared to Jacob again when he came from Paddan-aram, and He blessed him.

10 God said to him, “Your name is Jacob; Jacob shall no longer be your appellation, but Israel shall be your name. 11 I am God Almighty: be fruitful and multiply. A nation, indeed a confederacy of nations, shall spring from you, and kings shall issue from your loins. 12 The land that I bestowed upon Abraham and Isaac I shall bestow upon you, and to your posterity after you I shall give the land.”

13 Then God withdrew from him in the place where He had spoken with him.

14 Jacob erected a pillar of stone in the place where He had spoken with him; he poured a libation upon it and anointed it with oil. 15 Jacob called the name of the place where God had spoken with him Bethel.

Rachel's Death and the Birth of Benjamin

16 They departed from Bethel; and while they were yet some distance from Ephrath, Rachel went into labor and endured a grievous travail. 17 As her travail intensified, the midwife said to her, “Fear not; you have another son as well.”

18 As her life ebbed away—for she was dying—she named him Ben-oni; but his father called him Benjamin. 19 Rachel died and was buried on the way to Ephrath—that is, Bethlehem. 20 Jacob erected a pillar above her grave; that pillar marks Rachel's tomb to this day.

Israel's Household, Reuben's Transgression, and Isaac's Death

21 Israel journeyed onward and pitched his tent beyond the Tower of Eder. 22 While Israel sojourned in that region, Reuben went in and lay with Bilhah, his father's concubine, and Israel learned of it. Now the sons of Jacob numbered twelve.

23 The sons of Leah were Reuben, Jacob's firstborn, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, and Zebulun. 24 The sons of Rachel were Joseph and Benjamin. 25 The sons of Bilhah, Rachel's maidservant, were Dan and Naphtali. 26 The sons of Zilpah, Leah's maidservant, were Gad and Asher. These are the sons of Jacob who were born to him in Paddan-aram.

27 Jacob came to his father Isaac at Mamre—that is, Kiriath-arba, or Hebron—where Abraham and Isaac had sojourned.

28 The days of Isaac were one hundred and eighty years. 29 Isaac breathed his last and died, being gathered to his people, old and full of days; and his sons Esau and Jacob interred him.

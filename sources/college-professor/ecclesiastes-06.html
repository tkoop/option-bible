The Calamity of Prosperity Devoid of Enjoyment

1 I have discerned beneath the sun an evil that weighs heavily upon humankind, and its prevalence is grievous. 2 God bestows upon a man riches, possessions, and honor, so that he lacks nothing his soul might covet; yet God does not grant him sovereignty to consume what has been given, and a stranger devours it. This is vanity and a grievous malady.

The Stillborn Preferable to the Unsatisfied Patriarch

3 If a man begets a hundred children and lives through many years, however numerous the days of his life may be, yet his soul is not sated by prosperity, and he receives no burial—I declare that the stillborn is better than he. 4 For that one comes in futility and departs into darkness, while his name is shrouded in obscurity. 5 He has neither seen the sun nor known anything; nevertheless, he enjoys tranquility beyond that man. 6 Even if he had lived twice a thousand years without beholding the good, would not all proceed to a single destination?

The Restlessness of Labor and Unfulfilled Desire

7 All human toil is for the mouth, yet the appetite remains insatiable. 8 What advantage, then, does the wise possess over the fool, or what does the impoverished man gain by knowing how to conduct himself among the living? 9 Better is the contentment of the eyes than the wandering of desire; this too is vanity and a chasing after wind.

Human Limitation Before the Mightier Power

10 Whatever has come to be has already been named, and it is known what humanity is; he cannot contend with the one who is mightier than he. 11 Since many words multiply futility, what benefit accrues to humanity? 12 For who knows what is good for a person during the brief span of his vain life, which passes like a shadow? And who will disclose to humanity what shall occur after him beneath the sun?

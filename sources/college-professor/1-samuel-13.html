Saul's Accession and the Summons to Arms

1 Saul was one year old when he became king, and he reigned two years over Israel. 2 Saul chose three thousand men of Israel for himself; two thousand were with Saul in Michmash and in the hill country of Bethel, and one thousand were with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin, while the rest of the people he sent, each to his own tent.

3 Jonathan struck the Philistine garrison that was in Geba, and the Philistines heard of it. Then Saul blew the trumpet throughout the land, saying, "Let the Hebrews hear." 4 All Israel heard the report, "Saul has struck the Philistine garrison, and Israel has become a stench to the Philistines." Then the people were summoned after Saul to Gilgal.

The Philistine Host and Israel's Distress

5 The Philistines gathered to fight against Israel: thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen, and people as numerous as the sand on the seashore. They came up and encamped in Michmash, east of Beth Aven. 6 When the men of Israel saw that they were in dire straits, for the people were hard pressed, they hid themselves in caves, in thickets, among rocks, in strongholds, and in cisterns. 7 Some of the Hebrews crossed the Jordan into the land of Gad and Gilead, while Saul was still at Gilgal and all the people followed him trembling. 8 He waited seven days, to the time that Samuel had appointed, but Samuel did not come to Gilgal, and the people were scattering from him.

Saul's Unlawful Sacrifice and Samuel's Rebuke

9 Saul said, "Bring the burnt offering and the peace offerings to me." And he offered the burnt offering. 10 As soon as he had finished offering the burnt offering, behold, Samuel came; and Saul went out to meet him and to greet him.

11 Samuel said, "What have you done?"

Saul said, "Because I saw that the people were scattering from me, and that you did not come within the appointed days, and that the Philistines were assembling at Michmash, I said, 'Now the Philistines will come down against me at Gilgal, and I have not sought the favor of the the LORD; therefore I forced myself and offered the burnt offering.'"

13 Samuel said to Saul, "You have acted foolishly; you have not kept the commandment of the the LORD your God, which He commanded you. For now the the LORD would have established your kingdom over Israel forever. 14 But now your kingdom shall not endure; the the LORD has sought for Himself a man after His own heart, and the the LORD has commanded him to be prince over His people, because you have not kept what the the LORD commanded you."

15 Samuel then arose and went up from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin. Saul mustered the people who remained with him, about six hundred men.

The Remnant at Geba and the Philistine Raids

16 Saul and his son Jonathan, together with the people who remained with them, were dwelling in Geba of Benjamin, while the Philistines encamped at Michmash. 17 Then marauders issued from the Philistine camp in three companies: one company turned toward Ophrah, in the land of Shual; 18 another turned toward Beth-horon; and the third turned toward the border overlooking the Valley of Zeboim toward the wilderness. 19 No blacksmith was to be found throughout the land of Israel, for the Philistines said, "Otherwise the Hebrews will make swords or spears." 20 So all Israel went down to the Philistines, each to sharpen his plowshare, his mattock, his axe, and his sickle. 21 The charge was two-thirds of a shekel for the plowshares and mattocks, and one-third of a shekel for sharpening the axes and for setting the goads. 22 Thus on the day of battle neither sword nor spear was found in the hand of any of the people with Saul and Jonathan; only Saul and his son Jonathan possessed them. 23 And a garrison of the Philistines advanced to the pass of Michmash.

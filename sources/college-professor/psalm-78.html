A Maskil of Asaph: Instruction and Intergenerational Memory

1 Attend, O my people, to my instruction, and incline your ears to the words of my mouth. 2 I will open my mouth in a parable; I will propound riddles whose origins lie in antiquity— 3 things we have heard and known, and which our fathers have recounted to us. 4 We shall not conceal them from their children, but shall recount to the latest generation the praises of the LORD, His might, and the wonders He has wrought.

5 For He established a testimony in Jacob and instituted a law in Israel, which He commanded our fathers to make known to their children, 6 so that the generation to come might know them—the children yet unborn—that they might arise and declare them to their own children, 7 and that they might set their confidence in God, neither forgetting His works nor forsaking His commandments. 8 Thus they would not be like their fathers, a stubborn and rebellious generation, a generation whose heart was not steadfast and whose spirit was not faithful to God.

Ephraim’s Retreat and the Wilderness Provocations

9 The Ephraimites, though armed with bows, retreated on the day of battle. 10 They did not keep the covenant of God, and they refused to walk in His law. 11 They forgot His deeds and the wonders He had shown them.

12 In the sight of their fathers He performed wonders in the land of Egypt, in the fields of Zoan. 13 He split the sea and led them through, and He made the waters stand like a wall. 14 He guided them with a cloud by day, and all the night with a fiery light. 15 He split rocks in the wilderness and gave them drink as from the great deep. 16 He brought forth streams from the rock and made waters run down like rivers.

17 Yet they sinned still more against Him, rebelling against the Most High in the arid waste. 18 They tested God in their hearts by demanding food for their craving. 19 They spoke against God, saying, “Can God prepare a table in the wilderness? 20 Behold, He struck the rock, and waters gushed out, and streams overflowed. Can He give bread also? Can He provide meat for His people?”

21 Therefore the LORD heard and was enraged; a fire was kindled against Jacob, and anger ascended against Israel, 22 because they did not believe in God and did not trust in His salvation.

23 Yet He commanded the skies above and opened the doors of heaven, 24 and He rained down manna upon them to eat and gave them the grain of heaven. 25 Mortals ate the bread of angels; He sent them provision in abundance. 26 He made the east wind blow in the heavens, and by His power He drove the south wind. 27 He rained flesh upon them like dust, winged fowl like the sand of the seas. 28 He let it fall in the midst of their camp, round about their dwellings. 29 So they ate and were well filled, for He granted them their craving.

30 But before their craving was sated, while the food was still in their mouths, 31 the anger of God rose against them and slew their stoutest and struck down the chosen of Israel. 32 In spite of all this they sinned still and did not believe His wondrous works. 33 Therefore He consumed their days in futility and their years in terror. 34 When He slew them, they sought Him; they repented and sought God earnestly. 35 They remembered that God was their Rock, and that the Most High God was their Redeemer.

36 But they flattered Him with their mouth and lied to Him with their tongue. 37 Their heart was not steadfast toward Him, nor were they faithful to His covenant. 38 Yet He, being merciful, forgave their iniquity and did not destroy them; many times He turned His anger away and did not arouse all His wrath. 39 For He remembered that they were flesh, a passing breath that does not return.

Divine Chastisement and the Exodus Deliverance

40 How often they rebelled against Him in the wilderness and grieved Him in the desert! 41 They turned back and tested God, and they provoked the Holy One of Israel. 42 They did not remember His hand, nor the day when He redeemed them from the adversary, 43 when He displayed His signs in Egypt and His wonders in the fields of Zoan, 44 and turned their rivers into blood, so that their streams became undrinkable.

45 He sent among them swarms of flies that devoured them, and frogs that ruined them. 46 He gave their produce to the caterpillar and their labor to the locust. 47 He destroyed their vines with hail and their sycamore trees with frost. 48 He delivered their cattle to the hail and their flocks to bolts of lightning. 49 He let loose upon them the fierceness of His anger—wrath, indignation, and distress—a band of destroying angels.

50 He cleared a path for His anger; He did not spare their souls from death, but gave their lives over to the plague. 51 He struck down all the firstborn of Egypt, the firstfruits of their vigor in the tents of Ham. 52 But He led forth His people like sheep and guided them in the wilderness like a flock. 53 He led them safely, so that they feared not, while the sea overwhelmed their enemies. 54 He brought them to the border of His sanctuary, to this mountain that His right hand had acquired. 55 He drove out nations before them and apportioned them an inheritance by line, settling the tribes of Israel in their tents.

Renewed Apostasy, the Rejection of Shiloh, and the Choice of David

56 Yet they tested and rebelled against God the Most High, and did not keep His decrees. 57 They turned back and acted treacherously like their fathers; they proved as unreliable as a warped bow. 58 They provoked Him to anger with their high places and moved Him to jealousy with their graven images. 59 When God heard this, He was furious and utterly rejected Israel, 60 and He abandoned the tabernacle of Shiloh, the tent He had pitched among mortals, 61 and delivered His strength into captivity and His splendor into the hand of the foe.

62 He gave His people over to the sword and was enraged at His inheritance. 63 Fire consumed His young men, and His maidens were not given in marriage. 64 Their priests fell by the sword, and their widows could not weep. 65 Then the Lord awoke as from sleep, like a warrior roused by wine. 66 He beat back His enemies; He put them to everlasting reproach.

67 He rejected the tent of Joseph and did not choose the tribe of Ephraim, 68 but chose the tribe of Judah, Mount Zion, which He loved. 69 He built His sanctuary like the high heavens, like the earth which He established forever. 70 He chose David His servant and took him from the sheepfolds; 71 from following the nursing ewes He brought him to shepherd Jacob His people and Israel His inheritance. 72 So he shepherded them with integrity of heart and guided them with skillful hands.

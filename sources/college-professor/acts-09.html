The Christophany on the Damascus Road

1 Meanwhile, Saul, still exhaling murderous threats against the Lord’s disciples, went to the high priest 2 and solicited from him letters to the synagogues in Damascus, so that, should he discover any adherents of the Way—whether men or women—he might convey them, bound, to Jerusalem. 3 As he neared Damascus on his journey, a light from heaven suddenly flashed around him; 4 and, having fallen to the ground, he heard a voice saying to him, “Saul, Saul, why do you persecute Me?” 5 “Who are You, Lord?” Saul queried. “I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting. 6 Nevertheless, arise and enter the city, and you shall be apprised of what you must do.” 7 The men traveling with him stood speechless, hearing the voice yet seeing no one. 8 Saul rose from the ground, but though his eyes were open, he saw nothing; so they led him by the hand into Damascus. 9 For three days he was blind, and neither ate nor drank.

Ananias and the Vocation of the Chosen Vessel

10 In Damascus there was a disciple named Ananias, and the Lord said to him in a vision, “Ananias!” “Here I am, Lord,” he answered. 11 The Lord said to him, “Rise and go to the house of Judas on Straight Street, and inquire for a man of Tarsus named Saul; for behold, he is praying. 12 In a vision he has seen a man named Ananias entering and laying hands on him so that he might regain his sight.” 13 Ananias answered, “Lord, I have heard from many concerning this man, how much evil he has wrought upon Your saints in Jerusalem. 14 And here he possesses authority from the chief priests to bind all who call upon Your name.” 15 But the Lord said to him, “Go, for this man is a chosen vessel of Mine to bear My name before the Gentiles, their kings, and the sons of Israel. 16 For I will show him how great things he must suffer for My name’s sake.” 17 So Ananias departed and entered the house; and, laying his hands on him, he said, “Brother Saul, the Lord Jesus, who appeared to you on the road by which you came, has sent me that you may regain your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit.” 18 And immediately something like scales fell from his eyes, and he regained his sight; and, rising, he was baptized. 19 Then, having taken food, he was strengthened. For some days he was with the disciples in Damascus.

From Persecutor to Proclaimer in Damascus and Jerusalem

20 He immediately proclaimed Jesus in the synagogues, declaring that He is the Son of God. 21 All who heard were amazed and said, “Is not this the man who made havoc in Jerusalem of those who call upon this name, and has he not come here to bring them bound before the chief priests?” 22 But Saul increased in strength and confounded the Jews dwelling in Damascus, demonstrating that this Jesus is the Christ. 23 After many days, the Jews conspired to kill him; 24 but their plot became known to Saul. They watched the gates day and night to kill him; 25 but his disciples took him by night and let him down through the wall in a basket. 26 When he had come to Jerusalem, he attempted to join the disciples, but they all feared him, not believing that he was a disciple. 27 But Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles, and recounted how he had seen the Lord on the road and that He had spoken to him, and how he had spoken boldly at Damascus in the name of Jesus. 28 So he was with them, coming in and going out at Jerusalem, speaking boldly in the name of the Lord. 29 He spoke and disputed against the Hellenists, but they were seeking to kill him. 30 When the brothers learned of it, they brought him down to Caesarea and sent him away to Tarsus. 31 So the church throughout all Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and was edified; and, walking in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit, it was multiplied.

Apostolic Miracles in Lydda and Joppa

32 Now it came to pass that Peter, passing through all parts, descended also to the saints dwelling at Lydda. 33 There he found a certain man named Aeneas, who had been bedridden for eight years, being paralyzed. 34 Peter said to him, “Aeneas, Jesus Christ heals you; arise and roll up your mat.” And immediately he arose. 35 All who dwelt at Lydda and Sharon saw him, and they turned to the Lord. 36 Now in Joppa there was a certain disciple named Tabitha, which, being interpreted, is Dorcas; she was full of good works and acts of charity. 37 In those days she became sick and died; and, having washed her, they laid her in an upper room. 38 Since Lydda was near Joppa, the disciples, hearing that Peter was there, sent two men to him, entreating him, “Do not delay to come to us.” 39 Then Peter arose and went with them; and when he had come, they brought him into the upper room. All the widows stood by him, weeping and showing the tunics and garments which Dorcas made while she was with them. 40 Peter put them all outside, and, kneeling down, he prayed; and, turning to the body, he said, “Tabitha, arise.” And she opened her eyes, and seeing Peter she sat up. 41 He gave her his hand and raised her up; and, calling the saints and widows, he presented her alive. 42 It became known throughout all Joppa, and many believed in the Lord. 43 He stayed many days in Joppa with Simon, a tanner.

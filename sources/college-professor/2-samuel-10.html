David’s Compassion toward Hanun and the Ammonite Insult

1 After these events, the king of the Ammonites died, and his son Hanun succeeded to the throne. 2 David resolved, “I shall extend faithful kindness to Hanun son of Nahash, for his father extended such kindness to me,” and therefore dispatched his servants to console him over his father’s death. Thus David’s servants entered the land of the Ammonites. 3 Yet the princes of the Ammonites said to Hanun, their lord, “Do you suppose that David honors your father by sending consolers to you? Has David not sent his servants to you in order to reconnoiter the city, to spy upon it, and to subvert it?” 4 Consequently, Hanun seized David’s servants, shaved off half of each man’s beard, cut their garments at the hips—indeed, to the buttocks—and dismissed them. 5 When report reached David, he sent envoys to meet them, for the men were profoundly humiliated; and the king instructed, “Remain in Jericho until your beards have regrown, and afterward return.”

6 When the Ammonites perceived that they had rendered themselves abhorrent to David, they dispatched agents and hired twenty thousand Aramean infantry from Beth-rehob and Zobah, together with the king of Maacah and his thousand men, and twelve thousand men from Tob.

Joab’s Campaign against the Ammonite-Aramean Coalition

7 Upon hearing this, David sent Joab and the entire host, including the mighty men. 8 The Ammonites sallied forth and arrayed themselves for battle at the city gate, whereas the Arameans of Zobah and Rehob, along with the men of Tob and Maacah, remained apart in the open field. 9 Perceiving that the battle confronted him on both front and rear, Joab selected from all the elite troops of Israel and arrayed them against the Arameans. 10 He entrusted the remainder of the force to his brother Abishai, who arrayed them against the Ammonites. 11 He declared, “If the Arameans prove too strong for me, then you must come to my aid; but if the Ammonites prove too strong for you, then I shall hasten to deliver you. 12 Be strong, and let us comport ourselves courageously for our people and for the cities of our God, and may the LORD effect what is good in His sight.” 13 Joab and the troops accompanying him advanced to engage the Arameans, and the Arameans fled before him. 14 When the Ammonites observed the Arameans’ flight, they too fled before Abishai and withdrew into the city. Joab then returned from the Ammonites and went to Jerusalem.

David’s Final Campaign and the Subjugation of Aram

15 Perceiving their defeat before Israel, the Arameans regrouped. 16 Hadadezer dispatched agents and brought forth the Arameans from beyond the River; they advanced to Helam, with Shobach, commander of Hadadezer’s army, leading them. 17 When David received report, he mustered all Israel, crossed the Jordan, and arrived at Helam. The Arameans arrayed themselves against David and engaged him in battle. 18 The Arameans fled before Israel; David slew seven hundred charioteers and forty thousand horsemen among the Arameans, and he struck down Shobach, their army’s commander, who died there. 19 When all the kings who were vassals of Hadadezer saw that they had been defeated before Israel, they made peace with Israel and became its subjects. Thus the Arameans dared no longer to succor the Ammonites.

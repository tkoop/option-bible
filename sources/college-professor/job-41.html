The Futility of Subduing Leviathan

“1 Can you draw Leviathan forth with a hook, or with a cord weigh down his tongue? 2 Can you thread a rush through his nostril, or bore his jaw with a thorn? 3 Will he lavish you with supplications, or address you in blandishments? 4 Will he strike a covenant with you, that you might claim him as a perpetual bondservant?

“5 Can you sport with him as with a bird, or bind him for your maidens? 6 Will traders haggle over him, or partition him among merchants? 7 Can you fill his hide with harpoons, or his head with fishing spears? 8 Lay your hand upon him; you will remember the battle and never renew the attempt. 9 Behold, his expectation is deceived; at the mere sight of him one is overwhelmed.

“10 No one is so ferocious as to rouse him; who then could stand before Me? 11 Who has given to Me that I should repay him? Everything under heaven is Mine.

The Impenetrable Armor of Leviathan

“12 I will not keep silent concerning his limbs, nor the report of his might and the grace of his frame. 13 Who has uncovered the face of his raiment? Who can approach him within the fold of his bridle? 14 Who can open the doors of his face? Around his teeth is terror. 15 His back is rows of shields, shut up as with a close seal. 16 One approaches another so closely that no breath passes between them. 17 They are joined to one another; they clasp and will not be separated.

“18 His snorts flash forth light, and his eyes are like the eyelids of dawn. 19 Firebrands stream from his mouth; sparks of fire escape. 20 Smoke billows from his nostrils, as from a seething pot over burning reeds. 21 His breath kindles coals, and a flame pours from his mouth. 22 Strength lodges in his neck, and dismay leaps before him. 23 The folds of his flesh cleave together; they are firm upon him and immovable. 24 His heart is as hard as stone, even as hard as a lower millstone.

The Futility of Weapons Against Leviathan

“25 When he raises himself up, the mighty are terrified; they recoil before his thrashings. 26 The sword that reaches him avails nothing, nor the spear, dart, or javelin. 27 He reckons iron as straw, and bronze as rotten wood. 28 The bow will not make him flee; slingstones are to him as chaff. 29 A club is counted as stubble, and he laughs at the rattling of the spear.

“30 Beneath him are sharp potsherds; he spreads a threshing sledge upon the mire. 31 He makes the deep boil like a pot; he makes the sea like a pot of ointment. 32 He leaves a luminous wake behind him; one would think the deep had grown hoary. 33 On earth there is nothing like him, a creature made without fear. 34 He looks down upon all that is lofty; he is king over all the sons of pride.”

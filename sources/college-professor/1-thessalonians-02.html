Paul’s Defense of His Apostolic Integrity

1 For you yourselves know, brothers, that our advent among you was not in vain. 2 As you are aware, having already endured affliction and suffered ignominious treatment at Philippi, we nevertheless found, in our God, the boldness to proclaim to you the gospel of God amid great contention.

3 For our exhortation does not issue from delusion, nor from impurity, nor from guile. 4 But, just as we have been approved by God to be entrusted with the gospel, so we speak—not as men-pleasers, but as those who seek to please God, who scrutinizes our hearts. 5 For, as you know, we never resorted to flattering discourse, nor did we employ a pretext for avarice—God is our witness— 6 nor did we seek glory from human beings, whether from you or from others, though, as apostles of Christ, we might have imposed a burden.

Gentle, Parental Care in the Gospel

7 On the contrary, we proved gentle in your midst, like a nursing mother cherishing her own children. 8 Thus, yearning for you, we were delighted to share with you not only the gospel of God but our very lives as well, because you had become exceedingly dear to us.

9 For you remember, brothers, our labor and toil: working night and day, so as not to impose a burden upon any of you, we proclaimed to you the gospel of God. 10 You are witnesses, as is God, of how devoutly, justly, and blamelessly we conducted ourselves among you who believe.

11 As you know, we exhorted, consoled, and solemnly charged each one of you, as a father does his own children, 12 so that you might walk in a manner worthy of God, who calls you into His own kingdom and glory.

Gratitude for Their Reception of God’s Word and Their Suffering

13 And for this reason we too give thanks to God unceasingly: because, when you received the word of God that you heard from us, you accepted it not as the word of men, but, as it truly is, the word of God, which is also operative in you who believe.

14 For you, brothers, became imitators of the churches of God in Judea that are in Christ Jesus; for you suffered from your own countrymen the same things they suffered from the Jews, 15 who killed both the Lord Jesus and the prophets, and drove us out as well. They are displeasing to God and hostile to all people, 16 hindering us from speaking to the Gentiles so that they might be saved, with the result that they always fill up the measure of their sins; but wrath has come upon them at last.

Paul’s Longing and Eschatological Hope

17 But we, brothers, though torn away from you for a short time—in person, not in heart—were all the more eager, with intense longing, to see your face. 18 For we desired to come to you—I, Paul, indeed, more than once—but Satan obstructed us.

19 For what is our hope, or joy, or crown of boasting, if not you yourselves before our Lord Jesus at His coming? 20 For you are indeed our glory and joy.

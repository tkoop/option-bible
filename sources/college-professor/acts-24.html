Tertullus Brings Charges Against Paul Before Felix

1 After an interval of five days, the high priest Ananias descended to Caesarea with certain elders and an advocate named Tertullus, who laid charges against Paul before the governor. 2 When Paul was summoned, Tertullus commenced his accusation, declaring, “Through you we have attained much peace, and through your foresight salutary reforms are being effected for this nation. 3 In every respect and in every place, most excellent Felix, we receive these benefits with all gratitude. 4 Yet, lest I detain you overlong, I entreat you, in your clemency, to hear us with brevity. 5 For we have discovered this man to be a pestilence, inciting seditions among all the Jews throughout the world, and a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes. 6 He even attempted to profane the temple, and we apprehended him. 8 By examining him yourself, you may ascertain all these matters concerning which we accuse him.”

9 The Jews likewise assented, affirming that these allegations were accurate.

Paul Offers His Defense Before the Governor

10 When the governor motioned to him to speak, Paul answered, “Since I know that for many years you have been a judge over this nation, I make my defense concerning myself with good cheer. 11 You can verify that no more than twelve days have elapsed since I ascended to Jerusalem to worship. 12 Neither in the temple, nor in the synagogues, nor throughout the city did they find me disputing with anyone or fomenting a riot among the crowd. 13 Nor can they substantiate before you the charges they now bring against me. 14 But this much I confess to you: according to the Way, which they designate a sect, I worship the God of our fathers, believing all things written in the Law and in the Prophets. 15 I cherish a hope toward God—which these men themselves also anticipate—that there shall be a resurrection of both the righteous and the unrighteous. 16 In this I also strive always to maintain a conscience void of offense toward God and toward men. 17 After many years, I came to bring alms to my nation and to present offerings. 18 In the course of these things they found me purified in the temple, neither with a crowd nor with tumult. But certain Jews from Asia— 19 they ought to have been present before you and to accuse me, if they had anything against me— 20 or else let these men themselves declare what crime they discovered when I stood before the council, 21 except for this one utterance that I cried out while standing among them: ‘Concerning the resurrection of the dead I am being judged before you this day.’”

Felix Adjourns the Proceedings and Retains Paul in Custody

22 Felix, being more accurately acquainted with the Way, deferred them, saying, “When Lysias the tribune comes down, I will determine your case.” 23 He then gave orders to the centurion that Paul be kept in custody, yet afforded some liberty, and that none of his own people be hindered from ministering to him.

Felix and Drusilla Hear Paul Privately

24 After some days Felix arrived with his wife Drusilla, who was Jewish, and he sent for Paul and heard him concerning the faith in Christ Jesus. 25 As Paul discoursed about righteousness, self-control, and the judgment to come, Felix became terrified and answered, “Depart for the present; when I obtain a convenient season, I will summon you.” 26 At the same time he hoped that money would be given him by Paul, so he sent for him more frequently and conversed with him. 27 When two years had been completed, Felix received Porcius Festus as his successor; and, wishing to confer a favor upon the Jews, Felix left Paul bound.

Paul's Voyage Toward Italy

1 When it was decided that we should sail to Italy, Paul and some other prisoners were delivered to a centurion named Julius, of the Augustan cohort. 2 We boarded an Adramyttian vessel that was about to sail to the ports along the coast of Asia, and we set sail with Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica. 3 The next day we made landfall at Sidon; and Julius, treating Paul with humane consideration, permitted him to go to his friends and receive care. 4 Putting out from there, we sailed under the lee of Cyprus because the winds were contrary. 5 Having traversed the open sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came down to Myra in Lycia. 6 There the centurion found an Alexandrian ship bound for Italy and put us aboard. 7 For many days we made slow headway and with difficulty came off Cnidus; since the wind did not allow us to hold our course, we sailed under the lee of Crete off Salmone. 8 Coasting along it with difficulty, we reached a place called Fair Havens, near the city of Lasea.

Counsel Ignored and the Storm

9 Since much time had elapsed and the voyage was now dangerous, because the Fast had already passed, Paul advised them, 10 saying, "Men, I perceive that this voyage will be attended by hardship and great loss, not only of the cargo and the ship, but also of our lives." 11 But the centurion paid more heed to the pilot and the owner of the ship than to Paul's words. 12 Because the harbor was unsuitable for wintering, the majority resolved to sail on from there, in the hope that, if possible, they might reach Phoenix—a harbor of Crete facing southwest and northwest—and winter there.

13 When a south wind blew gently, thinking they had attained their purpose, they weighed anchor and skirted Crete. 14 But before long a tempestuous wind, called the Northeaster, swept down upon us. 15 When the ship was caught and could not face the wind, we yielded and were driven along. 16 Running under the lee of a small island called Cauda, we managed with difficulty to secure the ship's boat. 17 After hoisting it up, they used supports to undergird the ship; and, fearing that they would be driven onto the Syrtis, they lowered the sea anchor and so were driven. 18 We were being violently battered by the storm, so the next day they began to jettison the cargo. 19 On the third day they threw the ship's tackle overboard with their own hands. 20 When neither sun nor stars appeared for many days, and while a severe storm continued to assail us, all hope that we should be saved was at last abandoned.

Paul's Assurance and the Approaching Wreck

21 When there had been a long period of fasting, Paul stood up among them and said, "Men, you should have listened to me and not have set sail from Crete, and so avoided this hardship and loss. 22 And now I urge you to take courage, for there will be no loss of life among you, but only of the ship. 23 For this very night an angel of God, to whom I belong and whom I serve, stood beside me

24 "and said, 'Do not be afraid, Paul; you must stand before Caesar; and behold, God has graciously given you all who sail with you.'

25 "Therefore take courage, men, for I trust God that it will be exactly as it has been told to me. 26 Nevertheless, we must run aground on some island."

27 When the fourteenth night had come, as we were being driven across the Adriatic, about midnight the sailors suspected that they were nearing land. 28 They took soundings and found twenty fathoms; and after a short distance they took soundings again and found fifteen fathoms. 29 Fearing that we might run aground on the rocks, they let down four anchors from the stern and prayed for day to come. 30 But as the sailors were seeking to escape from the ship and had lowered the boat into the sea under the pretext of laying out anchors from the bow, 31 Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, "Unless these men remain in the ship, you cannot be saved." 32 Then the soldiers cut away the ropes of the boat and let it drift away.

The Shipwreck and Deliverance

33 As day was about to break, Paul urged them all to take some food, saying, "Today is the fourteenth day that you have continued in suspense and gone without food, having taken nothing. 34 Therefore I urge you to take some food, for this is for your preservation; for not a hair from the head of any of you will perish." 35 Having said this, he took bread and gave thanks to God in the presence of all, and breaking it he began to eat. 36 Then they all were encouraged and took food themselves. 37 We were in all two hundred seventy-six souls on the ship. 38 When they had eaten their fill, they lightened the ship by throwing the wheat into the sea.

39 When day came, they did not recognize the land, but they noticed a bay with a beach, and they resolved to run the ship aground there if they could. 40 So they cast off the anchors and left them in the sea, at the same time loosening the ropes that secured the rudders; then, hoisting the foresail to the wind, they made for the beach. 41 But striking a shoal where two seas met, they ran the ship aground; and the bow stuck fast and remained immovable, while the stern was being broken up by the force of the waves. 42 The soldiers' plan was to kill the prisoners, lest any should swim away and escape. 43 But the centurion, wishing to save Paul, kept them from their purpose, and commanded that those who could swim should jump overboard first and get to land, 44 and the rest on planks and on pieces of the ship. And so it happened that all were brought safely to land.

Persecution Scatters the Jerusalem Church

1 Now Saul was consenting to Stephen’s execution, and on that very day a severe persecution erupted against the church in Jerusalem; consequently, everyone except the apostles was dispersed throughout the regions of Judea and Samaria. 2 Devout men, however, carried Stephen to his burial and made an elaborate lamentation over him. 3 Saul, for his part, was ravaging the church: he would enter houses, drag away both men and women, and consign them to prison.

The Gospel Advances into Samaria

4 Those who had been scattered, therefore, went from place to place proclaiming the word. 5 Philip descended to the city of Samaria and began proclaiming the Christ to them. 6 The crowds, with one accord, gave heed to what Philip was saying, for they heard him and witnessed the signs he performed. 7 For unclean spirits, crying out with a loud voice, came out of many who were possessed; and many who were paralyzed or lame were healed. 8 So there was great joy in that city.

Simon the Sorcerer and the Apostolic Ministry in Samaria

9 Now there was a man named Simon who had previously been practicing magic in the city and astonishing the nation of Samaria, claiming himself to be someone great. 10 Everyone, from the least to the greatest, paid attention to him, saying, “This man is the Power of God that is called the Great.” 11 They paid heed to him because for a long time he had astounded them with his magical arts.

12 But when they believed Philip as he proclaimed the good news about the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ, both men and women were baptized. 13 Simon himself also believed, and after being baptized he was continually attending upon Philip; and as he beheld the great signs and mighty works being performed, he was utterly astounded.

14 When the apostles in Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God, they sent Peter and John to them. 15 These, having come down, prayed for them that they might receive the Holy Spirit, 16 for as yet He had not fallen upon any of them; they had merely been baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus. 17 Then they laid their hands upon them, and they received the Holy Spirit.

18 Now when Simon saw that the Spirit was given through the laying on of the apostles’ hands, he offered them money, 19 saying, “Give me this authority as well, so that on whomever I lay my hands he may receive the Holy Spirit.”

20 But Peter said to him, “May your silver perish along with you, because you thought you could acquire the gift of God with money! 21 You have neither part nor lot in this matter, for your heart is not right before God. 22 Repent, therefore, of this wickedness of yours, and pray to the Lord, if perhaps the intent of your heart may be forgiven you. 23 For I see that you are steeped in bitter gall and bound fast by iniquity.”

24 Simon answered and said, “You pray to the Lord for me, so that none of the things you have spoken may come upon me.” 25 So, after testifying and speaking the word of the Lord, they returned to Jerusalem, proclaiming the gospel to many villages of the Samaritans.

Philip and the Ethiopian Eunuch

26 Now an angel of the Lord spoke to Philip, saying, “Arise and go southward along the road that descends from Jerusalem to Gaza; it is a desert road.” 27 So he arose and went, and there was an Ethiopian eunuch, a court official of Candace, queen of the Ethiopians, who was in charge of all her treasure, and he had come to Jerusalem to worship. 28 He was returning, and sitting in his chariot he was reading the prophet Isaiah.

29 Then the Spirit said to Philip, “Approach this chariot and join yourself to it.”

30 Philip ran up and heard him reading the prophet Isaiah, and said, “Do you understand what you are reading?”

31 He replied, “How could I, unless someone guides me?” And he invited Philip to come up and sit with him.

32 The passage of Scripture he was reading was this: “Like a sheep he was led to the slaughter, and like a lamb before its shearer is silent, so he does not open his mouth. 33 In his humiliation his justice was taken away; who will declare his generation? For his life is taken away from the earth.”

34 The eunuch answered Philip and said, “I implore you, of whom does the prophet speak this—of himself or of someone else?”

35 Then Philip opened his mouth, and beginning from this Scripture he proclaimed Jesus to him.

36 As they were traveling along the road, they came to some water, and the eunuch said, “Look—water! What prevents me from being baptized?”

38 And he commanded the chariot to stop, and both Philip and the eunuch went down into the water, and he baptized him.

39 When they came up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord snatched Philip away, and the eunuch saw him no longer, for he went on his way rejoicing.

40 But Philip found himself at Azotus, and as he passed through he proclaimed the gospel to all the cities until he came to Caesarea.

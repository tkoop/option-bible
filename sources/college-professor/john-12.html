The Anointing at Bethany

1 Six days before the Passover, Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus was—the one whom He had raised from the dead. 2 There they prepared a banquet in His honor; Martha attended to the service, while Lazarus reclined among the guests beside Him. 3 Mary, accordingly, took a pound of exceedingly costly ointment of genuine nard and anointed Jesus’ feet, drying them with her hair; and the entire house was suffused with the fragrance of the unguent.

4 But Judas Iscariot, one of His disciples—the one poised to deliver Him up—said, 5 “Why was not this ointment sold for three hundred denarii and the proceeds bestowed upon the indigent?” 6 He uttered this not out of solicitude for the impoverished, but because he was a thief; possessing the common purse, he habitually abstracted what was deposited therein.

7 “Leave her in peace; let her keep this for the day of My burial. 8 The poor, indeed, you have with you always, but Me you do not have always.”

9 A great multitude of the Jews, having learned that He was there, came—not solely on account of Jesus, but also to behold Lazarus, whom He had raised from the dead. 10 The chief priests therefore conspired to put Lazarus to death as well, 11 since on his account many of the Jews were departing and placing their faith in Jesus.

The Triumphal Entry into Jerusalem

12 On the morrow, the great crowd that had come to the festival, having heard that Jesus was approaching Jerusalem, 13 took palm branches and went forth to meet Him, crying, “Hosanna! Blessed is He who comes in the Name of the Lord—even the King of Israel!”

14 Jesus, having found a young donkey, sat upon it, in fulfillment of what is written: 15 “Fear not, daughter of Zion! Behold, your King comes, seated upon a donkey’s colt.” 16 His disciples did not comprehend these matters initially; yet, once Jesus was glorified, they recollected that these things had been written concerning Him and had been enacted toward Him.

17 The crowd that had been with Him when He summoned Lazarus from the tomb and raised him from the dead continued to bear witness. 18 It was for this reason that the crowd went out to meet Him: they had heard that He had performed this sign. 19 The Pharisees therefore said to one another, “You perceive that you are accomplishing nothing. Behold, the whole world has gone after Him.”

The Coming of the Greeks and the Hour of Glory

20 Among those ascending to worship at the festival were certain Greeks. 21 These approached Philip, who was from Bethsaida in Galilee, and entreated him, saying, “Sir, we desire to see Jesus.” 22 Philip went and reported this to Andrew; then Andrew and Philip went and informed Jesus.

23 “The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified.”

24 “Truly, truly, I say to you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the ground and dies, it abides solitary; but if it dies, it bears abundant fruit.” 25 “He who cherishes his life forfeits it, and he who renounces his life in this world shall preserve it unto life eternal.” 26 “If anyone serves Me, let him follow Me; and where I am, there also shall My servant be. If anyone serves Me, the Father will honor him.”

27 “Now My soul is deeply troubled; and what shall I say? ‘Father, deliver Me from this hour’? Yet it was for this very purpose that I came to this hour.”

28 “Father, glorify Your Name.”

Then a voice came from heaven: “I have glorified it, and I will glorify it again.”

29 The crowd standing by and hearing it declared that thunder had sounded; others said, “An angel has spoken to Him.”

30 Jesus answered, “This voice came not for My sake, but for yours.”

31 “Now is the judgment of this world; now shall the ruler of this world be cast out.” 32 “And I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people unto Myself.” 33 He said this to signify the manner of death He was about to undergo.

34 The crowd answered Him, “We have heard from the Law that the Christ abides forever; how then do You say that the Son of Man must be lifted up? Who is this Son of Man?”

35 “The light is with you for a little while longer. Walk while you have the light, lest darkness overtake you; for he who walks in darkness knows not whither he goes.” 36 “While you have the light, believe in the light, that you may become sons of light.” Having spoken these things, Jesus departed and concealed Himself from them.

Unbelief, Prophecy, and the Final Summons

37 Although He had performed so many signs in their presence, they did not believe in Him, 38 that the word of Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled: “Lord, who has believed our report? And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?”

39 Therefore they could not believe, for Isaiah said again, 40 “He has blinded their eyes and hardened their heart, lest they should see with their eyes and understand with their heart and turn, and I would heal them.” 41 Isaiah said this because he beheld His glory and spoke concerning Him.

42 Nevertheless, many among the rulers believed in Him; yet, for fear of the Pharisees, they did not confess it, lest they be expelled from the synagogue, 43 for they cherished the glory that comes from men more than the glory that comes from God.

44 “He who believes in Me believes not in Me, but in Him who sent Me.” 45 “And he who beholds Me beholds Him who sent Me.” 46 “I have come into the world as light, so that everyone who believes in Me may not abide in darkness.”

47 “If anyone hears My words and does not observe them, I do not judge him; for I came not to judge the world, but to save it.” 48 “He who rejects Me and does not receive My words has one who judges him: the word which I have spoken will judge him on the last day.”

49 “For I have not spoken of Myself; rather, the Father who sent Me has given Me commandment concerning what I should say and what I should speak.” 50 “And I know that His commandment is eternal life. Therefore, whatever I speak, I speak exactly as the Father has instructed Me.”

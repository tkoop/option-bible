The Parable of the Persistent Widow and the Unjust Judge

1 Then Jesus told them a parable to demonstrate that they must pray at all times and never succumb to discouragement. 2 He said, “In a certain city there was a judge who neither feared God nor respected any human being.

3 And in that city there was a widow who kept appealing to him, saying, ‘Grant me justice against my adversary.’

4 For a while he refused; but afterward he said to himself, ‘Although I neither fear God nor respect any human being, 5 yet because this widow persists in troubling me, I will grant her justice, lest she ultimately wear me down by her relentless visits.’

6 And the Lord said, “Heed what the unjust judge declares. 7 And will not God secure justice for His elect, who cry out to Him day and night? Will He delay long over them? 8 I tell you, He will vindicate them swiftly. Yet when the Son of Man comes, will He find faith upon the earth?”

The Pharisee and the Tax Collector: A Study in Righteousness and Humility

9 He also propounded this parable to certain individuals who were convinced of their own righteousness and regarded everyone else with disdain. 10 “Two men ascended to the temple to pray, one a Pharisee and the other a tax collector.

11 The Pharisee, standing apart, prayed to himself, ‘O God, I thank You that I am not like the rest of humanity—plunderers, evildoers, adulterers—or even like this tax collector. 12 I fast twice each week; I tithe all that I acquire.’

13 But the tax collector, standing at a distance, would not even raise his eyes to heaven, but struck his breast, saying, ‘O God, be merciful to me, a sinner!’

14 I tell you, this man descended to his house justified rather than the other. For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted.”

Jesus Welcomes Children, Confronts the Rich Ruler, and Defines Discipleship

15 Now they were even bringing infants to Him so that He might touch them, and when the disciples observed this, they rebuked them. 16 But Jesus summoned them to Himself, saying, “Permit the little children to come to Me, and do not impede them, for the kingdom of God belongs to such as these.”

17 “Truly, I say to you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God as a little child will never enter it.”

18 And a certain ruler interrogated Him, saying, “Good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?”

19 Jesus replied, “Why do you call Me good? No one is good except God alone. 20 You know the commandments: Do not commit adultery, Do not murder, Do not steal, Do not bear false witness, Honor your father and mother.”

21 He said, “All of these I have observed from my youth.”

22 Upon hearing this, Jesus said to him, “One thing you still lack: sell all that you possess and distribute it to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; then come and follow Me.”

23 But upon hearing these words, he became exceedingly sorrowful, for he was exceedingly wealthy. 24 When Jesus saw that he had become sorrowful, He said, “How arduous it is for those who possess riches to enter the kingdom of God!”

25 “For it is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle than for a wealthy person to enter the kingdom of God.”

26 Those who heard this exclaimed, “Then who can be saved?”

27 But He said, “What is impossible for human beings is possible for God.”

28 Peter declared, “Behold, we have forsaken our own possessions and followed You.”

29 He said to them, “Truly, I say to you, no one who has relinquished house or wife or siblings or parents or children for the sake of the kingdom of God, 30 will fail to receive manifold recompense in this present age, and in the age to come eternal life.”

The Son of Man Foretells His Passion and Heals a Blind Beggar at Jericho

31 Taking the twelve aside, He said to them, “Behold, we are ascending to Jerusalem, and all that is written by the prophets concerning the Son of Man will be fulfilled. 32 For He will be handed over to the Gentiles and will be mocked, insulted, and spat upon. 33 After scourging Him, they will put Him to death, and on the third day He will rise again.”

34 Yet they comprehended none of these things; this utterance was concealed from them, and they did not understand what was being said.

35 As He approached Jericho, a blind man sat by the wayside begging. 36 Hearing a crowd passing by, he asked what the commotion meant. 37 They informed him, “Jesus of Nazareth is passing by.”

38 He cried out, “Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!”

39 Those marching ahead rebuked him, commanding him to be silent; but he cried out all the more, “Son of David, have mercy on me!”

40 Jesus halted and ordered him to be brought to Him; and when he had drawn near, He inquired of him, “What do you desire that I should do for you?”

41 He replied, “Lord, that I might recover my sight.”

42 Jesus said to him, “Receive your sight; your faith has saved you.” 43 And immediately he recovered his sight and followed Him, glorifying God; and all the people, having witnessed it, gave praise to God.

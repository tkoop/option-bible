Reverence in the Sanctuary and the Fear of God

1 Exercise circumspection in your gait when repairing to the house of God; draw near to hearken rather than to tender the sacrifice of fools, for they know not that they are perpetrating evil. 2 Do not precipitate your utterance, and let not your heart hasten to bring forth a word before God, for God is in heaven while you are upon the earth; therefore let your words be few. 3 For dreams arise from abundant preoccupations, and the voice of a fool from a multitude of words. 4 When you vow a vow to God, do not defer its fulfillment, for He takes no delight in fools; render what you have vowed. 5 It is better not to vow at all than to vow and then fail to discharge it. 6 Do not let your mouth cause your flesh to sin, and do not say before the messenger that it was an error; why should God be incensed by your voice and ruin the work of your hands? 7 For amid many dreams and much futility there are many words; rather, fear God.

Oppression, Wealth, and the Limits of Prosperity

8 If you behold oppression of the poor and the perversion of justice and righteousness in a province, do not be astonished at the matter, for a high official is watched by a higher one, and still higher ones are over them. 9 Moreover, the profit of the land is for all; even the king is served by the field. 10 One who loves silver is never satiated with silver, and whoever loves wealth is never satisfied with its income; this too is futility. 11 As goods multiply, their consumers multiply; and what advantage has their owner but the sight of them with his eyes? 12 The sleep of a laborer is sweet, whether he eats little or much; but the satiety of the rich denies him sleep. 13 I have observed a grievous evil in this world: wealth hoarded by its owner to his own detriment. 14 That wealth is lost in a disastrous enterprise, and he begets a son, yet nothing remains in his hand. 15 Just as he emerged naked from his mother's womb, so he returns; he takes nothing from his toil that he can carry away in his hand. 16 This too is a grievous evil: just as he came, so he departs; and what does he gain from laboring to no avail? 17 Throughout all his days he eats in darkness, amid much vexation, sickness, and resentment.

The Goodness of Enjoying God's Gift

18 Behold, I have seen what is good and fitting: that a person eat and drink and find enjoyment in all his toil in this world during the number of days of his life that God has given him, for this is his portion. 19 Moreover, for every person to whom God grants wealth and possessions and empowers him to eat of them, to receive his portion, and to rejoice in his toil—this is the gift of God. 20 For such a person will not dwell much on the days of his life, since God keeps him occupied with the gladness of his heart.

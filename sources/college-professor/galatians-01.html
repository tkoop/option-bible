Paul’s Apostolic Salutation and Doxology

1 Paul, an apostle—not derived from human beings, nor mediated through any mortal intermediary, but through Jesus Christ and God the Father, who raised Him from the dead— 2 together with all the brothers who accompany me, to the churches of Galatia: 3 Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, 4 who surrendered Himself on behalf of our transgressions, so that He might rescue us from this present malign epoch, in accordance with the volition of our God and Father, 5 to whom be glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.

Astonishment at a Distorted Gospel

6 I marvel that you are transferring yourselves so precipitously from Him who summoned you in the grace of Christ unto a different gospel— 7 which is not, in truth, another, except that certain individuals are agitating you and desiring to pervert the gospel of Christ. 8 Yet even if we, or an angel descending from heaven, should proclaim to you a gospel divergent from that which we proclaimed to you, let him be anathema. 9 As we have previously declared, so I now reiterate: if anyone proclaims to you a gospel divergent from that which you received, let him be anathema.

Paul’s Apostolic Authority and Divine Commission

10 For am I now seeking the approbation of human beings, or of God? Or am I striving to ingratiate myself with mortals? If I were still endeavoring to please human beings, I would not be a servant of Christ.

11 For I would have you know, brothers, that the gospel proclaimed by me is not of human derivation; 12 for neither did I receive it from a human being, nor was I instructed in it, but through an apocalypse of Jesus Christ.

13 For you have heard of my former manner of life in Judaism: that beyond measure I persecuted the church of God and ravaged it. 14 And I was advancing in Judaism beyond many contemporaries of my own generation, being exceedingly zealous for the patrimonial traditions.

15 But when God—who had segregated me from my mother’s womb and called me through His grace—was pleased 16 to reveal His Son in me, so that I might proclaim Him among the Gentiles, I did not immediately confer with human counsel, 17 nor did I ascend to Jerusalem to those who were apostles before me; rather, I departed into Arabia, and then returned again to Damascus.

Paul’s Early Ministry and Reception among the Churches

18 Then, after three years, I ascended to Jerusalem to become acquainted with Cephas, and I remained with him fifteen days. 19 But I saw none of the other apostles, except James, the brother of the Lord. 20 Now in what I write to you, before God, I do not fabricate falsehoods.

21 Thereafter I came into the regions of Syria and Cilicia. 22 Yet I remained personally unknown to the churches of Judea that are in Christ; 23 they had only been hearing, “The one who formerly persecuted us is now proclaiming the faith he once sought to demolish,” 24 and they glorified God on account of me.

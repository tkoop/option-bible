Generosity, Diversification, and the Folly of Excessive Caution

1 Commit your resources to uncertain ventures, for after many days you will recover them. 2 Allot a share to seven, and even to eight, since you cannot know what calamity may befall the land.

3 If the clouds are saturated, they pour forth rain upon the earth; and if a tree falls to the south or to the north, in the very place where it falls, there it shall remain. 4 The one who waits for the wind to settle will never sow, and the one who fixes his gaze upon the clouds will never reap.

The Inscrutability of Divine Providence

5 Just as you do not know the way of the wind, nor comprehend how bones coalesce within the womb of her who carries a child, so likewise you cannot know the work of God, who accomplishes all things. 6 Sow your seed in the morning, and do not withhold your hand in the evening, for you do not know which undertaking will prosper—this one or that—or whether both will prove equally fruitful.

The Fleeting Joys and Final Reckoning of Human Life

7 Light is sweet, and it is a delight for the eyes to behold the sun. 8 If a man lives for many years, let him rejoice throughout them all, yet let him remember the days of darkness—for they will be numerous; all that comes to pass is futility.

9 Rejoice, young man, in your youth, and let your heart cheer you in the days of your young manhood; walk in the inclinations of your heart and in the desires of your eyes; yet know that for all these things God will bring you into judgment. 10 So remove vexation from your heart, and put away evil from your flesh, for childhood and youth are futility.

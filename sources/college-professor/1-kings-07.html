Solomon’s Palace Complex and Its Architectural Refinements

1 Solomon devoted thirteen years to the construction of his palace, and he brought the whole of that domestic edifice to completion. 2 He built the Palace of the Forest of Lebanon—one hundred cubits in length, fifty in breadth, and thirty in height—resting upon four rows of cedar pillars, with trimmed cedar beams laid upon the pillars. 3 It was roofed in cedar above the side chambers that rested upon the pillars: forty-five beams, fifteen to each row. 4 The windows were elevated in three tiers, with aperture confronting aperture in threefold array. 5 Every doorway and doorpost was square in its framing, and opening faced opening in threefold order. 6 He fashioned a colonnade fifty cubits long and thirty wide, preceded by a porch, with pillars and an overhanging roof set before them. 7 He erected the throne hall—the hall of judgment—where he would adjudicate, and he sheathed it in cedar from pavement to soffit. 8 His own residence, situated in another court behind the hall, was of comparable workmanship; likewise, Solomon made a corresponding dwelling for Pharaoh’s daughter, whom he had married, matching that hall. 9 All these were of precious hewn stone, cut to measure with the saw, both within and without, from foundation to eaves, and outward to the great court. 10 The foundation was of costly great stones—some ten cubits, some eight. 11 Above them were costly stones cut to measure, together with cedar. 12 The great court was encircled by three courses of hewn stone and one course of trimmed cedar, both for the inner court of the house of the the LORD and for the porch of the house.

Hiram’s Bronze Work for the Temple

13 King Solomon dispatched envoys to Tyre and brought Hiram from there. 14 He was the son of a widow of the tribe of Naphtali, and his father was a Tyrian bronze-worker, endowed with wisdom, discernment, and knowledge for every kind of bronze craftsmanship; he came to King Solomon and executed all the work entrusted to him. 15 He cast two bronze pillars, each eighteen cubits high and twelve cubits in circumference. 16 He also made two capitals of cast bronze to set upon the heads of the pillars; each capital was five cubits high. 17 He fashioned networks of interwoven chains for the capitals upon the pillars, seven for each capital. 18 He made pomegranates in two rows encircling each network to adorn the capitals atop the pillars, and he did the same for the other capital. 19 The capitals atop the pillars in the portico were lily-shaped, four cubits high. 20 On the capitals at the top of both pillars, above the bulge beside the network, there were two hundred pomegranates in rows all around. 21 He raised the pillars at the temple portico, naming the right-hand pillar Jachin and the left-hand pillar Boaz. 22 The capitals on top were lily-shaped, and thus the work of the pillars was completed.

The Molten Sea and the Ten Bronze Stands

23 He made the Sea of cast metal, circular in shape, ten cubits from brim to brim and five cubits high; a line of thirty cubits measured around it. 24 Below its rim, gourds encircled it—ten to a cubit—and the gourds were cast in two rows as one piece with the Sea. 25 It stood upon twelve oxen: three facing north, three west, three south, and three east; the Sea rested upon them above, and their hindquarters were turned inward. 26 Its thickness was a handbreadth, and its rim was like the rim of a cup, like a lily blossom; it held two thousand baths. 27 He made ten bronze stands, each four cubits long, four wide, and three high. 28 The design of the stands was thus: they had side panels set between the crossbars. 29 On the panels between the crossbars were lions, bulls, and cherubim, and likewise upon the crossbars; above and below the lions and bulls were wreaths of hammered work. 30 Each stand had four bronze wheels with bronze axles, and it was supported by four foot projections; beneath the basin were cast projections on each side, embellished with wreaths. 31 Within the stand was an opening with a circular frame one cubit deep; the opening was round, and its design was a cubit and a half, with carvings around it, and the panels were square rather than round. 32 The four wheels were beneath the panels, and the axles of the wheels were attached to the stand; each wheel was a cubit and a half high. 33 The wheels were made like chariot wheels, their axles, rims, spokes, and hubs all cast. 34 Each stand had four handles, one at each corner, projecting from the stand. 35 At the top of the stand was a circular band half a cubit deep, and the supports and panels were attached to the top of the stand. 36 He engraved cherubim, lions, and palm trees on the surfaces of the supports and on the panels, in every available space, with wreaths all around. 37 Thus he made the ten stands: one casting, one measure, one form for all. 38 He made ten bronze basins, each holding forty baths and measuring four cubits across; one basin rested on each of the ten stands. 39 He set five stands on the south side of the temple and five on the north; he placed the Sea on the south side, at the southeast corner of the temple.

Hiram’s Completed Bronze Work and the Temple’s Golden Furnishings

40 Hiram also made the pots, shovels, and sprinkling bowls; so Hiram completed all the work he had undertaken for King Solomon in the temple of the the LORD: 41 the two pillars; the two bowl-shaped capitals on top of the pillars; the two sets of network decorating the two bowl-shaped capitals on top of the pillars; 42 the four hundred pomegranates for the two networks—two rows of pomegranates for each network, adorning the bowl-shaped capitals atop the pillars; 43 the ten stands with their ten basins; 44 the Sea and the twelve oxen beneath it; 45 the pots, shovels, and sprinkling bowls. All these objects that Hiram made for Solomon for the temple of the the LORD were of burnished bronze. 46 The king had them cast in clay molds in the plain of the Jordan between Succoth and Zarethan. 47 Solomon left all these things unweighed, because they were so numerous; the weight of the bronze was not determined.

48 Solomon also made all the furnishings in the house of the the LORD: the golden altar; the golden table upon which was the bread of the Presence; 49 the lampstands of pure gold—five on the right and five on the left, before the inner sanctuary—and the gold flowers, lamps, and tongs; 50 the basins, wick trimmers, sprinkling bowls, dishes, and ladles of pure gold; and the hinges for the doors of the innermost room, the Most Holy Place, and for the doors of the main hall of the temple, were of gold. 51 When all the work Solomon had done for the temple of the the LORD was finished, he brought in the things his father David had dedicated—the silver, the gold, and the furnishings—and he placed them in the treasuries of the the LORD’s temple.

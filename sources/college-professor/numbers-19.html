The Statute of the Red Heifer

1 Then the LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,

2 “This constitutes the statutory ordinance of the law that the LORD has enjoined: instruct the Israelites to bring to you a red heifer—immaculate, devoid of every blemish, and never subjected to a yoke. 3 You shall consign it to Eleazar the priest, who shall remove it beyond the encampment and slaughter it in his presence. 4 Eleazar the priest shall take of its blood with his finger and sprinkle it seven times toward the front of the tent of meeting. 5 The heifer must be incinerated under his supervision—its hide, flesh, blood, and even its dung being consumed by fire.

6 “The priest shall take cedar timber, hyssop, and scarlet wool, and cast them into the midst of the heifer’s conflagration. 7 Thereafter the priest shall launder his garments and bathe his person in water; only then may he reenter the camp, though he remains ritually unclean until evening. 8 Likewise, whoever incinerates the heifer must launder his garments, bathe his person in water, and remain unclean until evening.

9 “Then a man in a state of ritual purity shall gather the ashes of the heifer and deposit them in a ceremonially clean place beyond the camp; they are to be preserved for the congregation of the Israelites for the water of purification, since it is a sin offering. 10 The man who gathers the ashes of the heifer shall wash his garments and remain unclean until evening. This ordinance shall be an everlasting statute for the Israelites and for the sojourner residing among them.

Defilement by the Dead and Its Remedy

11 “Anyone who touches the corpse of any human being shall be ritually unclean for seven days. 12 He must purify himself with this water on the third day and again on the seventh day; then he shall be clean. If, however, he fails to purify himself on both the third and the seventh days, he shall not be clean. 13 Anyone who touches a human corpse and does not purify himself defiles the tabernacle of the LORD; that person shall be cut off from Israel, because the water of purification has not been sprinkled upon him. He remains unclean, and his impurity abides upon him.

14 “This is the law when a man dies in a tent: everyone who enters the tent, as well as everyone already present within it, shall be unclean for seven days. 15 Furthermore, every open vessel lacking a securely fastened lid is unclean. 16 Likewise, anyone in the open field who touches one slain by the sword, or a corpse, or a human bone, or a grave, shall be unclean for seven days.

The Purifying Waters and Their Application

17 “For the unclean person, they shall take some of the ashes of the purification offering, place them in a vessel, and pour living water over them. 18 Then a clean person shall take hyssop, dip it in the water, and sprinkle it upon the tent, upon all the vessels, upon the persons who were present, and upon anyone who touched a bone, a slain person, a corpse, or a grave. 19 The clean person shall sprinkle the unclean on the third day and on the seventh day. On the seventh day he shall complete the purification; then the one being cleansed shall wash his garments and bathe in water, and he shall be clean by evening.

20 “But if an unclean person does not purify himself, that individual shall be cut off from the assembly, because he has defiled the sanctuary of the LORD. The water of purification has not been sprinkled upon him; he remains unclean. 21 This shall be an everlasting statute for them: the one who sprinkles the water of purification must wash his garments, and whoever touches the water of purification shall be unclean until evening. 22 Furthermore, whatever the unclean person touches becomes unclean, and anyone who touches him shall be unclean until evening.”

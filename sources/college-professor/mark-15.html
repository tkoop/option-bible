The Dawn Council and the Delivery to Pilate

1 At the break of dawn, the chief priests, together with the elders, the scribes, and the full Sanhedrin, having convened their council, bound Jesus, led Him away, and surrendered Him to Pilate. 2 Pilate interrogated Him: “Are You the King of the Jews?”

He answered him, “You have said it.” 3 The chief priests then brought many accusations against Him.

4 Pilate questioned Him anew: “Do You offer no reply? Consider how many charges they lodge against You.” 5 Consequently, Pilate was astonished; yet Jesus afforded no further response.

6 Now during the festival it was his custom to release to them one prisoner whom they requested. 7 The man called Barabbas was incarcerated with the insurgents who had committed homicide during the sedition. 8 The crowd came up and began to ask for the customary favor. 9 Pilate answered them: “Do you wish me to release to you the King of the Jews?” 10 For Pilate perceived that the chief priests had delivered Him up out of envy.

11 But the chief priests agitated the crowd so that Barabbas would be released to them instead. 12 Pilate, replying again, said to them: “What then do you want me to do with the One you call the King of the Jews?”

13 They cried out again: “Crucify Him!”

14 Pilate said to them: “Why? What evil has He done?”

They shouted all the more: “Crucify Him!” 15 Desiring to satisfy the crowd, Pilate released Barabbas to them; and after scourging Jesus, Pilate consigned Him to crucifixion.

The Mockery and Crucifixion of the Royal Pretender

16 The soldiers led Him into the courtyard—that is, the Praetorium—and summoned the whole cohort. 17 They clothed Him in purple, and having plaited a crown of thorns, they placed it upon Him. 18 They began to salute Him: “Hail, King of the Jews!” 19 They struck His head with a reed, spat upon Him, and, kneeling, did Him obeisance. 20 When they had finished mocking Him, they stripped Him of the purple and put His own garments on Him; then they led Him out to be crucified.

21 They compelled a certain Simon of Cyrene, the father of Alexander and Rufus, who was coming from the countryside, to bear His cross. 22 They brought Him to the place called Golgotha, which is interpreted, “Place of a Skull.” 23 They offered Him wine mingled with myrrh, but He did not accept it. 24 They crucified Him and divided His garments, casting lots for them to determine who should take which. 25 It was the third hour when they crucified Him.

26 The inscription of the charge against Him read: “THE KING OF THE JEWS.” 27 With Him they crucified two robbers, one on His right and one on His left.

29 Those passing by reviled Him, shaking their heads and saying, “Aha! You who would destroy the temple and raise it again in three days, 30 come down from the cross and save Yourself.”

31 Likewise the chief priests, with the scribes, mocked Him among themselves, saying, “He saved others; He cannot save Himself. 32 The Christ, the King of Israel—let Him descend now from the cross, that we may see and believe.” Even those crucified with Him heaped insults upon Him.

The Darkness, the Cry of Dereliction, and the Death

33 When the sixth hour arrived, darkness covered the whole land until the ninth hour. 34 At the ninth hour Jesus cried out with a loud voice, “Eloi, Eloi, lema sabachthani?”—which is interpreted, “My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?”

35 Some of those standing by, upon hearing it, said, “Look, He is calling Elijah.”

36 Someone ran, filled a sponge with sour wine, put it on a reed, and gave Him a drink, saying, “Let us see whether Elijah comes to take Him down.” 37 Jesus, uttering a loud cry, expired.

38 The veil of the temple was rent in two from top to bottom. 39 When the centurion standing opposite Him saw that He thus expired, he said, “Truly this man was the Son of God.”

40 There were also women watching from afar, among whom were Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James the younger and of Joses, and Salome. 41 They had followed Him and ministered to Him while He was in Galilee, together with many other women who had ascended with Him to Jerusalem.

The Entombment by Joseph of Arimathea

42 When evening had already come—because it was the Preparation, that is, the day before the Sabbath—Joseph of Arimathea, a respected member of the council, who was himself awaiting the kingdom of God, took courage, went to Pilate, and requested the body of Jesus. 43 Pilate marveled that He was already dead; summoning the centurion, he asked him whether He had died long before. 44 Having learned from the centurion, he granted the corpse to Joseph. 45 Having purchased a linen shroud, he took Him down, wrapped Him in the linen, and laid Him in a tomb hewn out of rock; then he rolled a stone against the door of the tomb. 46 Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses observed where He was laid. 47

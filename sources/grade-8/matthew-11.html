John the Baptist Sends a Question

1 When Jesus had finished giving instructions to His twelve disciples, He left there to teach and preach in their towns.

2 Meanwhile, John heard in prison what Christ was doing, so he sent his disciples 3 to ask Him, “Are You the One who is to come, or should we expect someone else?”

4 Jesus replied, “Go back and tell John what you hear and see: 5 The blind can see, the lame can walk, people with leprosy are healed, the deaf can hear, the dead are raised, and the good news is preached to the poor. 6 Blessed is the person who does not lose faith because of Me.”

Jesus Speaks About John the Baptist

7 As John’s disciples were leaving, Jesus began to speak to the crowds about John: “What did you go out into the wilderness to see? A reed blowing in the wind? 8 What else did you go out to see? A man dressed in fine clothes? Look, people who wear fine clothes live in kings’ palaces. 9 Then what did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet. 10 This is the one the Scriptures talk about: ‘Look, I will send My messenger ahead of You, and he will prepare Your way before You.’ 11 I tell you the truth, no one born of a woman is greater than John the Baptist. Yet the least important person in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he is. 12 From the days of John the Baptist until now, the kingdom of heaven has been under attack, and violent people try to take it by force. 13 For all the Prophets and the Law spoke about things to come until John. 14 If you are willing to accept it, he is the Elijah who was supposed to come. 15 Anyone who has ears should listen.”

16 “What can I compare this generation to? They are like children sitting in the marketplaces and calling out to others: 17 ‘We played the flute for you, but you did not dance; we sang a funeral song, but you did not mourn.’ 18 For John came without eating or drinking, and people say, ‘He has a demon.’ 19 The Son of Man came eating and drinking, and people say, ‘Look at this man! He is a glutton and a drunk, and a friend of tax collectors and sinners.’ But wisdom is shown to be right by what it does.”

Jesus Warns Towns That Did Not Repent

20 Then Jesus began to rebuke the towns where He had done most of His miracles, because they did not turn from their sins. 21 “Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! If the miracles that were done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago, showing deep sorrow. 22 But I tell you, on the day of judgment it will be easier for Tyre and Sidon than for you. 23 And you, Capernaum, do you think you will be lifted up to heaven? No, you will be brought down to Hades. If the miracles that were done in you had been done in Sodom, it would still be here today. 24 But I tell you, on the day of judgment it will be easier for Sodom than for you.”

Jesus Praises the Father and Offers Rest

25 At that time Jesus said, “I praise You, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because You have hidden these things from the wise and clever and revealed them to little children. 26 Yes, Father, because this is what You wanted to do. 27 My Father has given Me everything. No one knows the Son except the Father, and no one knows the Father except the Son and anyone to whom the Son chooses to show Him.”

28 “Come to Me, all you who are tired and carry heavy loads, and I will give you rest. 29 Take My yoke on you and learn from Me, because I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. 30 For My yoke is easy to carry, and My load is light.”

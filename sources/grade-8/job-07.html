Job's Hard Life

1 Life on earth is hard work for people. Their days are like those of a hired worker. 2 Like a slave, a person longs for shade. Like a hired worker, he waits for his pay. 3 So I have been given months that feel empty, and nights of trouble have been set aside for me. 4 When I go to bed, I wonder, "When will I get up?" The evening drags on, and I toss and turn until dawn. 5 My body is covered with worms and dirt. My skin cracks and looks sickening. 6 My days go by faster than a weaver's shuttle. They end with no hope. 7 Remember that my life is only a breath. My eyes will never see good again. 8 The person who sees me now will not see me again. Your eyes are on me, and then I will be gone. 9 A cloud fades and disappears. In the same way, the one who goes down to the grave does not come back. 10 He will not return to his house, and his home will not know him anymore.

Job Speaks to God

11 So I will not keep quiet. I will speak out in my troubled spirit. I will complain in my bitter soul. 12 Am I the sea, or a sea monster, that You put a guard over me? 13 When I say, "My bed will comfort me, and my couch will ease my complaint," 14 You scare me with dreams and terrify me with visions. 15 My soul would rather choke to death than keep living in this body. 16 I hate my life. I do not want to live forever. Leave me alone, because my days are only a breath.

Job Asks Why God Watches Him

17 What is a person, that You make so much of him, and that You set Your heart on him? 18 You visit him every morning and test him every moment. 19 How long will You not look away from me? When will You leave me alone, even for a moment? 20 If I have sinned, what can I do to You, O Watcher of people? Why have You made me Your target, so that I am a burden to myself? 21 Why do You not pardon my sin and take away my guilt? For soon I will lie down in the dust, and You will look for me, but I will not be there.

Sennacherib Invades Judah

1 After these things and these acts of faithfulness, Sennacherib king of Assyria came and invaded Judah. He besieged the fortified cities, intending to capture them for himself. 2 When Hezekiah saw that Sennacherib had come and that he intended to wage war against Jerusalem, 3 he consulted with his officials and military leaders about blocking the water from the springs outside the city, and they helped him. 4 A large group of people gathered and stopped up all the springs and the stream that flowed through the land. They said, "Why should the kings of Assyria come and find plenty of water?"

Hezekiah Strengthens and Encourages Jerusalem

5 Hezekiah took courage and rebuilt all the broken sections of the wall. He raised towers on it and built another wall outside. He repaired the Millo in the City of David and made large numbers of weapons and shields. 6 He appointed military officers over the people and gathered them to him in the square at the city gate. He spoke encouragingly to them, saying, 7 "Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid or discouraged because of the king of Assyria and the huge army with him, for there is a greater power with us than with him. 8 He has only human strength, but we have the LORD our God to help us and to fight our battles." The people gained confidence from the words of Hezekiah king of Judah.

Sennacherib Threatens Jerusalem

9 After this, Sennacherib king of Assyria, while besieging Lachish with all his forces, sent his servants to Jerusalem to Hezekiah king of Judah and to all Judah who were in Jerusalem, saying, 10 "Thus says Sennacherib king of Assyria: On what are you trusting, that you stay in Jerusalem under siege? 11 Isn't Hezekiah misleading you, handing you over to die by famine and thirst, while he says, 'The LORD our God will rescue us from the hand of the king of Assyria'? 12 Hasn't Hezekiah removed his high places and altars and told Judah and Jerusalem, 'You must worship before one altar and burn incense on it'? 13 Do you not know what I and my fathers have done to all the peoples of the lands? Were the gods of the nations of those lands ever able to rescue their lands from my hand? 14 Who among all the gods of these nations that my fathers destroyed was able to rescue his people from my hand? How then can your God rescue you from my hand? 15 Now do not let Hezekiah deceive you or mislead you like this. Do not believe him, for no god of any nation or kingdom has been able to rescue his people from my hand or from the hand of my fathers. How much less will your God rescue you from my hand!"

16 His servants spoke even more against the LORD, the God of Israel, and against His servant Hezekiah. 17 He also wrote letters insulting the LORD, the God of Israel, and speaking against Him, saying, "Like the gods of the nations of the lands who did not rescue their people from my hand, so the God of Hezekiah will not rescue His people from my hand." 18 They shouted in Hebrew to the people of Jerusalem on the wall to frighten them and terrify them, so they could take the city. 19 They spoke about the God of Jerusalem as they did about the gods of the peoples of the earth, things made by human hands.

God Delivers Jerusalem and Hezekiah's Later Years

20 King Hezekiah and the prophet Isaiah son of Amoz cried out in prayer to heaven about this. 21 The LORD sent an angel, who destroyed all the fighting men, commanders, and officers in the camp of the king of Assyria. So he returned to his own land in disgrace. And when he went into the temple of his god, some of his own sons killed him with the sword. 22 So the LORD saved Hezekiah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem from the hand of Sennacherib king of Assyria and from the hand of all others, and He gave them peace on every side. 23 Many brought offerings to Jerusalem for the LORD and valuable gifts for Hezekiah king of Judah, so that he was highly respected by all nations after that.

24 In those days Hezekiah became ill and was at the point of death. He prayed to the LORD, who answered him and gave him a miraculous sign. 25 But Hezekiah did not respond to the kindness shown to him; instead his heart became proud. So the LORD's anger came upon him and upon Judah and Jerusalem. 26 Then Hezekiah humbled himself for the pride of his heart, he and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, so the LORD's wrath did not come upon them in the days of Hezekiah.

27 Hezekiah had very great riches and honor. He made treasuries for silver, gold, precious stones, spices, shields, and all kinds of valuable articles. 28 He made storehouses for grain, new wine, and olive oil, and stalls for all kinds of livestock and pens for flocks. 29 He built cities and acquired great numbers of flocks and herds, for God had given him very great wealth. 30 It was Hezekiah who blocked the upper outlet of the Gihon spring and channeled the water down to the west side of the City of David. Hezekiah prospered in all his work. 31 So when envoys of the rulers of Babylon were sent to him to ask about the miraculous sign that had happened in the land, God left him to test him and to know everything in his heart. 32 The rest of the acts of Hezekiah and his kind acts are written in the vision of the prophet Isaiah son of Amoz, in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel. 33 Hezekiah died and was buried on the upper part of the tombs of David's descendants. All Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem honored him at his death. And his son Manasseh reigned in his place.

David Gets Ready for Battle

1 David counted his soldiers and put commanders over groups of a thousand and a hundred.

2 He sent out a third of the army with Joab, a third with Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab's brother, and a third with Ittai the Gittite. The king told the troops, "I will go out with you too."

3 But the people said, "You must not go. If we run away, they won't care about us. Even if half of us die, they won't care. But you are worth ten thousand of us. It's better for you to stay in the city and help us from there."

4 The king said, "I'll do what seems best to you." So the king stood by the gate while all the troops marched out by hundreds and by thousands.

5 The king ordered Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, "For my sake, be gentle with the young man Absalom." All the people heard the king give this order to his commanders about Absalom.

The Battle in Ephraim's Forest

6 The army went into the field to fight Israel. The battle happened in the forest of Ephraim.

7 David's men defeated the people of Israel. That day, twenty thousand men died in a great defeat.

8 The fighting spread across the whole land. That day, the forest killed more people than the sword did.

Absalom Dies

9 Absalom ran into David's servants. He was riding a mule. The mule went under the thick branches of a big oak tree, and Absalom's head got stuck in the tree. He hung in the air while the mule kept going.

10 A man saw this and told Joab, "I just saw Absalom hanging in an oak tree!"

11 Joab said, "What? You saw him? Why didn't you strike him to the ground? I would have given you ten pieces of silver and a belt."

12 The man answered, "Even if a thousand pieces of silver were put in my hand, I wouldn't hurt the king's son. We heard the king order you, Abishai, and Ittai, 'Protect the young man Absalom.' 13 If I had killed him—and the king finds out everything—you would have left me to answer for it."

14 Joab said, "I won't wait for you." He took three spears and drove them into Absalom's heart while Absalom was still alive in the oak tree.

15 Ten young men who carried Joab's weapons surrounded Absalom, struck him, and killed him.

16 Then Joab blew the trumpet, and the troops stopped chasing Israel because Joab held them back.

17 They took Absalom, threw him into a large pit in the forest, and piled a huge mound of stones over him. Then all the Israelites went home.

18 While he was alive, Absalom had set up a stone pillar for himself in the King's Valley. He said, "I have no son to keep my name alive." He named the pillar after himself, and people still call it Absalom's Monument today.

David Hears the News

19 Ahimaaz son of Zadok said, "Let me run and tell the king the good news that the LORD has saved him from his enemies."

20 Joab said, "You are not the right man to bring news today. You can bring news another day, but not today, because the king's son is dead."

21 Then Joab said to a Cushite, "Go tell the king what you have seen." The Cushite bowed to Joab and ran.

22 Ahimaaz son of Zadok kept asking, "No matter what happens, please let me run after the Cushite."

Joab said, "Why do you want to run, my son? You won't get a reward."

23 "No matter what happens, I want to run," he said.

So Joab said, "Run!" Then Ahimaaz ran across the plain and passed the Cushite.

24 David was sitting between the two gates. The watchman went up to the roof of the gate by the wall, looked out, and saw a man running alone.

25 The watchman called out and told the king. The king said, "If he is alone, he must be bringing good news." As the man came closer,

26 the watchman saw another man running. So he called out to the gatekeeper, "Look! Another man is running alone!"

"This one is also bringing good news," said the king.

27 The watchman said, "I can see that the first man runs like Ahimaaz son of Zadok."

"He is a good man," said the king. "He must be bringing good news."

28 Then Ahimaaz called to the king, "All is well!" He bowed down with his face to the ground before the king and said, "Blessed be the LORD your God, who has handed over the men who raised their hands against my lord the king."

29 The king asked, "Is the young man Absalom safe?"

Ahimaaz said, "When Joab sent your servant, I saw a big uproar, but I don't know what it was."

30 The king said, "Step aside." So he stood to the side.

31 Just then the Cushite arrived and said, "May my lord the king hear good news! The LORD has given you justice today by saving you from all who rose up against you!"

32 The king asked the Cushite, "Is the young man Absalom safe?"

The Cushite answered, "May the enemies of my lord the king and all who rise up against you to harm you be like that young man!"

33 The king was deeply upset. He went up to the room above the gate and wept. As he walked, he cried, "Oh, my son Absalom! My son, my son Absalom! I wish I had died instead of you, Absalom, my son, my son!"

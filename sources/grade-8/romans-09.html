Paul's Sorrow for Israel

1 I speak the truth in Christ. I am not lying. My conscience, with the Holy Spirit as my witness, agrees with me. 2 I have great sorrow and pain that never stops in my heart. 3 I could wish that I myself were cursed and cut off from Christ for the sake of my brothers, my own people by birth. 4 They are Israelites. God gave them adoption as sons, His glory, the covenants, the law, true worship, and the promises. 5 They have the patriarchs. And from them, by human descent, came the Christ, who is God over all, praised forever. Amen.

God's Word Has Not Failed

6 But it is not as if God's word has failed. Not everyone born into Israel truly belongs to Israel. 7 Not all of Abraham's descendants are his true children. Instead, God said, "Through Isaac your descendants will be counted." 8 This means the children born by human effort are not God's children. The children born through God's promise are the ones counted as His descendants. 9 This is what the promise said: "At the right time I will come back, and Sarah will have a son."

God Chooses by His Purpose

10 Not only that, but Rebecca also had children by one man, our father Isaac. 11 Before the twins were born or had done anything good or bad, God's plan to choose stood firm. 12 It was not because of their works, but because of God's call. She was told, "The older will serve the younger." 13 As Scripture says, "I chose Jacob, but I rejected Esau."

14 What should we say then? Is God unfair? Not at all! 15 He said to Moses, "I will have mercy on anyone I choose, and I will show compassion to anyone I choose." 16 So it does not depend on human desire or effort. It depends on God's mercy. 17 Scripture says to Pharaoh, "I raised you up for this purpose: to show My power in you and to make My name known everywhere on earth." 18 So God has mercy on whom He wants, and He hardens whom He wants.

19 You might say to me, "Then why does God still blame us? Who can resist His will?" 20 But who are you, a mere man, to argue with God? Will the thing made say to its maker, "Why did You make me like this?" 21 Does the potter not have the right to make from the same clay one pot for special use and another for ordinary use?

22 What if God, wanting to show His anger and make His power known, put up with the people who deserved His anger—people prepared for destruction? 23 What if He did this to show the riches of His glory to the people He shows mercy to—people He prepared in advance for glory? 24 That includes us, whom He called—not only from the Jews but also from the Gentiles.

25 As He says in Hosea: "I will call them 'My people' who are not My people, and I will call her 'My loved one' who is not My loved one." 26 "And in the very place where it was said to them, 'You are not My people,' they will be called 'children of the living God.'"

27 Isaiah cries out about Israel: "Even if the number of Israelites is like the sand by the sea, only a remnant will be saved." 28 For the Lord will carry out His judgment on the earth quickly and completely. 29 And as Isaiah said before, "If the Lord of Hosts had not left us a few descendants, we would have become like Sodom and been like Gomorrah."

Israel and the Gentiles

30 What should we say then? The Gentiles, who were not chasing righteousness, have gained righteousness—the righteousness that comes from faith. 31 But Israel, who chased a law of righteousness, did not reach it. 32 Why? Because they did not pursue it by faith. They tried to get it by works. They stumbled over the stumbling stone. 33 As it is written: "Look, I place in Zion a stone that makes people stumble and a rock that makes them fall. But the one who trusts in Him will never be put to shame."

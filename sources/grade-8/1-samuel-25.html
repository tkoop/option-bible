Samuel Dies and David Goes to the Wilderness

1 Samuel died. All the people of Israel gathered, mourned for him, and buried him at his home in Ramah. Then David got up and went down to the Desert of Paran. 2 A man in Maon had property in Carmel. He was very rich, with three thousand sheep and one thousand goats. He was shearing his sheep in Carmel. 3 The man’s name was Nabal, and his wife’s name was Abigail. She was wise and beautiful, but he was harsh and mean in the way he acted. He was a Calebite.

Nabal Refuses David’s Request

4 While David was in the wilderness, he heard that Nabal was shearing his sheep. 5 So David sent ten young men and said, “Go up to Nabal at Carmel and greet him in my name. 6 Say to him, ‘Peace to you, to your house, and to all you have.’ 7 I have heard that you have shearers. Your shepherds were with us. We did not insult them, and nothing of theirs was missing the whole time they were in Carmel. 8 Ask your servants, and they will tell you. So let these young men find favor with you, because we have come on a feast day. Please give whatever you have on hand to your servants and to your son David.” 9 David’s young men went and said all this to Nabal in David’s name. Then they waited.

10 Nabal answered David’s servants, “Who is David? Who is the son of Jesse? There are many servants today who are running away from their masters. 11 Why should I take my bread, my water, and the meat I have cooked for my shearers and give it to men I do not even know where they come from?” 12 David’s young men turned and went back. They told him everything Nabal had said.

13 David said to his men, “Each of you, strap on your sword.” So David strapped on his sword, and about four hundred men went with him. Two hundred men stayed with the supplies.

14 One of the young men told Abigail, Nabal’s wife, “David sent messengers from the wilderness to greet our master, but he insulted them. 15 These men were very good to us. They did not mistreat us, and we did not miss anything the whole time we were with them in the fields. 16 They were like a wall around us, both day and night, the whole time we were with them tending the sheep. 17 Now think about what you should do, because trouble is coming to our master and to his whole household. He is such a worthless man that no one can talk to him.”

Abigail Brings a Gift to David

18 Abigail hurried and took two hundred loaves of bread, two skins of wine, five sheep that were already prepared, five measures of roasted grain, one hundred clusters of raisins, and two hundred cakes of pressed figs. She loaded them on donkeys. 19 She said to her servants, “Go on ahead of me. I will follow you.” But she did not tell her husband Nabal. 20 As she rode her donkey down the mountain path, David and his men were coming down toward her, and she met them.

21 David had said, “I guarded everything this man owned in the wilderness for nothing. Not one thing of his was missing. But he has paid me back evil for good. 22 May God punish David’s enemies, and even more, if by morning I leave alive even one man of all who belong to him.”

23 When Abigail saw David, she hurried and got off her donkey. She fell facedown on the ground and bowed before David.

24 She fell at his feet and said, “My lord, let the blame be on me. Please let your servant speak to you and listen to my words. 25 My lord, please do not pay attention to this worthless man Nabal. He is just like his name—Nabal means ‘fool,’ and that is what he is. But I, your servant, did not see the young men you sent. 26 Now, my lord, as surely as the the LORD lives and you live, the the LORD has kept you from bloodshed and from avenging yourself with your own hand. May your enemies and everyone who wants to harm my lord be like Nabal. 27 Now let this gift your servant has brought to my lord be given to the young men who follow my lord.”

“28 Please forgive your servant’s wrong. For the the LORD will surely make my lord’s family strong and lasting, because my lord fights the the LORD’s battles. May no evil be found in you as long as you live. 29 Even if someone rises up to chase you and take your life, your life will be kept safe by the the LORD your God. But He will throw away the lives of your enemies, like a stone thrown from a sling. 30 When the the LORD has done for my lord everything He promised, and has made you leader over Israel, 31 you will not have this grief or this guilty conscience from shedding blood without cause or from taking your own revenge. When the the LORD has been good to my lord, remember your servant.”

32 David said to Abigail, “Praise the the LORD, the God of Israel, who sent you to meet me today. 33 Blessed be your good sense, and blessed be you, for keeping me today from bloodshed and from avenging myself. 34 As surely as the the LORD, the God of Israel, lives, who kept me from harming you, if you had not hurried to meet me, not one man of Nabal’s would have been left alive by morning.”

35 Then David accepted what she brought and said to her, “Go home in peace. I have listened to you, and I have granted your request.”

Nabal Dies and David Marries Abigail

36 Abigail went to Nabal. He was in his house, holding a feast fit for a king. He was in good spirits and very drunk. She did not tell him anything until morning. 37 In the morning, when the wine had worn off, she told him everything. His heart failed, and he became like a stone. 38 About ten days later, the the LORD struck Nabal, and he died.

39 When David heard that Nabal was dead, he said, “Praise the the LORD! He has defended my cause against Nabal’s insult and has kept His servant from doing wrong. The the LORD has brought Nabal’s evil back on his own head.” Then David sent messengers to ask Abigail to become his wife.

40 David’s servants went to Abigail at Carmel and said, “David has sent us to you to take you as his wife.” 41 She got up, bowed facedown to the ground, and said, “Here is your servant, ready to wash the feet of my lord’s servants.”

42 Abigail hurried and got up. She rode on a donkey, with five of her young women walking behind her. She went with David’s messengers and became his wife. 43 David had also married Ahinoam from Jezreel. So both of them were his wives. 44 But Saul had given his daughter Michal, David’s wife, to Palti son of Laish, who was from Gallim.

The Same Fate for Everyone

1 I thought hard about all of this and decided that good people, wise people, and what they do are in God's hands. But no one knows whether love or hate is waiting for them. 2 Everyone has the same future. Good people and bad people, clean people and unclean people, those who offer sacrifices and those who don't, all share one fate. Good people are treated like sinners. People who swear an oath are treated like people who are afraid to swear one.

3 This is the sad part of everything that happens in this life: everyone has the same end. People's hearts are full of evil while they live, and madness is in their hearts. After that, they join the dead.

4 But anyone who is still alive has hope. Even a live dog is better off than a dead lion. 5 Living people know they will die, but dead people know nothing. They get no more reward, and even their name is forgotten. 6 Their love, hate, and jealousy are gone. They will never again share in anything that happens in this life.

Enjoy Life While You Can

7 So go eat your food with joy, and drink your wine with a happy heart, because God has already accepted what you do. 8 Always wear white clothes, and always put oil on your head.

9 Enjoy life with the wife you love all the days of this meaningless life God has given you in this world. Those meaningless days are your share in life and in the hard work you do in this world. 10 Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with all your strength, because there is no work, planning, knowledge, or wisdom in the grave, where you are going.

Time and Chance

11 I have seen something else in this life: the fastest runner does not always win the race. The strongest soldier does not always win the battle. Wise people do not always have food. Smart people do not always get rich. People with knowledge do not always find favor. Time and chance happen to all of them. 12 No one knows when their time will come. People are trapped by hard times that fall on them suddenly, like fish caught in a net or birds caught in a trap.

A Poor Wise Man Saves a City

13 I also saw this example of wisdom in this life, and it amazed me. 14 There was a small city with only a few people in it. A powerful king attacked it, surrounded it, and built large siege ramps against it. 15 But a poor, wise man lived there. He saved the city with his wisdom. Yet no one remembered that poor man.

16 So I said, "Wisdom is better than strength." But the poor man's wisdom was looked down on, and his words were not listened to. 17 The quiet words of wise people are heard better than a foolish ruler's shouts. 18 Wisdom is better than weapons of war, but one sinner can destroy much good.

The Israelites Complain in the Desert

1 On the fifteenth day of the second month after the Israelites left Egypt, the whole community of Israelites traveled from Elim and came to the Desert of Sin, between Elim and Sinai. 2 There in the desert, the whole community of Israelites grumbled against Moses and Aaron.

3 “If only we had died by the the LORD’s hand in Egypt! There we sat by pots of meat and ate all the bread we wanted, but you have brought us into this desert to starve this whole group to death!” the Israelites said to them.

God Promises Bread from Heaven

4 Then the the LORD said to Moses, “I am going to rain down bread from heaven for you. Each day the people must go out and gather enough for that day. In this way I will test them to see if they will follow My instructions. 5 On the sixth day, they must prepare what they bring in, and it will be twice as much as they gather on the other days.”

6 So Moses and Aaron said to all the Israelites, “This evening you will know that it was the the LORD who brought you out of Egypt. 7 In the morning you will see the the LORD’s glory, because He has heard your grumbling against Him. Who are we, that you should grumble against us? 8 The the LORD will give you meat to eat this evening and bread to satisfy you in the morning, because the the LORD has heard your grumbling against Him. Who are we? Your grumbling is not against us but against the the LORD.”

9 Then Moses said to Aaron, “Tell the whole community of Israelites to come before the the LORD, for He has heard their grumbling.”

10 As Aaron spoke to the whole community of Israelites, they looked toward the desert, and there in a cloud the the LORD’s glory appeared.

11 Then the the LORD said to Moses, 12 “I have heard the grumbling of the Israelites. Tell them, ‘At twilight you will eat meat, and in the morning you will be filled with bread. Then you will know that I am the the LORD your God.’”

God Sends Quail and Manna

13 That evening quail came and covered the camp, and in the morning dew lay around the camp. 14 When the dew was gone, thin flakes like frost lay on the desert floor.

15 When the Israelites saw it, they asked one another, “What is it?” For they did not know what it was.

So Moses told them, “It is the bread the the LORD has given you to eat.”

16 Moses said, “This is what the the LORD has commanded: ‘Each of you is to gather as much as he needs. You may take an omer for each person in your tent.’”

17 So the Israelites did this. Some gathered much, and some gathered little. 18 When they measured it by the omer, the one who gathered much did not have too much, and the one who gathered little did not have too little. Each gathered as much as he needed.

19 Then Moses said to them, “No one may keep any of it until morning.” 20 But they did not listen to Moses; some people kept part of it until morning, and it became full of maggots and began to smell. So Moses was angry with them.

21 Every morning each person gathered as much as he needed, and when the sun grew hot, it melted away.

22 On the sixth day, they gathered twice as much food—two omers for each person—and all the leaders of the community came and reported this to Moses.

23 He told them, “This is what the the LORD has said: ‘Tomorrow is a day of complete rest, a holy Sabbath to the the LORD. Bake what you want to bake, and boil what you want to boil. Then set aside whatever is left and keep it until morning.’” 24 So they set it aside until morning, as Moses had commanded, and it did not stink or have maggots.

25 “Eat it today,” Moses said, “for today is a Sabbath to the the LORD. Today you will not find any of it in the field. 26 For six days you may gather it, but on the seventh day, the Sabbath, there will be none.”

27 Yet on the seventh day some of the people went out to gather, but they did not find any.

28 Then the the LORD said to Moses, “How long will you refuse to keep My commandments and instructions? 29 Understand that the the LORD has given you the Sabbath; that is why on the sixth day He gives you bread for two days. On the seventh day, everyone is to stay where he is; no one is to leave his place.”

30 So the people rested on the seventh day.

Manna Is Remembered for Future Generations

31 The people of Israel called the bread manna. It was white like coriander seed and tasted like wafers made with honey.

32 Then Moses said, “This is what the the LORD has commanded: ‘Keep an omer of manna for the generations to come, so that they may see the bread I gave you to eat in the desert when I brought you out of Egypt.’”

33 And Moses said to Aaron, “Take a jar and put an omer of manna in it. Then place it before the the LORD to be kept for the generations to come.”

34 So Aaron placed it before the Testimony, as the the LORD had commanded Moses. 35 The Israelites ate manna for forty years, until they came to a land where they settled. They ate manna until they came to the border of Canaan. 36 An omer is a tenth of an ephah.

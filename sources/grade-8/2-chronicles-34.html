Josiah's Early Reforms

1 Josiah was eight years old when he became king, and he ruled in Jerusalem for thirty-one years. 2 He did what was right in the eyes of the the LORD, and he walked in the ways of his father David. He did not turn to the right or to the left.

3 In the eighth year of his rule, while he was still young, he began to seek the God of his father David. In the twelfth year, he began to clean Judah and Jerusalem of the high places, the Asherah poles, the carved images, and the metal images. 4 He had the altars of the Baals torn down. He broke the incense altars that stood above them. He smashed the Asherah poles, the carved images, and the metal images. He ground them to dust and scattered the dust over the graves of the people who had sacrificed to them. 5 He burned the bones of the priests on their altars, and he made Judah and Jerusalem clean. 6 He did the same in the towns of Manasseh, Ephraim, and Simeon, and as far as Naphtali, and in the ruined places around them. 7 He tore down the altars and the Asherah poles. He crushed the carved images to powder and cut down all the incense altars in the land of Israel. Then he went back to Jerusalem.

The Temple Is Repaired

8 In the eighteenth year of his rule, after he had made the land and the temple clean, he sent Shaphan son of Azaliah, Maaseiah the city official, and Joah son of Joahaz the recorder to repair the temple of the the LORD His God. 9 They went to Hilkiah the high priest. They gave him the money that had been brought into God's house. The Levites who guarded the door had collected it from Manasseh, Ephraim, and all the rest of Israel, and from all Judah and Benjamin and the people of Jerusalem. 10 They put it into the hands of the men in charge of the work on the the LORD's house. Those men gave it to the workers who were repairing the house of the the LORD. 11 They gave it to the carpenters and builders to buy cut stone and wood for the beams and to repair the buildings that the kings of Judah had let fall into ruin. 12 The men worked faithfully. The Levites in charge were Jahath and Obadiah from the family of Merari, and Zechariah and Meshullam from the family of Kohath. All of those Levites were skilled musicians. 13 They were in charge of the laborers and directed all the workers from job to job. Some of the Levites were secretaries, officials, and gatekeepers.

The Book of the Law Is Found

14 While they were bringing out the money that had been brought into the the LORD's house, Hilkiah the priest found the Book of the Law of the the LORD given through Moses. 15 Hilkiah said to Shaphan the secretary, "I have found the Book of the Law in the house of the the LORD." He gave the book to Shaphan.

16 Shaphan brought the book to the king and reported, "Your servants are doing everything they were given to do. 17 They have paid out the money found in the the LORD's house and given it to the supervisors and workers."

18 Then Shaphan the secretary told the king, "Hilkiah the priest has given me a book." Shaphan read from it in front of the king. 19 When the king heard the words of the Law, he tore his robes. 20 The king gave orders to Hilkiah, Ahikam son of Shaphan, Abdon son of Micah, Shaphan the secretary, and Asaiah the king's servant. He said, 21 "Go and ask the the LORD for me and for those left in Israel and Judah. Ask about the words of this book that was found. The the LORD's anger is great because our ancestors did not keep the the LORD's word. They did not do everything written in this book."

22 Hilkiah and the king's men went to Huldah the prophetess, the wife of Shallum son of Tokhath son of Hasrah, who kept the wardrobe. She lived in Jerusalem, in the Second District. They spoke to her as the king had told them.

23 She said to them, "This is what the the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Tell the man who sent you to me, 24 'This is what the the LORD says: I am going to bring disaster on this place and on its people. All the curses written in the book that was read in front of the king of Judah will come true. 25 Because they have left Me and burned incense to other gods, and because they have made Me angry with everything their hands have made, My wrath will be poured out on this place and will not be put out.' 26 Tell the king of Judah, who sent you to ask the the LORD, 27 'This is what the the LORD, the God of Israel, says about the words you heard: Because your heart was tender and you humbled yourself before God when you heard what He said against this place and its people, and because you humbled yourself before Me, tore your robes, and wept before Me, I have heard you, says the the LORD. 28 I will gather you to your ancestors, and you will be buried in peace. Your eyes will not see all the disaster I am bringing on this place and on its people.'" Then they took her answer back to the king.

Josiah Renews the Covenant

29 The king sent for all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem. 30 He went up to the house of the the LORD with all the people of Judah, the people of Jerusalem, the priests, and the Levites. Everyone from the least to the greatest was there. He read to them all the words of the Book of the Covenant that had been found in the house of the the LORD. 31 The king stood in his place and made a covenant before the the LORD. He promised to follow the the LORD and to keep His commands, His rules, and His laws with all his heart and all his soul. He promised to do the words of the covenant written in this book. 32 He had everyone in Jerusalem and Benjamin stand and agree to it. The people of Jerusalem did what the covenant with God, the God of their ancestors, required. 33 Josiah removed all the detestable idols from all the land that belonged to the Israelites. He made everyone in Israel serve the the LORD their God. As long as he lived, they did not stop following the the LORD, the God of their ancestors.

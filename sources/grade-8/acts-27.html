Paul Sails for Italy

1 When it was decided that we would sail to Italy, they handed Paul and some other prisoners over to a centurion named Julius, who was in the Augustan Regiment. 2 We boarded a ship from Adramyttium that was about to sail along the coast of Asia, and we set sail. Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica, was with us. 3 The next day we landed at Sidon. Julius treated Paul kindly and allowed him to go to his friends to receive care. 4 From there we put out to sea and sailed along the sheltered side of Cyprus because the winds were against us. 5 When we had sailed across the open sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia, we landed at Myra in Lycia. 6 There the centurion found an Alexandrian ship bound for Italy and put us on board. 7 We sailed slowly for many days and barely reached Cnidus. The wind did not let us go on, so we sailed along the sheltered side of Crete, off Salmone. 8 Coasting along it with difficulty, we came to a place called Fair Havens, near the town of Lasea. 9 Much time had passed, and the voyage was now dangerous because the Jewish day of fasting had already gone by. So Paul warned them. 10 He said, "Men, I can see that this voyage will end in disaster and great loss—not only of the cargo and the ship, but also of our lives." 11 But the centurion paid more attention to the pilot and the owner of the ship than to what Paul said. 12 Since the harbor was not a good place to spend the winter, most of the men decided to sail on, hoping to reach Phoenix, a harbor in Crete that faces southwest and northwest, and stay there for the winter. 13 When a gentle south wind began to blow, they thought they had their chance. So they pulled up anchor and sailed along the coast of Crete.

The Storm at Sea

14 But before long, a violent wind called the Northeaster swept down from the island. 15 When the ship could not face the wind, we stopped trying to steer and were driven along. 16 As we passed under the shelter of a small island called Cauda, we could barely bring the lifeboat under control. 17 After hoisting it aboard, the crew passed ropes under the ship to hold it together. Fearing that they would run aground on the sandbars of Syrtis, they lowered the sea anchor and let the ship drift. 18 Because we were being pounded by the storm, the next day the crew began to throw the cargo overboard. 19 On the third day, they threw the ship's equipment overboard with their own hands. 20 When neither sun nor stars appeared for many days, and the storm kept raging, we finally gave up all hope of being saved.

Paul Encourages the Crew

21 After they had gone a long time without eating, Paul stood up among them and said, "Men, you should have listened to me and not sailed from Crete. Then you would have spared yourselves this harm and loss. 22 But now I urge you to keep up your courage, because not one of you will be lost; only the ship will be destroyed. 23 For last night an angel of God stood beside me—the God I belong to and serve. He said,

24 'Do not be afraid, Paul. You must stand trial before Caesar. And God has graciously given you the lives of all who sail with you.'

25 "So take courage, men, for I have faith in God that it will happen just as He told me. 26 But we must run aground on some island."

27 On the fourteenth night, we were still being driven across the Adriatic Sea. About midnight the sailors thought they were approaching land. 28 They measured the depth and found twenty fathoms. A short time later they measured again and found fifteen fathoms. 29 Fearing that we would be dashed against the rocks, they dropped four anchors from the stern and prayed for daylight. 30 In an attempt to escape from the ship, the sailors let down the lifeboat into the sea, pretending they were going to lower anchors from the bow. 31 Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, "Unless these men stay with the ship, you cannot be saved." 32 So the soldiers cut the ropes holding the lifeboat and let it drift away. 33 Just before dawn, Paul urged everyone to eat. He said, "For fourteen days you have been waiting and going without food, and you haven't eaten anything. 34 So I urge you to eat. You need it to survive. Not one of you will lose even a single hair from your head." 35 After he said this, he took bread, gave thanks to God in front of everyone, broke it, and began to eat. 36 They all were encouraged and ate food themselves.

The Shipwreck

37 Altogether there were 276 of us on board. 38 When they had eaten enough, they lightened the ship by throwing the grain into the sea. 39 When daylight came, they did not recognize the land. But they saw a bay with a beach and decided to run the ship aground there if they could. 40 They cut the anchors loose and left them in the sea. At the same time they untied the ropes that held the rudders. Then they raised the foresail to the wind and made for the beach. 41 But the ship struck a sandbar and ran aground. The bow stuck fast and would not move, but the stern began to break up from the force of the waves. 42 The soldiers planned to kill the prisoners so that none of them could swim away and escape. 43 But the centurion wanted to spare Paul's life, so he stopped them. He ordered those who could swim to jump overboard first and get to land. 44 The rest were to follow, some on planks and some on pieces of the ship. In this way everyone reached land safely.

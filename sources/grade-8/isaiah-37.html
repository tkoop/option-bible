Hezekiah Asks Isaiah to Pray

1 When King Hezekiah heard this, he tore his clothes, put on sackcloth, and went into the house of the the LORD. 2 He sent Eliakim, the palace manager, Shebna the writer, and the leading priests, all wearing sackcloth, to the prophet Isaiah son of Amoz.

3 They told him, “This is what Hezekiah says: ‘Today is a day of trouble, correction, and shame. It is like a woman ready to give birth who has no strength to deliver her baby. 4 Perhaps the the LORD your God will hear the words of the Assyrian commander, whom his master, the king of Assyria, sent to insult the living God. Perhaps He will correct him for the words the the LORD your God has heard. So pray for the people who are left.’”

5 So King Hezekiah’s servants went to Isaiah. 6 Isaiah said to them, “Tell your master that this is what the the LORD says: ‘Do not be afraid of the words you have heard, the words the servants of the king of Assyria used to insult Me. 7 I will put a spirit in him. He will hear a report and go back to his own country. There I will make him die by the sword.’”

Assyria Sends Another Threat

8 When the Assyrian commander heard that the king of Assyria had left Lachish, he went back and found the king fighting against Libnah. 9 Sennacherib heard that Tirhakah, king of Cush, had come out to fight against him. So he sent messengers to Hezekiah with this message:

10 “Tell Hezekiah king of Judah, ‘Do not let your God, the One you trust, fool you by saying that Jerusalem will not be handed over to the king of Assyria. 11 You have heard what the kings of Assyria did to all the other lands. They destroyed them completely. Do you think you will be saved? 12 Did the gods of the nations my fathers destroyed save them? What about the gods of Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and the people of Eden in Telassar? 13 Where are the kings of Hamath, Arpad, Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivvah?’”

Hezekiah Prays to the LORD

14 Hezekiah took the letter from the messengers and read it. Then he went up to the house of the the LORD and spread it out before the the LORD. 15 Hezekiah prayed to the the LORD:

16 “O the LORD of Armies, God of Israel, who sits above the cherubim, You alone are God over all the kingdoms of the earth. You made the heavens and the earth. 17 Turn Your ear to me, O the LORD, and hear. Open Your eyes, O the LORD, and see. Listen to all the words Sennacherib sent to insult the living God. 18 It is true, O the LORD, that the kings of Assyria have destroyed all those lands. 19 They threw their gods into the fire and destroyed them, because those gods were not real gods. They were only wood and stone, made by human hands. 20 Now, O the LORD our God, save us from his hand, so that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that You alone are the the LORD.”

The LORD Answers and Saves Jerusalem

21 Then Isaiah son of Amoz sent this message to Hezekiah: “This is what the the LORD, the God of Israel, says: ‘Because you prayed to Me about Sennacherib king of Assyria, 22 this is the word the the LORD has spoken against him: “The Virgin Daughter of Zion despises you and laughs at you. The Daughter of Jerusalem shakes her head at you as you leave. 23 Whom did you insult and mock? Against whom did you raise your voice and lift your eyes in pride? Against the Holy One of Israel! 24 Through your servants you insulted the Lord, and you said, “With my many chariots I climbed to the tops of the mountains, to the farthest parts of Lebanon. I cut down its tallest cedars and its best cypresses. I reached its highest point and its thickest forest. 25 I dug wells and drank water in foreign lands. With the soles of my feet I dried up all the rivers of Egypt.” 26 Have you not heard? Long ago I planned this. From ancient times I designed it. Now I have made it happen. You were to crush strong cities into piles of rubble. 27 That is why their people were weak. They were afraid and ashamed. They were like grass in a field, like tender green plants, like grass on a roof, burned before it can grow. 28 But I know when you sit down, when you go out, and when you come in. I know how you rage against Me. 29 Because you rage against Me and your pride has reached My ears, I will put My hook in your nose and My bit in your mouth. I will send you back the way you came.

30 “‘This will be a sign for you: This year you will eat what grows by itself. In the second year you will eat what grows from that. But in the third year you will plant and harvest, plant vineyards and eat their fruit. 31 The surviving remnant of the house of Judah will take root below and bear fruit above. 32 A remnant will go out from Jerusalem, and survivors will come from Mount Zion. The strong love of the the LORD of Armies will make this happen. 33 Therefore this is what the the LORD says about the king of Assyria: He will not enter this city or shoot an arrow here. He will not come against it with a shield or build a siege ramp against it. 34 He will go back the way he came. He will not enter this city, declares the the LORD. 35 I will defend this city and save it for My own sake and for the sake of My servant David.’”

36 Then the angel of the the LORD went out and struck down 185,000 soldiers in the Assyrian camp. When the people got up the next morning, they were all dead bodies. 37 So Sennacherib king of Assyria broke camp and left. He returned to Nineveh and stayed there. 38 One day, while he was worshiping in the temple of his god Nisroch, his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer killed him with a sword. They escaped to the land of Ararat. His son Esarhaddon became king after him.

The Words of the Teacher

1 These are the words of the Teacher, the son of David and king in Jerusalem.

Everything Is Meaningless

2 "Meaningless! Meaningless!" says the Teacher. "Everything is meaningless." 3 What does a person gain from all their hard work under the sun? 4 Generations come and go, but the earth remains forever. 5 The sun rises and sets, and hurries back to where it rises. 6 The wind blows south, then turns north; it goes around and around, then comes back. 7 All streams flow into the sea, yet the sea is never full. They return to where they began, to flow again. 8 Everything is tiring; no one can say it all. The eye never gets enough of seeing, and the ear never gets enough of hearing. 9 What has been will be again; what has been done will be done again. There is nothing new under the sun. 10 Is there anything someone can point to and say, "Look, this is new"? It has already existed long ago, before our time. 11 No one remembers the people of long ago, and the people who come later will not be remembered by those who come after them.

The Teacher's Search for Wisdom

12 I, the Teacher, was king over Israel in Jerusalem. 13 I applied my mind to study and explore by wisdom all that is done under heaven. What a heavy burden God has given people to work on! 14 I have seen all the things done under the sun. They are all meaningless, a waste of effort. 15 What is crooked cannot be straightened; what is missing cannot be counted. 16 I said to myself, "Look, I have grown in wisdom more than anyone who ruled Jerusalem before me. My mind has seen much wisdom and knowledge." 17 Then I applied my mind to understand wisdom, and also madness and folly. I learned that this too is pointless. 18 For with much wisdom comes much sorrow; the more knowledge, the more grief.

Paul Before the Sanhedrin

1 Paul looked straight at the Sanhedrin and said, “Brothers, I have lived before God with a clear conscience in every way up to this day.”

2 The high priest Ananias ordered those standing near him to strike Paul on the mouth.

3 Then Paul said to him, “God is going to strike you, you hypocrite! You sit to judge me by the law, yet you order me to be beaten against the law?”

4 Those standing by said, “Do you dare insult God’s high priest?”

5 Paul replied, “Brothers, I did not know that he was high priest. For it is written, ‘You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people.’”

6 Paul knew that one group was Sadducees and the other Pharisees, so he cried out in the Sanhedrin, “Brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees. I am on trial because of the hope of the resurrection of the dead.”

7 When he said this, a dispute broke out between the Pharisees and Sadducees, and the crowd was split in two.

8 The Sadducees say there is no resurrection, and no angel or spirit, but the Pharisees believe in both.

9 A loud uproar started. Some of the teachers of the law who were Pharisees stood up and argued, saying, “We find nothing wrong with this man. What if a spirit or an angel has spoken to him?”

10 The argument grew so fierce that the commander feared Paul would be torn to pieces by them. He ordered the soldiers to go down, take Paul away from them by force, and bring him into the barracks.

11 The next night the Lord stood by Paul and said, “Take courage! As you have testified about Me in Jerusalem, so you must also testify in Rome.”

A Plot to Kill Paul

12 When daylight came, the Jews formed a plot and bound themselves under a curse, saying they would not eat or drink until they had killed Paul.

13 More than forty men took part in this plan.

14 They went to the chief priests and elders and said, “We have bound ourselves under a solemn curse to taste nothing until we have killed Paul. 15 Now then, you and the Sanhedrin must tell the commander to bring Paul down to you, as if you were going to look into his case more carefully. We are ready to kill him before he gets close.”

16 But when Paul’s sister’s son heard about the ambush, he went to the barracks and told Paul.

17 Paul called one of the centurions and said, “Take this young man to the commander, because he has something to report to him.”

18 So the centurion took him to the commander and said, “Paul, the prisoner, called for me and asked me to bring this young man to you, because he has something to say to you.”

19 The commander took the young man by the hand, stepped aside with him, and asked, “What is it you have to report to me?”

20 He said, “The Jews have agreed to ask you to bring Paul down to the Sanhedrin tomorrow, as if they were going to question him more closely. 21 Do not give in to them, because more than forty of their men are waiting to ambush him. They have bound themselves under a curse not to eat or drink until they have killed him, and now they are ready, waiting for your permission.”

22 The commander sent the young man away and ordered him, “Do not tell anyone that you have reported these things to me.”

Paul Is Sent to Caesarea

23 Then he called two centurions and said, “Get ready two hundred soldiers, seventy horsemen, and two hundred spearmen to travel to Caesarea at the third hour of the night. 24 Provide horses for Paul to ride and bring him safely to Governor Felix.”

25 He wrote a letter that said:

26 “Claudius Lysias, to the most excellent Governor Felix: Greetings. 27 This man was seized by the Jews and was about to be killed by them, but I came with the soldiers and rescued him after learning that he is a Roman citizen. 28 Wanting to know the charge they had against him, I brought him down to their Sanhedrin. 29 I found that he was being accused over questions about their law, but there was no charge against him that deserved death or chains. 30 When I was told that a plot was being made against the man, I sent him to you at once. I also ordered his accusers to state their case against him before you.”

31 So the soldiers followed their orders and took Paul by night to Antipatris.

32 The next day they returned to the barracks, leaving the horsemen to go on with him.

33 When they reached Caesarea, they delivered the letter to the governor and brought Paul before him.

34 After reading it, he asked what province Paul was from. Learning that he was from Cilicia, 35 he said, “I will hear your case when your accusers arrive.” Then he ordered that Paul be kept under guard in Herod’s Praetorium.

Josiah Celebrates the Passover

1 Josiah kept a Passover to the the LORD in Jerusalem, and the Passover lamb was slaughtered on the fourteenth day of the first month. 2 He appointed the priests to their duties and encouraged them in the work of the house of the the LORD. 3 He said to the Levites who taught all Israel and who were holy to the the LORD, "Put the holy ark in the house that Solomon son of David, king of Israel, built. It is not to be carried on your shoulders. Now serve the the LORD your God and His people Israel. 4 Prepare yourselves by families in your divisions, according to the instructions written by David king of Israel and by his son Solomon. 5 Stand in the holy place according to the groups of the families of your fellow countrymen, the lay people, and according to the divisions of the Levite families. 6 Slaughter the Passover lambs, make yourselves holy, and prepare the lambs for your fellow countrymen, so they may observe the Passover according to the word of the the LORD given through Moses."

Josiah Provides for the Passover

7 From his own possessions Josiah contributed to the lay people, to all who were present, 30,000 lambs and goats for the Passover offerings, and 3,000 bulls. 8 His officials also contributed willingly to the people, priests, and Levites. Hilkiah, Zechariah, and Jehiel, the chief officials of the house of God, gave the priests 2,600 Passover offerings and 300 bulls. 9 Additionally, Conaniah and his brothers Shemaiah and Nethanel, along with Hashabiah, Jeiel, and Jozabad, the leaders of the Levites, contributed 5,000 Passover offerings and 500 bulls to the Levites. 10 So the service was ready; the priests stood in their places and the Levites in their divisions according to the king's command. 11 They slaughtered the Passover lambs, and the priests splashed the blood, while the Levites skinned the animals. 12 They set aside the burnt offerings to give them to the divisions of the families of the people to offer to the the LORD, as it is written in the Book of Moses; and they did the same with the bulls. 13 They roasted the Passover animals over the fire according to the rule, and boiled the holy offerings in pots, cauldrons, and pans, and brought them quickly to all the lay people. 14 Afterward, they made preparations for themselves and for the priests, because the priests, the descendants of Aaron, were busy from morning till night offering the burnt offerings and the fat portions. So the Levites made preparations for themselves and for the priests, the descendants of Aaron. 15 The singers, the descendants of Asaph, were at their stations according to the command of David, Asaph, Heman, and Jeduthun the king's seer. The gatekeepers at each gate did not need to leave their posts, because their fellow Levites made preparations for them. 16 So all the service of the the LORD was carried out that day to celebrate the Passover and to offer burnt offerings on the altar of the the LORD, according to the command of King Josiah. 17 The Israelites who were present also celebrated the Passover at that time, as well as the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days. 18 No such Passover had been observed in Israel since the days of Samuel the prophet; none of the kings of Israel had ever celebrated such a Passover as did Josiah, together with the priests, Levites, all Judah and Israel who were present, and the people living in Jerusalem. 19 This Passover was observed in the eighteenth year of Josiah's reign.

Josiah's Death in Battle

20 After all this, when Josiah had set the temple in order, Neco king of Egypt marched up to fight at Carchemish on the Euphrates, and Josiah went out to meet him. 21 But Neco sent messengers to him, saying, "What is the issue between you and me, O king of Judah? I have not come against you today, but I am fighting another kingdom. God has told me to hurry. Stop opposing God, who is with me, or He will destroy you." 22 But Josiah did not turn away from him; instead, he disguised himself to fight. He did not listen to Neco's words from the mouth of God, but went to fight in the Valley of Megiddo. 23 There the archers shot King Josiah, who said to his servants, "Take me away, for I am badly wounded!" 24 So his servants took him out of his chariot, put him in his second chariot, and brought him to Jerusalem, where he died. He was buried in the tombs of his fathers, and all Judah and Jerusalem mourned for Josiah. 25 Then Jeremiah mourned for Josiah, and to this day all the singing men and women speak of Josiah in their sad songs. They made this a custom in Israel, and indeed they are written in the Laments. 26 As for the rest of the acts of Josiah, along with his deeds of faithful love, they are written in the Law of the the LORD. 27 And his first and last acts are written in the Book of the Kings of Israel and Judah.

David and Jonathan Make a Plan

1 David fled from Naioth in Ramah and came to Jonathan. He said, "What have I done? What is my guilt? What sin have I committed against your father that he wants to kill me?"

2 Jonathan said to him, "Never! You will not die. Look, my father does nothing, big or small, without telling me. Why would my father hide this from me? It is not so."

3 David swore again and said, "Your father knows very well that I have found favor in your eyes. He has said, 'Do not let Jonathan know this, or he will be upset.' But as surely as the the LORD lives, and as your soul lives, I am only one step away from death."

4 Jonathan said to David, "Whatever you say, I will do for you."

5 David said to Jonathan, "Look, tomorrow is the new moon, and I am supposed to sit and eat with the king. But you can send me away, and I will hide in the field until the third evening. 6 If your father misses me at all, then say, 'David begged me to let him run to Bethlehem, his hometown, because the whole family has a yearly sacrifice there.' 7 If he says, 'That is fine,' then your servant is safe. But if he is angry, then know that he has decided to harm me. 8 So show kindness to your servant, because you have brought your servant into a covenant with the the LORD. But if I am guilty, kill me yourself. Why should you bring me to your father?"

9 Jonathan said, "Never! If I find out that my father has decided to harm you, I will certainly tell you."

10 David said to Jonathan, "Who will tell me if your father answers you harshly?"

11 Jonathan said to David, "Come, let us go out into the field." So they both went out into the field.

Jonathan Promises to Warn David

12 Jonathan said to David, "By the the LORD, the God of Israel, when I have checked on my father by this time tomorrow—or the third day—and if things are good for David, I will send word to you and let you know. 13 But if my father wants to harm you, may the the LORD punish me severely if I do not tell you and send you away so you can go in peace. May the the LORD be with you, as He has been with my father. 14 If I am still alive, show me the the LORD's kindness so I will not die. 15 And do not cut off your kindness from my family forever—not even when the the LORD has cut off every one of David's enemies from the earth."

16 So Jonathan made a covenant with David's family, saying, "May the the LORD hold David's enemies accountable."

17 Jonathan also made David swear again because of his love for him, for he loved him as he loved his own life.

18 Jonathan said to him, "Tomorrow is the new moon, and you will be missed, because your seat will be empty. 19 On the third day, go down quickly to the place where you hid before, and stay by the stone Ezel. 20 I will shoot three arrows to the side, as if I were shooting at a target. 21 Then I will send the boy, saying, 'Go, find the arrows.' If I say to the boy, 'Look, the arrows are on this side of you; pick them up,' then come, for as the the LORD lives, it is safe for you, and there is no danger. 22 But if I say to the young man, 'Look, the arrows are beyond you,' go your way, for the the LORD has sent you away. 23 As for the matter we have spoken about, the the LORD is between you and me forever."

Saul's Anger at the Feast

24 So David hid in the field. When the new moon came, the king sat down to eat.

25 The king sat in his usual place by the wall. Jonathan sat across from him, and Abner sat beside Saul, but David's place was empty.

26 Saul said nothing that day, for he thought, "Something has happened to him; he must not be clean."

27 But on the second day, the day after the new moon, David's place was still empty. Saul said to his son Jonathan, "Why hasn't the son of Jesse come to the meal, either yesterday or today?"

28 Jonathan answered Saul, "David begged me to let him go to Bethlehem. 29 He said he needed to go because their family had a sacrifice in the city and his brother had told him to come. He also asked that, if he had found favor in my eyes, he be allowed to get away and see his brothers. That is why he has not come to the king's table."

30 Saul's anger burned against Jonathan. He said to him, "You son of a stubborn rebel! Do I not know that you have chosen the son of Jesse to your own shame and to the shame of your mother? 31 As long as the son of Jesse lives on the earth, neither you nor your kingdom will be secure. Now send for him and bring him to me, for he must die."

32 Jonathan answered his father Saul, "Why should he be put to death? What has he done?"

33 But Saul threw his spear at him to kill him. Then Jonathan knew that his father was determined to kill David. 34 Jonathan got up from the table in fierce anger. He did not eat any food on the second day of the new moon, because he was upset about David and because his father had shamed him.

Jonathan Warns David

35 In the morning Jonathan went out into the field for the meeting with David, and a small boy was with him.

36 He said to his boy, "Run and find the arrows I shoot." As the boy ran, Jonathan shot an arrow beyond him. 37 When the boy came to the place where Jonathan's arrow had landed, Jonathan called after him, "Isn't the arrow beyond you?" 38 Jonathan called again, "Hurry! Go fast! Do not stop!" Jonathan's boy picked up the arrows and brought them to his master.

39 The boy did not know what was going on, but Jonathan and David knew. 40 Jonathan gave his weapons to his boy and said, "Go, carry them into the city."

41 As soon as the boy had gone, David rose from the south side of the stone and fell facedown to the ground. He bowed three times. Then the two men kissed and wept together, David weeping more.

42 Jonathan said to David, "Go in peace. We have both sworn by the name of the the LORD that the the LORD will be between me and you and between my descendants and your descendants forever." Then David got up and left, and Jonathan went back into the city.

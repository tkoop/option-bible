Nehemiah Asks the King for Help

1 In the month of Nisan, in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes, wine was set before him. I took the wine and gave it to the king. I had never been sad in his presence before.

2 The king said to me, "Why is your face sad? You aren't sick. This can only be sadness of heart." I was very afraid.

3 I said to the king, "May the king live forever! Why shouldn't my face be sad when the city where my fathers are buried lies in ruins and its gates have been burned?"

4 The king said to me, "What are you seeking?" Then I prayed to the God of heaven.

5 I said to the king, "If it pleases the king, and if your servant has found favor with you, send me to Judah, to the city where my fathers are buried, so I can rebuild it."

6 The king, with the queen sitting beside him, asked me, "How long will your trip take, and when will you return?" It pleased the king to send me, and I set a time for him.

7 I also said to the king, "If it pleases the king, let letters be given to me for the governors beyond the River, so they will let me pass through until I reach Judah."

8 And give me a letter to Asaph, keeper of the king's forest, so he will give me timber to make beams for the gates of the fort by the temple, for the city wall, and for the house I will live in. The king granted these things because the good hand of my God was on me.

9 Then I came to the governors beyond the River and gave them the king's letters. The king had also sent army officers and horsemen with me.

10 When Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite official heard about this, they were very upset that someone had come to seek the good of the people of Israel.

Nehemiah Inspects the Walls

11 I came to Jerusalem and stayed there three days.

12 Then I got up during the night with a few men. I had not told anyone what my God had put in my heart to do for Jerusalem. The only animal with me was the one I rode.

13 I went out at night through the Valley Gate toward the Dragon Spring and the Dung Gate, looking over the walls of Jerusalem. They were broken down, and the gates had been burned.

14 Then I went on to the Fountain Gate and the King's Pool, but there was no room for the animal under me to get through.

15 So I went up the valley at night and inspected the wall. Then I turned back and entered through the Valley Gate.

16 The officials did not know where I had gone or what I was doing. I had not yet told the Jews, the priests, the nobles, the officials, or anyone else who would do the work.

Nehemiah Calls the People to Rebuild

17 Then I said to them, "You see the trouble we are in. Jerusalem is in ruins, and its gates have been burned. Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem so we will no longer be a disgrace."

18 I told them how the hand of my God had been good to me and what the king had said to me. They said, "Let us rise up and build." So they strengthened their hands to do the good work.

19 But when Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite, and Geshem the Arab heard about it, they mocked us and despised us. They said, "What is this you are doing? Are you rebelling against the king?"

20 I answered them, "The God of heaven will give us success. We, His servants, will rise up and build. But you have no part, right, or claim in Jerusalem."

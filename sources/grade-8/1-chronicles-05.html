Reuben's Family Line and Birthright

1 The sons of Reuben, Israel's firstborn, were listed first because Reuben was the oldest. But his birthright was given to Joseph's sons, since Reuben had disgraced his father by sleeping with his father's concubine. So Reuben was not counted as the firstborn in the family records. 2 Judah became the strongest of his brothers, and the ruler came from him. Still, the birthright belonged to Joseph.

3 Reuben's sons were Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi. 4 Joel's sons were Shemaiah, then Gog, then Shimei. 5 Then came Micah, Reaiah, and Baal. 6 Beerah was carried away by Tilgath-pilneser, king of Assyria. He was a leader of the Reubenites. 7 Their relatives were listed by family groups and family lines. Jeiel was the chief, and Zechariah came next.

8 Bela son of Azaz, son of Shema, son of Joel, lived in Aroer and as far as Nebo and Baal Meon. 9 He settled eastward as far as the edge of the desert near the Euphrates River, because they had many animals in Gilead. 10 In Saul's days, they fought the Hagrites. They won and lived in the Hagrites' tents all across the east side of Gilead.

Gad's Family Line

11 The Gadites lived across from them in Bashan, as far as Salcah. 12 Joel was chief, Shapham was second, then Jaanai and Shaphat in Bashan. 13 Their relatives, by family groups, were Michael, Meshullam, Sheba, Jorai, Jacan, Zia, and Eber—seven in all. 14 These were sons of Abihail son of Huri, son of Jaroah, son of Gilead, son of Michael, son of Jeshishai, son of Jahdo, son of Buz. 15 Ahi son of Abdiel, son of Guni, was chief of their family groups. 16 They lived in Gilead, Bashan, its towns, and all the pasturelands of Sharon, to their borders. 17 All of them were recorded in the days of Jotham king of Judah and Jeroboam king of Israel.

The War of the Eastern Tribes

18 The Reubenites, Gadites, and half of Manasseh had 44,760 soldiers. They were brave men who carried shields and swords, drew bows, and knew how to fight. 19 They fought the Hagrites, Jetur, Naphish, and Nodab. 20 They cried to God in battle. He helped them, and the Hagrites and everyone with them were handed over to them, because they trusted in Him. God answered their prayers. 21 They took 50,000 camels, 250,000 sheep, 2,000 donkeys, and 100,000 captives. 22 Many of the enemy died, because the battle was God's. They lived in the enemy's place until the exile.

The Exile of the Eastern Tribes

23 Half of Manasseh lived in the land from Bashan to Baal Hermon, Senir, and Mount Hermon. They were many. 24 Their family leaders were Epher, Ishi, Eliel, Azriel, Jeremiah, Hodaviah, and Jahdiel. They were strong warriors, famous men, and heads of their families. 25 They were unfaithful to the God of their fathers. They worshiped the gods of the people of the land, whom God had destroyed before them. 26 So the God of Israel stirred up Pul king of Assyria and Tilgath-pilneser king of Assyria. They carried away the Reubenites, Gadites, and half of Manasseh. They brought them to Halah, Habor, Hara, and the Gozan River, where they are to this day.

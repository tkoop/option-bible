Paul and Timothy

1 Paul went to Derbe and then to Lystra. There he found a disciple named Timothy. Timothy’s mother was a Jewish believer, but his father was Greek. 2 The believers in Lystra and Iconium said good things about Timothy. 3 Paul wanted Timothy to travel with him. So he took him and circumcised him because of the Jews who lived there, since everyone knew Timothy’s father was Greek.

4 As they traveled from city to city, they gave the people the rules that the apostles and elders in Jerusalem had decided they should follow. 5 So the churches grew strong in faith and increased in number every day.

The Holy Spirit Leads Them to Macedonia

6 They traveled through Phrygia and Galatia, because the Holy Spirit kept them from speaking the word in Asia. 7 When they came to Mysia, they tried to go into Bithynia, but the Spirit of Jesus would not let them. 8 So they passed by Mysia and went down to Troas.

9 During the night Paul had a vision. A man from Macedonia stood there and begged him, “Come over to Macedonia and help us.” 10 After Paul saw the vision, we quickly decided to leave for Macedonia. We believed God had called us to tell them the good news.

They Arrive in Philippi

11 We sailed from Troas straight to Samothrace, and the next day we went to Neapolis. 12 From there we went to Philippi, a Roman colony and the leading city of that part of Macedonia. We stayed there for several days.

13 On the Sabbath we went outside the city gate to the river. We thought there was a place of prayer there. We sat down and talked with the women who had gathered. 14 One of them was a woman named Lydia. She sold purple cloth and was from the city of Thyatira. She worshiped God. The Lord opened her heart so she would pay attention to what Paul said. 15 After she and her household were baptized, she invited us, “If you think I am faithful to the Lord, come and stay at my house.” She kept urging us.

Trouble in Philippi

16 One day, as we were going to the place of prayer, a female slave met us. She had a spirit that could tell the future. She made a lot of money for her owners by telling fortunes.

17 She followed Paul and us and shouted, “These men are servants of the Most High God. They are telling you the way to be saved.”

18 She kept doing this for many days. Paul became annoyed. He turned and said to the spirit, “In the name of Jesus Christ I command you to come out of her.” And the spirit left her at that very hour.

19 When her owners saw that their hope of making money was gone, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace to the authorities. 20 They brought them to the magistrates and said, “These men are Jews, and they are troubling our city. 21 They are teaching customs that we Romans are not allowed to accept or practice.”

22 The crowd joined in attacking them. The magistrates ordered that they be stripped and beaten with rods. 23 After giving them many blows, they threw them into prison and ordered the jailer to guard them carefully. 24 The jailer put them in the inner prison and locked their feet in stocks.

25 About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the other prisoners were listening to them. 26 Suddenly a great earthquake shook the prison so hard that its foundations moved. At once all the doors opened, and everyone’s chains came loose.

27 The jailer woke up and saw the prison doors open. He thought the prisoners had escaped. He drew his sword and was about to kill himself. 28 But Paul shouted in a loud voice, “Don’t do anything to hurt yourself! We are all here!”

29 The jailer called for lights, rushed in, and fell trembling before Paul and Silas. 30 He brought them out and asked, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?”

31 They answered, “Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved—you and your household.” 32 Then they spoke the word of the Lord to him and to everyone in his house.

33 At that hour of the night, he took them and washed their wounds. Then he and all his family were baptized right away. 34 He brought them into his house and set food before them. He was full of joy because he had come to believe in God, along with his whole household.

35 When day came, the magistrates sent the officers with the order, “Release those men.”

36 The jailer reported these words to Paul: “The magistrates have sent orders to release you. So now come out and go in peace.”

37 Paul said to them, “They beat us in public without a trial, even though we are Roman citizens, and threw us into prison. Now are they going to throw us out secretly? No! Let them come themselves and lead us out.” 38 The officers reported these words to the magistrates. When they heard that Paul and Silas were Roman citizens, they were afraid. 39 They came and apologized to them. Then they led them out and asked them to leave the city. 40 After leaving the prison, they went to Lydia’s house. They saw the brothers, encouraged them, and then left.

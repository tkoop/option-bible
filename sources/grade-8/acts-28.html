Paul on Malta

1 After we were brought safely to shore, we learned that the island was called Malta. 2 The local people showed us unusual kindness. They lit a fire and welcomed all of us because it was raining and cold. 3 Paul gathered a pile of brushwood and put it on the fire. A poisonous snake came out of the heat and bit his hand.

4 When the local people saw the snake hanging from his hand, they said to one another, “This man must be a murderer. He was saved from the sea, but Justice will not let him live.”

5 But Paul shook the snake off into the fire and was not hurt. 6 The people expected him to swell up or suddenly fall down dead. After they waited a long time and saw nothing bad happen to him, they changed their minds and said he was a god.

Paul Heals the Sick

7 Near that place were fields belonging to Publius, the chief official of the island. He welcomed us and took care of us kindly for three days. 8 Publius’s father was in bed, sick with fever and dysentery. Paul went in to see him, prayed, placed his hands on him, and healed him. 9 After this happened, the rest of the sick people on the island came and were healed. 10 They honored us in many ways and gave us what we needed for our trip.

Paul Sails to Rome

11 After three months, we sailed in a ship from Alexandria that had spent the winter on the island. Its sign was the Twin Brothers. 12 We landed at Syracuse and stayed there three days. 13 From there we traveled along the coast and reached Rhegium. After one day, a south wind came up, and on the second day we came to Puteoli.

14 There we found some brothers and were invited to stay with them seven days. After that, we came to Rome. 15 The brothers there had heard about us and came as far as the Forum of Appius and the Three Taverns to meet us. When Paul saw them, he took courage and thanked God. 16 When we got to Rome, Paul was allowed to live by himself, with a soldier to guard him.

Paul Speaks to the Jewish Leaders in Rome

17 Three days later, Paul called together the leading men of the Jews. When they had gathered, he said to them, “Brothers, I have done nothing against our people or against the customs of our fathers. Yet I was arrested in Jerusalem and handed over to the Romans. 18 The Romans questioned me and wanted to release me, because they found no reason for me to die. 19 But when the Jews objected, I was forced to appeal to Caesar. I did not do this to accuse my own people. 20 That is why I asked to see you and speak with you. It is because of the hope of Israel that I am wearing this chain.”

21 They replied, “We have not received any letters from Judea about you, and none of the brothers who came here has reported or said anything bad about you. 22 But we would like to hear what you think, because people everywhere are speaking against this group.”

23 They set a day to meet with him. Many people came to his lodging. From morning until evening, he explained and testified about the kingdom of God. He tried to convince them about Jesus from the Law of Moses and the Prophets. 24 Some were convinced by what he said, but others did not believe.

25 They did not agree with each other and were leaving after Paul said one thing: “The Holy Spirit spoke well to your fathers through the prophet Isaiah: ‘Go to this people and say, “26 You will hear by hearing and not understand, and seeing you will see and not perceive. 27 For the heart of this people has grown dull, and they hear with difficulty with their ears, and they have closed their eyes. Otherwise they might see with their eyes, hear with their ears, understand with their heart, turn, and I would heal them.”’ 28 Therefore, let it be known to you that this salvation from God has been sent to the Gentiles, and they will listen.”

30 Paul stayed two full years in a house he rented. He welcomed everyone who came to him. 31 He preached the kingdom of God and taught about the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness and without anyone stopping him.

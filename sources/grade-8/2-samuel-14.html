Joab's Plan to Bring Absalom Home

1 Joab, the son of Zeruiah, knew that the king longed for Absalom. 2 So Joab sent to Tekoa and brought a wise woman from there. He said to her, “Please pretend to mourn. Put on mourning clothes. Do not anoint yourself with oil. Act like a woman who has mourned a dead person for a long time. 3 Go to the king and speak to him like this.” Then Joab told her what to say.

The Woman's Plea

4 The woman from Tekoa went to the king. She fell on her face to the ground, bowed, and said, “Help me, O king!”

5 The king asked, “What is wrong?” She said, “I am a widow. My husband is dead. 6 Your servant had two sons. They fought in the field, and no one was there to stop them. One struck the other and killed him. 7 Now the whole family has risen against your servant. They say, ‘Hand over the one who struck his brother. We will put him to death for the life of his brother whom he killed. We will also destroy the heir.’ They want to snuff out the last hope I have left. Then my husband will not have a name or a descendant on the earth.”

8 The king said to the woman, “Go home, and I will give orders concerning you.”

9 The woman of Tekoa said to the king, “My lord the king, let the guilt be on me and on my father's family. The king and his throne are innocent.”

10 The king said, “If anyone says anything to you, bring him to me. He will not bother you again.”

11 She said, “Please let the king remember the LORD your God, so the avenger of blood will not destroy anymore, and they will not destroy my son.”

He said, “As the LORD lives, not one hair of your son will fall to the ground.”

12 Then the woman said, “Please let your servant speak one more word to my lord the king.”

He said, “Speak.”

13 The woman said, “Why then have you planned such a thing against God's people? By saying this, the king condemns himself, because he has not brought back his banished son. 14 We all must die. We are like water spilled on the ground that cannot be gathered up again. But God does not take away life. Instead, He makes a way so a banished person can come back. 15 Now I have come to speak this word to my lord the king because the people made me afraid. Your servant thought, ‘I will speak to the king. Maybe the king will do what his servant asks. 16 The king will hear and deliver his servant from the hand of the man who would destroy me and my son together from the land God gave us.’ 17 Then your servant said, ‘The word of my lord the king will give me rest. For my lord the king is like an angel of God in telling good from evil. May the LORD your God be with you.’”

18 The king answered the woman, “Please do not hide anything from me that I am about to ask you.”

The woman said, “Let my lord the king speak.”

19 The king said, “Is Joab's hand in all this with you?”

The woman answered, “As your soul lives, my lord the king, no one can turn aside from anything my lord the king says. Yes, your servant Joab commanded me. He put all these words in the mouth of your servant. 20 Your servant Joab did this to change how this matter would turn out. My lord is wise, like the wisdom of an angel of God, to know everything on earth.”

Absalom Returns to Jerusalem

21 The king said to Joab, “All right, I have done this. Go bring back the young man Absalom.”

22 Joab fell with his face to the ground, bowed, and blessed the king. Joab said, “Today your servant knows he has found favor in your eyes, my lord the king, because the king has granted his servant's request.”

23 Joab got up and went to Geshur and brought Absalom to Jerusalem. 24 The king said, “He may go to his own house, but he must not see my face.” So Absalom went to his own house and did not see the king's face.

25 In all Israel there was no one as handsome as Absalom. From the sole of his foot to the top of his head there was no blemish in him. 26 When he cut his hair—he cut it at the end of every year because it became heavy on him—the weight of his hair was two hundred shekels by the king's standard. 27 Three sons and one daughter were born to Absalom. His daughter's name was Tamar. She was a beautiful woman.

28 Absalom lived in Jerusalem for two full years without seeing the king's face.

Absalom Forces Joab to Help

29 Then Absalom sent for Joab to send him to the king, but Joab would not come. He sent a second time, but Joab still would not come.

30 So Absalom said to his servants, “Look, Joab's field is next to mine, and he has barley there. Go and set it on fire.” So Absalom's servants set the field on fire.

31 Joab got up and went to Absalom's house. He said, “Why did your servants set my field on fire?”

32 Absalom said to Joab, “Look, I sent to you and said, ‘Come here, so I can send you to the king to ask, “Why did I come from Geshur? It would have been better for me to stay there.” Now let me see the king's face. If there is guilt in me, let him put me to death.’”

33 Joab went to the king and told him. The king called for Absalom, and he came. Absalom bowed with his face to the ground before the king. The king kissed Absalom.

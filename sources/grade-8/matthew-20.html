Workers in the Vineyard

1 For the kingdom of heaven is like a landowner who went out early in the morning to hire workers for his vineyard. 2 He agreed to pay them a denarius for the day and sent them into his vineyard.

3 About nine o'clock in the morning he went out again and saw others standing idle in the marketplace. 4 He said to them, “You go into the vineyard too, and I will pay you what is right.” So they went. 5 He went out again about noon and about three in the afternoon and did the same thing.

6 About five o'clock in the afternoon he went out and found others standing there. He asked them, “Why have you been standing here all day doing nothing?” 7 They said, “Because no one hired us.” He said to them, “You go into the vineyard too.”

8 When evening came, the owner of the vineyard said to his foreman, “Call the workers and pay them their wages. Begin with the last ones hired and go to the first.”

9 The workers who were hired at five o'clock came and each received a denarius. 10 When the first workers came, they thought they would get more, but each of them also received a denarius.

11 When they received it, they grumbled against the landowner. 12 They said, “These last men worked only one hour, and you have made them equal to us. We carried the load and worked in the heat all day.”

13 He answered one of them, “Friend, I am not cheating you. Didn't you agree with me for a denarius? 14 Take what is yours and go. I want to give this last man the same as you. 15 Don't I have the right to do what I want with my own money? Or is your eye jealous because I am generous?”

16 So the last will be first, and the first will be last.”

Jesus Predicts His Death

17 As Jesus was going up to Jerusalem, He took the twelve disciples aside privately and said to them, “18 Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and the Son of Man will be handed over to the chief priests and teachers of the law. They will condemn Him to death. 19 They will hand Him over to the Gentiles to be mocked, flogged, and crucified. On the third day He will be raised to life.”

True Greatness

20 Then the mother of Zebedee's sons came to Him with her sons. She knelt down and asked Him for a favor. 21 “What do you want?” He asked. She said to Him, “Say that these two sons of mine may sit, one at your right hand and one at your left, in your kingdom.”

22 “You do not know what you are asking. Can you drink the cup I am about to drink?” They said to Him, “We can.”

23 “You will indeed drink My cup, but to sit at My right hand and at My left is not Mine to give. It is for those for whom My Father has prepared it.”

24 When the other ten disciples heard this, they were angry with the two brothers.

25 But Jesus called them together and said, “You know that the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, and their leaders exercise authority over them. 26 It will not be so among you. Instead, whoever wants to be great among you must be your servant. 27 Whoever wants to be first among you must be your slave. 28 Just as the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life as a ransom for many.”

Two Blind Men Healed

29 As they were leaving Jericho, a large crowd followed Him.

30 Two blind men were sitting by the road. When they heard that Jesus was passing by, they cried out, “Lord, Son of David, have mercy on us!” 31 The crowd told them to be quiet, but they shouted even louder, “Lord, Son of David, have mercy on us!”

32 Jesus stopped and called them. “What do you want Me to do for you?” He asked. 33 “Lord,” they said, “let our eyes be opened.”

34 Jesus had compassion on them. He touched their eyes, and at once they could see. Then they followed Him.

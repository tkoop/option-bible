Haman Is Honored

1 After these events, King Xerxes honored Haman, the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, by giving him a position higher than all the other officials.

Mordecai Refuses to Bow

2 All the king's officials at the royal gate bowed down to Haman because the king had commanded it, but Mordecai refused to bow or show respect. 3 The officials asked Mordecai, "Why do you disobey the king's command?" 4 They spoke to him day after day, but he would not listen, so they reported the matter to Haman to see whether Mordecai would keep refusing, since he had told them he was a Jew. 5 When Haman saw that Mordecai would not bow or show respect, he became furious.

Haman Plans to Destroy the Jews

6 Haman also learned who Mordecai's people were, and he scorned the idea of punishing Mordecai alone. Instead, he planned to destroy all the Jews throughout King Xerxes' kingdom. 7 In the twelfth year of King Xerxes, during the first month, called Nisan, Haman had the lot cast before him to choose a day and month. The lot is called Pur, and it selected the twelfth month, called Adar.

8 Then Haman said to King Xerxes, "There is a certain people scattered among the peoples in all the provinces of your kingdom. Their laws differ from those of every other people, and they do not obey the king's laws, so it is not in the king's best interest to tolerate them. 9 If it pleases the king, let an order be written to destroy them, and I will pay ten thousand talents of silver into the royal treasury for the men who carry out this business."

10 So the king removed his signet ring from his finger and gave it to Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of the Jews. 11 The king said to Haman, "Keep the money and do with the people as you please."

The Order Is Sent

12 On the thirteenth day of the first month, the royal scribes were summoned. They wrote out every command Haman had dictated to the king's governors and officials in each province, using the script and language of that province. They wrote it in the name of King Xerxes and sealed it with the king's signet ring. 13 The letters were sent by couriers to all the king's provinces with orders to destroy, kill, and wipe out all the Jews—young and old, women and children—and to seize their possessions on a single day, the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, Adar. 14 A copy of the text was to be issued in every province and published to all the peoples so they would be ready for that day. 15 The couriers went out, spurred on by the king's command, and the edict was issued in the citadel of Susa. Then the king and Haman sat down to drink, but the city of Susa was in confusion.

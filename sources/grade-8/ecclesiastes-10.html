Foolishness and Wisdom

1Dead flies can make a perfumer's oil stink. In the same way, a little foolishness can outweigh wisdom and honor. 2A wise person's heart leans to the right, but a fool's heart leans to the left. 3Even when a fool walks down the road, his heart lacks sense, and he shows everyone that he is a fool.

Rulers and Calmness

4If a ruler's anger rises against you, don't leave your place, because calmness can settle even great wrongs. 5I've seen something wrong under the sun: a mistake that comes from a ruler. 6Foolishness is given high positions, while rich people sit in low places. 7I've seen slaves on horseback, while princes walk on the ground like slaves.

Work, Speech, and Folly

8Anyone who digs a pit can fall into it, and anyone who breaks through a wall can be bitten by a snake. 9Someone who moves stones can get hurt by them, and someone who splits wood can be hurt by it. 10If the iron is dull and its edge isn't sharpened, more strength is needed, but wisdom helps you succeed. 11If a snake bites before it's charmed, the snake charmer gains nothing. 12A wise person's words are kind, but a fool's own words destroy him. 13His talk starts with foolishness and ends in wicked madness. 14A fool talks too much, even though no one knows what's coming. Who can tell him what will happen after him? 15Fools' hard work wears them out, because they don't even know the way to town.

Kings and Nations

16Trouble comes to you, land, when your king is a child and your leaders feast in the morning! 17You are blessed, land, when your king comes from noble birth and your leaders eat at the right time—for strength, not for getting drunk! 18Because of laziness, the roof sags, and because of idle hands, the house leaks. 19Bread is made for laughter, wine makes life happy, and money answers everything. 20Don't curse the king, even in your thoughts, and don't curse a rich person in your bedroom, because a bird of the air may carry your words, and a winged creature may repeat what you said.

Absalom Wins the People's Support

1 After this, Absalom got a chariot and horses. He also had fifty men run ahead of him. 2 Absalom would get up early and stand beside the road to the city gate. Whenever someone had a case to bring to the king for judgment, Absalom would call to him and ask, "Which city are you from?"

The man would answer, "I am your servant from one of the tribes of Israel."

3 Absalom would say to him, "Look, your case sounds good and fair. But the king has no one to hear you." 4 Absalom would also say, "If only someone would make me judge in the land! Then everyone who had a dispute or case could come to me, and I would give him justice." 5 Whenever anyone came near to bow to him, Absalom would reach out his hand, take hold of the man, and kiss him. 6 Absalom did this for all the Israelites who came to the king for judgment. In this way, he won the loyalty of the people of Israel.

Absalom Plans His Rebellion

7 After forty years, Absalom said to the king, "Please let me go to Hebron to keep a promise I made to the LORD." 8 "For when I lived in Geshur in Aram, I promised, 'If the LORD brings me back to Jerusalem, I will worship the LORD.'"

9 The king said to him, "Go in peace." So Absalom got up and went to Hebron.

10 Then Absalom sent secret messengers to all the tribes of Israel. They said, "As soon as you hear the trumpet, say, 'Absalom is king in Hebron!'"

11 Two hundred men from Jerusalem went with Absalom. They had been invited, and they went without knowing the plan. 12 While Absalom was offering sacrifices, he sent for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David's advisor, from his city of Giloh. The plot grew strong, and more and more people joined Absalom.

David Flees Jerusalem

13 A messenger came to David and said, "The people of Israel have given their loyalty to Absalom."

14 Then David said to all his servants who were with him in Jerusalem, "Come, let us run away! If we don't, we won't escape from Absalom. Hurry, or he will catch us, bring disaster on us, and kill the people of the city with swords."

15 The king's servants said to the king, "We are your servants. We will do whatever our lord the king chooses."

16 The king left, and his whole household went with him. But he left ten concubines to take care of the house. 17 The king left, and all the people followed him. They stopped at the last house. 18 All his servants passed by him, along with all the Cherethites, Pelethites, and six hundred Gittites who had followed him from Gath. They passed in front of the king.

David's Friends Stay Loyal

19 The king said to Ittai the Gittite, "Why are you going with us? Go back and stay with the king, for you are a foreigner and an exile from your homeland." 20 "You came only yesterday. Should I make you wander with us today when I don't know where I'm going? Go back and take your brothers with you. May the LORD show you kindness and faithfulness."

21 Ittai answered the king, "As surely as the LORD lives, and as surely as my lord the king lives, I will go wherever my lord the king goes, whether it means life or death."

22 David said to Ittai, "Go ahead, then." So Ittai the Gittite marched on with all his men and all the little ones who were with him.

23 The whole land was crying loudly as all the people passed by. The king crossed the Kidron Valley, and all the people moved toward the wilderness road.

24 Zadok was there too, with all the Levites. They were carrying the ark of the covenant of God. They set down the ark of God, and Abiathar came up while all the people finished passing out of the city.

25 The king said to Zadok, "Take the ark of God back to the city. If I find favor in the LORD's eyes, He will bring me back and let me see it and His home again." 26 "But if He says, 'I am not pleased with you,' then here I am. Let Him do to me whatever seems good to Him."

27 The king said to Zadok the priest, "Are you not a seer? Go back to the city in peace, with your son Ahimaaz and Jonathan son of Abiathar. You and Abiathar take your two sons with you." 28 "See, I will wait at the fords in the wilderness until word comes from you to tell me."

29 So Zadok and Abiathar took the ark of God back to Jerusalem and stayed there. 30 David went up the Mount of Olives, weeping as he went. His head was covered, and he walked barefoot. All the people with him also covered their heads and went up, weeping as they climbed.

31 David had been told, "Ahithophel is among the conspirators with Absalom."

So David prayed to the LORD, "Please make Ahithophel's advice foolish."

32 When David reached the summit, where people used to worship God, Hushai the Arkite came to meet him. His robe was torn, and dust was on his head.

33 David said to him, "If you go with me, you will only be a burden to me." 34 "But if you return to the city and say to Absalom, 'I will be your servant, O king; I was your father's servant in the past, but now I am your servant,' then you can help me by working against Ahithophel's advice." 35 "Aren't Zadok and Abiathar the priests there with you? Tell them everything you hear from the king's house." 36 "Their two sons, Ahimaaz son of Zadok and Jonathan son of Abiathar, are there with them. Send me word through them about everything you hear."

37 So Hushai, David's friend, went into the city. Absalom was just entering Jerusalem.

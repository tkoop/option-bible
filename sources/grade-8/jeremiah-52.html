Zedekiah Rules in Jerusalem

1 Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem eleven years. His mother’s name was Hamutal, the daughter of Jeremiah; she was from Libnah.

2 He did evil in the sight of the the LORD, just as Jehoiakim had done. 3 Because of the the LORD’s anger, this happened to Jerusalem and Judah, until He cast them out from His presence. And Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.

Babylon Surrounds and Captures Jerusalem

4 In the ninth year of Zedekiah’s reign, on the tenth day of the tenth month, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon marched against Jerusalem with his whole army. They camped outside the city and built siege works all around it. 5 The city was under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah. 6 By the ninth day of the fourth month, the famine in the city had become so severe that there was no food for the people of the land. 7 Then the city wall was broken through, and all the soldiers fled and left the city at night through the gate between the two walls near the king’s garden. They fled toward the Arabah, though the Chaldeans had surrounded the city. 8 But the Chaldean army pursued the king and caught up with Zedekiah in the plains of Jericho. All his army scattered from him. 9 They captured the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah in the land of Hamath, where he passed sentence on him. 10 There at Riblah, the king of Babylon slaughtered Zedekiah’s sons before his eyes, and he also slaughtered all the officials of Judah. 11 Then he put out Zedekiah’s eyes, bound him with bronze shackles, and took him to Babylon, where he put him in prison until the day of his death.

The Temple and City Are Destroyed

12 On the tenth day of the fifth month, in the nineteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar’s reign, Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard, who served in the presence of the king of Babylon, entered Jerusalem. 13 He burned down the house of the the LORD, the royal palace, and all the houses of Jerusalem; every important building he burned with fire. 14 The whole Chaldean army under the commander of the guard broke down all the walls around Jerusalem. 15 Then Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard carried into exile some of the poorest people, along with the rest of the people who remained in the city, the deserters who had gone over to the king of Babylon, and the rest of the skilled workers. 16 But Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard left behind some of the poorest of the land to work the vineyards and fields.

17 The Chaldeans also broke up the bronze pillars, the stands, and the bronze Sea in the house of the the LORD, and they carried all the bronze to Babylon. 18 They also took away the pots, shovels, wick trimmers, sprinkling bowls, dishes, and all the other bronze items used in the temple service. 19 The commander of the guard took away the basins, incense burners, sprinkling bowls, pots, lampstands, pans, and bowls—whatever was made of pure gold or silver. 20 The two pillars, the Sea, and the twelve bronze bulls under the Sea, and the stands that King Solomon had made for the house of the the LORD—all this bronze was too heavy to weigh. 21 Each pillar was eighteen cubits high and twelve cubits around; it was hollow and four fingers thick. 22 The bronze top on one pillar was five cubits high, with a network of bronze pomegranates all around. The second pillar was like the first, with pomegranates. 23 There were ninety-six pomegranates on the sides; in all, one hundred pomegranates were above the network all around.

24 The commander of the guard took Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the priest of the second rank, and the three doorkeepers. 25 From the city he also took the officer who had been in charge of the soldiers, seven trusted royal advisers, the secretary of the commander of the army who gathered the people of the land, and sixty men of the people of the land who were found in the city. 26 Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah. 27 There at Riblah in the land of Hamath, the king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death. So Judah was carried into exile away from its land.

Judah Carried into Exile and Jehoiachin Released

28 These are the people Nebuchadnezzar carried into exile: in the seventh year, 3,023 Jews; 29 in the eighteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar, 832 people from Jerusalem; 30 in the twenty-third year of Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard carried into exile 745 Jews. In all, 4,600 people were taken captive.

31 On the twenty-fifth day of the twelfth month, in the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, in the first year of his reign, Evil-merodach king of Babylon released Jehoiachin king of Judah and freed him from prison. 32 He spoke kindly to him and set his throne above the thrones of the kings who were with him in Babylon. 33 So Jehoiachin changed out of his prison clothes, and he ate bread regularly in the king’s presence for the rest of his life. 34 And from the king, a daily portion was given to him for the rest of his life, until the day of his death.

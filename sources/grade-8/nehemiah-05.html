The People Complain About Hard Times

1 About that time, the people and their wives raised a loud complaint against their fellow Jews.

2 Some said, "We have many sons and daughters. We need grain so we can eat and stay alive."

3 Others said, "We are pledging our fields, vineyards, and homes to get grain during the famine."

4 Still others said, "We have borrowed money to pay the king's tax on our fields and vineyards."

5 Then some said, "We are from the same family as our fellow Jews, and our children are just as good as theirs. Yet we are forcing our sons and daughters into slavery. Some of our daughters are already slaves, but we can do nothing because our fields and vineyards belong to others."

Nehemiah Confronts the Nobles

6 When I heard their outcry and these complaints, I became very angry. 7 After thinking it over, I rebuked the nobles and officials. I said to them, "You are charging interest to your own brothers!" Then I called a large meeting against them.

8 I said, "We have done our best to buy back our Jewish brothers who were sold to other nations. Now you are selling your own brothers, and they would have to be bought back by us." They were silent because they had nothing to say.

9 So I said, "What you are doing is wrong. Shouldn't you live in fear of our God so our enemies from other nations won't mock us? 10 I and my brothers and my servants are also lending them money and grain. Please, let's stop charging this interest. 11 Please restore to them this very day their fields, vineyards, olive groves, and houses, along with the interest on the money, grain, new wine, and oil that you have been taking from them."

The Nobles Promise to Make Things Right

12 "We will restore it," they replied, "and we won't take anything more from them. We will do as you say." Then I summoned the priests and made them swear to do as they had promised.

13 I also shook out the folds of my robe and said, "May God shake out of his house and property every man who doesn't keep this promise. May he be shaken out and left with nothing!"

Then the whole assembly said, "Amen," and praised the the LORD. And the people did as they had promised.

Nehemiah's Example as Governor

14 Also, from the day King Artaxerxes appointed me governor of Judah, from his twentieth year to his thirty-second year—twelve years in all—neither I nor my brothers ate the food allowed for the governor.

15 The earlier governors before me had made life hard for the people. They took bread and wine from them, plus forty shekels of silver. Their servants also ruled over the people. But I didn't do that, because I feared God.

16 Instead, I devoted myself to the work on this wall, and my servants were gathered there to work. We didn't buy any land.

17 There were 150 Jews and officials at my table, along with visitors from the surrounding nations.

18 Each day one ox, six choice sheep, and some birds were prepared for me. Every ten days there was plenty of wine of all kinds. Yet I didn't ask for the food allowed for the governor, because the people were already carrying a heavy load.

19 Remember me kindly, my God, for all I have done for this people.

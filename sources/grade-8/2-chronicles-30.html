Hezekiah Invites All Israel to Passover

1 Hezekiah sent word to all Israel and Judah. He also wrote letters to Ephraim and Manasseh, asking them to come to the LORD's house in Jerusalem and celebrate Passover to the LORD, the God of Israel. 2 The king, his officials, and the whole group in Jerusalem decided to celebrate the Passover in the second month. 3 They could not celebrate it at the usual time because the priests had not made themselves holy yet, and the people had not gathered in Jerusalem. 4 This plan pleased the king and all the group. 5 They decided to send a message through all Israel, from Beersheba to Dan, telling people to come to Jerusalem to celebrate Passover to the LORD, the God of Israel. Most of the people had not kept it the way the law said.

6 Following the king's command, runners went through all Israel and Judah with letters from the king and his leaders. The letters said, "People of Israel, come back to the LORD, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel. Then He will come back to the few of you who escaped from the kings of Assyria.

7 "Do not be like your fathers and brothers. They were unfaithful to the LORD, the God of their fathers, and He let them be ruined, as you can see.

8 "Now do not be stubborn like your fathers. Give yourselves to the LORD and come to His holy place, which He set apart forever. Serve the LORD your God, so His anger will turn away from you.

9 "If you come back to the LORD, your brothers and children will be treated with kindness by the people who took them away. They will come back to this land, because the LORD your God is kind and merciful. He will not turn away from you if you come back to Him."

People Respond to the Invitation

10 The runners went from town to town in Ephraim and Manasseh, all the way to Zebulun. The people laughed at them and made fun of them. 11 But some men from Asher, Manasseh, and Zebulun humbled themselves and came to Jerusalem. 12 God's hand was also on Judah. He gave them one heart to obey the king and his leaders, as the LORD had said.

The Passover Is Celebrated

13 A very large crowd came to Jerusalem in the second month to celebrate the Feast of Unleavened Bread. It was a huge group. 14 They stood up and removed the altars in Jerusalem. They also took away all the incense altars and threw them into the Kidron Valley. 15 On the fourteenth day of the second month, they killed the Passover lambs. The priests and Levites felt ashamed, so they made themselves holy. Then they brought burnt offerings to the LORD's house. 16 They stood where they were supposed to stand, following the Law of Moses, the man of God. The priests sprinkled the blood that the Levites handed to them. 17 Many in the crowd had not made themselves holy, so the Levites killed the Passover lambs for everyone who was not clean. This made the lambs holy for the LORD.

18 Many people had come from Ephraim, Manasseh, Issachar, and Zebulun. They had not cleaned themselves, but they ate the Passover in a way not allowed by the law. So Hezekiah prayed for them: "May the LORD, who is good, forgive 19 everyone who has set his heart on seeking God, the LORD, the God of his fathers, even if he is not clean in the way the holy place requires." 20 The LORD heard Hezekiah and healed the people.

Joy, Offerings, and Blessing

21 The Israelites in Jerusalem celebrated the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days with great joy. The Levites and priests praised the LORD every day with loud music, using the LORD's instruments. 22 Hezekiah spoke kindly to all the Levites who served the LORD with skill. They ate the festival food for seven days, offering peace offerings and thanking the LORD, the God of their fathers. 23 The whole group decided to celebrate for seven more days, so they celebrated seven more days with joy. 24 King Hezekiah of Judah gave the group 1,000 bulls and 7,000 sheep. The leaders gave 1,000 bulls and 10,000 sheep. The priests made themselves holy in large numbers. 25 All the people of Judah rejoiced, along with the priests, the Levites, all the people who came from Israel, the foreigners who came from Israel, and the people living in Judah. 26 There was great joy in Jerusalem. Nothing like it had happened in Jerusalem since the days of Solomon son of David, king of Israel. 27 Then the priests and Levites stood up and blessed the people. Their voice was heard, and their prayer reached God's holy home in heaven.

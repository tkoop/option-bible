David and Jonathan Become Friends

1 After David finished speaking to Saul, Jonathan and David became close friends. Jonathan loved David as much as he loved himself. 2 That day Saul kept David with him and did not let him go back to his father's house. 3 Jonathan made a special agreement with David because he loved him as much as himself. 4 Jonathan took off the robe he was wearing and gave it to David, along with his armor, sword, bow, and belt.

David Succeeds and Saul Becomes Jealous

5 David went out wherever Saul sent him and did well. Saul put him in charge of the soldiers. This pleased all the people and Saul's officers. 6 When they came back, as David returned from killing the Philistine, women came out from all the cities of Israel to meet King Saul. They sang and danced with joy, playing tambourines and lyres.

7 The women sang as they played, "Saul has killed thousands, but David has killed tens of thousands."

8 Saul became very angry, and this song upset him. He said, "They gave David tens of thousands, but they gave me only thousands. What is left for him but the kingdom?" 9 From that day on, Saul watched David with suspicion.

10 The next day an evil spirit from God came powerfully on Saul. He raved inside the house while David played the harp, as he did each day. Saul held a spear in his hand. 11 Saul threw the spear and said, "I will pin David to the wall." But David dodged it twice.

12 Saul was afraid of David because the LORD was with David but had left Saul. 13 Saul moved David away from him and made him commander of a thousand. David went out and came in before the people. 14 David was successful in everything he did, and the LORD was with him. 15 When Saul saw how successful David was, he was afraid of him. 16 All Israel and Judah loved David because he led them.

Saul Tries to Trap David

17 Saul said to David, "Here is my older daughter Merab. I will give her to you as your wife. Only be brave for me and fight the LORD's battles." Saul thought, "I won't harm him myself. Let the Philistines do it."

18 David said to Saul, "Who am I, and what is my family in Israel, that I should become the king's son-in-law?"

19 When the time came to give Merab to David, she was given instead to Adriel the Meholathite. 20 Michal, Saul's daughter, loved David. When people told Saul, he was pleased.

21 Saul said, "I will give her to him so she becomes a trap for him, and the Philistines will turn against him." Saul said to David a second time, "Today you will become my son-in-law."

22 Saul ordered his servants, "Speak to David privately and say, 'The king is pleased with you, and all his servants love you. Now become the king's son-in-law.'"

23 The servants told David these words. David said, "Do you think it is easy to become the king's son-in-law? I am a poor man and not very important." 24 The servants reported to Saul what David had said.

25 Saul said, "Tell David, 'The king does not want a bride price. He wants one hundred Philistine foreskins to get revenge on his enemies.'" Saul planned to make David fall by the Philistines' hands.

26 The servants told David, and he was pleased to become the king's son-in-law. Before the time was up, 27 David and his men went out and killed two hundred Philistines. He brought their foreskins and gave the full number to the king so he could become the king's son-in-law. Then Saul gave him his daughter Michal as his wife.

28 Saul saw and knew that the LORD was with David. Michal loved David. 29 Saul became even more afraid of David. Saul was David's enemy from then on. 30 Whenever Philistine leaders came out to fight, David had more success than all of Saul's servants. So his name became very respected.

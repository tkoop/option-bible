The Earthly Tabernacle and Its Worship

1 The first covenant had rules for worship and an earthly holy place. 2 A tabernacle was set up. In its first room were the lampstand, the table, and the bread set out for God. This room was called the Holy Place. 3 Behind the second curtain was a room called the Most Holy Place. 4 It held the gold incense altar and the ark of the covenant, covered with gold all around. Inside the ark were the gold jar of manna, Aaron's staff that had budded, and the stone tablets of the covenant. 5 Above the ark were the cherubim of glory, spreading their wings over the mercy seat. We won't go into all these details now.

6 Once everything was arranged this way, the priests went into the first room again and again to do their work. 7 But only the high priest went into the inner room, and only once a year. He never went without blood, which he offered for himself and for the sins the people had done without knowing. 8 The Holy Spirit was showing that the way into the Most Holy Place had not yet been opened while the first tabernacle was still in use. 9 This picture points to the present time. It shows that the gifts and sacrifices being offered could not make the worshiper's conscience fully clean. 10 They had to do with food, drink, and different washings. These were rules for the body, and they lasted until the time of the new order.

Christ's Greater Sacrifice

11 But Christ came as High Priest of the good things that are now here. He went through the greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made by human hands and not part of this created world. 12 He did not enter by the blood of goats and calves. Instead, He entered the Most Holy Place once for all by His own blood, and He won eternal redemption. 13 The blood of goats and bulls, and the ashes of a young cow sprinkled on people who were unclean, made them clean on the outside. 14 How much more will the blood of Christ make us clean! Through the eternal Spirit, He offered Himself without fault to God. His blood cleans our consciences from dead works so we can serve the living God.

15 Because of this, Christ is the go-between for a new covenant. Those who are called can receive the promised eternal inheritance. This happened because His death set them free from the sins done under the first covenant. 16 Where there is a covenant, the death of the one who made it must be shown. 17 A covenant is valid only after someone has died. It has no power while the person who made it is still alive. 18 That is why even the first covenant was not started without blood.

The Blood of the Covenant

19 After Moses had told all the people every command of the law, he took the blood of calves and goats, along with water, red wool, and hyssop. He sprinkled the scroll and all the people. 20 He said, "This is the blood of the covenant, which God has commanded you to keep." 21 In the same way, he sprinkled the tabernacle and all the tools used in worship. 22 The law says that almost everything is made clean with blood. Without blood being poured out, there is no forgiveness.

23 So the copies of the heavenly things had to be purified with these sacrifices. But the heavenly things themselves need better sacrifices than these. 24 Christ did not enter a holy place made by human hands, which was only a copy of the true one. He entered heaven itself to stand for us before God's face. 25 He did not enter heaven to offer Himself again and again, the way the high priest enters the Most Holy Place every year with blood that is not his own. 26 If that were true, Christ would have had to suffer many times since the world was made. But now He has appeared once for all at the end of the ages to remove sin by sacrificing Himself.

Christ Will Come Again

27 People are meant to die once, and after that comes judgment. 28 In the same way, Christ was offered once to take away the sins of many. He will appear a second time, not to deal with sin, but to bring salvation to those who are waiting for Him.

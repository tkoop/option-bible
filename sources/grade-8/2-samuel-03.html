David Grows Stronger While Saul’s House Weakens

1 The war between Saul’s house and David’s house lasted a long time. David kept growing stronger, while Saul’s house kept growing weaker.

2 David had sons born to him in Hebron. His firstborn was Amnon, by Ahinoam of Jezreel. 3 His second son was Chileab, by Abigail, Nabal’s widow from Carmel. His third was Absalom, son of Maacah, daughter of Talmai, king of Geshur. 4 His fourth was Adonijah, son of Haggith. His fifth was Shephatiah, son of Abital. 5 His sixth was Ithream, by Eglah, David’s wife. These sons were born to David in Hebron.

Abner Breaks with Ish-bosheth

6 While the war between Saul’s house and David’s house continued, Abner was strengthening Saul’s house.

7 Saul had a concubine named Rizpah, daughter of Aiah. Ish-bosheth said to Abner, “Why did you go in to my father’s concubine?”

8 Abner was very angry at Ish-bosheth’s words and said, “Am I some worthless dog from Judah? I have shown kindness to the house of your father Saul, to his brothers and friends, and I have not handed you over to David. Yet today you charge me with a wrong about this woman! 9 May God deal with Abner, be it ever so severely, if I do not do for David what the LORD has sworn to him: 10 to move the kingdom from Saul’s house and set up David’s throne over Israel and Judah, from Dan to Beersheba.”

11 Ish-bosheth could not answer Abner a word, because he was afraid of him.

David and Abner Make Peace

12 Abner sent messengers to David on his behalf, saying, “Whose land is this? Make your covenant with me, and I will help you bring all Israel to you.”

13 David said, “Good! I will make a covenant with you. But I ask one thing of you: Do not come to see me unless you bring Michal, Saul’s daughter, with you.”

14 David sent messengers to Ish-bosheth son of Saul, saying, “Give me my wife Michal. I paid for her with a hundred Philistine foreskins.” 15 So Ish-bosheth sent and took her from her husband Paltiel son of Laish.

16 Her husband Paltiel followed her, weeping, all the way to Bahurim. Then Abner said to him, “Go back!” and he went back.

17 Abner spoke with the elders of Israel and said, “For some time now you have wanted David to be king over you. 18 Now do it! For the LORD has said about David, ‘By My servant David I will save My people Israel from the Philistines and from all their enemies.’” 19 Abner also spoke to the people of Benjamin. Then he went to Hebron to tell David everything that Israel and all Benjamin thought was good.

20 Abner came to David at Hebron with twenty men, and David made a feast for Abner and his men. 21 Abner said to David, “Let me go and gather all Israel to my lord the king, so they can make a covenant with you, and you can rule over all your heart desires.” Then David sent Abner away in peace.

Joab Kills Abner and David Mourns

22 Just then Joab and David’s men came back from a raid, bringing much spoil with them. Abner was not with David in Hebron, because David had sent him away in peace. 23 When Joab and all his men arrived, he was told, “Abner son of Ner came to the king, and the king sent him away in peace.”

24 Joab went to the king and said, “What have you done? Abner came to you. Why did you send him away? Now he is gone. 25 You know Abner son of Ner. He came to deceive you and learn your movements and everything you are doing.”

26 Joab left David and sent messengers after Abner. They brought him back from the well at Sirah, but David did not know about it. 27 When Abner returned to Hebron, Joab took him aside at the gate to speak with him privately. There he stabbed Abner in the stomach, and Abner died for the blood of Asahel, Joab’s brother.

28 When David heard about it, he said, “I and my kingdom are forever innocent before the LORD in the matter of Abner son of Ner’s blood. 29 May the guilt fall on Joab’s head and on all his father’s house. May Joab’s house never be without someone who has a discharge, or a leper, or someone who leans on a staff, or someone who dies by the sword, or someone who has no bread.”

30 Joab and his brother Abishai killed Abner because he had killed their brother Asahel in the battle at Gibeon.

31 David said to Joab and all the people with him, “Tear your clothes, put on sackcloth, and mourn for Abner.” Then King David followed the coffin.

32 They buried Abner in Hebron. The king wept aloud at Abner’s tomb, and all the people wept.

33 The king sang a lament for Abner, saying, “Should Abner die like a fool dies? 34 Your hands were not bound, and your feet were not in chains. You fell as one falls before wicked men.” And all the people wept over him again.

35 All the people came to make David eat bread while it was still day, but David swore, “May God deal with me, be it ever so severely, if I taste bread or anything else before the sun goes down.”

36 All the people noticed, and it pleased them. Everything the king did pleased all the people. 37 So all Israel knew that day that the king had no part in killing Abner son of Ner.

38 The king said to his servants, “Do you not know that a prince and a great man has fallen today in Israel? 39 I am weak today, though I am the anointed king. These men, the sons of Zeruiah, are too hard for me. May the LORD repay the evildoer according to his wickedness.”

The LORD Speaks to Moses and Aaron

1 The LORD said to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt, 2 “This month is to be the first month of the year for you. 3 Tell the whole community of Israel that on the tenth day of this month, each man must take a lamb for his family—one lamb for each household. 4 If a household is too small for a whole lamb, he and his nearest neighbor may share one, counting the people by how much each one can eat. 5 The lamb must be a year-old male without defect. You may take it from the sheep or the goats. 6 Keep it until the fourteenth day of the month. Then the whole community of Israel must kill their lambs at twilight. 7 Take some of the blood and put it on the two doorposts and the top of the doorframe of the houses where you eat the lamb.

8 “That night you must eat the meat roasted over fire, along with unleavened bread and bitter herbs. 9 Do not eat it raw or boiled in water. Roast it over fire, with its head, legs, and inner parts. 10 Do not leave any of it until morning. Burn whatever is left in the morning. 11 This is how you must eat it: with your belt fastened, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand. Eat it quickly. It is the LORD’s Passover. 12 On that night I will pass through Egypt and strike every firstborn—both people and animals. I will bring judgment on all the gods of Egypt. I am the LORD. 13 The blood will be a sign for you on the houses where you are. When I see the blood, I will pass over you. No deadly plague will touch you when I strike Egypt.

14 “This day will be a memorial for you. You must celebrate it as a feast to the LORD. It is a law for you and your descendants forever. 15 For seven days you must eat unleavened bread. On the first day, remove all yeast from your houses. Anyone who eats anything with yeast from the first day through the seventh day must be cut off from Israel. 16 On the first day you must hold a holy meeting, and on the seventh day you must hold another holy meeting. No work may be done on those days, except preparing food for everyone. 17 Keep the Feast of Unleavened Bread, because on this very day I brought your armies out of Egypt. Keep this day as a law for all your generations forever. 18 In the first month, from the evening of the fourteenth day until the evening of the twenty-first day, you must eat unleavened bread. 19 For seven days no yeast may be found in your houses. Anyone who eats anything made with yeast, whether a foreigner or a native of the land, must be cut off from the community of Israel. 20 Eat nothing made with yeast. In all your homes, eat unleavened bread.”

Moses Tells the Elders What to Do

21 Moses called all the elders of Israel and said to them, “Draw out and take lambs for your families, and slaughter the Passover lamb. 22 Take a bunch of hyssop, dip it in the blood in the basin, and put some of the blood on the top of the doorframe and on both doorposts. None of you may go outside the door of his house until morning. 23 For the LORD will pass through to strike Egypt. When He sees the blood on the top of the doorframe and on both doorposts, the LORD will pass over that door. He will not let the destroyer enter your houses to strike you. 24 You must keep this command as a law for you and your sons forever.

25 “When you enter the land that the LORD will give you, just as He promised, you must keep this service. 26 When your children ask you, ‘What does this service mean to you?’ 27 you must say, ‘It is the Passover sacrifice to the LORD. He passed over the houses of the Israelites in Egypt when He struck Egypt and spared our houses.’” The people bowed down and worshiped.

28 The Israelites did just as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron.

The LORD Strikes Egypt’s Firstborn

29 At midnight the LORD struck every firstborn in Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh on his throne to the firstborn of the prisoner in the dungeon, and all the firstborn of the livestock. 30 Pharaoh and all his officials and all the Egyptians got up during the night. There was a loud cry in Egypt, because there was no house without someone dead.

31 During the night Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron and said, “Get up and leave my people, both you and the Israelites. Go and worship the LORD, as you have said. 32 Take your flocks and herds, as you said, and go. Bless me too.”

33 The Egyptians urged the people to leave the country quickly, because they said, “We will all die!” 34 So the people took their dough before it was leavened, with their kneading bowls wrapped in their clothes on their shoulders. 35 The Israelites did what Moses had said and asked the Egyptians for silver and gold jewelry and for clothing. 36 The LORD had given the people favor in the eyes of the Egyptians, so they gave them what they asked for. In this way they plundered Egypt.

Israel Leaves Egypt

37 The Israelites traveled from Rameses to Succoth. There were about six hundred thousand men on foot, not counting children. 38 A mixed crowd also went up with them, along with very large flocks and herds. 39 They baked the dough they had brought from Egypt into unleavened cakes, because it had not risen. They had been driven out of Egypt and could not wait, and they had not prepared any other food.

40 The Israelites had lived in Egypt for four hundred thirty years. 41 At the end of four hundred thirty years, on that very day, all the LORD’s armies left Egypt. 42 It was a night of watching for the LORD, to bring them out of Egypt. This same night is a night of watching for the LORD for all the Israelites throughout their generations.

Rules for the Passover

43 The LORD said to Moses and Aaron, “This is the rule for the Passover: no foreigner may eat it. 44 Any slave you have bought may eat it after you circumcise him. 45 A temporary resident or hired worker may not eat it. 46 It must be eaten in one house. You must not take any of the meat outside the house, and you must not break any of its bones.

47 “The whole community of Israel must keep this. 48 If a foreigner living among you wants to celebrate the LORD’s Passover, all his males must be circumcised. Then he may take part and be like a native of the land. But no uncircumcised person may eat it. 49 The same law applies to the native and to the foreigner living among you.” 50 All the Israelites did just as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron. 51 On that very day the LORD brought the Israelites out of Egypt by their armies.

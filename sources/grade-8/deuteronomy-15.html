The Year of Release

1 At the end of every seven years, you must cancel debts. 2 This is how the release works: Every creditor must release what he has lent to his neighbor. He must not press his neighbor or brother for payment, because the the LORD's release has been announced. 3 You may collect from a foreigner, but your hand must release what belongs to your brother.

4 There should be no poor among you, because the the LORD your God will richly bless you in the land the the LORD your God is giving you to possess. 5 If you carefully obey the the LORD your God and are careful to follow all these commands I am giving you today, 6 then the the LORD your God will bless you, as He promised. You will lend to many nations, but you will not borrow. You will rule over many nations, but they will not rule over you.

Helping the Poor

7 If there is a poor person among you—one of your brothers—in any of your towns in the land the the LORD your God is giving you, you must not harden your heart or close your hand against your poor brother. 8 Instead, you must open your hand wide to him and lend him enough to meet his need.

9 Be careful not to think this evil thought: "The seventh year, the year of release, is near," so that you are stingy toward your poor brother and give him nothing. Then he may cry out to the the LORD against you, and you will be guilty of sin. 10 You must surely give to him, and your heart must not be sad when you give, because the the LORD your God will bless you in all your work and in everything you do. 11 There will always be poor people in the land. That is why I command you to open your hand to your brother, to the needy and the poor in your land.

Hebrew Servants

12 If a Hebrew man or woman is sold to you and serves you six years, in the seventh year you must let him go free. 13 When you send him away free, do not send him away empty-handed.

14 Give him generously from your flock, your threshing floor, and your winepress. Give to him as the the LORD your God has blessed you. 15 Remember that you were a slave in Egypt and the the LORD your God redeemed you. That is why I give you this command today.

16 But if he says to you, "I do not want to leave you," because he loves you and your household and is well off with you, 17 then you must take an awl and push it through his ear into the door, and he will be your servant for life. Do the same for your female servant. 18 Do not be upset when you send him away free, because for six years he has served you twice as much as a hired worker would. The the LORD your God will bless you in all you do.

Firstborn Animals

19 Set apart to the the LORD your God every firstborn male from your herds and flocks. Do not work the firstborn of your ox, and do not shear the firstborn of your sheep. 20 You and your household must eat it each year before the the LORD your God in the place He chooses.

21 But if it has a defect—lame or blind or any serious flaw—do not sacrifice it to the the LORD your God. 22 You may eat it in your towns. The unclean and the clean may eat it alike, like gazelle or deer. 23 But do not eat its blood; pour it out on the ground like water.

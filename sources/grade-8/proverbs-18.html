Fools, Pride, and Words

1 A person who keeps to himself chases his own desires and fights against wise advice. 2 A fool doesn't care about understanding; he only wants to share his own opinions. 3 A wicked person brings contempt, and shame and disgrace follow him. 4 A person's words are like deep water, and wisdom flows from him like a bubbling stream.

5 It's not right to favor a wicked person or to deny justice to a good person. 6 A fool's words start fights, and his mouth asks for a beating. 7 A fool's mouth brings his ruin, and his words trap his life. 8 A gossip's words taste like a tasty treat, but they sink deep into a person's heart. 9 A lazy worker is no better than someone who destroys.

The LORD and True Security

10 The name of the LORD is a strong tower; righteous people run to it and are safe. 11 A rich person's money is like a strong city to him; he imagines it protects him like a high wall. 12 Pride leads to ruin, but humility leads to honor.

13 Answering before listening is foolish and shameful. 14 A person's spirit can endure sickness, but a crushed spirit is too heavy to bear.

Knowledge, Speech, and Wealth

15 A smart person wants to learn, and a wise person listens to learn more. 16 A gift can open doors for a person and bring him before important people. 17 The first person to tell his side sounds right until someone else questions him. 18 Drawing lots can end an argument and settle things between strong opponents.

19 An offended brother is harder to win over than a walled city, and arguments are like strong castle bars. 20 A person's words can fill his stomach; what he says can bring satisfaction. 21 The tongue has power over life and death; those who love to talk will live with the results.

Wives, Wealth, and Friendship

22 A man who finds a wife finds something good and receives favor from the LORD. 23 A poor person begs for mercy, but a rich person answers harshly. 24 A person with many friends may still be ruined, but a true friend sticks closer than a brother.

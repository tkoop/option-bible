The Queen of Sheba Visits Solomon

1 The queen of Sheba heard how famous Solomon was. She came to Jerusalem to test him with hard questions. She brought a very large group with her. Camels carried spices, gold, and precious stones. She met Solomon and told him everything on her mind. 2 Solomon answered every question she asked. Nothing was too hard for him to explain. 3 The queen saw Solomon's wisdom. She also saw the house he built. 4 She saw the food on his table. She saw his servants and how they stood. She saw their clothes. She saw his drink servers and their clothes. She saw the steps he took up to the house of the the LORD. She was so amazed she could hardly breathe.

5 She said to the king, "What I heard in my country about your words and your wisdom is true. 6 I did not believe it until I came and saw it with my own eyes. People did not tell me even half of it. You are even wiser than the report I heard. 7 How happy your people must be! How happy these servants of yours must be, who stand before you all the time and hear your wisdom! 8 Praise be to the the LORD your God, who was pleased with you and put you on His throne as king for the the LORD your God. Because the the LORD loved Israel forever, He made you king over them. He made you king to do what is fair and right."

The Queen Gives Gifts to Solomon

9 She gave the king 120 talents of gold. She also gave him many spices and precious stones. No spice was ever like the spice the queen of Sheba gave King Solomon. 10 The servants of Hiram and Solomon brought gold from Ophir. They also brought valuable wood. 11 The king used that wood to make steps for the house of the the LORD and for his own house. He also made harps and lyres for the singers. Nothing like them had been seen before in Judah. 12 King Solomon gave the queen everything she wanted and asked for. This was besides what she had brought him. Then she turned and went back to her country with her servants.

Solomon's Great Wealth

13 Solomon received 666 talents of gold in one year. 14 This was besides the gold and silver brought by traders, merchants, all the kings of Arabia, and the governors of the land. 15 King Solomon made 200 large shields of hammered gold. Each shield used 600 pieces of gold. 16 He made 300 smaller shields of hammered gold. Each used 300 pieces of gold. The king put them in the building called the Forest of Lebanon. 17 The king made a great throne of ivory. He covered it with pure gold. 18 The throne had six steps. A footrest of gold was attached to it. There were armrests on each side. Two lions stood beside the armrests. 19 Twelve lions stood on the six steps, one on each side. No other kingdom had a throne like it. 20 All of Solomon's drinking cups were gold. All the dishes in the building called the Forest of Lebanon were pure gold. In Solomon's time, silver was not worth much. 21 The king had ships that went to Tarshish with Hiram's servants. Once every three years the ships came back. They brought gold, silver, ivory, monkeys, and peacocks.

22 Solomon was greater than all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom. 23 All the kings of the earth came to see Solomon. They wanted to hear the wisdom God had put in his heart. 24 Every year each visitor brought gifts: silver and gold items, clothes, weapons, spices, horses, and mules. 25 Solomon had 4,000 stalls for horses and chariots. He had 12,000 horsemen. He kept some in chariot cities and some with him in Jerusalem. 26 He ruled over all the kings from the river to the land of the Philistines, and to the border of Egypt. 27 The king made silver as common as stones in Jerusalem. He made cedar wood as common as fig trees in the lowlands. 28 Horses were brought to Solomon from Egypt and from every land.

The End of Solomon's Rule

29 The rest of what Solomon did, from first to last, is written in the records of Nathan the prophet. It is also in the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite and in the visions of Iddo the prophet about Jeroboam son of Nebat. 30 Solomon ruled in Jerusalem over all Israel for 40 years. 31 Then Solomon died and was buried in the city of his father David. His son Rehoboam became king after him.

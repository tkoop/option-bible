Paul Lands on Malta

1 After we were saved, we knew the island was called Malta. 2 The local people were very kind to us. They built a fire and welcomed all of us, because it was raining and cold. 3 Paul picked up a bundle of sticks and put them on the fire. A snake came out because of the heat and bit his hand. 4 When the local people saw the snake hanging from his hand, they said to each other, "This man must be a killer. He got out of the sea, but justice did not let him live." 5 But Paul shook the snake into the fire. He was not hurt. 6 They thought he would swell up or fall down dead. They waited and saw nothing bad happen to him. Then they changed their minds and said he was a god.

Paul Heals People on Malta

7 Near that place were fields owned by a man named Publius. He was the leader of the island. He welcomed us and took care of us for three days. 8 Publius's father was sick in bed with fever and stomach trouble. Paul went in, prayed, and put his hands on him. He healed him. 9 After that, the other sick people on the island came to Paul and were healed. 10 They honored us in many ways. When we were ready to sail, they gave us what we needed.

Paul Sails to Rome

11 After three months, we sailed on a ship from Alexandria. It had stayed on the island for the winter. It had a picture of the twin gods on it. 12 We landed at Syracuse and stayed there three days. 13 From there we sailed along and came to Rhegium. The next day a south wind blew, and the second day we came to Puteoli. 14 There we found some Christian friends. They asked us to stay with them for seven days. After that we went to Rome. 15 The Christians in Rome heard about us. They came as far as the Forum of Appius and the Three Taverns to meet us. When Paul saw them, he thanked God and felt brave. 16 When we got to Rome, Paul was allowed to live by himself, with a soldier to guard him.

Paul Talks to Jewish Leaders

17 Three days later, Paul called the Jewish leaders together. When they came, he said, "Brothers, I have done nothing against our people or our fathers' ways. But I was arrested in Jerusalem and handed over to the Romans. 18 The Romans asked me questions. They wanted to let me go, because I had done nothing that deserved death. 19 But the Jewish leaders spoke against me. I had to ask to be tried by Caesar. I did not do this to accuse my own people. 20 That is why I asked to see you and talk with you. I am in chains because of the hope of Israel."

The Leaders Visit Paul Again

21 They said to him, "We have not received any letter about you from Judea. No Christian friend has come and said anything bad about you. 22 But we want to hear from you what you think. We know that everywhere people speak against this group."

23 They set a day. Many people came to Paul where he was staying. From morning until evening he told them about God's kingdom. He tried to convince them about Jesus, using the Law of Moses and the Prophets. 24 Some believed what he said. Others did not.

Paul Quotes Isaiah

25 Paul said one thing: "The Holy Spirit spoke well to your fathers through Isaiah the prophet. 26 Isaiah said, 'Go to this people and say, You will hear but not understand. You will see but not know what you see. 27 For the hearts of these people are hard. They hear with difficulty. They have closed their eyes. This is so they will not see with their eyes, hear with their ears, understand with their hearts, turn to God, and be healed.' 28 So let it be known to you that this salvation from God was sent to the people who are not Jews, and they will listen."

Paul Stays in Rome

30 Paul stayed there two whole years in a house he rented. He welcomed everyone who came to him. 31 He preached God's kingdom with all boldness. No one stopped him. He taught about the Lord Jesus Christ.

Rules About Sin Offerings

1 If someone hears a curse and knows what happened, but does not tell what he saw or knew, he has sinned. 2 If someone touches something unclean, like a dead wild animal, a dead farm animal, or a dead small crawling thing, and does not know it, he becomes unclean and guilty. 3 If someone touches something unclean from a person and does not know it at first, but later knows, he is guilty. 4 If someone makes a promise without thinking, to do good or bad, and later remembers, he is guilty. 5 When he is guilty of one of these, he must say he did wrong.

6 He must bring his guilt offering to the LORD for the wrong he did. It must be a female lamb or a female goat from the flock. It is a sin offering. The priest will make him right with God for that sin.

7 If he cannot afford a lamb, he must bring two turtledoves or two young pigeons to the LORD for the wrong he did. One is for a sin offering. The other is for a burnt offering. 8 He must bring them to the priest. The priest will first offer the bird for the sin offering. He will break its neck, but not cut the head off all the way. 9 He will sprinkle some of the blood of the sin offering on the side of the altar. The rest of the blood will be poured out at the bottom of the altar. It is a sin offering.

10 He will prepare the second bird as a burnt offering, the way the law says. The priest will make him right with God for the sin he did, and he will be forgiven. 11 If he cannot afford two turtledoves or two young pigeons, he must bring one-tenth of a basket of fine flour for the sin he did. It is a sin offering. He must not put oil on it. He must not put sweet-smelling spice on it. 12 He must bring it to the priest. The priest will take a handful of it as a special part to remember God. He will burn it on the altar with the other gifts to the LORD. It is a sin offering. 13 The priest will make him right with God for the sin he did in any of these cases, and he will be forgiven. The rest of the flour will be for the priest, like a grain offering.

Rules About Guilt Offerings

14 The LORD spoke to Moses. He said, 15 "If someone sins by mistake with the holy things of the LORD, he must bring a guilt offering to the LORD. It must be a perfect male sheep from the flock. It must be worth the right amount of silver, using the holy weight. This is a guilt offering. 16 He must pay back the value of the holy thing he harmed. He must also add one-fifth more. He must give it to the priest. Then the priest will make him right with God with the ram guilt offering, and he will be forgiven. 17 If someone sins by doing something the LORD said not to do, even if he did not know it, he is still guilty. 18 He must bring a perfect male sheep from the flock to the priest as a guilt offering. The priest will make him right with God for the wrong he did without knowing, and he will be forgiven. 19 It is a guilt offering. He was truly guilty before the LORD."

Joash Becomes King

1 In the seventh year of Jehu, Joash became king. He ruled in Jerusalem for forty years. His mother’s name was Zibiah. She was from Beersheba. 2 Joash did what was right in the eyes of the LORD all the days Jehoiada the priest taught him. 3 But the high places were not removed. The people still offered sacrifices and burned incense there.

Joash Fixes the Temple

4 Joash said to the priests, “Collect all the money brought as holy gifts into the house of the LORD. This includes the money people paid to be counted, money promised to God, and any money people choose to bring to the the LORD’s house. 5 Each priest should take the money from the people he knows and use it to fix any damage found in the temple.”

6 But by the twenty-third year of King Joash’s rule, the priests still had not fixed the damage to the temple.

7 So King Joash called Jehoiada the priest and the other priests. He asked, “Why have you not fixed the damage to the temple? From now on, do not take money from the people you know. Give it for the repair of the temple.”

8 The priests agreed not to take money from the people or to fix the temple themselves. 9 Then Jehoiada the priest took a box. He made a hole in its lid. He put it beside the altar, on the right side as you enter the house of the LORD. The priests who guarded the door put all the money brought into the the LORD’s house into the box.

10 When they saw a lot of money in the box, the king’s writer and the high priest went up. They counted the money in the the LORD’s house. They put it in bags. 11 They gave the counted money to the men in charge of the work on the the LORD’s house. They paid the carpenters and builders who worked there. 12 They also paid stone workers and bought wood and cut stone to fix the damage. They paid for everything needed to make the house strong again.

13 But the money brought into the the LORD’s house was not used to make silver bowls, lamp trimmers, sprinkling bowls, trumpets, or any other gold or silver things for the house. 14 Instead, they paid the workers, who used it to repair the the LORD’s house. 15 Also, they did not ask the men who handled the money to show records. Those men were honest and faithful. 16 Money from guilt offerings and sin offerings was not brought into the the LORD’s house. It belonged to the priests.

Hazael Threatens Jerusalem

17 At that time, Hazael king of Aram went up and fought against Gath. He captured it. Then he decided to attack Jerusalem. 18 So King Joash of Judah took all the holy things his fathers had given: Jehoshaphat, Jehoram, and Ahaziah, kings of Judah. He also took his own holy things and all the gold in the storehouses of the the LORD’s house and the king’s palace. He sent them to Hazael king of Aram. Then Hazael left Jerusalem.

19 The rest of what Joash did is written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah.

Joash Dies

20 His servants made a plan against him. They killed Joash at Beth-millo, on the road that goes down to Silla. 21 His servants Jozabad son of Shimeath and Jehozabad son of Shomer struck him, and he died. They buried him with his fathers in the City of David. His son Amaziah became king after him.

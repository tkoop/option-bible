Israel Travels to the Desert of Sin

1 On the fifteenth day of the second month after they left Egypt, all the people of Israel traveled from Elim. They came to the Desert of Sin. It is between Elim and Sinai. 2 There in the desert, all the people of Israel complained against Moses and Aaron.

The People Complain

3 They said, "We wish the the LORD had killed us in Egypt! There we sat by pots of meat and ate all the bread we wanted. But you brought us out here to this desert to starve all of us to death!"

The LORD Promises Bread from Heaven

4 Then the the LORD said to Moses, "Look, I will make bread fall from the sky like rain for you. Each day the people will go out and gather enough for that day. This way I will test them. I will see if they follow My rules. 5 On the sixth day, they will get ready what they gather. It will be twice as much as they gather on other days."

Moses and Aaron Speak to the People

6 So Moses and Aaron said to all the Israelites, "Tonight you will know that the the LORD brought you out of Egypt. 7 In the morning you will see the glory of the the LORD, because He has heard your complaining against Him. Who are we, that you should complain against us?"

8 Moses also said, "When the the LORD gives you meat to eat in the evening and bread to fill you in the morning, it is because He has heard your complaining against Him. Who are we? Your grumblings are not against us but against the the LORD."

The People Come Near the LORD

9 Then Moses said to Aaron, "Tell all the people of Israel to come near to the the LORD, because He has heard your complaining."

10 When Aaron spoke to all the people, they looked toward the desert. There in a cloud, the glory of the the LORD appeared.

The LORD Sends Food

11 Then the the LORD spoke to Moses. 12 "I have heard the complaining of the Israelites. Tell them, 'In the evening you will eat meat, and in the morning you will be full of bread. Then you will know that I am the the LORD your God.'"

13 That evening, small birds called quail came and covered the camp. In the morning, dew was all around the camp. 14 When the dew dried up, thin flakes like frost were on the ground.

15 When the Israelites saw it, they asked each other, "What is it?" They did not know what it was. So Moses said to them, "It is the bread the the LORD has given you to eat."

Rules for Gathering the Bread

16 Moses said, "This is what the the LORD has commanded: 'Each of you gather as much as you need. You may take an omer for each person in your tent.'"

17 The Israelites did this. Some gathered more, and some gathered less. 18 When they measured it by the omer, the one who gathered much had no extra. The one who gathered little had no lack. Each person gathered as much as he needed.

19 Moses said to them, "No one may keep any of it until morning."

20 But they did not listen to Moses. Some people left part of it until morning. It became full of worms and began to smell bad. So Moses was angry with them.

21 Every morning each person gathered as much as he needed. When the sun got hot, it melted away.

The Sabbath Day

22 On the sixth day, they gathered twice as much food. That was two omers for each person. All the leaders of the people came and told Moses.

23 He said to them, "This is what the the LORD has said: 'Tomorrow is a day of rest. It is a holy Sabbath to the the LORD. Bake what you want to bake, and boil what you want to boil. Then save what is left and keep it until morning.'"

24 So they saved it until morning, as Moses had commanded. It did not smell bad, and there were no worms in it.

25 "Eat it today," Moses said, "because today is a Sabbath to the the LORD. Today you will not find it in the field. 26 For six days you may gather it. But on the seventh day, the Sabbath, there will be none."

27 Yet on the seventh day some of the people went out to gather. But they did not find any.

28 Then the the LORD said to Moses, "How long will you refuse to keep My commands and My teachings? 29 See, the the LORD has given you the Sabbath. That is why on the sixth day He gives you bread for two days. On the seventh day, everyone must stay where he is. No one may leave his place."

30 So the people rested on the seventh day.

Manna Kept for the Future

31 The people of Israel called the bread manna. It was white like coriander seed and tasted like thin cakes made with honey.

32 Then Moses said, "This is what the the LORD has commanded: 'Keep an omer of manna for the people who come after you. Then they can see the bread I fed you in the desert when I brought you out of Egypt.'"

33 So Moses said to Aaron, "Take a jar and put an omer of manna in it. Then place it before the the LORD to be kept for the people who come after you."

34 And Aaron placed it before the Testimony to be kept, just as the the LORD had commanded Moses.

Manna for Forty Years

35 The Israelites ate manna for forty years, until they came to a land where people lived. They ate manna until they came to the border of the land of Canaan.

36 An omer is a tenth of an ephah.

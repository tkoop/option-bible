Satan Makes David Count the People

1 Satan turned against Israel. He made David want to count the people of Israel. 2 David said to Joab and the leaders, "Go and count the people of Israel from Beersheba to Dan. Bring me the number so I will know how many there are."

3 Joab said, "May the LORD make His people a hundred times more than they are. But, my lord the king, are they not all your servants? Why do you want this? Why should Israel be blamed?"

4 But the king's word was stronger than Joab's. So Joab left and went all over Israel. Then he came back to Jerusalem. 5 Joab gave David the number of the people. There were 1,100,000 men in all Israel who could fight with a sword. Judah had 470,000 men who could fight with a sword. 6 But Joab did not count the people of Levi and Benjamin, because he hated what the king asked. 7 God was not pleased with this. He punished Israel.

David Asks God to Forgive Him

8 David said to God, "I have sinned very much by doing this. Please forgive me. I was very foolish."

David Chooses a Punishment

9 The LORD spoke to Gad, David's prophet. He said, 10 "Go and tell David, 'This is what the LORD says: I will give you three choices. Pick one, and I will do it to you.'"

11 So Gad came to David and said, "This is what the LORD says: Choose one. 12 You may choose three years of famine. Or you may choose three months of running from your enemies while their swords chase you. Or you may choose three days of the sword of the LORD and sickness in the land, with the angel of the LORD destroying all over Israel. Now think about what answer I should give to the One who sent me."

13 David said to Gad, "I am in great trouble. Let me fall into the LORD's hands, because His mercy is very great. Do not let me fall into human hands."

The Sickness Hits Israel

14 So the LORD sent a sickness on Israel, and 70,000 men died. 15 God sent an angel to destroy Jerusalem. But while he was destroying it, the LORD saw and stopped the trouble. He said to the angel, "That is enough. Stop now." The angel of the LORD was standing at the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite. 16 David looked up and saw the angel of the LORD standing between heaven and earth. The angel held a sword in his hand, ready to strike Jerusalem. David and the leaders wore rough sackcloth and fell face down.

17 David said to God, "Wasn't it I who told them to count the people? I have sinned and done wrong. These people are like sheep. What did they do? The LORD my God, let Your hand be against me and my family, not against Your people, so they will not be sick."

David Builds an Altar

18 The angel of the LORD told Gad to tell David to go up and build an altar to the LORD at Ornan's threshing floor. 19 So David went up, because Gad had spoken in the LORD's name. 20 Ornan was beating wheat. He turned and saw the angel. His four sons hid. 21 When David came to Ornan, Ornan saw him and came out of the threshing floor. He bowed down to David.

22 David said to Ornan, "Let me have this threshing floor. I want to build an altar to the LORD here. Sell it to me for the full price, so the sickness will stop for the people."

23 Ornan said, "Take it, my lord the king. Do what seems good to you. Look, I give you the oxen for burnt offerings, the threshing sledges for wood, and the wheat for the grain offering. I give it all."

24 But King David said, "No. I will buy it for the full price. I will not take what is yours to give to the LORD. I will not offer a burnt offering that costs me nothing." 25 So David gave Ornan 600 shekels of gold for the place. 26 David built an altar to the LORD there. He offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. He called on the LORD, and the LORD answered him with fire from heaven on the altar. 27 Then the LORD told the angel to put his sword back in its sheath.

28 At that time, David saw that the LORD had answered him at Ornan's threshing floor. So he offered sacrifices there. 29 The tent of the LORD, which Moses made in the desert, and the altar for burnt offerings were at the high place in Gibeon. 30 But David could not go there to ask God, because he was afraid of the angel's sword.

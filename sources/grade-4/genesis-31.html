Jacob Hears Laban's Sons

1 Now Jacob heard what Laban's sons said. They said, "Jacob took all that was our father's. From our father's things he got all this wealth." 2 Jacob saw Laban's face. It was not kind to him like before.

The LORD Tells Jacob to Go Home

3 Then the LORD said to Jacob, "Go back to the land of your fathers and to your family. I will be with you."

Jacob Talks to Rachel and Leah

4 So Jacob sent for Rachel and Leah. He called them to the field where his sheep were. 5 He said to them, 'I see your father's face. It is not kind to me like before. But my father's God has been with me. 6 You know I worked hard for your father. 7 Your father tricked me. He changed my pay ten times. But God did not let him hurt me. 8 If he said, "Your pay will be the spotted sheep," then all the sheep had spotted babies. If he said, "Your pay will be the striped sheep," then all the sheep had striped babies. 9 So God took your father's animals and gave them to me. 10 When the sheep were ready to have babies, I looked up and saw a dream. The male sheep that jumped on the flock were striped, spotted, and mixed colors. 11 God's Angel spoke to me in the dream. He said, "Jacob." I said, "Here I am." 12 He said, "Look up now. See, the male sheep that jump on the flock are striped, spotted, and mixed colors. I have seen what Laban is doing to you." 13 "I am the God of Bethel. You poured oil on the stone there. You made a promise to Me. Now get up. Leave this land. Go back to the land where you were born."'

14 Rachel and Leah answered, 'Do we still have any share in our father's house? 15 He treats us like strangers. He sold us. He also used up our money. 16 All the wealth God took from our father is ours and our children's. Now do what God told you.'

Jacob Leaves with His Family

17 Jacob got up. He put his sons and wives on camels. 18 He took all his animals and things. These were things he had gotten in Padan Aram. He went to his father Isaac in Canaan. 19 Laban had gone to cut the wool from his sheep. Rachel stole the small gods that were her father's. 20 Jacob did not tell Laban he was running away. He hid it from Laban. 21 He ran away with all he had. He crossed the river. He went toward the hills of Gilead.

Laban Chases Jacob

22 On the third day, someone told Laban that Jacob had run away. 23 Laban took his relatives and chased him for seven days. He caught up to him in the hills of Gilead. 24 But God came to Laban in a dream at night. God said, "Be careful. Do not say anything good or bad to Jacob." 25 Laban caught up to Jacob. Jacob had put up his tent in the hills. Laban and his relatives put up their tents there too.

Laban and Jacob Argue

26 Laban said to Jacob, 'What have you done? You tricked me. You took my daughters away like prisoners taken in war. 27 Why did you sneak away? Why did you not tell me? I would have sent you away happy, with songs, a drum, and a harp. 28 You did not let me kiss my sons and daughters. You did a foolish thing. 29 I could hurt you. But your father's God spoke to me last night. He said, "Be careful. Do not say anything good or bad to Jacob." 30 Now you have gone because you miss your father's home. But why did you steal my gods?'

31 Jacob answered, 'I was afraid. I thought you might take your daughters from me by force. 32 If you find your gods with anyone, that person will not live. Let our relatives see what is yours with me, and take it.' Jacob did not know Rachel had stolen them.

Laban Looks for His Gods

33 Laban went into Jacob's tent, Leah's tent, and the two servant girls' tents. He did not find the gods. He left Leah's tent and went into Rachel's tent. 34 Rachel had taken the small gods. She put them in a camel saddle. She sat on them. Laban searched the whole tent, but did not find them. 35 She said to her father, 'Do not be angry, sir. I cannot get up. I am having my monthly time.' He searched, but did not find the gods.

36 Jacob was angry. He argued with Laban. Jacob said, 'What did I do wrong? What is my sin? Why did you chase me so hard? 37 You searched all my things. What did you find that is yours? Put it here before our relatives. Let them decide between us. 38 I have been with you twenty years. Your sheep and goats did not lose their babies. I did not eat your male sheep. 39 I did not bring you animals killed by wild animals. I paid for the loss myself. You made me pay for anything stolen by day or night. 40 I worked in the hot sun by day and the cold at night. Sleep left my eyes. 41 I have been in your house twenty years. I worked fourteen years for your two daughters. I worked six years for your sheep. You changed my pay ten times. 42 If my father's God, the God of Abraham and the Fear of Isaac, had not been with me, you would have sent me away with nothing. God saw my hard work and my trouble. He told you to stop last night.'

43 Laban answered, 'These daughters are my daughters. These children are my children. These sheep are my sheep. All you see is mine. But what can I do today to my daughters or to their children? 44 So come. Let us make an agreement, you and I. Let it be a witness between us.'

Jacob and Laban Make an Agreement

45 Jacob took a stone and set it up as a marker. 46 Jacob said to his relatives, 'Gather stones.' They gathered stones and made a pile. They ate there by the pile. 47 Laban called it Jegar Sahadutha. Jacob called it Galeed. 48 Laban said, 'This pile is a witness between us today.' So it was called Galeed. 49 It was also called Mizpah, because he said, 'May the LORD watch between us when we are apart.' 50 'If you hurt my daughters, or take other wives besides my daughters, no one may be with us. See, God is a witness between us.'

51 Laban said, 'Here is this pile. Here is this marker I set up between us. 52 This pile is a witness. This marker is a witness. I will not go past this pile to you. You will not go past this pile and marker to me, to hurt each other.'

53 'The God of Abraham, the God of Nahor, and the God of their father will judge between us.' Jacob swore by the Fear of his father Isaac. 54 Jacob offered a sacrifice on the mountain. He called his relatives to eat bread. They ate bread and stayed all night on the mountain. 55 Early in the morning, Laban got up. He kissed his sons and daughters and blessed them. Then Laban left and went home.

Jonathan and His Helper Attack the Philistines

1 One day Jonathan, Saul's son, said to the young man who carried his weapons, "Come, let's go over to the Philistines' guards on the other side." But he did not tell his father. 2 Saul was sitting under a pomegranate tree in Migron, at the edge of Gibeah. About six hundred men were with him. 3 Ahijah was wearing the special priest vest. He was the son of Ahitub, who was Ichabod's brother. Ahitub was the son of Phinehas, and Phinehas was the son of Eli, the priest of the LORD in Shiloh. The people did not know Jonathan had gone.

4 Between the passes Jonathan wanted to cross to the Philistines, there were sharp rocks on each side. One was called Bozez, and the other was called Seneh. 5 One rock stood to the north, across from Michmash. The other stood to the south, across from Geba.

6 Jonathan said to his armor-bearer, "Come, let's go to the guards of these people who are not God's people. Maybe the LORD will help us. Nothing can stop the LORD from saving, whether by many or by few."

7 His helper said, "Do all that is in your heart. Go ahead; I am with you. My heart is with your heart."

8 Jonathan said, "We will cross over to the men and show ourselves to them. 9 If they say, 'Stay there until we come to you,' then we will stand still and not go up to them. 10 But if they say, 'Come up to us,' then we will go up, for that will be our sign that the LORD has given them into our hands."

11 Both of them showed themselves to the Philistines' guards. The Philistines said, "Look! Hebrews are coming out of the holes where they were hiding."

12 The men at the guard post said to Jonathan and his helper, "Come up to us, and we will teach you a lesson."

Jonathan said to his helper, "Follow me, for the LORD has given them into the hand of Israel."

13 Jonathan climbed up on his hands and feet, and his helper followed. The Philistines fell before Jonathan, and his helper killed them behind him. 14 In that first attack, Jonathan and his helper killed about twenty men in about half a field's length.

15 Then fear came on the camp, in the field, and among all the people. The guards and the attackers were afraid too. The ground shook. It was a very great fear sent by God.

16 Saul's watchmen in Gibeah of Benjamin saw the crowd melting away. They were going here and there. 17 Saul said to the people with him, "Count now and see who has gone from us." They counted, and Jonathan and his armor-bearer were not there. 18 Saul said to Ahijah, "Bring the ark of God." For at that time the ark of God was with the Israelites.

19 While Saul was talking to the priest, the noise in the Philistines' camp kept getting louder and louder. Saul said to the priest, "Stop. Put your hand back."

20 Saul and all the people with him gathered and came to the battle. They saw that every Philistine had turned his sword against his friend. It was a very great confusion. 21 The Hebrews who had been with the Philistines before, and had gone up with them into the camp, turned to join the Israelites who were with Saul and Jonathan. 22 When all the men of Israel who had hidden in the hill country of Ephraim heard that the Philistines were running away, they also chased them in the battle. 23 So the LORD saved Israel that day. The battle passed beyond Beth-aven.

Saul's Rash Promise

24 The men of Israel were worn out that day, for Saul had made the people promise, saying, "Cursed is the man who eats food before evening, before I have punished my enemies." So none of the people tasted food.

25 All the people came into the forest. There was honey on the ground. 26 When the people came into the forest, they saw honey dripping, but no one put his hand to his mouth, because the people feared the oath. 27 But Jonathan had not heard when his father made the people promise. He put out the end of the stick in his hand and dipped it into the honeycomb. He put his hand to his mouth, and his eyes became bright.

28 Then one of the people said, "Your father made the people promise, saying, 'Cursed is the man who eats food today.' That is why the people are so tired."

29 Jonathan said, "My father has brought trouble on the land. See how bright my eyes are because I tasted a little of this honey. 30 If only the people had eaten freely today from the things they took from their enemies! Then the killing of the Philistines would have been much greater."

31 That day they struck down the Philistines from Michmash to Aijalon. The people were very tired.

The People Eat Meat the Wrong Way

32 The people rushed on the spoil. They took sheep, cattle, and calves. They killed them on the ground and ate them with the blood still in them.

33 People told Saul, "Look, the people are sinning against the LORD by eating with the blood."

Saul said, "You have broken faith. Roll a big stone to me today."

34 Saul said, "Go among the people and say, 'Each of you bring your ox or your sheep here. Kill them here and eat. Do not sin against the LORD by eating with the blood.'" So all the people brought their ox with their hand, and they killed it there.

35 Saul built an altar to the LORD. It was the first altar he built to the LORD.

Saul Asks God for Help

36 Saul said, "Let us go down after the Philistines by night. Let us take their things until morning light. Let us not leave one of them."

The people said, "Do whatever seems good to you."

The priest said, "Let us go near to God here."

37 Saul asked God, "Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will You give them into the hand of Israel?" But God did not answer him that day.

38 Saul said, "Come here, all you leaders of the people. Find out and see how this sin happened today. 39 For as the LORD lives, who saves Israel, even if it is in Jonathan my son, he shall surely die." But none of the people answered him.

40 Then he said to all Israel, "You stand on one side, and I and Jonathan my son will stand on the other side."

The people said to Saul, "Do what seems good to you."

41 Saul said to the LORD, the God of Israel, "Give a clear answer." Saul and Jonathan were taken, but the people went free.

42 Saul said, "Draw lots between me and Jonathan my son." Jonathan was taken.

Jonathan Is Spared

43 Saul said to Jonathan, "Tell me what you have done."

Jonathan said, "I only tasted a little honey with the tip of the stick in my hand. Here I am. I am ready to die."

44 Saul said, "May God do so to me, and more, if you do not die, Jonathan."

45 The people said to Saul, "Shall Jonathan die? He has brought this great victory to Israel. No! That must not happen! As the LORD lives, not one hair of his head shall fall to the ground, for he has worked with God today." So the people saved Jonathan, and he did not die.

46 Then Saul went up from following the Philistines, and the Philistines went to their own place.

Saul's Family and Battles

47 When Saul had taken the kingdom over Israel, he fought against all his enemies on every side: against Moab, the Ammonites, Edom, the kings of Zobah, and the Philistines. Wherever he turned, he defeated them. 48 He fought bravely and struck down Amalek. He saved Israel from the hands of those who robbed them. 49 Saul's sons were Jonathan, Ishvi, and Malchishua. His two daughters were named Merab, the older, and Michal, the younger. 50 Saul's wife was Ahinoam, the daughter of Ahimaaz. The leader of his army was Abner, the son of Ner, Saul's uncle. 51 Kish was Saul's father. Ner was Abner's father. Abiel was Ner's father. 52 There was hard fighting against the Philistines all the days of Saul. When Saul saw any strong or brave man, he took him into his service.

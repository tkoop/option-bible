The Workers in the Vineyard

1 For the kingdom of heaven is like a man who owned a vineyard. He went out early in the morning to hire workers for his vineyard. 2 He agreed to pay them one coin for the day. Then he sent them to work in his vineyard.

3 About nine in the morning, he went out again. He saw some other people standing in the town square doing nothing. 4 He said to them, “You go and work in my vineyard too. I will pay you what is right.”

5 So they went. He went out again at noon and at three in the afternoon and did the same thing.

6 About five in the afternoon, he went out and found some other people standing there. He asked them, “Why have you been standing here all day doing nothing?”

7 They said, “No one hired us.”

He said to them, “You go and work in my vineyard too.”

8 When evening came, the owner of the vineyard said to his helper, “Call the workers and pay them. Start with the last ones hired and go to the first.”

9 The workers who were hired about five in the afternoon came. Each one got one coin.

10 When the first workers came, they thought they would get more. But each one also got one coin.

11 When they got their pay, they complained to the owner.

12 They said, “These last workers only worked one hour. You made them equal to us! We worked hard all day in the hot sun.”

13 The owner answered one of them, “Friend, I am not cheating you. Didn't you agree to work for one coin? 14 Take your pay and go. I want to give this last worker the same as I gave you. 15 Can't I do what I want with my own money? Or are you upset because I am kind?”

16 So the last will be first, and the first will be last.

Jesus Tells About His Death

17 Jesus was going up to Jerusalem. On the way, He took the Twelve aside and said to them,

“18 We are going up to Jerusalem. The Son of Man will be handed over to the chief priests and the teachers of the law. They will say He must die. 19 They will hand Him over to people from other nations. They will make fun of Him, beat Him, and put Him on a cross. On the third day, He will rise again.”

A Mother Asks for Places for Her Sons

20 Then the mother of Zebedee's sons came to Jesus. She knelt down and asked Him for something.

21 Jesus said to her, “What do you want?”

She said, “Let these two sons of mine sit one at Your right and one at Your left in Your kingdom.”

22 Jesus said to them, “You do not know what you are asking. Can you go through the hard things I will go through?”

They said, “We can.”

23 Jesus said to them, “You will go through hard things too. But sitting at My right or left is not for Me to give. Those places are for the people My Father has chosen.”

24 When the ten disciples heard this, they were angry with the two brothers.

25 Jesus called them together and said, “You know that the rulers of other nations boss their people around. Their great leaders push people around. 26 It will not be like that with you. Anyone who wants to be great among you must be your servant. 27 Anyone who wants to be first must be your slave. 28 The Son of Man did not come to be served. He came to serve and to give His life to set many people free.”

Two Blind Men Are Healed

29 As Jesus and His disciples were leaving Jericho, a large crowd followed Him.

30 Two blind men were sitting by the road. They heard that Jesus was passing by. They shouted, “Lord, Son of David, please help us!”

31 The crowd told them to be quiet. But they shouted even louder, “Lord, Son of David, please help us!”

32 Jesus stopped and called them. He said, “What do you want Me to do for you?”

33 They said, “Lord, we want to be able to see.”

34 Jesus felt sorry for them. He touched their eyes. Right away they could see, and they followed Him.
